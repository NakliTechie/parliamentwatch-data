REPORT
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the report on its
 behalf, present this Forty-sixth Report, on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the Department of Science & Technology (Ministry of Science & Technology) for the
 year 1997-98 (Demand No. 74).
2. In the meeting of the Committee held on the 2nd April, 1997 the Secretary and other officers of the Department of Science & Technology gave an overview of
 the various activities of the Department and the Committee sought clarifications on various aspects of the performance of the Department particularly in the fields of
 Basic Research and allied­areas and their financial aspects.
3. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the Officers of the Department of Science & Technology for their cooperation answering the queries raised by
 Members on various aspects on programmes initiated by the Department.
4. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on the 22nd April, 1997.
5. The Secretary, Department of Science and Technology, in his presentation before the Committee gave an overview of the thrust areas in which the Department
 is engaged in pursuance of the national objective of making scientific and technological progress for attaining self-reliance. Over the years, the Department and
 autonomous institutions under the Department of Science and Technology have been making consistent efforts in formulation of policy statements and guidelines on
 Science and Technology; promotion of new areas of scientific research; Co-ordination of inter-departmental Science and Technological efforts; Support basic and
 applied research in universities/labs/institutions; International Science and Technology co-operation; Support high quality research in autonomous bodies; Catalysing
 Science and Technology interaction in societal programmes, entrepreneurship development; Science popularisation, communication and Science and Technology
 Management Information System; Promotion of commercialisation of home grown technology; support to technology development programmes, technology
 information and assessment; providing scientific services in the areas of Surveying and Mapping through its autonomous organisations e.g. Survey of India (SOl) and
 National Atlas Thematic and Mapping Organisations (NATMO); Meteorology through India Meteorological Department and National Centre for Medium Range
 Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) and Technology Development Board (TDB).
6. After listening to the presentation made by the Secretary, Department of Science and Technology, the Committee examined the budgetary allocation of the
 Department for the year 1997-98* While considering the Demands for Grants of the Department, the Committee had an opportunity to review the work of various
 projects and programmes which fall within the mandate of the Department.
7. The Budget Estimates of 1997-98 have registered an increase from Rs. 242.55 crores in the previous year to Rs. 268.00 crores on plan side and from Rs. 238.50
 crores to Rs. 291.87 crores on Non-Plan side. The increase in Plan provision of 1997-98 as compared to RE 1996­97 is mainly due to undertaking of increased Plan
 activities under Technology Missions, India Meteorological Department, Integrated Long Term Programme of Cooperation in S&T between India and the Republics
 of Commonwealth of Independent States; enhanced financial support to Autonomous Institutions and Professional bodies.

8. The Secretary informed the Committee that in the field of international co-operation, there are three types of programmes viz., Integrated long-term programme
 of co-operation in Science & Technology with India and Republic of Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS), programmes of co-operation with the developed
 countries and lastly technical co­operation with the developing countries. Among the foremost areas in which the Government have co-operation with CIS countries
 are material technology, bio-technology, energy related issues and information technology. The Secretary further informed that they were facing some problems
 because of severe financial crunch existing in CIS countries whereas with developed countries they have equal partnership.
9. The Committee is of the view that the Department should devise programmes in such a way so that international co-operation in the field of Science and
 Technology will enable us to establish technology information centres in the long run which may facilitate not only training of our scientific community but will also
 lead to creation of centres with basic infrastructure in the field of research.
9.1 The Committee would like to reiterate its earlier recommendations as contained in its 41st report that the scientists of CIS countries today are migrating to
 developed countries in search of better opportunities. The Department should devise some programmes whereby their services could be utilised productively. The
 Committee recommends that the Department should not only bear all the expenses of the scientists who give us technology and blue-print, but also should devise a
 compensation package for them.
9.2 The Committee observes that at the same time Science & Technology has vast scope in the development of a country. Invention of a wheel has solved many
 of the problems facing the mankind which has brought tremendous transformation in the pattern of iudustrialisation of countries. As the nation progresses they come
 across problems in simplifying industrial processes and solving technological problems that have never cropped up earlier.
9.3 Level of development of a country in the field of Science & Technology plays a vital role in solving these problems and the ones that may crop up. It is
 therefore necessary to ensure rapid development in the field of technology which can be brought about by the trained work force available in the country.
9.4 Technical development would require adequate infrastructure in the form of laboratories, technical institutions and building of a scientific temper in the
 country. Latter is correlated with the former as much as former is with the latter.
9.5 The Committee in its 38th report recognised the need for developing infrastructure in the form of laboratories and technical institutions which should be
 accessible to lay men and student community for experimenting their ideas in different fields of technology. The Committee reiterates that DST should make a sincere
 beginning by initiating steps for making available a few existing laboratories to students and lay men alike under the supervision of officers and staff managing these
 laboratories. At best these laboratories could, to begin with, charge nominal fees for their use. So far as students are concerned they should be encouraged to use these
 laboratories without making any payment.
10. The Committee was informed that the mandate of the Technology Development Board (TDB) is to commercialise indigenously developed technologies on
 large-scale application of imported technologies having a commercial angle to it. The Secretary also informed the Committee that after evaluation of some 50 odd
 proposals nearly 5 of them have already been cleared and financial help has already been released.
The Committee feels satisfied that the Technology Department Board has finally sanctioned some of the projects and recommends that the Department should
 ensure that the funds allocated to TDB should be fully utilised in the current year which may facilitate R&D linkages with industry as well as be a source of revenue
 for future technological innovation.
11. While the Committee seeks to ensure that the Department of Science and Technology is not starved of funds in order to carry out its scientific and research
 programmes, the attention of the Committee was invited to some of the audit observations regarding non-receipt of utilisation-certificates to the tune of Rs. 11,553
 crores since 1977. The Secretary informed the Committee that a special drive was launched in the present year and as of 1995-96 there were no pending utilisation
 certificates.
12. The Committee is of the view that the Department should immediately stop release of funds to grants-in-aid institutions unless pending utilisation certificates
 of the current year duly certified are made available.
13. The Committee observes that' the demand made by the department under Societal programmes is Rs. 20.00 crores as compared to Rs. 26.02 crores last year.
 The Committee is of the view that the DST should explore the possibility of implementation of these programmes through networking with their scheme jointly with
 other Ministries/Departments.
14. As regards the Demands for India Meteorological Department (IMD) the Committee is of the view that they are inadequate. The functioning of the
 Department has to be more accurate and the reports have to be updated. It has been observed that Department's failure in its predictions are attributed to change in
 climatic conditions. The Committee is strongly of the view that the Department should improve its system so that it can update the information and provide accurate
 predictions as far as possible and well in time.
15. The Department, the Committee  was informed, was conscious of the problem of industrial pollution. The Department was carrying out National leather
 mission to achieve efficiency in sewage treatment in leather industries. Another project initiated is that of use of natural gas instead of coal-based energy sources and
 the research was being conducted by National Meteorological Laboratory.
The Committee is of the view that DST should encourage R&D institutions to look at problems of industrial pollution which are specific to individual
 areas1ndustries and come out with solutions.
16. The Secretary while explaining the question of linkage between R&D and user community outlined some achievements e.g. the Bose Institute, Calcutta in
 regard to its wave particle duality in the filed of Photon scatering in physics; Agharkar Research Institute, Pune's contribution in development of disease-resistant
 wheat and soyabean and the development of heart-valve by Sree Chitra Tirunal Institute for Medical Sciences & Technology, Thiruvananthapuram, etc.

The Committee appreciates the efforts of Autonomous Scientific Institutions in disseminating the results of their research but feels that the linkages which enable
 the flow of knowledge generated by scientific institutions to the industry move at a snail's pace. The linkages between industry and scientific institutions should be
 strengthened so that the outcome is reflected in shortest possible time.
17. After considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Science & Technology and assessing the progress of various important programmes and
 projects administered by it, the Committee recommends that the Demands for Grants of Rs. 559.87 crores, as sought by the Department of Science & Technology, for
 the year 1997-98, under Demand No. 74, may be approved.
