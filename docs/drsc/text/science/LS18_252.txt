REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of
Biotechnology on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred and Forty-fifth Report of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the Demands for Grants (2013-14) of the Department of Biotechnology, which
was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 8
th May, 2013. There were seventeen
recommendations in the Two Hundred and Forty Fifth Report of the Committee. Action Taken
notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on
9th December, 2013. The Department has intimated that it has accepted all the recommendations of
the Committee. The Committee, on examination of the Action Taken Notes, however, is not
satisfied with Action Taken by the Department in respect of three recommendations and would like
the Department to take further action in respect of such recommendations as indicated in this
report.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee
on the Action Taken by the Department of Biotechnology are set out in the subsequent paragraphs:–
Recommendation (Para 3.3)
The committee appreciates the Department for putting in place an innovative online system
i.e. “EPROMIS” since 2011-12 for the registration and monitoring of projects. The Committee is
also appreciative of the good results obtained by the Department in the R&D assessment for the
years 2010-11 and 2011-12, which suggests that various mechanisms put in place by the
Department have worked effectively in these two years. The Committee hopes that the Department
would aim and achieve even better RFD scores in future.
Action Taken by Government
The Department while noting the appreciation by the committee on the past performance,
would continue to perform as per directives given by the committee through further strengthening
of project management and monitoring system.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 4.2)
The Committee notes that there is a significant increase of 143.89% in allocation of this
Department for the Twelfth Plan over the allocation made for the Eleventh Plan, though it
substantially falls short of the outlay of R18,000 crores sought by the Department for the Twelfth
Plan period. The Committee is happy to note that the Department during the Plan period inter-alia
proposes promotion of new generation biotech industries and sustained support to public private
partnership, which are amongst the thrust areas of the Department for the Twelfth Five Year Plan.
The Committee hopes that the Department through its concerted efforts would be able to promote
public private partnership in a significant way and help establish new generation biotech industries
on a large scale which will not only generate employment but would also help country reap in a
big way the benefits of new generation biotech industries.

2
Action Taken by Government
Public private partnership schemes started during the Eleventh Plan are continuing for
Twelfth Plan with new modalities of funding to promote modern biotechnology industries targeting
agriculture and health care products. To promote increased academia – industry interactions several
supporting schemes such as strengthening of incubators, ignition grant for fresh entrepreneurs,
international collaborative ventures with major non-governmental scientific and technological
funding agencies such as Bill Gate and Malinda Foundation, Wellcome Trust UK are also
implemented in the Twelfth Plan. Thus, action has already been taken in the direction given by the
Committee.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 5.3)
The Committee notes with concern the reduction of plan allocation of the Department at
R.E. stage in the years 2011-12 and 2012-13 despite good utilization of allocated funds by the
Department. If this trend is not reversed immediately, at the end of the Twelfth Five Year Plan,
the Department would end up having a plan outlay which will be much less than the approved plan
outlay of the Department and this in turn would adversely impact achievement of targets set by
the Department for the Twelfth Five Year Plan. The Committee is of the view of that growing
international stature of India in the field of biotechnology should not suffer a setback on account
of fund crunch and accordingly, recommends that no reduction should be made in the approved
plan outlay of the Department and reduced plan allocation for the year 2012-2013 should be
balanced by the higher allocation at R.E. stage in the year 2013-14.
Action Taken by Government
The observations of the committee have been taken up with Planning Commission and
Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance. It was informed that department may take-up the
additional demand for activities that could not be taken-up due to shortage of funds.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department and hope that Department would
be able to pursue and convince the concerned authorities for increase in allocation at R.E.
stage in the year 2013-2014.
Recommendation (Para 6.5)
The Committee notes with concern low level of private sector investment in R&D and is
of the view that Department should effectively leverage its Public Private Partnership Programme
to catalyze increasing private sector investment in R&D in Biotechnology. The Committee notes
with appreciation that through Biotechnology Industry Partnership Programme of the Department,
85 companies are already investing in 102 projects and recommends that the Department of
Biotechnology should pursue such programmes, which would build a conducive environment for
increased private sector investment in R&D in Biotechnology, so that the national goal of increasing
expenditure in Research and Development in S&T to 2% of GDP in next five years is realized. The
Committee is of the firm opinion that national aspiration of positioning India among the top five
global scientific powers by 2020 can only be realized by significantly augmentation investment in

3
R&D in S&T and accordingly, the Committee recommends that the Planning Commission must
provide sufficient funds to the R&D programme of the Department to meet this national objective.
Action Taken by Government
The observations of the committee have been communicated to planning commission.
Meanwhile, DBT has taken action in establishing joint technical and financial collaborations with
several countries for value addition in development of technologies and products along with needed
human resource development.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that action taken by the Department would
yield the desired results.
Recommendation (Para 7.6)
The Committee is happy to note that in response to its recommendation contained in
228
th Report, the Department has as a part of its efforts to address the issue of financial
sustainability of the centre, constituted a high-level Committee to examine the matters related to
operation of the International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology (ICGEB) and the
high level Committee would inter-alia  recommend the possible restructured model for the
sustainability of the institution on a long-term basis. The Committee notes that the main thrust of
the centre is to carry out basic research in the areas of human diseases and agriculture focusing
mainly on research relevant to the Indian sub-continental and considering the importance of work
entrusted to the centre, the Committee is keen that the centre functions on a self sustained basis
and accordingly, recommends that the Department should ensure that the restructured model of the
centre makes it financially viable on self sustainable basis.
Action Taken by Government
A meeting of the high level committee under the chairmanship of Deputy Chairman,
Planning Commission with Member (S&T), Planning Commission, Senior officials of Department
of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance, Legal and Treaty Division of Ministry of External Affairs and
DBT was held in August, 2013. Various recommendations to retain international character of New
Delhi component of ICGEB; arranging funds from Government of India during transition period;
exploring funds from other national and international sources; rationalizing the staff number and
continuation of pensions; and income tax exceptions were made. A cabinet note is being moved
from DBT for necessary approval which also includes the directives of the committee.
Comments
The Committee notes the progress made in the case and would like to be apprised
of the status of the matter during the examination of the next Demands for Grants of the
Department.
Recommendation (Para 8.6)
The Committee is aware of the pressing need for finding non-conventional sources of clean
energy and is happy to know that Department in collaboration with the Institute of Chemical
Technology, Mumbai had succeeded in developing bio-ethanol technology and that technology
would become available for commercial exploitation by the end of this year. The Committee

4
congratulates the Department on its success and hopes that the Department would be in a position
to make available the technology for commercial use by the year end. The Committee further
recommends that the Department should earnestly explore and expedite its Research and
Development in the field of bio-energy and bio-fuels for developing a cost effective, viable
commercial alternative for conventional fuel at the earliest. The Committee also recommends that
Government should provide adequate funds to the Department so that resource crunch does not
come in the way of the Department in meeting this goal.
Action Taken by Government
The department while noting the appreciation by the committee on the performance of
schemes related to biofuels and bioenergy, as recommended by the Committee necessary steps have
been taken to ensure that the sector is given high priority in terms of funding by consolidating
other programmes and reprioritizing the scheme within the budget allocated.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 9.3)
The Committee is happy at the declaration of 2010-20 as the ‘Decade of Innovation’, and
hopes that it would help create an environment in all the research institutions for fostering
innovation. The Committee is pleased to note that BIRAC since its formation in March, 2012 has,
through its various schemes been able to support 15 startups and 60 Small and Medium
Enterprises. The Committee hopes that BIRAC would speed up its efforts to support more and
more, small and medium enterprises to become globally competitive.
Action Taken by Government
As per the directives of the Committee, the efforts have been made to provide supporting
infrastructure for cost effective operation for SMEs through establishment of incubators,
supporting State biotechnology parks research resources, and service facilities for various R&D
activities. Special calls for proposals in the area of healthcare, nutrition and agriculture
biotechnologies in collaboration with international agencies have also been made recently for
facilitating setting up of more SMEs. The process would continue in this direction during remaining
period of Twelfth Plan also.
Comments
The Committee is happy to note the steps taken by the Department to provide
supporting infrastructure for cost effective operation for SMEs and hope that the
Department would continue to achieve encouraging results in this area.
Recommendation (Para 10.5)
The Committee is of the opinion that the development of skilled and quality human resource
to cater to the rising needs of the biotech industry, which has grown many folds over the last few
years, is a major challenge for the country. The Committee commends the human resource
development initiatives of the Department, such as the initiation of the three year M.Sc./MBA dual
degree programme in Biotechnology and Bio-enterprise Management, the Biotech Education and Skill
Development Council and its ‘Star College Scheme’ etc. The Committee is further happy to note

5
that the Star College Scheme of the Department has led to increase in the number of students
opting for life science courses, increased pass percentage and enhanced enrolment of students for
higher studies in science courses and all candidates trained by it in Biotech Industries have been
absorbed in the industry. The Committee recommends that the Department should be provided
adequate funds by the Government for its Human Resources Development Programme.
Action Taken by Government
Human resource development scheme is highest priority of the Department. In order to
meet the funding requirement, the Department has made adequate provisions in the regular scheme
“Human Resource Development” and in additions provisions are also made in other scheme such
as “Researc and  Development” for Purposes of subject specific HR programmes.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply and hopes that the positive approach shown
by the Department would help availability of quality human resource for the biotech
industry.
Recommendation (Para 11.3)
The Committee notes the number of research publications of the Department and hopes that
in consonance with the Science, Technology and Innovation Policy, 2013, the Department would
gear its efforts towards doubling the global share of its publications and quadrupling the existing
number of its papers in the top 1% journals by 2020.
Action Taken by Government
It may be informed that as such there is no database even at global level to measure directly
the research publications of biotechnology. Indirectly, therefore overall life science research and
development related performance in terms of publications and citation in international scientific
index journals is taken as criteria. According to the database of country rankings of scientific
publications based on SCOUPS database during 1996-2011 period, India published a total of 70,195
publication in the basic research areas of biochemistry, genetics and molecular biology and was
ranked 10 followed by China (ranked 5), USA (ranked 1).
Similarly in case of applied healthcare, agriculture and biological sciences India ranked
11 with 70,753 publications wherein China was at ranked 3 and Brazil ranked 10.
Comments
The Committee notes India’s ranking in the field of basic and other sciences. Its
recommendation for doubling or quadrupling its papers/publications in top journals was
meant to see Indian biotechnologists making the country proud by getting recognition
through research, publication, paper etc. The Committee hopes that the Department would
endeavour to reach that level and set high standards of research papers in its areas of
operation.
Recommendation (Para 12.3)
The Committee appreciates the programme initiated by the Department to promote the use
of biotechnological processes and tools for the benefit of entrepreneurs and to create a platform
for employment generation among the target population and diffusion of proven and field-tested

6
technologies through demonstration, training and extension activities. The Committee also notes
with appreciation that the successful examples of the programme inter-alia  included the running
of mobile ambulatory clinics for the farmers, low cost poly house construction for raising nursery-
planting material for cultivation of medicinal and aromatic plants etc. The Committee, however,
would like the Department to develop a mechanism to actually assess as to what extent the
programme has actually helped ‘benefited persons’ to get gainfully employed or become
entrepreneurs.
Action Taken by Government
As suggested by the committee, a survey will be undertaken based on primary and
secondary data collection to assess the extent the programme has actually helped ‘benefited
persons’ to get gainfully employed or become entrepreneurs during the period of Eleventh
Plan (2007-2012).
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department. The Committee desires that the
above survey should be undertaken at the earliest and the findings of the survey may be
brought to the notice of the Committee.
Recommendation (Para 13.4)
The Committee is pleased to know that many useful products such as insect resistant
chickpea; bio-fertiliser for rice and wheat; bio-pesticide for legume pests and biosensor for
pesticide residue detection, have been developed as a result of Indo-Swiss programme and these
products are being pursued for regulatory clearances and commercialization using PPP mode. The
Committee recommends that the Department should focus more on international collaboration that
would bring direct benefit to the country particularly those that would lead to technology transfer
to Indian industries.
Action Taken by Government
The department has noted the suggestions of the committee and would accordingly expand
international collaboration activities. Under the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council
(BIRAC) a technology acquisition fund is provided and a mechanism has also been put in place.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 14.3)
The Committee takes note of the proposal of the Department to set up five new Centres
of Excellence for translational science and high-level innovative research in the cutting edge areas
of biotechnology as also the proposal to setup over five Technology Transfer Organization (TTO)
spread across the country for supporting IPR cells. The Committee is aware of the urgent need
for augmenting and strengthening of the basic and translational research capacity in areas of
biotechnology and therefore the initiatives taken by the Department in this direction are welcome.
The Committee, however, would like to caution the Department that its Centre of Excellence should
be manned by deserving and capable professionals, appointed solely on merit, so that high level
innovative research could be carried out in these centres and purpose of establishing these centres
could be achieved.

7
Action Taken by Government
To ensure that centres of excellence are manned by deserving and capable professionals, the
department has already been very selective in supporting such projects. An elaborate mechanism of
technical and financial appraisal through a three tier competitive grant mechanisms is in place and
the same has been strengthened in the recent months as suggested by the committee.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department and hopes that the mechanisms
mentioned by the Department are followed in letter and spirit and yield the desired results.
Recommendation (Para 15.3)
The Committee commends the Department on very encouraging results achieved from its
SBIRI and BIPP programmes. The Committee is pleased to note that SBIRI programmes have led
to development of 20 technologies out of which 6 had already been commercialized. The success
and popularity of BIPP programme in evident from the fact that 60% of costs of projects
undertaken under the programme have been funded by private sector. The Committee recommends
that Department should take large number of projects under these programme so that a large base
of biotech industries is established in the country and greater private sectors investment flows in
R&D in Biotechnology, apart from substantially increasing India’s share in global trade in
biotechnology.
Action Taken by Government
The recommendation of the committee shall be implemented and adequate budget provision
has already been for the purpose.
Comments
The Committee is happy to note the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 16.4)
The Committee notes that the Department has been mandatorily allocating 10% of its total
Budgetary outlay since the year 2007-08, for the projects/schemes specially designed for the benefit
of the North-Eastern region and Sikkim, for Human Resource Development, Biotechnology
infrastructure and Research and Development in priority areas of North East in collaboration and
partnership with other public sector institutions and universities and even the private sector. The
Committee recommends that the Department should carry out a study to assess to what extent its
programmes in North-Eastern States and Sikkim have been able to achieve their objectives and
goals so that based on the results of this study, if needed, programmes could be further dovetailed
to the specific needs and requirements of the region and made more useful and effective.
Action Taken by Government
The Department as directed by the committee has already initiated required action.
Comments
The Committee would like to be apprised of the findings of the action initiated by
the Department to assess the extent its programmes in North-Eastern States and Sikkim
have been able to achieve their objectives and goals.

8
Recommendation (Para 16.9)
The Committee appreciates the action taken by the Department on its aforesaid
recommendation. The Committee is happy that the Department is making sincere efforts to
implement industry oriented development programmes in Public Private Partnership mode in North
East and hopes this would help in growth of Biotech industries in North East and help in
development of this region. The Committee is also happy to note that in 2012-13 several projects
have been funded in universities in biotechnology deficient States such as Bihar, Chhattisgarh and
Madhya Pradesh for overall development of life sciences department in these universities. The
Committee recommends that it should also implement industry oriented development in Public
Private Partnership mode in other backward regions of the country including Himalayan region to
help growth of Biotech industries in these regions as well. The Committee, further recommends
that in order to assess the potency and utility of its training programmes, the Department should
develop a mechanism to find out as to what extent these training programmes have actually resulted
in trained persons becoming gainfully employed or becoming entrepreneurs because that alone will
demonstrate the usefulness of these Programmes.
Action Taken by Government
The observations of the committee have been noted and it may be informed that in all the
programmes funded in biotechnology deficient regions, an in-built monitoring mechanism of experts
has been constituted to measure the achievements against the objectives and suggest corrective
mechanisms. Further, as suggested by the committee efforts will be made to popularize the existing
public-private partnership schemes in the regions unreached through ongoing road shows and
workshops mechanism.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 17.2)
The Committee is happy to note that the Department has become sufficiently sensitized of
the risk of bio-terrorism and has initiated steps to deal with it. The Committee is also pleased to
know of the setting up of Bio-safety Laboratories, which are highly secured. The Committee hopes
that further necessary measures in this respect would be taken by the Government at the earliest.
Action Taken by Government
Strengthening biosafety assessment and monitoring is integral part of the proposed
Biotechnology Regulatory Authority of India Bill, 2013 referred to Parliament Standing Committee
of S&T and ES for examination. Pending enactment of the bill, the DBT as directed by the
planning commission is also establishing an elaborate biosafety assessment and training secretariat
as a non-statutory entity to support the ongoing efforts under the existing regulatory frame-work.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply of the Department.
Recommendation (Para 18)
The Committee after examining the various aspects of the Demands for Grants (2013-14)
of the Department of Bio-Technology, is of the view that the Department, through its various

9
schemes and programmes, apart from promoting research and development in biotechnology, has
demonstrated the ability to use this technology for societal benefit. Some of the initiatives of the
Departments discussed above are commendable and if properly implemented will bring immense
benefits to the targeted groups. The Committee, while taking note of it, is of the opinion that
programmes of the Department deserve to be adequately funded by Government.
Action Taken by Government
The department noted with encouragement the observations of the committee on the overall
implementation of the mandate of DBT and has informed suggestions of the committee to
adequately fund the Department to Planning Commission.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Department and hopes that the Department
gets adequate budgetary support from the Government to fulfil its objectives to the
maximum possible extent.
