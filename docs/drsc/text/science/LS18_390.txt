1. INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Department of Science & Technology (DST) stands as the premier nodal agency in 
India dedicated to fortifying the nation's Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI) landscape. It 
plays a pivotal role in identifying critical gaps within the S&T sectors, formulating strategic plans 
and policies to address societal needs, and fostering emerging areas of science and technology to 
meet future demands. DST acts as a vital bridge, connecting the science and technology domains 
with various Government horizontals and verticals, academia, Research and Development (R&D) 
laboratories, institutions, and industries, ensuring a cohesive and collaborative ecosystem. 
1.2 With the overarching goal of enhancing India's S&T capacity and capability, DST 
spearheads the largest extramural R&D support in the country. This is achieved through three 
comprehensive umbrella schemes: (i) Science and Technology (S&T) Institutional and Human 
Capacity Building, (ii) Research and Development, and (iii) Innovation, Technology Development, 
and Deployment. These schemes are designed to empower scientists across diverse institutions and 
disciplines through a competitive framework, thereby reinforcing India's education system, 
scientific and industrial R&D. 
1.3 In a significant move to streamline operations and enhance flexibility in fund allocation, 
these three umbrella schemes have been consolidated into a single, unified initiative named ‘Vigyan 
Dhara’. This integration ensures a more efficient and adaptive flow of resources across programs, 
fostering greater innovation and impact. 
1.4 DST remains steadfast in its mission to strengthen the national STI ecosystem, positioning 
India as a global leader in science and technology. By driving positive transformations, DST aims 
to contribute to a safer, more secure, and prosperous society, while proactively preparing for future 
challenges and disruptions. Through its relentless efforts, DST continues to catalyze advancements 
that not only address current societal needs but also pave the way for a sustainable and 
technologically advanced future. 
1.5 The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, 
Environment, Forests and Climate Change considered the Demands for Grants (2025-26) of the 
Department of Science & Technology in its meeting held on 19th February, 2025.

2.9 The National Research Foundation (NRF), which was initially allocated ₹2,000 crore in 
2024-25 (BE), had its revised allocation drastically reduced to just ₹200 crore, with no 
expenditure recorded till 31st January 2025. However, the same ₹2,000 crore allocation has been 
reinstated for 2025-26. The Committee enquired the Department for reasons. The Department 
submitted that the provisions of the ANRF Act were notified on 5th Day of February, 2024 and 
with this the ANRF came into existence. After operationalization, the Executive Council (EC) and 
the Governing Board (GB) of ANRF were constituted and notified by the Government on 
29.06.2024. The first meeting of Executive Council (EC) of ANRF and the Governing Board (GB) 
of ANRF were held on 22.08.2024 and 10.09.2024 respectively. Thereafter, ANRF initiated call 
for online proposals during the period from October, 2024 to February, 2025 under the following 
new ANRF schemes; namely (i) Prime Minister Early Career Research Grant (PMECRG) (ii) 
Mission for Advancement in High-impact Areas (MAHA): EV-Mission, (iii) Inclusivity Research 
Grant (IRG)  and (iv) Partnerships for Accelerated Innovation and Research (PAIR) and these 
research proposals are under scrutiny/finalization at various stages for funding by ANRF. As 
ANRF was left with only two months (February-March) in FY 2024-25, the budget allocation, 
was reduced from ₹2000 crore (BE 2024-25) to ₹200 crore in RE 2024-25, keeping the financial 
prudence in mind. For the Financial Year 2025-26, the Department will ensure that the entire 
budget allocation of ₹2000 crore (BE 2025-26) would be utilized fully for funding the schemes 
already initiated and the new schemes to be introduced during the year 2025-26, under the 
guidance/directions of EC, ANRF. 
2.10  The Committee emphasizes the need to ensure that administrative and procedural 
challenges do not become significant obstacles to the Department's planned activities in the 
long term. It, therefore, recommends that the Department exercise due diligence in 
managing its expenditures to guarantee optimal fund utilization for 2025-26 while ensuring 
the successful achievement of its set objectives. Furthermore, the Committee urges the 
Department to make every possible effort in the current financial year to compensate for the 
suboptimal performance recorded during 2024-25.

3. RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT & INNOVATION FUND 
3.1 In the Interim Budget for the fiscal year 2024-25, a ₹1 lakh crore corpus was announced to 
promote private sector research and innovation in deep tech and sunrise sector. This corpus was 
intended to provide long-term financing or refinancing at low or zero interest rates through a 50-
year interest-free loan. 
3.2 Building on this initiative, the Budget for FY 2025-26 introduced the Research, 
Development, and Innovation (RDI) Fund with a budgetary allocation of ₹20,000 crore. This fund 
is designed to facilitate long-term financing or refinancing with extended tenors and minimal or 
zero interest rates for science, technology, and other research-driven initiatives. The objective is to 
incentivize the private sector to expand its research endeavors and position India as a global leader 
in research, development, and innovation within emerging domains. The fund aims to support 
programs that leverage the potential of India's youth and technological advancements. 
3.3 Under this scheme, a Special Purpose Fund would be set up and implemented by 
Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) for funding Startups and private sector 
corporates. The focus and coverage of RDI Scheme is envisaged to be in sunrise sectors which are 
likely to have transformative and strong ripple impact on the economy, besides having potential for 
high growth and commercial success. The identification of sectors and sub-sectors, for the purpose 
of funding, will also account for the Indian context including the economic landscape, level of 
technological advancement and the societal needs. The relevance for global value chain integration 
as well as potential for creating market access shall also be considered. 
3.4 The Department submitted that the specific sectors and sub-sectors to be supported by the 
Scheme would be identified upfront.  Some of the sunrise sectors have been identified for 
consideration of funding under the Scheme which may  subject to further refinement include 1) 
Energy security/transition and climate action 2) “Deep Technology” including quantum 
computing, robotics and  space  3) Artificial Intelligence and its application to Indian problems 
including  in agriculture, health, and education  4) Biotechnology, biomanufacturing, synthetic 
biology, pharma, medical devices  5) Digital economy including Digital agriculture, technologies 
whose indigenization for strategic reasons or for economic security and any other sector or 
technology where it is deemed necessary in  public interest. 
3.5 The Department plans to allocate ₹20,000 crore as a 50-year interest-free loan to a special 
purpose fund. This fund will be managed by second-level fund managers, including alternate 
investment funds, fund of funds, and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). These managers 
will invest in deep tech sectors such as clean energy, bio-manufacturing, quantum computing, AI, 
sustainable agriculture, gene therapy, and computational drug discovery, either through loans or 
equity investments in start-ups and private companies. A key condition is that private sector

4.2 ANRF has launched several initiatives – (i) the Prime Minister Early Career Research Grant

Grant (IRG) – This scheme will provide funding support to researchers belonging to the Scheduled 
Caste /Scheduled Tribe to undertake research in frontier areas of science and engineering.

5. NATIONAL QUANTUM MISSION (NQM)  
5.1 The Department informed the Committee that one of the thrust areas for the year 2025-26 is 
National Quantum Mission (NQM). It was approved by the Union Cabinet on 19th April 2023 at a 
total cost of ₹6003.65 crore for a period of eight years from 2023-24 to 2030-31.  
 
5.2 Under the Mission, four Thematic Hubs comprising of 14 Technical Groups having 17 
Project Teams across India, involving nearly 152 researchers from 43 institutions have been 
established, each dedicated to a specific technology vertical:  
(i) Quantum Computing at IISc. Bengaluru; 
(ii) Quantum Communication at IIT Madras in association with C-DOT, New Delhi; 
(iii) Quantum Sensing & Metrology at IIT Bombay; and 
(iv) Quantum Materials & Devices at IIT Delhi 

10 
 
5.3 The Mission objectives include developing intermediate-scale quantum computers with 20-
50 physical qubits in 3 years, 50-100 physical qubits in 5 years and 50-1000 physical qubits in 8 
years in various platforms like superconducting and photonic technology. Satellite-based secure 
quantum communications between ground stations over a range of 2000 kilometers within India, 
long-distance secure quantum communications with other countries, inter-city quantum key 
distribution over 2000 km as well as multi-node Quantum networks with quantum memories are 
also some of the deliverables of the Mission. The National Quantum Mission will focus on 
developing magnetometers with high sensitivity in atomic systems and atomic clocks for precision 
timing, communications, and navigation. It will also support the design and synthesis of quantum 
materials such as superconductors, novel semiconductor structures, and topological materials for the 
fabrication of quantum devices. Single photon sources/detectors, and entangled photon sources will 
also be developed for quantum communications, sensing, and metrological applications. The 
Mission would greatly benefit various sectors including communication, health, financial, energy 
with applications in drug design, space, banking, security etc. The Mission will also provide a huge 
boost to National priorities like Digital India, Make in India, Skill India and Stand-up India, Start-
up India, Self-reliant India and Sustainable Development Goals (SDG). 
5.4 The Committee enquired about the physical achievements of the Mission, and was informed 
that IIT Bombay has established the section – 8 Company in the area of Quantum Sensing & 
Metrology namely Qmet Tech Foundation in October 2024. Similarly, IIT Madras in association 
with C-DoT, New Delhi has established IITM CDOT Samgnya Technologies Foundation in 
December 2024 in the area of Quantum Communication. 
5.5 The Committee notes that the ₹ 427 crores were allocated in B.E. 2024-25 which were 
significantly reduced to ₹ 86 crores and Department could spent only 64.30 per cent of the 
R.E. till 31st January, 2025. Even after such gross under-utilization of funds, ₹ 600 crores has 
been allocated for this Mission in B.E. 2025-26. The Committee enquired about the increased 
budget allocation for the National Quantum Mission (NQM) in B.E. 2025-26 and the 
Department informed that the increased allocations will play a pivotal role in expanding and 
scaling up ongoing initiatives while accelerating the development of a robust quantum 
technology ecosystem in India. A significant portion of this funding will be dedicated to 
strengthening the four established Thematic Hubs (T-Hubs), each focused on a key vertical—
Quantum Computing, Quantum Communication, Quantum Sensing & Metrology, and 
Quantum Materials & Devices. The enhanced funding will empower these T-Hubs to fulfill 
their mandates effectively by: Technology Development, Human Resource Development, 
Entrepreneurship Development and Industry Collaborations and International 
Collaborations. 
5.6 The Committee acknowledges that the launch of a dedicated Mission for research in 
Quantum Technologies is a strategic and forward-looking initiative, aligning with global 
technological advancements. However, the underutilization of allocated funds raises concerns 
about the Department's commitment and operational efficiency. The Committee emphasizes 
the need for the Department to adopt a more proactive and results driven approach to ensure 
the Mission's success. Rather than merely playing catch-up to Google’s Willow and 
Microsoft’s Majorana 1, the Committee strongly recommends that the Department adopt an 
ambitious vision to position India as a global leader in Quantum Technology.

calibrations and tests, the first light was obtained in early February and since then, the instrument 
has been making scientific observations.

Advanced Research Centre for Powder Metallurgy and New Materials (ARCI) in advancing 
research across critical domains such as energy storage, nanomaterials, engineered coatings, 
additive manufacturing, and advanced powder metallurgy. The successful development and 
technology transfer of battery-grade Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) as a cathode material for 
Li-ion batteries to M/s. Altmin Pvt. Ltd., Hyderabad, marks a commendable step towards 
strengthening India's indigenous battery manufacturing capabilities.                         (Para 6.8) 
 
In view of the growing demand for energy storage solutions, the Committee 
recommends that the Department provide enhanced support to ARCI for scaling up its 
research and accelerating commercialization efforts in battery technology and other advanced 

22 
 
materials. The Committee also emphasizes the need for wider industry collaboration, strategic 
partnerships, and policy interventions to facilitate domestic production, reduce import 
dependency, and position India as a global leader in energy storage technologies. 
Furthermore, it is imperative to ensure that such technological advancements are leveraged 
for broader industrial applications and made accessible to multiple stakeholders, thereby 
fostering self-reliance and sustainability in the sector.                                                    (Para 6.9) 
 
The Committee recognizes the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA) as a premier 
institution dedicated to research and development in Astronomy, Astrophysics, and 
Astronomy Instrumentation. The successful fabrication and verification of the first 1.44-
meter mirror segment for the international Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT) at the India-TMT 
Optics Fabrication Facility is a significant achievement, demonstrating India's capability in 
high-precision optics and advanced astronomical instrumentation.                            (Para 6.10) 
The Committee recommends that the Department extend continued support to IIA for 
the successful completion of India’s commitments to the TMT project and other international 
collaborations in astrophysics. Additionally, the Committee urges the Department to explore 
opportunities for further strengthening indigenous expertise in astronomical instrumentation, 
including the development of next-generation telescopes and space-based observational 
facilities. Ensuring adequate funding, infrastructure, and skilled workforce development will 
be crucial for positioning India as a global leader in cutting-edge astronomical research and 
technology.                                                                                                                          (Para 6.11) 
 
The Committee commends the Raman Research Institute (RRI) for its outstanding 
contributions to scientific research across vast spatial and temporal scales, from cosmology to 
atomic physics.                                                                                                                   (Para 6.12) 
The successful design and development of POLIX, the world's first instrument 
operating in the medium X-ray band (8 to 30 keV), and its launch onboard ISRO’s XPoSat 
mission on 1st January 2024, is a remarkable achievement in space-based X-ray astronomy. 
The Committee appreciates the Institute’s efforts in pioneering X-ray polarisation 
measurements, which hold immense potential for advancing our understanding of high-
energy astrophysical phenomena. In light of these achievements, the Committee recommends 
that the Department provide continued support to RRI for further advancements in space-
based astronomy and astrophysics. Strengthening collaborations with national and 
international agencies, investing in cutting-edge instrumentation, and facilitating knowledge 
transfer will be crucial in reinforcing India’s leadership in high-energy astrophysics research. 
The Committee also emphasizes the need for sustained funding, enhanced infrastructure, and 
skilled human resources to propel future discoveries and innovations in this critical domain.  
(Para 6.13) 
HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT AND CAPACITY BUILDING  
The Committee expresses deep concern over the significant number of vacancies in the 
Department, as well as in the Autonomous Institutions (AIs) under its administrative control. 
It is particularly dismayed by the persistent reliance on contractual appointments as a 
stopgap measure to address short-term manpower shortages. The Committee firmly believes 
that a highly skilled and dedicated workforce is the cornerstone of any scientific institution’s 
success and output. Therefore, it strongly recommends that the Department adopt a mission-
mode approach to expeditiously fill all vacant positions. This will ensure the availability of a 
competent and stable workforce, essential for advancing scientific research and institutional 
excellence.                                                                                                                            (Para 7.4) 

23 
 
The Committee observes that the existing provisions for capacity building and skill 
enhancement are inadequate in effectively cultivating scientific talent. To address this gap, the 
Committee strongly recommends that the Department formulate a comprehensive policy 
dedicated to capacity building programs for its staff. This policy should focus on structured 
training initiatives, skill development, and continuous learning opportunities. Additionally, 
the Department should actively explore and establish collaborations with internationally 
renowned scientific institutions to facilitate knowledge exchange, exposure to global best 
practices, and advanced research training.                                                                       (Para 7.6)

4. The Chairman welcomed the Secretary, Department of Science and Technology and his 
team of officers and asked them to give a brief presentation on the Demands for Grants (2025-26). 
Thereafter, The Secretary made a Power Point Presentation (PPT) apprising the Committee about 
the budget allocation for the upcoming fiscal year. The Committee was informed that proposed 
budget for 2025-26 is ₹28,508.90 crores, a substantial increase from the ₹5,661.45 crores allocated 
in 2024-25. The presentation highlighted key initiatives, including the National Quantum Mission, 
aimed at developing cutting-edge quantum computing capabilities; the National Supercomputing 
Mission, focused on enhancing computational power across academic and research institutions; The 
National Geospatial Mission, set to modernize India's geospatial mapping infrastructure; the 
National Action Plan on Climate Change, addressing environmental sustainability and mitigation 
efforts; the Cyber-Physical Systems Mission, which promotes advancements in artificial 
intelligence, robotics, Internet of Things (IoT), Big Data and Industry 4.0 with applications in 
healthcare and agriculture. A major policy shift was also introduced with the consolidation of 
multiple schemes into a single initiative named 'Vigyan Dhara', designed to provide flexible and 
streamlined funding for scientific research and institutional development. The Secretary highlighted 
the ₹20,000 crore corpus allocated under the Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) Fund, 
aimed at bolstering deep-tech startups and industrial innovation. In addition, a significant ₹1,300 
crore investment was planned to support 91 universities in enhancing their R&D infrastructure. 
5. Following the presentation, the Chairman and Members of the Committee raised several 
critical concerns. A key issue was the underutilization of funds in previous years, particularly in 
institutional capacity building and innovation-driven programs. The Members sought clarification 
on measures being taken to ensure efficient fund disbursement and optimal resource utilization. 
Concerns were also raised regarding equitable distribution of funds, particularly to ensure that State 
universities receive adequate support alongside premier institutions like IITs and IISc. The 
Committee highlighted the need for retaining top scientific talent within the country and the role of 
artificial intelligence in national security and economic growth. Several queries were raised on 50 
year interest-free loans to be disbursed under Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) Fund 
such as equitable distribution of funds, repayment of these loans, eligibility of startups for these 
loans, and the accountability mechanism. 
6. The Secretary and other representatives of the Department of Science and Technology, 
while presenting their views, also responded to some of the queries raised by the Chairman and 
Members of the Committee. The Chairman also directed the Secretary to furnish written replies to 
other queries/points/issues raised by him and other Members of the Committee, which could not be 
replied during the meeting, to the Secretariat.
