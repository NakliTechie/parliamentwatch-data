R E P O R T
 
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) which forms part of the Ministry of Science and Technology, provides
 financial support for industrial R&D projects and promotion of industrial research in industry.

2.   The DSIR comprises of the activities of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), Departmental Schemes viz.
 Research and Development by Industry (RDI), Programme Aimed at Technological Self Reliance (PATSER), Scheme to Enhance the
 Efficacy of Transfer of Technology (SEETOT) and National Information System for Science and Technology (NISSAT) and two public
 enterprises viz. National Research Development Corporation (NRDC) and Central Electronics Limited (CEL). 
3.   CSIR is the National R&D organisation providing scientific and industrial research for India’s economic growth and human welfare. 
 It has a country-wide network of forty laboratories and eighty field centres covering fundamental and applied R&D in all areas of science
 and technology barring atomic research, developing and nurturing S&T human resource for the country through extra mural support and
 promoting scientific talent through awards, fellowships etc. Its mission is to provide Scientific Industrial R&D that maximises the
 economic, environmental & societal benefit for the people the country.  Some of the thrust areas under the consideration of CSIR are
 catalysing civilian aircraft industry, greening of leather industry, new drug discovery through symbiosis of biodiversity, heritage of
 therapeutic knowledge and modern S&T, enhancing hydrocarbons resource base: gas hydrates, promoting National IPR movement,
 spearheading global technology leadership by industry, pioneering knowledge base exports, nurturing budding talent for science.
4.   As regards the performance and achievements of CSIR, the Committee has been informed that the impact and imprint of CSIR is
 manifested in diverse ways namely: industrial production worth more than Rs.1300 crores, productivity savings worth Rs.800 crores and
 rural employment generation of 80 lakh mandays were realised during Plan period.  Besides, 800 technology licence agreements were
 executed; 250 new technologies made available for licensing; 1140 patents filed in India and 194 abroad; 10,000 papers contributed of
 which more than 65% are in SCI journals; technical assistance rendered to about 4000 entrepreneurs, external cash-flow generated
 through sponsored/contract work of Rs.670 crores.

7.   The allocation on the plan side has been kept at Rs.355 crores, which represents an increase of about Rs.83 crores (including
 provision of Rs.30.50 crores for North-Eastern Region & Sikkim), over the R.E. and about Rs.66 crores over the B.E. of 1999-2000.  On
 this aspect of enhanced allocation, the Committee has been informed that out of the increase of Rs.66 crores over the B.E. of 1999-2000,
 Rs.1.6 crores is for DSIR, Rs.1.4 crores for PSEs and Rs.63 crores for CSIR. 
8.   The Department proposes to undertake a new scheme namely New Millenium Indian Technology Leadership Initiative (NMITLI) for
 which an allocation of Rs.50 crores has been sanctioned.  Its objective is to focus on a few areas which would fulfil the National
 objective of a global leadership in the technology.  The Committee has been further informed that the emphasis of this new initiative
 would be to identify four to five such areas, build a true ‘Team India’ effort inviting both the public institutions and private sector.  It has
 also been given to understand that the funds will be made available through a competitive bidding process for the individual elements of
 the programme.
9.   The Committee observes that the Department has already begun the process of putting in place a system of identifying and nurturing a
 few niche technology areas where the country could attain an undisputed global leadership position in coming decade.  However, it
 recommends that this new initiative may be accomplished by judiciously selecting technology areas where the country has a potential of
 emerging as a global leader.  While making selection of projects, it may also be ensured that these projects are directed towards the
 public good and demands of society at large, so that benefits of the scientific advances could percolate to the most deserving sections of
 the society. 
RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT BY INDUSTRY (RDI)
10.Recognising the need for developing an indigenous technological base for technology absorption and indigenous technology
 development for achieving technological self reliance, the Government has initiated several measures to encourage industry carry out
 R&D for meeting their needs and also for establishing close links between the national laboratory system and industry.  In house R&D
 units in industry provide a desirable and essential interface between
efforts within the national laboratories and the educational sector as well as production in industry.  At present there are around 1200 in
 house R&D units duly recognised by DSIR.  They employ over 50,000 R&D personnel. The R&D expenditure incurred by these units

11.The Committee  has been given to understand that the investment in R&D by the industry is about 0.8 percent of the GNP.  The

 taken to promote and strengthen R&D infrastructure in the industry and if necessary by giving incentives.  The Department should
 endeavour to develop  better interface with industry and strive to bring it at par with the rest of world.
SCHEME TO ENHANCE THE EFFICACY TRANSFER OF TECHNOLOGY (SEETOT)
12.An effective technology transfer process is crucial for industrial development, be it related to acquisition and management of
 technologies or export of technologies directly or indirectly.  The scheme essentially aims to facilitate acquisition and management of
 technologies, accelerate export of technologies and services, enhance the consultancy capabilities and increase awareness about the
 usefulness of consultancy services among its customers.  Development and commrecialisation of indigenous technologies is encouraged
 and international organizations concerned with technology transfer such as Asian and Pacific Centres for Transfer of Technology
 (APCTT) are supported.  The main objectives of the scheme are to provide technical and technological inputs for acquisition and
 management of technologies; accelerate technology intensive export including export of technologies, projects and service; enhance
 consultancy capabilities; and support mechanisms for technology transfer.
13.While the Committee agrees that an effective technology transfer process is crucial for industrial development, it is of the view that the
 transfer of technology being a complex and continuous process, there is a need to develop human resource to achieve desired goals.  It
 therefore recommends that the technology should be introduced to the potential user in a manner that he can understand easily and the
 successful demonstrations should be repeated for the sake of user to avoid conflicting results, which may lead to retardation in the
 process of transfer of technology.  The Committee therefore recommends the need for strong linkages between the research extension
 centres and client system.
PROGRAMME AIMED AT TECHNOLOGICAL SELF RELIANCE (PATSER)
14.Programme Aimed at Technological Self-Reliance (PATSER) covers the activities relating to technology absorption, adaptation and
 demonstration and also capital goods development.  The objectives of the Programme are to catalyse industry’s efforts in absorption and
 upgradation of imported technology and promote indigenous development of capital goods.  While DSIR support has been catalytic and
 partial, the bulk of the financial contribution in any project has been from the industry.
15.The Committee is of the view that a separate budget may be provided for the marketing of the successful  technologies and whatever
 research and technological innovations have been made they should be thrown open to potential buyers for commercial exploitation.  The
 Committee recommends that the department should focus its research activities according to the requirements of the industry.
 
16.The Committee would appreciate if it is apprised of the results of the newly launched Technopreneur Promotion Programme (TePP)
 being funded through PATSER.
NATIONAL LABORATORIES
17.CSIR today has a network of 40 Laboratories and 80 Field Stations/Extension Centres/Regional Centres located all over the country. 
 The synergy of CSIR’s competence is sought to be optimised by aligning its R&D programmes/activities more directly with socio-
economic objectives of the country focussing sharply on areas where investment can produce maximum returns.  The R&D programmes
 of National Laboratories have been broadly categorised to various socio-economic sectors.
18.While examining financial allocation for National Laboratories, the Committee observes that the planned expenditure has been
 declining whereas non-plan expenditure has been on the rise at least at B.E. and R.E. stages of last year and budgetary allocation in the
 year under review.  The Department has informed that the financial allocation of Rs.574.75 crore (which includes the NE component)
 under National Laboratories consists of Rs.182.75 crore under Plan and Rs.392.00 crore under Non-Plan.  The increase of Rs.1.75 crore
 under Plan and an increase of Rs.43.75 crore under the Non-Plan allocation is mainly due to increase in the salaries.  It has further been
 informed that as some of the plan projects, initiated in the Ninth Plan, are nearing completion (Light Transport Aircraft (LTA), Bioactive
 molecules etc.), their allocation is tapering off, thus reflecting on the near constant plan allocation for National labs.  The Non-Plan
 expenditure has been mainly increasing due to the committed liabilities on salaries and linked.  allowances to the staff arising from the
 implementation of recommendations of the Fifth Pay Commission.
19.The Committee hopes that the Department would be able to fulfil its non-plan commitments with the allocations made and would not
 resort to diversion of funds from the plan to non-plan sector.  The Committee also recommends that Department should endeavour to
 raise the funds from industry by successful transfer of technologies.  The money thus earned can be ploughed back to R&D activities.
20.The Committee is constrained to find that a large amount of technology intended to benefit the villages has not found its way to the
 villages for want of publicity.  There is thus a need for wider dissemination of information preferably through mass media.
MODERNISATION OF CSIR LABORATORIES
21.An allocation of Rs.60 crores has been made for modernisation of laboratories for the year 2000-2001 as against Rs.58 crores in B.E.
 and R.E. of last year. On being asked about the status of upgradation of equipment and R&D facilities in the CSIR’s laboratories, the
 Committee was informed that modernisation of equipment and R&D facilities in CSIR is being done on the actual minimum year to year
 requirement for up-gradation and modernisation of equipment and R&D facilities and the same is proceeding as per the approved
 schedule and the desired objectives in the form of enhanced efficiency, productivity, quality have been achieved.
22.The Committee expects that the modernisation of laboratories and equipments used therein for providing various facilities should keep
 pace with the prevalent global standards and requirements of trade and industry.  The National Laboratories should be fully 
 computerised and endeavour to create better communication facilities.

23.CSIR has been a pioneer in spearheading stray Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) in the country.  It has set for itself a formidable goal
 of holding a valuable portfolio of at least 1000 Indian patents and 500 foreign patents by 2001.  The Department would use them for
 forging strategic alliances with some of the global technological leaders in specific areas and generate at least 1% of its R&D budget
 from IP licensing/alliances.  CSIR would also endeavour to be a national resource and an international reference Centre on IP matters. 
 Major programmes envisaged include: comprehensive protection of intellectual property generated and safeguarding against
 infringement; patent information services etc.
24.The Committee commends the efforts made by the Council in emerging as a pioneer in spreading patent literacy in the country and as
 one of the largest patent holder.  The Committee hopes that the Department could achieve the targets set for itself of achieving at least a
 valuable portfolio of 1000 Indian patents and 500 foreign patents by 2001.  The Committee therefore recommends that the Department
 should gear up to that end for pooling of traditional knowledge of excellence to check the runaway theft of country’s genetic wealth and
 ensure protection of Intellectual Property Rights for validated traditional knowledge systems such as Ayurveda.
25.Asked about the steps taken by the Department to counter the patent on Basmati rice obtained by an American company, the
 Committee has been informed that the Ministry of Agriculture is coordinating the national effort to challenge the Basmati patent and
 CSIR is assisting it by providing its know-how and IPR expertise.
26.The Committee notes with satisfaction the initiative taken in this direction.
DRUGS AND PHARMACEUTICALS
27.The Committee has been informed that the Department through its Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI) is engaged in developing
 new drugs and immunodiagnostics, technology for drugs, intermediates and products, contraceptive agents and devices and conducts
 basic biomedical studies to understand disease process and reproductive physiology and conducts systematic evaluation of natural
 resources for lead identification. Under new drug development a new molecule Centchroman has been discovered and developed by
 CDRI in the first instance as a new non-steroidal once a week female oral contraceptive, which has been introduced in the National
 Family Welfare Programme.  Also a Piperine isolated from an indigenous plant, has been developed by Regional Research Laboratory
 (Jammu) a novel bioenhancer that enables the dosages of the common anti-TB drugs, particularly Rifampicin, to be reduced by at least
 50% resulting not only in the reduction of cost of medication but more importantly in the reduced side effects such as hepatotoxicity and
 neurotoxicity.  The know how has been transferred to the industry.  Its use with other modern drugs is being pursued.  Besides, CSIR has
 developed novel and cost-effective processes for over 30 drugs as well as four drug intermediates which have been licensed and most of
 them viz; Anti-AIDS, Anti-viral, Anti-cancer, Anti-bacterials, Anti-malarial, Cardio-Vascular, Analgesics & Anti-inflammatory, Anti-
allergic, Anti-ulcer, Anti-glaucoma, Anti-inflammatory, Antihypertensive have also been commercialised.
28.The Committee recommends that Department should endeavour to develop cost effective indigenous technologies in the field of drugs
 and pharmaceuticals  keeping in view the ample biodiversity in herbal products available in our country.  At the same time effort should
 be made to develop medicines which help eliminate the sufferings and are not merely an addition to the ones already existing in the
 market.
 
FOOD PROCESSING TECHNOLOGIES AND MODIFIED ATMOSPHERE STORAGE PACKING
29.CSIR is playing a vital role in the Food Processing sector.  The R&D programmes being pursued by the Council are related to
 development of pre and post harvest technologies for a wide range of fruit with an objective to minimise wastage and increase the shelf
 life of the produce with desirable and acceptable ripening characteristics.
30.Asked whether any research was being conducted in the entire gamut of export worthy cost-effective packing to help exporters.  The
 Committee was informed that the cost-effective packaging is an integral part of technology development at Central Food Technology
 Research Institute (CFTRI), Mysore for both fresh and processed fruit and vegetables for the purpose of exports.  CFTRI has carried out
 R&D work on development of technologies based on Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP) for the extension of storage life of fresh
 fruit and vegetables and demonstrated extension of storage life of banana, mango, capsicum and tomato to 6,4,3 and 3 weeks
 respectively.  Also CFTRI, in collaboration with Agricultural and Processed Food products Export Development Authority (APEDA),
 had successfully carried out trial export shipments of mango variety Alphonso, Banganpalli and Kesar in Modified Temperature
 containers to UK.  However large scale adoption of these techniques for export of fruit and vegetables would need proper infrastructural
 facilities such as packaging houses, cold storage etc. at different centres and ports.
31.The Committee recommends that Department should take all the necessary steps for commercial exploitation of
 techniques/technologies developed in the MAP.  The Committee is also of the view that for successful demonstration and transfer of
 technology there is a need for strong linkage within and between the research extension and client system.  Moreover, emphasis should
 be given on production of farm literature in all local languages and with the help of print & electronic media so that the farmers can
 understand the message clearly.
RURAL DEVELOPMENT
32.The Committee was apprised of the various development schemes initiated by the National Laboratories of CSIR with a view to
 developing vast majority of rural masses.  It was informed that Industrial Toxicology Research Centre (ITRC), Lucknow has developed a
 low cost online water purification system that quickly makes water free from bacteria as well as removes toxic metals like iron,
 chromium, lead, zinc and nickel to safe levels.  National Environmental Engineering Research Institute (NEERI), Nagpur has developed
 a sturdy, aesthetic and easily portable kit for detecting arsenic concentration as low as 0.01 mg/L, the WHO guideline limit for drinking
 water.  A superior and fast growing mint variety called Kosi that can produce three crops instead of two each year has been developed by
 Central Institute of Medicinal & Aromatic Plants (CIMAP), Lucknow.  It has also developed an environment friendly menthol based pest
 repellent to protect stored grains. Under  Technology Mission on Oil Seeds and Pulses  funded project, ten sets of appropriate low cost
 apricot seed oil extraction machines comprising, apricot decorticator, kernel sorter, meal grinder and mechanical oil extractor has been
 designed by Regional Research Laboratory, Jammu.   Regional Research Laboratory, Bhu has developed a candleless teracotta water
 filter “Terafil” made from pottery red clay, sand and saw dust mixture.  The filter brings down the turbidity of water from 100 NTU to
 0.5 to 2.0 NTU.  The know-how has been transferred to Orissa Renewable Energy Development Agency (OREDA) for propagation
