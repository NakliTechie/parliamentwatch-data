REPORT
Department of Space (DoS) and Space Commission were established in 1972. The DoS Secretariat
and the Headquarters of ISRO are located in Bengaluru, Karnataka. DoS is responsible for executing the
policies framed by the Space Commission which are carried out through the Indian Space Research
Organisation (ISRO) and the Grant-in-aid institutions, i.e., Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National
Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), North Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC), Semi
Conductor Laboratory (SCL) and Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST).
1.2 The Programme offices at ISRO Headquarters co-ordinate the overall programmes including satellite
communication, satellite navigation, earth observation, launch vehicle, space science, atmospheric science,
space industry development, disaster management support, international co-operation, publications and
public relations, budget and economic analyses and human resource development.
1.3 The launching of India's maiden mission to Planet Mars i.e. Mars Orbiter Mission, experimental
flight of GSLV-MK III which is of great relevance to future Human Space flight, GSLV-D5 using
indigenous cryogenic engine and many other missions and programmes, are amongst the historic
achievements of the Department of Space.
2. OBJECTIVES OF INDIAN SPACE PROGRAMME
2.1 The primary objective of the Indian Space Programme is to promote the development and application
of space technology and space science for the socio-economic benefits of the nation through establishment
of operational space services in a self-reliant manner. The main thrust areas of the programme are:
• Satellite-based communication and navigation for various applications.
• Satellite-based resources survey and management and environmental monitoring.
• Meteorological applications.
• Space science Research and Planetary Exploration, and
• Development and operationalisation of indigenously built satellites, launch vehicles and associated
ground segment for providing these space-based services.
2.2 The major centres, units and establishments of DoS/ISRO, responsible for carrying out R&D
activities as well as for undertaking various projects and programmes, are as under:-
(i) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala provides the technology
base for the country's indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development;
(ii) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities at Bengaluru, and
Thiruvananthapuram is responsible for development of liquid propulsion and cryogenic propulsion
systems for satellite launch vehicles;
(iii) ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC), erstwhile LPSC, Mahendragiri has the responsibility for
assembly, integration and testing of storable and cryogenic liquid rocket engines and stages,

2
space craft thrusters testing in sea level and high altitude conditions, storage of liquid and
cryogenic propellants and production of liquid hydrogen etc.
(iv) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, is the launch base
and also provides the facilities for processing of solid rocket motors;
(v) ISRO Satellite Centre (ISAC), Bengaluru, Karnataka is entrusted with the prime responsibility
of the satellite development;
(vi) Laboratory for Electro-Optics Systems (LEOS), Bengaluru, Karnataka is responsible for
development and production of electro optical sensors for satellites;
(vii) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad, Gujarat carries out R&D in space applications
as well as development of application payloads;
(viii) Development and Educational Communication Unit (DECU), Ahmedabad, Gujarat is involved
in the conception, definition, planning and socio/techno-economic evaluation of developmental
space applications;
(ix) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad, Andhra Pradesh is responsible for satellite
data acquisition and processing, data dissemination, aerial remote sensing and decision support
for disaster management;
(x) Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), Dehradun, Uttrakhand is a premier training and
educational institute set up for honing skills of trained professional in the field of Remote
Sensing, Geoinformatics and Global Positioning System (GPS) Technology for Natural Resources,
Environmental and Disaster Management;
(xi) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network (ISTRAC), with its ground stations at
Bengaluru, Sriharikota, Lucknow, Bearslake (USA), Biak (Indonesia), Brunei and Mauritius
provides the telemetry, track and command support and spacecraft mission management for
Indian remote sensing satellite missions, space science missions and satellite launch vehicle
programmes;
(xii) Master Control Facility (MCF) with its main facility at Hassan, Karnataka and backup facility
at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh have the responsibility for control and operation of INSAT/GSAT/
METSAT satellites; and
(xiii) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala carries out research and
development in the area of inertial systems for satellites and launch vehicles.
2.3 A National Natural Resources Management Systems (NNRMS), with DoS as the nodal agency, has
been established to ensure optimal management/utilisation of natural resources using remote sensing data
in conjunction with conventional techniques. Under NNRMS, five Regional Remote Sensing Service
Centres (RRSSCs) at Bengaluru, Dehradun, Jodhpur, Kharagpur and Nagpur have also been set up for
the execution of remote sensing application programmes in the respective regions. With effect from
December 2009, four out of five RRSSCs have been redesignated as Regional Remote Sensing Centres
(RRSCs), while RRSSC, Dehradun has been merged with Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS).

3
2.4 The mandate of the organisations receiving Grants-in-aid from Department of Space is as follows:
(i) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous institution supported mainly
by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences;
(ii) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), set up at Gadanki near Tirupati,
conducts atomospheric research;
(iii) The North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC), established as an autonomous body
under the Department of Space, provides space technology inputs for developmental activities
in the North-Eastern Region;
(iv) The Semi-conductor Laboratory, Chandigarh, an R&D society, is entrusted with the design
and development of Very Large Scale Integrated Circuit (VLSI) devices and development of
micro-electronic systems to meet the strategic needs of the country; and
(v) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), an autonomous body under the
Department, aims at creating quality human resources tuned to suit the requirements of state-
of-the-art space research programme. The Institute offers graduate, post-graduate and doctoral
programmes in the area of space science, technology and applications. Initially, IIST started
the courses from the academic year 2007-08 with the existing infrastructure at
Thiruvananthapuram.
2.5 The ANTRIX Corporation Limited, Bengaluru, established in September, 1992, a wholly owned
Government of India company under the administrative control of DoS, is a marketing arm of ISRO for
promotion and commercial exploitation of Space products, technical consultancy services and transfer
of technologies developed by ISRO. It also facilitates development of Space related capabilities in India.
2.6 Besides, the RESPOND programme of ISRO supports sponsored research activities in space science,
space applications and space technology in several national academic/research institutions and Space
Technology Cells in premier technological institutes of the country through grants-in-aid.

4.2 The Committee takes note of the fact that despite the Department of Space being capable
of utilizing 100% of its allocated grants, it has had to face cuts in grants at the R.E. stage in
the successive financial years and the Committee feels that this would delay or disrupt some of
its programmes of strategic importance to the country. The Committee believes that the
Departments such as Space and Atomic Energy cannot be burdened by resource crunch in the
implementation of projects of strategic importance. The Committee recommends accordingly and
the priorities determined by the heads of these Departments should be respected.
5. BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2015-16
The Department has intimated that the budgetary allocation for 2015-16 stands at ` 7388.19 crores
comprising of ` 6000.19 crores 'Plan' outlay and ` 1388.00 crores 'Non-Plan' outlay. There has been
an increase of ` 150.15 crores in the B.E. 2015-16 as against the B.E. 2014-15. The 'Plan' outlay of
the budgetary allocation in 2014-15 was ` 6000.00 crores. There has been a minimal increase of ` 0.19
crore in the Plan allocation in 2015-16 in comparison to the 2014-15 allocation, which is not enough even
to offset the inflation. However, there had been a drastic reduction in the budgetary allocation in the 'Plan'
component in programmes such as Trisonic Wind Tunnel Project, Procurement of Heavier class of
Satellites, PSLV launch programmes, Indian Lunar Mission Chandrayaan 1 & 2 and ADITYA Mission.
5.1 The Committee feels that drastic reduction of Plan components of some of the major
programmes of the Department scheduled for 2015-16 ought not to have been made in view of
the strategic importance of these projects. In fact, there is a case for higher outlays for projects
of national importance. The Committee therefore, recommends that the budget allocation of the
Department should be suitably enhanced at the RE stage.
6. PHYSICAL ACHIEVEMENTS OF THE DEPARTMENT IN 2014-15
6.1 The Committee desired to know about the major achievements of the Department in the year
2014-15. In response, the Department listed its major achievements in 2014-15 which are as under:-
(i) India's maiden mission to planet Mars, Mars Orbiter Mission, was successfully launched on-
board PSLV-C25 on November 5, 2013. The space craft was successfully inserted into
Martian orbit on September 24, 2014 after a long voyage of more than 300 days. Three
operational flights of PSLV have been successfully conducted in 2014 i.e. operational flight of
PSLV-C24 carrying IRNSS-IB (on April 4, 2014), PSLV-C23 carrying French Remote Sensing
Satellite SPOT-7 and four co-passenger satellites (on June 30, 2014) and PSLV-C26 carrying
IRNSS-IC (on October 16, 2014).
(ii) The experimental flight of GSLV-MK III was successfully carried out on December 18, 2014
from Sriharikota carrying Crew Module Atmospheric Reentry Experiment (CARE) module as

its pay load. The department is planning to complete a series of qualification tests which is
to be conducted during 2015-16 for qualifying the CE-20 Cryogenic engine which is the upper
stage of the GSL V-MK III engine.
(iii) GSLV -D5 with indigenous cryogenic engine was successfully launched on January 5, 2014
and it placed the communication satellite GSA T-14 into its designed orbit. GSAT-14
communication satellite has added 14 communication transponders (6 ext-C band, 6 Ku-band
and 2 ka-Band) to INSAT/GSAT capacity. Stage preparation, integration and testing activities
of different stages of GSLV-D6, using indigenous cryogenic engine, is progressing well. Flight
testing of GSLV-D6 is scheduled for launch during 2nd half of 2015. It will put into orbit the

first technology demonstrator mission of Reusable Launch Vehicle is targeted during 2015-16.
6.2 The Committee takes note of the achievements of the Department in 2014-15. The Department
had successfully placed the spacecraft into the Martian orbit making India the first nation to do
so in its first attempt. The launch of GSLV Mk III experimental flight has demonstrated India's
capability of launching satellites of more than 2 ton capacity. The launch of GSLV D5 exhibits
India's might in developing indigenous cryogenic engine. The Committee hopes that with the
launch of the fourth satellite IRNSS-1D, an independent regional navigation system will be
accomplished very soon. These major achievements of the Department make India a world leader
in space technology.

11. GEOSTATIONARY SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLE (GSLV)
11.1 The advancement of Indian Space Programme greatly depends upon the upgradation of the Space
transportation like launch vehicles capable of 2-5 ton capacity communication satellite into Geosynchronous
Transfer Orbit (GTO) for which Geostationary Satellite Launch Vehicles (GSLV) technology needs
enhancement.
11.2 The first flight of GSLV took place from SDSC-SHAR on April 18, 2001 by launching 1540 kg
GSA T-1 Seven more launches have taken place, GSLV D2 on May 8, 2003 (GSAT - 2 1825 kg), GSLV-
F01 on September 20, 2004 (EDUSA T 1950 kg), GSLV - F02 on July 10, 2006, GSLV - F04 on
September 2, 2007 (INSA T - 4CR 2130 kg), GSLV - D3 on April 15, 2010, GSLV - F06 on December
25, 2010 and GSLV - D5 on 5th January, 2014.
11.3 The second flight of GSLV , GSLV-D5, with indigenous Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS) developed
by Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC) was successfully launched on 5th January, 2014. This was
the first successful flight of the cryogenic stage which was developed in India. The next flight of GSLV ,
GSLV-D6, the third developmental flight with indigenous Cryogenic engine, is targeted during the third
quarter of 2015. The realisation of subsystems/stages for the next flight is in advanced stage. The solid
booster (S139) motor with all pyro-elements and one of the liquid propellant strap-on (L40 stage) are
ready. The remaining three liquid propellant strap-ons and liquid propellant second stage (GS2) of the
vehicle are under realisation. Flight Acceptance tests of cryogenic engine and the steering engines have
been successfully completed and assembled with Liquid Oxygen propellant stage tank. Assembly and
integration of Cryogenic flight stage is in progress.
11.4 GSLV MK III Project will augment the capacity of Indian Space Programme to launch 4 tonne class
of communication satellites to Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO). The experimental flight of GSLV
MK III was successfully launched on December 18, 2014. The development, qualification and testing
of the higher thrust cryogenic engine and stage (C 25) is in progress. The first developmental flight
(GSLV MK III - DI) is scheduled during 2016-2017 and the second developmental flight (GSLV MK III
- D2) is scheduled one year later in 2017-18.

8
11.5 Avenues being explored by the Department to improve the payload capacity of vehicles launched
under GSLV Programme in the coming launches are upratement and increment in specific impulses and
reduction in dry mass of the indigenous cryogenic engine and stage. Adoption of a gridded payload
adapter in future will also improve the payload performance.
11.6 The Committee is of the view that upgradation and enhancement of GSLV technology is
extremely important for advancement of launch vehicles. The Committee appreciates the launching
of GSLV D5 and successful experimental flight of GSLV MK III which has demonstrated India's
capability and progressiveness in developing indigenous cryogenic engines. The Committee, however,
feels that improving the payload performance and advancement of GSLV is needed and recommends
that the Department should take necessary steps in this regard. The Committee also expects the
successful launch of GSLV-D6 in the third quarter of 2015 and the first and second developmental
flights i.e. (GSLV MK III - D1) and (GSLV MK III - D2) in the scheduled time frame of
2016-17 and 2017-18 respectively.

12. TRISONIC WIND TUNNEL PROJECT
12.1 The Committee desired to know about the Trisonic Wind Tunnel Project. In response, the Department
informed the Committee that the project is planned to be set up at the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre,
Thiruvananthapuram for meeting the test requirements of future launch vehicle development. This project
is to provide test facilities in Acro-Dynamic characterisation in subsonic, transonic and supersonic mach
number. The Department stated that a project report for setting up this facility is already prepared and
currently being processed for obtaining necessary approvals. ` 10.00 crores was allocated to the project
for the first time in B.E. 2014-15 which was reduced to Nil at R.E. stage due to the fact that necessary
approvals were not received by Department. Now an amount of ` 1.00 crore has been allocated for the
project in B.E. 2015-16.
12.2 The Committee is of the view that the Trisonic Wind Tunnel Project is very important to
meet the test requirements for future launch vehicles as it will provide research facility for
conducting research tests for upgrading technology of satellite launch vehicles. However, the
Committee constrained to note that the budget provision for the projects in 2014-15 could not
be utilised due to the inability of the Department to obtain necessary approvals. The Committee,

consists of Ground segment, Space Segment and User Segment.

26.1 The Committee was informed by the Department that ISRO is operationally carrying out the

• The Flood Early Warning System (FLEWS) initially carried out in 14 districts in Assam State
was extended to the whole of Assam State.
• A Flood Hazard layers for Odisha and Uttar Pradesh based on past flood incidents was
prepared and currently under ground validations by respective government agencies.
• Flood prone area demarcation methodology using satellite data and Digital Elevation data
(DEM) has been developed for Purbalang basin in Orissa with 90% accuracy.
• During the Hudhud cyclone in October 2014, besides space imageries and aerial flying, an
android based application was deployed for enabling citizens to upload field information on the
damages in the Vishakhapatnam city. More than 25,000 imageries of damage details were
collected instantaneously from the field which helped the district authorities to attend to the
problems of citizens distributed in the inaccessible areas.
• New modules related to Shortwave Angle Slope Index (SASI) computation, Soil moisture
estimation through water balance methodology and generation of crop sown area products
were added to the NADAMS Analysis Software operationalised at Mahalanobis National Crop
Forecasting Centre (MNCFC), MoA/GoI.
• Rainfall induced Landslide Early Warning is being experimentally carried out for pilgrimage
routes leading to Badrinath and Kedarnath in Uttarakhand during the monsoon season (July-
October) and from 2014 onwards the same is being published through Bhuvan portal.
• INSAT-3D and SAPHIR (Megha-Tropiques) data assimilation in operational weather prediction
models have been carried out and operationalized at India Meteorological Department (IMD)
and National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF).
• A Methodology has been developed for now-casting of Thunderstorms and heavy rainfall
events in Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh and surrounding region using satellite data.

• Storm surge prediction methodology is developed and is operationally provided through
MOSDAC portal.
• Methodology developed for assimilation of Sea Surface Heights (SSH) and Significant Wave

the accuracy of the forecasts.

of utilizing 100% of its allocated grants, it has had to face cuts in grants at the R.E. stage in
the successive financial years and the Committee feels that this would delay or disrupt some of
its programmes of strategic importance to the country. The Committee believes that the
Departments such as Space and Atomic Energy cannot be burdened by resource crunch in the
implementation of projects of strategic importance. The Committee recommends accordingly and
the priorities determined by the heads of these Departments should be respected. (Para 4.2)
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2015-16
The Committee feels that drastic reduction of Plan components of some of the major programmes
of the Department scheduled for 2015-16 ought not to have been made in view of the strategic
importance of these projects. In fact, there is a case for higher outlays for projects of national
importance. The Committee therefore, recommends that the budget allocation of the Department
should be suitably enhanced at the RE stage. (Para 5.1)
PHYSICAL ACHIEVEMENTS OF THE DEPARTMENT IN 2014-15
The Committee takes note of the achievements of the Department in 2014-15. The Department
had successfully placed the spacecraft into the Martian orbit making India the first nation to do
so in its first attempt. The launch of GSLV Mk III experimental flight has demonstrated India's
capability of launching satellites of more than 2 ton capacity. The launch of GSLV D5 exhibits
India's might in developing indigenous cryogenic engine. The Committee hopes that with the
launch of the fourth satellite IRNSS-1D, an independent regional navigation system will be
accomplished very soon. These major achievements of the Department make India a world leader
in space technology. (Para 6.2)
POLAR SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLES
The Committee expresses its concern over the trend of reduction of B.E. outlay at R.E. stage
for PSLV Projects. The Committee notes that some important launches of PSLV are scheduled
during 2015-16 and hopes that PSLV project would not be allowed to be adversely affected this
year due to budgetary constraints. The Committee recommends that the Department of Space
utilise the allocated amount efficiently and in time to have minimal cuts at the R.E. stage to have
smooth continuation of PSLV launches. (Para 10.6)
GEOSTATIONARY SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLE (GSLV)
The Committee is of the view that upgradation and enhancement of GSLV technology is
extremely important for advancement of launch vehicles. The Committee appreciates the launching
of GSLV D5 and successful experimental flight of GSLV MK III which has demonstrated India's
capability and progressiveness in developing indigenous cryogenic engines. The Committee, however,
feels that improving the payload performance and advancement of GSLV is needed and recommends
that the Department should take necessary steps in this regard. The Committee also expects the
21

22
successful launch of GSLV-D6 in the third quarter of 2015 and the first and second developmental
flights Le. (GSLV MK III - Dl) and (GSLV MK III - D2) in the scheduled time frame of
2016-17 and 2017-18 respectively. (Para 11.6)
The Committee feels that since GSLV MK II is a very crucial launch vehicle with a capability
to put about 4 ton payload to geo-synchronous Transfer orbit, the Department must make all out
efforts to make it successful expeditiously and make full utilization of budgetary resources made
available for this project. Technical hurdles coming in the way must also be overcome so as to
ensure that the project is completed within the scheduled time frame as we are already way
behind USA and China in this regard. (Para 11.11)
TRISONIC WIND TUNNEL PROJECT
The Committee is of the view that the Trisonic Wind Tunnel Project is very important to
meet the test requirements for future launch vehicles as it will provide research facility for
conducting research tests for upgrading technology of satellite launch vehicles. However, the
Committee constrained to note that the budget provision for the projects in 2014-15 could not
be utilised due to the inability of the Department to obtain necessary approvals. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the approval for the project should be obtained at the earliest and
the Department should ensure that all necessary efforts are made and adequate funds provided
to achieve the desired targets of the project during 2015-16 and the project is not delayed any
further. (Para 12.2)
MANNED MISSION/HUMAN SPACE FLIGHT PROGRAMME
The Committee notes that most of the pre-project activities for development of critical
technologies had made significant progress in this critically important mission. However, some
of the other critical technologies like Crew Escape System (CES) and Environmental Control and
Life Support System (ECLSS) are still being developed. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Department should develop other technologies of the project in an expeditious and time
bound manner. The Committee hopes that the Department would make earnest efforts to optimally
utilise the allocation made for the project during 2015-16. (Para 13.5)
RADAR IMAGING SATELLITE - RISAT - 1A
The Committee is aware that the original target for launch of RISAT-1A with indigenous
PSLV rocket was scheduled for 2017-18 which was later revised to 2019. However, the Committee
notes with concern that there has hardly been any progress in the development of RISAT-1A and
the project report is yet to be submitted for necessary approval. The Department of Space has
stated that subsequent to the project approval, it would take about 3½ years to realise the
spacecraft. As such, it is amply clear that the project is way behind schedule. Given the fact that
RISAT-1A is an important optical satellite, relying on sunlight to illuminate the ground below,
having many benefits in flood mapping, agriculture and also glacial studies, the Committee
recommends that the Department should make all out efforts for the completion of the programme
even before its scheduled target in 2019, if possible. (Para 15.4)

INDIAN REGIONAL NA VIGATIONAL SATELLITE SYSTEM (IRNSS)
The Committee is of the view that the launching of the remaining satellites of IRNSS will
be a great achievement of Indian Space Programme in accomplishing an independent robust
navigation system and the Committee hopes that the Department will successfully launch the
satellites in the scheduled time frame. The Committee, therefore, recommends that construction
of Satellite Control Centre at Hassan, backup IRNSS Navigation Centre at Lucknow and the
backup Satellite Control facility at Bhopal should be completed at the earliest so that their
services are available to the nation without delay. (Para 17.6)
ASTROSAT MISSION
The Committee is constrained to note that ASTROSAT satellite, which was originally scheduled
to be launched in 2009-10, has not been launched so far. Further, the Department has been
reiterating almost the same reasons for the postponement of the project. The Committee
recommends that the Department should ensure that mission is accomplished in the revised time
frame set by the Department. It is unacceptable that such projects are being repeatedly delayed
on account of stated challenges and reasons that can early be addressed. (Para 19.7)
INDIAN LUNAR MISSION - CHANDRAYAAN-2
The Committee is concerned about the reduction in the budgetary allocation for the mission
at the R.E. stage in 2014-15 and also the reduction in allocation to the tune of ````` 20.00 crores
in B.E. 2015-16 as compared to B.E. 2014-15. The Committee recommends that shortage of funds
may not be allowed to adversely affect the completion of the project. (Para 20.5)
NASA ISRO SYNTHETIC APERTURE RADAR MISSION (NISAR)
The Committee recommends that keeping in view the importance of the NISAR Mission,
the possibility of preponing its launch before its schedule in 2021 should be explored particularly
when components of the hardware satellite building as well as launch vehicle are likely to be
ready before 2019. The Department should also pursue JPL NASA to accordingly get ready before
2019. (Para 22.3)
The Committee notes that an amount of ````` 50.00 crores has been allocated for the NISAR
Mission this year and the Department had hopes that the amount shall be suitably augmented
at R.E. stage once the project is approved. The Committee recommends that adequate funds be
provided for expeditious completion of the Mission. (Para 22.5)
SATELLITE FOR SAARC COUNTRIES
The Committee notes the benefits that the satellite for SAARC countries would bring about in
matters related to education, tele medicine, disaster response, resource management, weather
forecasting and communication sectors. The Committee feels it will also help foster good relations

24
of India with SAARC countries. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the approval for
realization of this project may be given at the earliest. (Para 23.4)
CONTRIBUTION TO THE SOCIETY
The Committee is disappointed to note that a programme like Educational Communication
Unit is facing cuts in the budget which severely hampers the goal of taking education to the
people. The Committee, therefore, recommends that allocations made for societal programmes
should not be reduced so that the society at large is able to benefit by making full use of the
applications of space technologies. The Committee also takes note of the use of the spin off
technologies for socially useful purposes by the Department. However, the Committee feels that
much more needs to be done in view of newer challenges coming up before the society and the
people. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should review their priorities
in this sector to come up to the expectations of the people and provide them with greater
facilities and benefits. (Para 25.4)
DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT
The Committee feels that unseasonal rains and floods pose great danger to the lives of the
people and cause severe loss of property and damages to crops. As such, there is an urgent need
to improve our Flood Early Warning System. The Committee, therefore, recommends that with
the improved observation capability of our satellites, the capability of the Flood Early warning
System should also be improved at the earliest for securing the lives and property of the people
from such unseasonal climatic disasters. (Para 26.4)
SPACE PROGRAMME AND INDUSTRY PARTICIPATION
The Committee is of the view that participation of industry in the Indian Space Programme
is of utmost importance for various reasons, particularly for promoting indigenous capability in
the related manufacturing sector. Taking note of the fact that the Department till now has not
set up the National Committee which will enhance participation of Industry, the Committee
recommends that the proposed National Committee should be set up without further delay.
(Para 27.3)
It has been reported that China is exploring the potentiality of solar power at the altitude
of 36000 kms. The Committee feels that the Department of Space should also look into such path
breaking initiatives to be a world leader in the field of space. (Para 27.4)
The Committee was informed that the Department has the manpower capacity of about
18000 and around 150-200 are retiring every year. It was also informed that during the last 15
years, manpower has been increased only once. The last was 5 years back. The Committee feels
that in view of the big strides made and also proposed to be made in various sectors in the area
of space, the availability of adequate, well trained and well equipped manpower is vital. The
Committee recommends that while the Department present its requirement for adequate manpower
with cogent reasons keeping the needs of its future programme and projects, Ministry of Finance
must consider the same positively considering the strategic importance of these projects. The
Committee feels that the aim of the Department of Space should be to establish the position of
India as a global leader in its area of operations. The approach of the governmen should be to
encourage and facilitate in all manner possible the realization of this aspirational goal in and of
national development and security. (Para 27.5)

2. At the outset, the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee; Secretary, Department of
Space and his team of officers and informed that the Committee was scheduled to hear the Secretary
on various aspects relating to Demand for Grants (2015-16) of the Department of Space. While
congratulating the Department for its achievements and various other initiatives it had undertaken in the
year 2014, the Chairman desired to know as to how the Department proposed to make the full benefits
of space technology available to the common man. He also asked the Secretary to brief the Committee
about the budgetary allocations for different schemes during the year 2015-16 and whether the allocation
was sufficient to implement the programmes of the Department.
3. Thereafter, the Secretary made a power-point presentation on achievements of the Department in
the recent past and the various aspects of the Demands for Grants of the Department.
4. The Chairman desired to know whether the scheduled target of NISAR i.e. 2021 could be pre-
poned for the betterment on the people. The Secretary replied that they were willing to pre-pone the
target but the activities to be undertaken by JPL, NASA would take time. The Chairman observed that
there was huge amount of mineral wealth in the deep sea and desired to know whether the space
programmes of the Department were well equipped to specifically point out the mineral reserves in the
deep sea and whether they can develop a new programme with the Ministry of Earth Sciences for the
purpose. The Secretary replied that the intermediate segment on the surface of ocean can only provide
such data which can reach the satellites and that they were providing a number of signals regarding
tsunami-related activities to the Ministry of Earth Sciences.

29
5. The Committee then raised the issue of China planning to build a huge solar power station at a height
of 36,000 km. above the ground and desired to know whether India was contemplating anything in this
regard. The Secretary replied that they did not have any such plan for the present. Replying to a further
query on the issue, the Secretary stated that harnessing solar energy at such an altitude can disturb many
systems. The Chairman asked the Department to consider the possibilities of harnessing the solar energy
at the height of 36,000 kms.
6. The Committee asked the Department about the benefits that the programme NISAR will yield for
meeting challenges of increasing population, food and water scarcity in future and its application in
agriculture biomass, soil moisture etc. The Secretary replied that the Mahalanobis Centre was making
use of the RISAT data for crop yield forecasting and crop assessment whereby the soil moisture effect
and the biomass estimation will significantly improve the ability to make assessment on the crop yield
and the soil moisture information would also be used for improving irrigation, thereby providing significant
inputs to the farmers for planning their activities.
7. The Committee then raised various issues such as creating a system that can give early warning
to the people on the unprecedented floods and rains, data regarding water level and exploitation of the
fresh water in coastal areas and about landslides in Western Ghats, lack of manpower in ISRO which
had been minimally increased only once during the last fifteen years, initiating programmes to encourage
young scientists towards the field of space, availability of data relating to conservation of rivers, forests,
climate change etc. to the public at large etc. The representatives of the Department also replied to some
of the queries raised by the Members. The Chairman then asked the Department to send written replies
to all the queries raised during the meeting to the Secretariat at the earliest.
8. The representatives of Department of Space then withdrew.
