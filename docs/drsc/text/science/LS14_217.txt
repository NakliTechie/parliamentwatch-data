REPORT 
  The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research on the 
recommendations contained in the Two Hundred and Eighth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-11) of the Department of 
Scientific and Industrial Research, which was presented to both  the Houses of Parliament on 23rd April, 2010. There were 
twenty-five recommendations in the Two Hundred and Eighth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the 
recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 28th July, 2010. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken by the 
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:- 
Recommendation (Para 4.3) 
 The Committee appreciates the achievements made, so far, by  the Department during the Xth Five Year Plan and hopes 
that the Department will strive towards continuing and strengthening its programme to support the small and medium industrial 
units to develop cutting edge technologies of high commercial  value and intensify exports and at the same time developing 
technologies for the use of the common man. However, the Committee takes note of the fact that although six schemes initially 
operated by CSIR five are now continuing into Xth plan, while Infrastructure Refurbishing & Renovation (IRR) scheme has 
been discontinued in the XIth plan. The Committee however notice d no significant reasons have been provided by DSIR for 
discontinuance of IRR Scheme. Moreover sixteen network projects under National Laboratory scheme were permitted to 
spillover into the XIth plan. The reasons quoted for such spillo ver is the late start of these projects as Standing Finance 
Committee (SFC) and Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) approvals took time. 
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR initiated a new scheme during Tenth Five Year Plan namely “Infrastructure Renovation & Refurbishment” which 
had two components namely ‘ICT Infrastructure’ and ‘Refurbishing the antiquated physical infrastructure’. 
 ‘ICT infrastructure’ was aimed to provide necessary ICT infrastructure for scientists/technical staff to manage activities of 
CSIR in more efficient and effective manner. 
 The various functional units/divisions located in CSIR Headquarters provided the R&D Management support to the 
national laboratories through the scheme. It provided support to the laboratories for human resource development, international  
scientific collaboration, publicity and public relations, performance appraisal, scientific audit-etc. 
 Many CSIR laboratories were built more than 30 years ago. Most of the laboratories were not suited for modern day R&D 
especially in term of Good laboratory Practices, ISO and NAB L standards. Moreover, most of those building were in 
dilapidated condition and needed strengthening of foundations and roofs etc. 
 The desired objectives, more or less, were met and new opportu nities were on the anvil at the onset of Eleventh Five Year 
Plan. For example there were many technological breakthroughs,  which have made it possible to analyze the genome at a scale 
that was unthinkable earlier. 
 The Department reiterates that the reason for spillover of Sixteen Network Projects of Tenth Five Year Plan into Eleventh 
Five Year Plan is due to late start of these projects as SFC and EFC approvals took time. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and desires that the Department should try its le vel best to meet the targets set for 
the Eleventh five year plan and should  ensure that the procedural aspects su ch as SFC and EFC approvals are sought 
well in time so that projects are completed is a time bound manner. 
 

Recommendation (Para 4.4) 
 The Committee feels that non agreeability between Stan ding Finance Committee and Expenditure Finance Committee 
might have led to the late approval of sixteen network projec ts under ‘National Laboratories Scheme’ for which these projects 
have been spilled over to Eleventh plan leading to delay in project implementation. The Committee apprehends that 
disagreeability between SFC and EFC might have taken place due to the fact that there might be some major changes in the 
project content in comparison to initial proposal. The Committee th erefore feels that the revised cost estimates in respect of 
every scientific project should be made in such a manner that escalation in the cost of project s due to prevailing inflation at  the 
revised estimate stage should not exceed more than 20 percent of the original cost estimate. As per the opinion of the 
Committee this can be achieved if there is no major changes proposed in the content of the project at R.E. level, then only 
disagreeability between SFC and EFC could be avoided and project approvability may be granted without any delay. 
Action Taken by Government 
 In a humble submission, it is to be informed that there was no disagreeability between Standing Finance Committee 
(SFC) and Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC). Project proposal is appraised and approved by SFC or EFC depending on the 
outlay of the projects. For example, at present, projects cost ing upto Rs.25.00 crore can be appraised by Secretary of the 
Department concerned, projects costing between Rs. 25.00 cror e and Rs.100.00 crore by SFC, projects costing between Rs. 
100.00 crore and Rs. 300.00 crore by EFC (Chaired by Secretary of the concerned De partment), and projects costing more than 
Rs. 300 crore by EFC (Chaired by Secretary, Expenditure). 
 As far as the spillover of the projects from Tenth Five Year  Plan to Eleventh Five Year Plan is concerned reasons are 
varied, which, in most cases, were beyond the administrative control of the Department. 
Comments 
 The Committee feels that the spill over of projects from one five year plan to the other is a matter of concern and is 
of the view that whatever the reasons this needs to be avoided. 
Recommendation (Para 5.5) 
 The Committee notes with serious concern that there has be en a drastic reduction in the Plan allocation for the 
department, amounting to Rs.1015.00 crores. Moreover the allocation for DSIR during 2009-2010 was slashed from Rs.58.50 
crore at BE stage to Rs.31.31 crore at RE stage. Due to this reduction, the Department will need to re-prioritize allocation within 
various heads to initiate work and will even have to defer some of the activities planned for that year to next year due to pau city 
of funds. 
Action Taken by Government 
 There was a drastic cut in DSIR’s plan  allocation for TPDU scheme from Rs. 33.00 crore to Rs. 9.00 crore. This led to 
deferment of issuance of sanctions, in respect of many projects under the TDDP and TcPP schemes to the next financial year 
due to non-availability of funds, even though they were concurred by IFD. 
Comments 
 The Committee finds that the Department in its reply ha s neither spelt out the reasons, for the drastic cut in 
DSIR’s plan allocation nor underlined the efforts made by it to revert the cut in  the allocation. The Committee is of the 
opinion that the Department should forcefully plead its genuine demands before the Planning Commission. 
Recommendation (Para 5.6) 
 The Committee. also notes with serious concern the significant difference between the demand projected by the 
Department and the amount actually allocated. The Committee is of the opinion that investment in scientific research is a step 
towards ensuring the development of the countr y. Therefore at least the scientific Departments should not be deprived of funds 
as it would adversely affect research and development which is crucial for the development of the nation. The Committee, 

therefore, recommends that the well thought out projected demand of scientific departments should be honoured and accepted 
by the Planning Commission, specially in view of the fact that scientific research is a capital intensive field. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department thanks the Committee for its recommendation to the Planning Commission regarding acceptance of its 
well thought out demands for promoting scientific and industrial research. 
Comments 
 The Committee desires that the Department should take up this issue with the Planning Commission and convince 
them to take a realistic view and alloca te the much needed funds for the various schemes initiated by the Department at 
the R.E. stage. 
Recommendation (Para 5.7) 
 The Committee observes that though the Department has been making an outstanding contribution towards the research 
and development in the various sectors of science & technology and has played a pivotal role in the progress of the country, ye t 
the acknowledgement of its activities has been utterly disappoin ting and its activities are not popular as compared to those of  
the other scientific departments. The Committee feels that this  is one of the root causes for the reduction of the budgetary 
allocation by the Planning Commission. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department should have a 
comprehensive plan and a separate budget for popularizing its technologies and should double its efforts to communicate its 
activities and market its products to the Government and the priv ate sector both through audio-visual and print media in a more  
effective way. In this direction the initiative of Department to have a CSIR logo on all the products using CSIR technology 
should be vigorously pursued. The Planning Commission should also on its part encourage the scientific departments with 
adequate fund allocation in keeping with their proposed allocations  so that most of the programme may not be ,spilled over to 
the future years and could be completed within the targeted time frame. 
Action Taken by Government 
 This is to submit that CSIR has been regularly disseminating information about its activities and achievements using 
various communication channels, taking into consideration the nature of the audiences it is addressing to. 
 CSIR’s research outputs of new knowledge, products/techno logies are placed in the public domain through publications, 
annual reports, newsletters etc. Thus information about variou s R&D programs, new technologies and other activities of CSIR 
laboratories is disseminated through CSIR Samachar (Hindi, m onthly) and CSIR News (English, fortnightly). CSIR News and 
CSIR Samachar not only provide information on R&D programmes bu t also help in forming useful links between various CSIR 
constituents and users, other R&D organisations, university departments and industries. 
 CSIR is bringing out a journal of Rural Technology (Engl ish) aimed at disseminating information on emerging rural 
technologies and their outreach to rural communities. 
 CSIR also organizes exhibition and participating in exhibitions organized by other Ministries/ Departments.  
 Few of the steps being taken up by the Departments are: 
 Image Building through Print Media Effective media relations helped in furthering result-oriented relationship with 
the key press persons covering science in their respective dailies. 
 Image Building through Broadcast Media including Audio-Visual Campaign and Radio Serial ‘Nai Subah’ in all 
languages as 13 episodes of 30 minutes each and their br oadcast on 40 Vividh Bharti st ations of AIR highlighting 
the technological achievements. 
 Image Building through Interactive Media including Exhibitions. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses satisfaction over the efforts made by the Department. 

Recommendation (Para 6.5) 
 The Committee notes with serious concern that the TPDU a llocation for the 2009-2010 was drastically reduced to 
Rs.12.50 crores at the R.E. stage, which is less than half of the initial allocation of Rs. 37.50 crores at the B.E. stage, as the 
Department was unable to ensure proper utilisation. of the funds allocated till October, 2009. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Fund utilization by the department till October, 2009 was inadequate Since discussions on re-orientation of departmental 
activities and revision of its on-going TPDU programme were being finalized. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses its concern at the lack of proper planning required for complete utilization of funds and 
desires that such a situation should not be allowed to recur in future. 
Recommendation (Para 6.6) 
 The Committee has also noticed that while the Department was successful in achieving the physical targets of Tenth Five 
Year Plan, however there were shortfalls in achieving the financial targets under the TPDU Programme for which initially under 
Tenth plan Rs. 99.00 crore were allocated out of which only Rs. 67.74 crore were spent and Rs. 31.26 crore remain unspent. 
The reason quoted by DSIR for such shor tfall in actual expenditure under TPDU programme was inadequate publicity of the 
programme through printed and tele-media, d earth of good proposals received for the programme and non-receipt of utilization 
certificates from the beneficiaries of the programme in spite of rigorous follow-up. By going through the entire explanation 
given by the Department about TPDU programme, the Committee feels that the monitoring of the programme is not at all 
satisfactory on the part of the Department and that is why the utilization certificate could not have been obtained from the 
beneficiaries in spite of the strict follow up of TPDP. The Committee therefore is of  the opinion that the monitoring mechanism 
of the projects adopted by the Department contains deficiencies at every stage which needs to be strengthened and spilling over  
of projects to future plan period should be avoided as far as  possible so that strategic projects could be completed within a 
reasonable time frame. The Committee feels th at it is a serious lacunae on the part of the Department as regards the proper 
management and utilization of funds allocated for the TPDU. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has undertaken  to freshly implement IT and e-governance mechanism to ensure efficient and satisfactory 
monitoring of all projects in the Results Framework Document  (RFD), submitted to the Cabinet Secretariat. The Department 
has also committed - in RFD, to reduce the processing time of applications received from applicants under various schemes. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and reiter ates that the monitoring  mechanisms of the Department needs to be 
strengthened and made efficient. In this regard the Committee hopes that the commitment of the Department to reduce 
the processing time of applications in the RFD, would be followed in letter and sprit. 
Recommendation (Para 6.7) 
 The Committee feels that the various programmes and acti vities under the TPDU are essential for the nation as it 
promotes industrial research and developmen t, commercialization and export of indigenous technologies, acquisition of cutting 
edge technologies from abroad, promotion of consultancy capabilities and also ensures participation of both public and private 
sectors in technology development efforts. Therefore the Committee strongly recommends that the Department should 
formulate a comprehensive plan and take follow-up measures to overcome procedural problems such as non-receipt of 
utilization certificates from the beneficiaries, for optimal uti lization of funds for the scheme in future. The Department shou ld 
also ensure proper publicity of the TPDU schemes through the print and audio-visual media to draw more proposals for the 
schemes. 
 

Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has undertaken to formulate well conceived, st ructured and transparent guidelines for its schemes so as to 
avoid any procedural problems. It has adopted a system of peri odic review of programmes so as to ensure optimum utilization 
of funds. The Department shall make extensive use of print and audio-visual media to provide adequate publicity of the 
schemes for inviting more and more proposals from aspiring applicants. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that new guidelines would help in removing procedural delays and make 
monitoring more efficient and robust. Th e Committee also hopes that the use of print and audio-visual media will 
provide the schemes the required impetus and facilitate in getting more proposals for TPDU schemes. 
Recommendation (Paras 6.9 and 6.10) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that such ‘Global Knowledg e Partnerships’ are the need of the hour, where both the 
constituent partners stand to benefit from each others competence . Such programmes besides building up the confidence of our 
researchers would also be econom ically beneficial for the country as the inve stments costs of the research programmes are 
shared by both the partnering countries. The Committee th erefore recommends that such programmes should be further 
intensified with more partnerships particularly with South East Asian countries like Japan, Korea, China etc. apart from other 
scientifically advanced countries of Europe and strengthened with more funds allocation. 
 The Committee is glad to learn that CSIR had planned two mega projects and specific programmes in R&D domain such 
as Chemistry and Environment and Smart an d Functional Materials and Engineering Structures/Design and Electronics. Within 
these programmes the green chemistry mission as mentioned in para 6.7 above may be encouraged in view of the global 
warming and climate changes. The Committee feels that for developing green chemistry technology in addition to collaboration 
with Max Planck Group, Germany, collaborat ion with America, France and Britain should  also be encouraged in the field so 
that the global leaders in the technology could contribute to their maximum in developing the green chemistry in the country in  
the wake of persistent climate change. 
Action Taken by Government 
 As a part of CSIR’s overall sustainable energy initiative including climate change mitigation programme, international 
networking has been initiated through: 
a)  Setting up of Indo-French Centre for Sustainable Green Chemistry at IICT, Hyderabad 
b)  Taking up joint research projects with global leadership:- 
 USA: Climate Change impact in the ecosystem of Arabian Sea (NIO/Bigeland Lab, USA) 
 Russia: Impact of Climate Change induced stress on health (NEERI/Russian Academy of Sciences) 
 Japan: Green Chemistry Process by using solid catalysts (IICT/University of Gifer, Japan) 
 Australia: Trace Gas monitoring of climate forcing gases (NIO/CSIRO Marine Research) 
 UK: Electrode material for high density lithium battery (CECRI/University of Glasgow) 
c)  Many other joint projects are being negotiated in the ar ea of sustainable energy (Solar  energy, biofuels etc.) which 
has direct relevance to the climate change issues with major international research centres such as Univ. of Brasillia, 
Brazil (with IIP on Second generation of biofuels); Univ. of Byreuth, Germany (with NIIST on Hybrid Solar Cells), 
AIST, Japan (with NPL on Reliability of Photovoltaic modules), KIGAM, Korea (with IICT on Nanocomposite 
Photocatalysts for hydrogen production from water) etc. 
 All these programmes/initiatives are based on mutual interest  with sharing of costs, resources and intellectual property. 
 

Comments 
 The Committee expresses its satisfaction at the intern ational partnership programmes which are under operation. 
The Committee hopes that such programmes would be further encouraged.

 The Committee appreciates the fact that under the Eleventh  Five Year Plan allocation of the National Laboratories

10. The Committee hopes that the enhanced activities for the priority projects which have been projected. During the fourth year

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation (Para 7.6) 
 The Committee recommends that the expansion of National Labo ratories should be extended to all the States of the 
country, with at least one laboratory in each State so that there is equitable scientific progress in each State. The Committee also 
desires that comprehensive plans for setting of the National Laboratory in States which lack such facility may be forwarded to 
the State Governments, for preferential allotment of land. 
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR is willing to consider proposal of  establishing national laboratory/extension centres in such States where, there is no 
CSIR laboratory. However in order to bring in partnership with the State Government, the proposal could be mooted by the 
concerned State. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hope s that the Department will take up the matter with the concerned State 
Governments, instead of waiting for the proposal to be mooted by the State Government. 
Recommendation (Para 7.7) 
 The Committee is of the view that the investment in scie nce can only be fruitful in case its benefits reach the common 
man. Presently, the Committee feels that though the National Labo ratories are doing a lot of ingenious work as regards research  
and development in various scientific spheres but most of its  achievements are not reaching the common man. In fact’ the 
Committee feels that the Department of Scientific and Industria l Research can justify an increase in the Budgetary allocation 
only if substantial benefits reach the common man. The Comm ittee therefore recommends that the department should focus 
towards transfer of technologies from the National Laboratories to  the society particularly strategic areas to give benefit to the 
user.  
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR transfers its developed technologies regularly to industries through its constituent laboratories. Some recent 
technology transfers include: Improved process for centchroman (a non-steroidal oral contraceptive); treatment of Bio refractory 
wastewater without sludge and odor emission; A number of rural technologies to micro industries of different States of the 
country; Pulse oximeter; Surgical microscope for cataract surgery, Anaesthesia ventilator etc. 
 
 

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Depa rtment and hopes that such technolo gy transfers may be intensified so 
that the benefits of scientific research carried out in the CSIR Laboratories reach the common man. 
Recommendation (Para 7.9) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that since its inception, CSIR has played a very crucial role in the development of 
industrial technologies and research and development in various scientific areas. A time has come when it should increase its 
societal role and generate technologies for the common and deprived masses. In this direction the Department has taken a pro-
active step towards facilitating a socialistic, yet commercially , viable business model linked to the economic prosperity of a 
large and deprived masses through its CSIR-800 programme. The Committee therefore recommends that such programmes 
should be pursued vigorously and adequately funded so that the objectives of the programme are attained. 
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR is taking all necessary steps to earmark more funding during remaining period of the Eleventh Five Year Plan to 
pursue CSIR-800 programme more vigorously. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the CSIR-800 programme will be a success and justify the allocation of resources to the 
department for societal benefit. 
Recommendation (Para 7.11) 
 The Committee is aware that even in this age of medical adva ncement, there are a lot of fatalities in our country due to 
common tropical diseases like Malaria, Tuberculosis etc. due to absence of proper medication. The Committee therefore feels 
that the department should invest substantially in the research and development and devise innovative methods for inventing 
new drugs. In this direction, the ‘Open Drug Discovery Progr amme is an innovative method wherein the researchers can 
collectively utilize their knowledge base for the discovery of drugs. This will also bring down the cost involved in the resear ch 
and development process. The Committee therefore strongly recommends that the Department should give adequate budgetary 
support to this innovative programme. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Open Source Drug Discovery (OSDD) project has allocation of Rs 45.97 crore for the period 2007-2012. A budget 
Rs. 6.81 crore has been earmarked for the period 2010-11 for the purpose. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Department.  
Recommendation (Para 8.2) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that India being a tropical country has a lot of potential in the field of solar energy and 
appreciates the initiative of the Department in starting the Mega Project for large scale u tilization of solar energy. The 
Committee feels that the Department should explore the possibilities of exploiting more and more solar energy and in the long 
run it should aim at replacing the conventional source of energy with solar energy more so because conventional source of 
energy adds to the environmental pollution and global warming. Since the setting up of a solar power plant is capital intensive , 
the Committee recommends that the Department should be adequately funded to enable it to attain its objective. 
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR has submitted a Mega project on “Harvesting Solar Energy” to Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE), 
Government of India for financial support. The proposed CSIR and MNRE led National R&D initiative on solar energy 
‘Technologies and Products for Solar energy utilization through Networks (TAP-SUN)’ envisages to create networks of CSIR 
institutes, academia and indust ry with an objective to  integrate various components of t echnology development. The proposal 

envisages developing number of technology options for harvesting solar energy including technologies for grid power and 
distributed power generation. The proposed objectives of CSIR solar energy programme are to: 
 carry out R&D on next generation materials and devices for harvesting solar energy;  
 make available less expensive solar energy; 
 provide off-grid electricity to comm unities/villages to power small appliances and fulfil domestic needs; and 
 develop, demonstrate and deploy new applications of solar energy for rural, urban and industrial sectors. 
 The estimated cost of the program is Rs. 977.00 crore. The programme is expected to involve about hundred scientists 
drawn from twelve laboratories of CSIR and will constitute a virtual Network of Institutes in Solar Energy (CSIR-NISE). 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that adequate steps would be taken to strengthen the initiatives already 
taken in this regard as per the ‘Mega project on Solar Enegy’, so that Solar Energy can emerge as a cost effective source 
of energy. 
Recommendation (Para 8.4) 
 The Committee is of the view that the Department should invest substantially in establishing technologies for the 
treatment of diabetes which is afflicting a large portion of In dian population regardless of age and also decreasing their work  
potential. The Committee feels that in case the multinational companies are not in its favour, the Department can approach the 
Government aided hospitals, train their technicians and transfer the technology, so that the poor masses can avail of the cheap er 
alternative for the diagnosis of this disease. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Under New Millennium Indian Technology Leadership Initiative (NMITLI) scheme of CSIR, an auyrveda based 
formulation comprising single herb has been developed using reverse pharmacology approach as adjuvant to anti-
hyperglycemics like metformin. The formulation has been scientifically validated through pre-clinical, clinical Phase I, Phase 
IIA and Phase IIB and limited Phase III multi-centric clinical st udies in patients unc ontrolled on existing drugs such as 
sulphonylurea and metformin, thus decreasing the dosage requirement of existing drugs. 
 In another NMITLI project, oral delivery of insulin is being attempted. 
 During Eleventh Five Year Plan, CSIR laboratories ha ve undertaken a programme on “Diabetes mellitus-New drug 
discovery R&D, molecular mechanisms and genetic factors”. The objective of the programme is to design, synthesis/isolation 
and development of new therapeutic agents  from natural sources for type II diabetes ; elucidation of mechanism of action of 
promising agents; genetic, epigenetic and proteomic basis of th e disease and identification of biomarkers and mechanism of 
disease progression. The project is progressing well. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses satisfaction at the steps taken by the Department for the treatment for diabetes. The 
Committee hopes that the initiatives of th e Department bear fruit a nd a cheap and effective medicine for combating the 
disease would be available soon. 
Recommendation (Para 8.6) 
 The Committee takes serious note of the fact that there is a lack of initiative on the part of the Government to promote 
indigenous products, which is evident from the fact that none of the Government Departments or companies in the Private 
Sector were interested in the indigenous computer technology, though it has been acknowledged internationally i.e. by Malaysia 
and Brazil. The Committee feels that injustice is being meted out to the Indian scientists who developed the technology and wil l 
inevitably lower their morale and dissuade them from making such inventions in future. The Committee recommends that the 

Government should take the initiative to popularize such indigenous technologies and remove the bureaucratic hurdles which 
prevents such innovative inventions from becoming realities and internationally competitive. 
Action Taken by Government 
 In order to increase the utility and market potential as well as  awareness of the three variants of SofComp (Simple Office 
Computer) and Mobilis developed under the NMITLI scheme, second  phase of the project for market seeding of the products 
was supported under the scheme and soft loan amounting to Rs. 5 37 lakh was released to the indu stry partner Encore Software 
Limited, Bangalore. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the De partment and hopes that the initiati ves taken by the Department in the 
market seeding of the product would result in commercial utilization of the cheap and useful technology. 
Recommendation (Para 9.4) 
 The Committee notes that while the Research Fellowships an d Research Associateships schemes has shown an increasing 
trend; the Research Scheme and Emeritus Scientists Scheme has decreased in the year 2008-2009 as compared to the year 2007-
2008. The Committee fails to understand the reasons for the d ecreasing trend and expects that the decreasing trend would be 
reversed in future. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Projects under the Research Scheme and Emeritus Scientis ts Scheme are granted only on the basis of the evaluation 
of qualitative research object ives and merit of the proposal by discipline -wise committees comprising of researchers of 
eminence. Thus, the number of projects recommended under these sc hemes may vary from year to year. It is apprised that the 
number of projects sanctioned under these schemes has increased in the year 2009-10 as compared  to the previous year 2008-
09. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation (Para 9.5) 
 The Committee notes that there has been an increase of a m eager amount of Rs.12.00 crore only in the B.E. 2010-2011 as 
compared to the R.E. 2009-2010 and expresses concern over the negative trend in fund allocation towards Research Schemes, 
fellowships and Scholarships. The Committee is of the opinion that  creating a bank of well-traine d researchers is of paramount 
importance for the promotion of the diverse disciplines of science and technology in the country and would ultimately help in 
the nation building process and therefore the budgetary support too is necessary to create such a bank. The Committee hopes 
that the sufficient funds would be allotted for this purpose as th ere is an urgent need to enc ourage scientific research in 
universities, academic, scientific, engineering institutions and attract the brightest young persons to pursue their career in 
Science and Technology. 
Action Taken by Government 
 CSIR realizes the importance of well-trained researcher s for promotion of the diverse disciplines of science and 
technology resulting in nation building. Accordingly CSIR moved a Cabinet Note for increasing the number of Junior Research 
Fellowship (JRF) through CSIR-UGC National Eligibility Test (NET). Cabinet has approved the proposal and, hence, CSIR can 
support around 12,000 research students during Eleventh Five Year Plan period. 
Comments 
 The Committee appreciates the action taken by the Department to augment the number of researchers to provide 
the much needed support to the scientific progress in the country. 

Recommendation (Para 11.1) 
 The Committee feels that if science in India has to progress,  the scientists have to be respected and provided proper 
incentives through faster promotional avenues. The Committee feels that bureaucratic hurdles such as delay in performance 
assessment should be strictly avoided to attract the best possible talent to join the organization. The Committee also 
recommends that the Department should take up the issue regardin g improvement in the pay scales of scientists vigorously with 
the government to ensure best possible career prospects for them. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has noted the recommendation of the Committ ee. The Department is making all the efforts to clear the 
backlog of the assessment of the Scientific cadre in the Organiza tion. At present, Pay scale for the Scientists is as per the S ixth 
Pay Commission recommendations. In addition, scientists get two additional increments and Professional Update Allowance 
(Rs. 10000, Rs. 20000 and Rs.30000 per annum depending upon the level). The Department will initiate a dialogue with the 
concerned ministries for a better pay package to the scientists, as recommended by the Committee. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation (Para 12.4) 
 The Committee appreciates the CSIR’s patent utilization rate of  8.67% compared to worldwide statistics, but is still of the 
opinion that due to lack of knowledge about Intellectual Pr operty Rights (IPR) amongst the researchers and a lack of an 
appropriate mechanism for transfer of the Intellectual Property a lot of research outcomes in universities, academic and research 
institutions, which hold potential for public good does not reach the industry or the public and instead are languishing in the  
laboratories. In some cases, there has also been delay in filing of patents specially as regards to our traditional knowledge a nd 
processes, on the part of our scientific institutions and other countries have taken patents on the products and processes whic h 
had been traditionally used in our country for ages. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should increase 
its focus towards the awareness of Intellectual Property issues so that our traditional knowledge is well protected and also ma ke 
sincere efforts for optimal transfer and utilization of Intellectual Property from research institution to industry. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Scientists in CSIR laboratories have been well exposed to  IPR protection through sustainable awareness efforts made 
during the last 15 years. However, this is a continual effort and CSIR keeps organizing specialized seminars/workshops on 
various aspects of IP management every year. Further, mechanis m for valorization of IP is being strengthened by setting up 
CSIR- Tech and by making available equity participation to CSIR and scientists and through facilitating licensing and 
development of IPR. CSIR has also made available access to  Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) developed by 
CSIR & Department of AYUSH, Ministry of Health & Family  Welfare to USPTO & EPO to prevent the grant of patents 
involving Indian Traditional Knowledge already known for centuries as given in our ancient texts. However, new knowledge 
generated based on Traditional knowledge is taken care of by patenting national and internationally. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation (Para 13.4) 
 The Committee expresses satisfaction at the achievements of the New Millennium Indian Technology Leadership 
Initiative (NMITLI) Scheme which seeks to encourage innovation centered scientific and technological developments and 
propel Indian industry towards a global leadership position, by utilizing the best available comp etencies of public funded R&D 
institutions, academia and private industry. The Committee also acknowledges its appreciation at the fact that the NMITLI 
programme is being operated in diverse areas  of technologies to yield cutting edge new technologies which are sustainable and 
eco-friendly such as ‘bio- refinery concept’ or the biopro cesses for tanneries, which will he lp in reducing  environmental 
pollution. The Committee feels that there is a lot of scope in  the development of computer software arena and therefore 

recommends that the Department should vigorously pursue this field, and try to manufacture products such as the low cost 
Personal Computer which has a huge market. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Under the scheme a cost effective simple Office Computing (SafCamp) platform has been developed. Based on the 
platform technology, three variants have been developed. The device has a small set of canned applications for easy use and 
maintenance. It has low power consumption and is operable with poor quality mains power supply and during extended periods 
of power failure also. With a battery backup of 7 -8 hours, it is ideal for rural conditions where power. breakdown is a freque nt 
feature. It can also be customized for deployment in field areas. The development of SafCamp can serve the computing needs of 
small businesses, Government offices, schools and self employed professionals. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that technologies for the benefit of common man viz. Sofcomp, would be commercialized and 
would reach the people from the research laboratories expeditiously. 
Recommendation (Para 14) 
 The Committee is of the view that the Department of Scien tific & Industrial Research through its various research and 
development programmes is striving to uplift the standard of living in the country. Keeping in view the excellent work that the  
Department has undertaken, the kind of competence it has exhibited and the kind of core and critical programmes it has 
envisaged for the future, the Committee strongly recommends that the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research should be 
properly and adequately funded and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. stage should be strictly avoided. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department notes appreciation by the Committee. It will uphold the Committee’s expectations. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply.
