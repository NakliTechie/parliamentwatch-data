  INTRODUCTION 
 
 
           I, the Chairman of the Department -related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science 
and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change, having been authorized by the 
Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Four Hundred  Third Report on ―Review 
of the functioning of CSIR -Central Scientific Instruments Organization (CSIR -CSIO) and CSIR -
Institute of Genomics and Integrative Biology (CSIR-IGIB)‖. 
 
2.  CSIR - Central Scientific Instruments Organization (CSIR -CSIO), a constitue nt unit of Council 
of Scientific & Industrial Research (CSIR), is a premier national laboratory dedicated to research, 
design and development of scientific and industrial instruments.  Recognizing the rapidly 
growing global importance of the instrumentatio n sector, the Committee visited CSIR -CSIO, 
Chandigarh on 19th September 2025 as part of its study tour to Chandigarh and Mumbai from 19th 
to 22nd September 2025 to gain a comprehensive, first -hand understanding of CSIO’s pioneering 
research, innovative technologies, and ongoing challenges.   
 
3.     CSIR-IGIB is a premier Institute of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), 
engaged in research of national importance in the areas of genomics, molecular medicine, 
bioinformatics and proteomics.  As the work conducted at IGIB is critical not just for advancing 
the frontiers of science but also for its direct impact on public health, personalized medicine, and 
biotechnology in India, the Committee considered it imperative to witness the operatio ns in their 
state-of-the-art laboratories from fundamental genomic sequencing to the development of 
innovative diagnostic tools and to grasp the on -ground realities of scientific innovation. The 
Committee undertook a local study visit to CSIR-IGIB on 7th November, 2025 to understand, first-
hand, the path -breaking research, operational challenges, and significant potential of IGIB in the 
realm of genomics and integrative biology.  
4.  The Committee expresses its thanks to the officers of the Department of Sc ientific and 
Industrial Research, Council of Scientific and Industrial Research, CSIR -Central Scientific 
Instruments Organization and CSIR - Institute of Genomics and Integrative Biology for placing 
before the Committee the required material and replying to  the clarifications sought by the 
Members on the subject. 
5.      In the meeting held on 9 th December, 2025, the Committee considered the draft Report and 
adopted the same.

CSIR-CENTRAL SCIENTIFIC INSTRUMENTS ORGANIZATION (CSIR-CSIO) 
Introduction 
1.1 CSIR- Central Scientific Instruments Organization (CSIR -CSIO), a 
constituent unit of Council of Scientific & Industrial Research (CSIR), is a premier 
national laboratory dedicated to research, design and development of scientific and 
industrial instruments. It i s a multi -disciplinary and multi -dimensional apex 
industrial research & development organization in the country whose mission is to 
stimulate the growth of scientific and industrial instrument industry in India.  
1.2 CSIO has well equipped laboratories man ned by highly qualified and well 
trained staff with infrastructural facil ities in the areas of Agrionics, Medical 
Instrumentation and Prosthetic Devices, Optics and Cockpit based Instrumentation, 
Fiber/Laser Optics based Sensors & Instrumentation, Analytic al Instrumentation, 
Advanced Materials based Transducers etc. Large number of instruments ranging 
from simple to highly sophisticated ones, have been designed and developed by the 
Institute and their know -how has been passed on to the industry for commerci al 
exploitation. 
1.3 The primary R &D domains of CSIR -CSIO include optical instrumentation, 
biomedical instrumentation, Artificial Intelligence enabled systems, sensors and  
instrumentation technologies to support India’s strategic, societal and industrial  
sectors. The research areas of CSIR -CSIO are mainly focused on agrionics, 
medical instrumentation and prosthetic devices, optics and cockpit  based 
instrumentation, Fiber/Laser Optics based Sensors and Instrumentation, Analytical 
Instrumentation, Advanced Materials based Instrumentation and Devices. 
1.4 Currently, the organization structure of CSIR-CSIO comprises various R&D 
verticals, Centres of Excellence , S&T facilities, R&D Management and HRD 
support system. There are 6 primary R& D verticals at CSIR -CSIO namely 
Intelligent Machines and Computing Systems (IMCS), Applied Materials and 
Instrumentation (AMI), Optics & Photonics Instrumentation (OPI), Bio -medical 
Instrumentation, Advanced Engineering and Design, Energy Management & 
Instrumentation (Chennai Centre).The organization structure of CSIR -CSIO is as

1.12 Noting that the sanctioned strength and the in-position strength of CSIO for the 
year 2024-25 stood at 532 and 257 respectively, the Committee inquired about the 
reasons for such a significant number of vacancies. In response, CSIO submitted that 
vacancies exist due to the following reasons: 
i. The COVID-19 pandemic in India reported on January 30, 2020, and its major 
impact lasted till 2023, with waves of infections, lockdowns, and vaccination 
drives. There have been a large number of superannuations in CSIO, during the 
last few years. The prolonged CO VID-19 pandemic from early 2020 severely 
disrupted recruitment cycles and administrative operations. Movement 
restrictions, suspension of physical interviews, and reallocation of resources 
toward pandemic management led to unavoidable delays in hiring processes. 
ii. In the past, Scientific & Technical posts vacant for more than 5 years were not 
considered as deemed abolished by Department of Expenditure. Therefore, in 
order to avoid large number of recruitment/superannuation in one go only 10% 
of vacancies were  advertised in one recruitment cycle. This practice of 
staggered advertisement was followed to ensure smooth transition in Labs at 

6 
 
the time of superannuation in future. Presently, posts vacant for more than 5 
years are considered as deemed abolished by Dep artment of Expenditure. 
Therefore, proposal for revival of deemed abolished posts vacant for more than 
5 years are pending with the Department of Expenditure. As per Ministry of 
Finance Office Memorandum No. 7(1)/E.Coord -I/2017(Part.II) dated 
04.01.2024, p osts lying vacant for more than five years have been deemed 
abolished. This has directly impacted the ability to fill certain longstanding 
vacancies.  
iii. Presently, CSIR-CSIO has initiated multiple recruitment drives to address the 
existing live vacancies. Th e details of ongoing recruitment processes, which 
are at the stage of examination and selection, are as under:  
I. Scientific Posts (Gr. IV) – Out of 30 vacant posts, 17 posts have been 
advertised vide Advt. No. Regular 02/2025. For remaining posts, 
recruitment process will be initiated after requisite approval from the 
CSIR/GOI.

III. Technical Posts (Gr. II) – Out of 93 vacant posts, 44 posts have been

IV. Group I admin related posts – Out of 71 vacancies, 20 vacancies have 
been filled up after 1.4.2025. For remaining posts, re cruitment process

CSIR. 
V. Due to all above adm inistrative procedural & process issues these

1.13 The Committee notes with concern that CSIR -CSIO is operating at less 
than half of its sanctioned strength in 2024 -25. The Committee is of the 

7 
 
considered opinion that an institution like CSIO, which is fundamentally driven 
by scientific and technical expertise, cannot function effectively without 
adequate manpower, and the current shortage poses a serious threat to its 
research output, technological innovation, an d future potential . If existing 
vacancies are kept  unfilled, CSIO’s performance and ability to deliver on 
critical projects will stand severely compromised.  
1.14 The Committee is unable to comprehend the rationale behind advertising 
only 10 percent of vac ancies in each cycle and leaving such a large number of 
posts unfilled for years, a practice that has clearly contributed to the present 
crisis. The Committee is of the view that this staggered approach to recruitment, 
coupled with the issue of deemed -abolished posts, has been a significant 
administrative lapse. Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that 
CSIR-CSIO should take all necessary action to ensure that the advertised 
vacancies are filled up expeditiously. Further, DSIR should impress upon th e 
Department of Expenditure to revive the deemed -abolished posts without any 
further delay, so that CSIO can restore its functional capacity and fulfil its 
strategic role in the national science and technology ecosystem. 
Agrionics 
1.15 Agrionics, one of the research areas of CSIR-CSIO, is a multidisciplinary field 
that combines agriculture and electronics/instrumentation to enhance productivity, 
safety, precision and automation in farming and allied application areas. Agrionics 
involves the design, development and implementation of electronics, smart sensors, 
control circuit, transducers, embedded system and communication modules to 
monitor, control the operational parameters of agriculture such as soil moisture, 
temperature, hu midity, nutrients levels, pH and crop health. Such systems enable 
safety, precision agriculture with data -driven decisions to improve  yield, conserve 
resources and reduced human efforts.  
1.16 While briefing the Committee about the technological advancemen ts made by 
CSIR-CSIO, the Committee was also informed that  CSIR-CSIO has developed 
various technologies in the domain of agrionics. CSIR-CSIO has been at the forefront 
of developing advanced sensors and instrumentation technologies to support India’s 
agricultural sector. Recognizing the need for precision, sustainability, and efficiency 

8 
 
in farming practices, CSIR -CSIO has designed a wide range of Agri -instrumentation 
solutions that integrate sensors, associated electronics, and automation. These include 
soil nutrient and moisture sensors, crop health monitoring systems, electrostatic 
sprayers, post -harvest quality assessment tools, and technologies for detecting 
adulteration in food products. By enabling data -driven decision-making, improving 
resource utilization and ensuring quality control across the agricultural value chain, 
CSIR-CSIO’s innovations aim to empower farmers, enhance productivity, and 
contribute to national food security through indigenous, cost-effective technologies.  
1.17 The key achievemen ts of CSIR-CSIO in this field include  development of 
several technologies and their technology transfers to the relevant industries. The 
agricultural instrumentation technologies developed by CSIR -CSIO, are poised to 
significantly enhance the safety, susta inability, and productivity of India’s 
agricultural ecosystem. Sensitive bio (chemo) sensing strips, portable electrochemical 
sensor arrays, and pocket colorimeters for multi  parameter testing enable rapid, on -
site detection of pesticides, antibiotics, myc otoxins, and adulterants, ensuring food 
safety and protecting public health. Affordable IoT -enabled water service delivery 
and monitoring systems for rural deployment strengthen water management practices, 
ensuring equitable distribution and efficient util ization of this critical resource in 
farming communities. Advanced sensing methods for detecting gluten adulteration 
and edible oil contamination, along with SERS -based analytical platforms, directly 
contribute to quality assurance across the food supply chain.  
1.18 Post-harvest innovations, such as edible coating materials, biodegradable 
electrostatic coatings  and novel preservation technologies, extend the shelf life of 
perishable crops, reduce wastage, and improve farmers’ income. Region -specific 
smart agro-technologies, coupled with aerial electrostatic spraying systems for locust 
and pest control, bring precision agriculture closer to reality, reducing chemical use 
while increasing crop protection efficiency. Collectively, these indigenous, 
affordable, and field -deployable solutions empower farmers with actionable data, 
enhance rural resilience, promote sustainable practices, and support India’s food 
security and self-reliance goals.  
1.19 The Committee commends CSIR -CSIO for its outstanding and sustain ed 
contribution to the emerging field of agrionics.  The Committee notes that 
CSIO’s innovations have empowered farmers with real -time, actionable data 

9 
 
and have also strengthened food safety, improved resource management, and 
enhanced productivity across th e agricultural value chain.   While significant 
progress has been made in precision agriculture and quality assurance, the issue 
of stubble burning remains one of the most complex and persistent challenges 
facing Indian farming today. Farmers often resort t o burning due to limited 
time between crop cycles, high costs of residue management machinery, labour 
shortages, and the lack of accessible, affordable alternatives for residue 
utilization. The resulting air pollution, soil nutrient loss, and greenhouse ga s 
emissions make stubble burning a problem of national significance . In view of 
this, the Committee recommends CSIR -CSIO to extend its proven expertise in 
the field of agrionics towards developing practical, low-cost, innovative, scalable 
and farmer-friendly technology-driven solutions for effective stubble 
management. 
Instrumentation, devices, and technologies developed by CSIO 
1.20 The Committee asked CSIR -CSIO about India’s current global standing in the 
field of Instrumentation Sciences and the efforts being undertaken by CSIR -CSIO 
and DSIR to position the country as a global leader in this domain. In response, 
CSIR-CSIO stated that India presently holds an emerging yet strategically important 
position in the global instrumentation landscape . The country  has demonstrated a 
rapid growth in various  sectors such as process automation, biomedical 
instrumentation, analytical instrumentation,  optical instrumentation, and also few of 
the sensor technologies. India’s market size of  industrial sensors and transmit ters 
alone is expected to grow with a CAGR of 9.42% during  2025-30. Similarly, Indian 
market of analytical instrumentation is forecasted to have a  growth of 8.2% during 
the period of 2025 -33. The growth of market and R&D innovations  shows India’s 
accelerated transition towards digitization, Industry 4.0 programs, Industrial  
automation, self-reliance in avionics and aerospace, instrumentation associated with 
the space technology, nuclear technology, energy and healthcare domain. 
1.21 The global demand for In dian-made aerospace components, devices and 
vehicles is steeply  increasing. Although, the aerospace market is driven by 
international giants like Airbus and  Boeing, India is playing a vital role in the global 
aerospace market through components and services. Due to the cost competitiveness 
and skilled workforce, major global aerospace manufacturers are seeking to diversify 

10 
 
their supply chain of aerospace  components and devices. In aerospace sector, the 
revenue of India-based components and services has grown from USD 250 million to 
USD 2 billion. The Indian manufactured fixed  wing aircrafts (such as Dornier 228 
etc.), rotary wing advanced light helicopter (including  Chetak, Dhruv etc.) have huge 
global demand. The CSIR research laboratories, including  CSIR-National Aerospace 
Laboratory, CSIR-CSIO has played a vital role in developing  various components, 
display systems (Head-up Display) and illumination systems for light  combat aircraft 
(LCA). Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) has played a key role as  manufacturer 
or production partner for LCA Tejas and other aerospace related technologies. 
1.22 The Committee was further informed that i n the domain of aerospace 
instrumentation, CSIR -CSIO Chandigarh is playing a pivotal  role through 
development of indig enous instrumentation, sensing systems, display devices(Head -
up display (HUD), Helmet mounted display system (HMDS) etc.) illumination  
systems (Drogue light, Anti-collision light, Fin navigation lights, Lap light, Taxi and  
landing lights, Wing navigation lights, Marker lights, etc.). In futuristic research plan, 
CSIR-CSIO is aiming to develop the hybrid optical gyroscope (HOG) by leveraging 
its capability to design and fabricate the integrated photonic components at recently  
established Centre for Nano-Optics Fabrication (CNOF). 
1.23  In the areas of avionics, CSIR -CSIO has successfully developed a number of 
technologies for the Indian Armed Forces, particularly for the  Indian Air Force. The 
researchers have successfully developed the Head -Up Display (HUD) for LCA Mk-I 
and delivered more than 100 airworthy units of HUD. Additionally,  HUD test 
platform was developed and commissioned.  Apart from HUD, Landing and Taxi 
Lights, Navigation Lights, Air -to-Air Refueling Lighting  Systems and associated 
supporting test equipment for LCA Mk-I were developed at CSIR-CSIO Chandigarh. 
The organization has received a significant amount of royalty (~6.5 Crore  Rupees) 
from Bharat Electronics Ltd. (BEL)  against the production  of HUD and avionics 
technologies of LCA Mk-I. In addition to instrumentation and technologies for LCA, 
the researchers at CSIR -CSIO has also  developed optical sight for DO -Dornier-228 
and Marine bore sighting tool for Indian Navy. 
1.24 During the discussion, the Committee was also informed that CSIR-CSIO was 
approached by Indian Navy for repair/refurbishment of Attack Periscope of 
submarines. CSIR -CSIO in  collaboration with M/s ELCOME Pvt. Ltd has 

11 
 
refurbished the periscope by  repairing/fabricating the optical components and 
complete redesign of the electroni cs of the  system. The attack periscope is currently 
being installed onboard INS Arihant at Naval  Dockyard, Vishakhapatnam. There is 
approximately 10-fold cost saving in foreign exchange.  Approximate cost of a new 
periscope is Rs. 300 Crores, whereas the in digenous one is projected to  cost Rs. 30 
Crores. 
1.25 CSIR-CSIO has been actively engaged in the development of  biomedical 
instrumentation to address the growing healthcare needs of the country. With a strong 
emphasis on translational research, CSIR -CSIO’s biomedical instrumentation 
activities focus on designing and developing innovative, affordable, and indigenous 
medical devices,  diagnostic tools, and assistive technologies such as eye gaze 
tracking-based assistive  technologies. Furthermore, CSIR -CSIO has  developed a 
machine for automating the double  volume exchange transfusion process through 
umbilical route in neonates, for which Ex -Vivo satisfactory clinical evaluation and 
trials were carried out at Neonatal ICU, PGIMER,  Chandigarh using donor blood 
bag. Other therapeutic tools and systems were developed at CSIR -CSIO including an 
instrument for  solid tumor targeting using plasmonic photo -thermal technique, an 
image-guided vein  visualizer (Vein -Viz). Apart from therapeutic applications, 
researchers at CSIR-CSIO ha ve developed a few rehabilitation and monitoring 
systems such as gait training tool and mobility aids for Parkinson’s patients, Dialysis 
machine for hemodialysis of chronic kidney disease patients, additively manufactured 
orthopedic implants. 
1.26 CSIR-CSIO has become a technology developer in the field of advanced  
sensors and instrumentation for agriculture sector. Various technologies have been

Prospective Research Frontiers for the Coming Years 
1.30 The Committee then sought information on some of the futuristic and cutting

I. Next-Generation Display Technologies and Diffractive Optical Elements : 
One of the major research projects of CSIR -CSIO is focused towards the 
design and development of waveguide based Head -Up Displ ay (HUD). The 
waveguide optics for Head -Up-Display requires the nanofabrication of large -
area diffraction grating including input grating, turn -grating and out coupling 
grating for HUD. The nanoscale feature size will be fabricated and realized at 
Centre f or Nano -Optics Fabrication (CNOF) – recently established at CSIR -
CSIO Chandigarh. The in -house fabricated waveguide slab will also find

project on development of mini -/micro-LED display will drive the display 
industry in India.  
II. Quantum Photonic Chip and Photonics Integrated Circuits (PIC):  
Photonic Integrated Circuits (PICs), unlike Electronic Integrate d Circuits 
(EIC), uses photons instead of electrons which enables high -speed, low-power 
optical data transmission and processing on a chip -scale platform. The 
development of silicon, silicon nitride, and indium phosphide platform will 
enable realization of  high-speed optical interconnects. The researchers at 
CSIR-CSIO Chandigarh are also focused on developing advanced PIC 
architectures including transceivers, MUX/DEMUX, photonic logic gates, and 

14 
 
quantum photonic chips. These quantum photonic chips are future of chip-scale 
quantum computing across the globe. 
III. Meta-Optics and Meta -surfaces: Meta-surfaces, which are subwave  length 
structures of desired shape, size, orientation and periodicity, artificially 
fabricated on a planar substrate, are capable of tailori ng amplitude, phase and 
polarization of incident light. The research teams at CSIR -CSIO are utilizing 
these capabilities to design and develop meta -lens, meta -surface absorber, 
meta-surface for AR/VR goggles and meta -surface-enabled THz nano antenna 
for reconfigurable intelligent.  
IV. 6G Components and Terahertz devices : After 5G , the world is moving 
towards 6G in commercial sector. The researchers at CSIR -CSIO are also 
focusing on the development of on chip terahertz emitters, detectors, and 
waveguides using plasmonics and photonic techniques. The CMOS compatible 
terahertz resonators and antenna array with 0.1 – 0.3 THz bandwidth are in 
R&D phase. Furthermore, the next generation on -chip optical sensors such as 
compact hybrid optical gyroscope (HOG) and on -chip biosensors will be 
crucial in the futuristic applications such as long -range drones and wearable 
healthcare devices.  
V. Hydrogen Generation Technology:  CSIR-CSIO is advancing hydrogen 
technology research to support India’s clean energy transition. The institute has 
taken some initiatives on developing efficient and scalable electrochemical and 
photo-electrochemical processes for hydrogen generation from water, 
including low-grade wastewater. Efforts include novel material development, 
system design, and ele ctrolyzer optimization in collaboration with EIL and 
Cheema Boilers. CSIR-CSIO is also working with TATA Motors on hydrogen 
sensing for H2 -IC engines and portable hydrogen storage systems. These 
initiatives aim to enhance indigenous hydrogen instrumentatio n capability, 
promote green hydrogen adoption, and contribute to India’s net-zero goals.

titanium implants, a polymer 3D printer for anatomical models and surgical

Transfer of technologies  
1.32 During the interaction, the Committee sought information on multiple aspects 
of technology transfer, including the transfer process, criteria for selecting user 
industries, the list of industries that benefited from technology transfers over the past 
five years, product commercialization, revenue from royalties and license fees, 
marketing and promotion of industry -ready technologies, and the conduct of third -
party audits of the technology transfer process. 
1.33 In response, CSIR -CSIO stated that t he technology is first offered for transfer 
by the concerned Project Investigator, after which the Technology Evaluation 
Committee validates the technology. Subsequently, the duly consti tuted ToT 
Committee finalizes the license fee, royalty, exclusivity, duration, and other related 
terms. Accordingly, an Expression of Interest (EoI) is published to identify potential 
industrial partners for the transfer of technology. Interested parties s ubmit their 
proposals, which are reviewed by a committee. Based on this review, shortlisted 

17 
 
industry partners are invited to deliver technical presentations before the Empowered 
Committee. Following the evaluation, the committee finalizes the selection of 
industry partners. 
1.34 On finalization of ToT, CSIR -CSIO receives license fee, royalty based on the 
agreed terms and conditions at the time of signing the ToT agreement, after the 
product is commercialized. The typical duration of license validity varies from 1 to 
15 years, depending on the case. After the expiry of the license, renewal is carried out 
by charging the applicable license fee on a case-by-case basis.  
1.35 CSIR-CSIO also actively engages with industries by showcasing its developed 
technologies at various events such as exhibitions, conferences, and workshops. 
CSIO has a dedicated Business Development Group that ensures the effective 
showcasing of technologies developed at CSIR -CSIO in collaboration with the 
concerned Project Investigator of each technology.  
1.36 CSIO informed the Committee that t he main challenge faced  during 
technology transfer to industry is the delay in product commercialization by the ToT  
partner, which often results in the obsolescence of the technology or the launch of 
similar technologies by competitors in the market. To address this issue and enable 
faster commercialization, CSIR-CSIO will henceforth try to engage industry partners 
from the early stages of technology development.

Introduction 
2.1 CSIR-Institute of Genomics & Integrative Biology (IGIB) is a premier Institute 
of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), engaged in research of 
national importance in the areas of genomics, molecular medicine, bioinformatics and 
proteomics. Their mission is t o translate concepts developed in basic biological 
research to commercially viable technologies for health care.

Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change undertook a local study visit 
to CSIR -IGIB on 7 th November, 2025 to understand, first -hand, the path -breaking 
research, operational challenges, and  significant potential of IGIB in the realm  of 
genomics and integrative biology.  As the work conducted  at IGIB is critical not just 
for advancing the frontiers of science  but also for its direct impact on public health, 
personalized medicine, and biotechnology in India , the Committee considered it 
imperative to w itness the  operations in their  state-of-the-art laboratories from  
fundamental genomic sequencing to the development of  innovative diagnostic tools  
and to grasp the on-ground realities of scientific innovation.  During the visit, the

2.3 During the visit, the Committee inspected the following facilities at IGIB: 
i. Biobank facility for Phenome India project - IGIB has a state -of-the-art 
Phenome India National Biobank on the campus. The Committee was informed 
that the biobank stores and maintains biological samples collected for Phenome 
India project, a nationwide cohort study which involves collecting 
comprehensive genomic, lifestyle, and clinical data from over 10,000 
individuals across India. This initiative will aid in early diagnosis, therapeutic 

21 
 
targeting, and the fig ht against complex diseases such as diabetes, cancer, and 
rare genetic disorders. 
ii. Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) facility for gene therapy of sickle cell 
anemia- The Committee noted that, i n a mission to eradicate Sickle -Cell 
Anemia from the country, IGIB has created a Good Manufacturing Practice 
(GMP) Facility on its campus in collaboration with multiple stakeholders such 
as the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, Niti Aayog, Serum Institute of India, and the 
Ministry of Science and Technology. The Committee was told that t his facility 
will provide  ex-vivo CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing therapy options that use the 
patient's own blood, thereby, reducing the risk of immune rejection. This 
facility will receive blood samples from patients wit h sickle cell anemia and 
edit them using CRISPR -Cas9 technology. The blood sample will then be 
transfused back to the same patient in the hospital. Once fully functional, this 
facility will offer the potential for a one -time treatment approach towards 
sickle-cell anemia. 
iii. Biosafety Level 3 ( BSL3) facility towards TB -Mukt Bharat- The Biosafety 
Level 3 facility (BSL -3) at CSIR -IGIB is dedicated primarily to Tuberculosis 
research. The facility is equipped with negative -pressure rooms, High 
Efficiency Particulate Air/High Efficiency Particulate Arresting (HEPA) filters, 
and biosafety cabinets that allow students to work safely with Mycobacterium 
tuberculosis. Biosafety PPE kits and masks are worn by researchers for safety 
while they work with bacteria in this fac ility. The Committee observed that 
research being undertaken at this facility will give a better understanding of 
Tuberculosis and how the host immune response plays a role in Mycobacterium 
tuberculosis pathogenicity and how these mechanisms can be exploit ed to 
develop host-directed therapies. 
iv. Zebrafish facility for genetics, cancer, drug discovery and neuro -
behavioural diseases –The Committee was informed that  CSIR-IGIB’s 
zebrafish facility is among the largest zebrafish  facilities in the country. It 
houses many strains of wild -type fish and even more transgenic lines. The 
facility specialises in disease modelling, genetic manipulation, behaviour 
testing and developmental studies. The Committee was apprised that the facility 
is primarily student -run and uses state -of-the-art water circulation and 
purification systems. It houses multiple strains of wild -type fish, including 
Assamese wild-type (ASWT), which is native to the Brahmaputra River. CSIR-

22 
 
IGIB was the first to perfo rm high -depth genome seq uencing of the ASWT 
strain and put it on the map as a model for genomic studies. A variety of 
transgenic lines, including neural markers, are also maintained. The facility 
specialises in disease modelling, gene manipulation, genetic , epigenetic, 
behavioural and developmental studies. Zebrafish serves as an animal model to 
understand the pathophysiology of multiple genetic disorders and to help 
elucidate the effects of genetic and chemical interventions in alleviating or 
preventing th e defects. Currently, IGIB is focusing on creating zebrafish 
models of neuro -developmental disorders resulting from defects in brain 
development and on understanding defects in neuronal development, function, 
and behaviour. 
v. Genomics facility for genome sequencing, cancer genomics and rare 
genetic disorders - Sequencing facility -IGIB has a stand -alone sequencing 
facility equipped with MiSeq and NovaSeq machines used for human genomic 
sequencing. The Committee was told that  NovaSeq is a high -throughput 
instrument that generates large amounts of data in terabytes, while the MiSeq 
generates about 500 Mb of data in a single run and is usually used for bacterial 
whole-genome sequencing. Both of these machines are used for collaborative 
Pan-India projects such a s the Indian Breast Cancer Genome Atlas (IBCGA) 
and Phenome India. IGIB generates whole -genome, transcriptome, and 
metagenome sequences for these projects. Around 6,000 gut microbiome 
samples have been sequenced under the Phenome India project.

Indian Population Genomics  
2.12 During the presentation, the Committee was informed that CSIR-IGIB played a 
pioneering role in creating the Indian Reference Genomes, the first comprehensive

They have sequenced the entire genomes of 1008 Indians in 2020. Under the Genome 
India programme, initiated in collaboration with CSIR and DBT, IGIB has sequenced 
thousands of genomes to create an inclusive database representing the genetic 
diversity of India. CSIR -IGIB is one of the four whole genome sequencing centers 
and is responsible for the collection of 3390 samples and the sequencing of over 2500 
human genome samples for the Genome  India 10000 target. The data forms the 
foundation for personalized and precision m edicine, enabling better understanding of 
disease predispositions and drug responses among India. 
2.13 The Committee inquired about India’s global standing in genomic research and 
the steps taken by CSIR -IGIB and DSIR to establish India as a leader in this  field. In 
response, it was submitted that India has sequenced about 10,000 genomes, compared 
with several hundred thousand worldwide. IGIB is contributing through initiatives 
such as Genome India, Phenome India, microbiome atlas projects, breast cancer 
genomics, and CRISPR programmes. It was emphasized that consistent and enhanced 
funding is essential to position India as a global leader in this field. 
2.14 The Committee acknowledges the significant achievements of CSIR -IGIB 
in advancing genomics in India.  However, the Committee notes that I ndia’s 
current genomic coverage, approximately 10,000 sequenced genomes, remains 
far from global benchmarks and does not represent the extrao rdinary genetic 
diversity of this  country. For a population as large and as heterogeneous as 
India, the Committee recommends that CSIR -IGIB, in collaboration with 
national scientific agencies, develop a long -term roadmap for sequencing at least 
10 million genomes and lead efforts to create a comprehensive National 
Reference Genome that captures the full spectrum of India’s geographic, ethnic 

27 
 
and linguistic diversity. The Committee feels that this endeavour will enable the

development of population -specific diagnostics and personalized  and precision 
medicine and provide  a deeper  understanding of  population history  such as

effective disease mapping. The Committee recommends that DSIR should extend 
sufficient funding, policy support, and infrastructure to CSIR-IGIB to enable the 
sequencing of at least 10 million genomes. 
 
National Rare Disease Diagnostics Programme 
 
2.15 The Committee was informed that CSIR-IGIB has developed a comprehensive

offering early diagnosis and actionable medical insights.

with All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Postgraduate Institute of

genomics into clinica l practice. IGIB has also been designated as a nodal institution 
supporting the National Policy for Rare Diseases (2021) and contributes to the 
national registry of diagnosed cases.

CHAPTER –III 
Strengthening CSIR’s Global Competitiveness  
3.1  The Committee notes that scientists constitute the intellectual backbone of 
CSIR, and enhancing CSIR’s global competitiveness requires empowering them 
with greater autonomy, adequate resources, and operational flexibility. The 
Committee feels that CSIR’s i nnovation capacity is constrained by 
administrative delays, further compounded by an imbalance wherein 
administrative staff outnumber scientists. Timely and predictable funding, 
robust administrative support, streamlined approvals, and online administrativ e 
processes are essential for accelerating scientific output. In view of the above, the 
Committee recommends that CSIR should review and reform its existing 
institutional ecosystem to ensure that administrative mechanisms facilitate 
rather than hinder scie ntific work. The Committee further recommends that 
Directors of the 37 laboratories under CSIR be granted autonomy in the 
approval of research proposals and be made accountable for outcomes. The 
Committee also impresses upon CSIR to digitize all administra tive processes 
end-to-end to enhance efficiency, transparency, and responsiveness.        
3.2 The Committee observes that, extant rules often limit scientists from 
taking sabbaticals, pursuing faculty visits, or engaging in collaborative research 
with institutions such as MIT, Stanford, or Harvard and setting up start -ups. 
These restrictions lead t o the underutilization of global opportunities and limit 
the flow of knowledge and technology. The Committee feels that, inorder to 
compete globally, CSIR must modernize its policies related to international 
engagement and professional mobility. A proactiv e global outreach strategy 
should be adopted to position CSIR as a preferred international collaborator. 
The Committee, therefore recommends CSIR to reform its existing policies and 
strengthen global engagement. The Committee further recommends CSIR to 
empower its scientific workforce through improved flexibility and resource 
access, to begin with, by designating scientists as project heads with full 
budgetary control and access to adequate administrative support. 
3.3 CSIR must also foster a strong culture of innovation and 
entrepreneurship, by empowering its scientists to co -found startups within 
institutional incubators. Encouraging deeper industry collaboration, greater IP 

36 
 
generation, and vibrant startup activity will significantly strengthen CSIR’s 
ability to contribute to national priorities. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that CSIR may consider permitting its scientific and technical 
personnel to establish or co -found startups, similar to what is already practiced 
in the IITs. CSIR may also explor e launching structured entrepreneurship 
programmes and supporting such startups through initial funding, grants, and 
seed capital to help convert laboratory innovations into viable commercial 
enterprises. This approach would enhance technology transfer, dr ive socio -
economic impact, and expand CSIR’s role in the national innovation ecosystem. 
The Committee further suggests introducing peer -driven, deliverable -based 
evaluation of research output, along with cumulative Pro fessional Development 
Allowance on the lines of IITs, to incentivize excellence and sustained scientific 
productivity. 
3.4 The Committee also notes that the current budgetary allocation to 
DSIR/CSIR is inadequate to meet the growing demands of frontier scientific 
research, development of advan ced laboratories, global collaboration, and 
sustained innovation-led national growth. In an era where countries are rapidly 
scaling their R&D investments, India must ensure that CSIR remains globally 
competitive and capable of delivering strategic technolo gies of national 
importance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government of India 
to substantially enhance the budgetary allocation to DSIR/CSIR to strengthen 
scientific infrastructure, modernize laboratories, and support high -risk, high -
impact research. The Committee further observes that corporate participation 
in national R&D remains limited, despite the provisions for Corporate  Social 
Responsibility (CSR). The Committee, therefore,  recommends the Government 
to evolve a clear and robust mechanis m to facilitate greater CSR contributions, 
both from public  and private sector enterprises, towards DSIR/CSIR -led 
research, innovation, and technology translation programmes.

prior consent from each partner bef ore disclosure. IGIB has assured the 
Committee that this process will be initiated without delay so that accurate 
details of the technologies transferred and the private entities involved can be 
made publicly accessible. Since IGIB is a publicly funded res earch institution, it 
must uphold standards of transparency, accountability, and equitable access to 
knowledge. The Committee, therefore, recommends CSIR -IGIB to p ublish 
details, within the limits of confidentiality agreements , regarding transferred 
technologies, participating firms, and  fees etc.. on its website as well as in its 
annual reports.                                                                                             (Para 2.33) 
 
Academics 
 
 The Committee notes that CSIR -IGIB conducts it s doctoral programme 
through the Academy of Scientific and Innovative Research (AcSIR), a premier 
institution dedicated exclusively to high -quality science and engineering 
education integrated with cutting -edge research carried out across CSIR 
laboratories. The Committee is of the view that AcSIR represents an ecosystem 
fully comparable to other leading research institutions currently eligible for the 
Prime Minister’s Research Fellowship (PMRF).The Committee feels that 
restricting PMRF only to IITs, IISERs, IISc and some Central Universities limits 
opportunities for exceptional doctoral candidates seeking to work within CSIR 
labs, despite the fact that these laboratories contribute significantly to India’s 
strategic research output and innovation landscape. The Committee therefore, 
recommends that the Government consider expanding PMRF eligibility to 
AcSIR, enabling top -tier research students at CSIR -IGIB and other CSIR 
laboratories to benefit from this prestigious fellowship and further enhancing 
India’s scientific and technological competitiveness.                               (Para 2.36)

CHAPTER –III 
Strengthening CSIR’s Global Competitiveness  
  The Committee notes that scientists constitute the intellectual backbone of 
CSIR, and enhancing CSIR’s global competitiveness requires empowering them 
with greater autonomy, adequate resources, and operational flexibility. The 
Committee feels that CSIR’s innovation capacity is constrained by 
administrative delays, further compounded by an imbalan ce wherein 
administrative staff outnumber scientists. Timely and predictable funding, 
robust administrative support, streamlined approvals, and online administrative 
processes are essential for accelerating scientific output. In view of the above, the 
Committee recommends t hat CSIR should review and reform its existing 
institutional ecosystem to ensure that administrative mechanisms facilitate 
rather than hinder scientific work. The Committee further recommends that 
Directors of the 37 laboratories under CSIR be granted auto nomy in the 
approval of research proposals and be made accountable for outcomes. The 
Committee also impresses upon CSIR to digitize all administrative processes 
end-to-end to enhance efficiency, transparency, and responsiveness.      (Para 3.1) 
 The Committee observes that, extant rules often limit scientists from 
taking sabbaticals, pursuing faculty visits, or engaging in collaborative research 
with institutions such as MIT, Stanford, or Harvard and setting up start -ups. 
These restrictions lead t o the underutilization of global opportunities and limit 
the flow of knowledge and technology. The Committee feels that, inorder to 
compete globally, CSIR must modernize its policies related to international 
engagement and professional mobility. A proactiv e global outreach strategy 
should be adopted to position CSIR as a preferred international collaborator. 
The Committee, therefore recommends CSIR to reform its existing policies and 
strengthen global engagement. The Committee further recommends CSIR to 
empower its scientific workforce through improved flexibility and resource 
access, to begin with, by designating scientists as project heads with full 
budgetary control and access to adequate administrative support.         (Para 3.2) 
 CSIR must also foster a  strong culture of innovation and 
entrepreneurship, by empowering its scientists to co -found startups within 
institutional incubators. Encouraging deeper industry collaboration, greater IP 

46 
 
generation, and vibrant startup activity will significantly strengt hen CSIR’s 
ability to contribute to national priorities. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that CSIR may consider permitting its scientific and technical 
personnel to establish or co -found startups, similar to what is already practiced 
in the IITs. CSIR  may also explore launching structured entrepreneurship 
programmes and supporting such startups through initial funding, grants, and 
seed capital to help convert laboratory innovations into viable commercial 
enterprises. This approach would enhance technol ogy transfer, drive socio -
economic impact, and expand CSIR’s role in the national innovation ecosystem. 
The Committee further suggests introducing peer -driven, deliverable -based 
evaluation of research output, along with cumulative Pro fessional Development 
Allowance on the lines of IITs, to incentivize excellence and sustained scientific 
productivity.                                                                                                  (Para 3.3) 
 The Committee also notes that the current budgetar y allocation to 
DSIR/CSIR is inadequate to meet the growing demands of frontier scientific 
research, development of advanced laboratories, global collaboration, and 
sustained innovation-led national growth. In an era where countries are rapidly 
scaling their R&D investments, India must ensure that CSIR remains globally 
competitive and capable of delivering strategic technologies of national 
importance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government of India 
to substantially enhance the budgetary alloc ation to DSIR/CSIR to strengthen 
scientific infrastructure, modernize laboratories, and support high -risk, high -
impact research. The Committee further observes that corporate participation 
in national R&D remains limited, despite the provisions for Corpora te Social 
Responsibility (CSR). The Committee, therefore,  recommends the Government 
to evolve a clear and robust mechanism to facil itate greater CSR contributions, 
both from public  and private sector enterprises, towards DSIR/CSIR -led 
research, innovation, and technology translation programmes.              (Para 3.4)
