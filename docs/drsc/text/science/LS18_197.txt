REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific
and Industrial Research on the recommendations contained in the One Hundred and Eighty-sixth
Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology,
Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2008-09) of the Department of
Scientific and Industrial Research, which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on
29th April, 2008. There were Nineteen recommendations in the One Hundred Eighty-sixth Report
of the Committee. Action Taken Notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received
from the Department on 31st July, 2008.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and further comments of the
Committee on the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research are set out
in the succeeding paragraphs:
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 2.3)
The Committee expresses serious concern over the token hike in the allocation for the
Department. It feels that this hike is basically no hike if the growth rate of 9 per cent and the
inflation of 5 per cent is taken into consideration. It only shows the amount of importance that
the Government today attaches to scientific organization like DSIR. The Committee is of the
opinion that the axe of routine cut should not fall on scientific departments as it would adversely
affect research and development which is vital for the development of the nation. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the well thought out projected demand of scientific department should
be honoured and accepted by the Planning Commission, more so when the department has the
capacity and the readiness to absorb additional funds.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department takes note of Committee’s recommendation. The Department indeed has the
capacity to absorb more funds and is accordingly approaching Planning Commission, again, to hike
plan allocation so as to enable the Department to take up more of R&D projects.
Comments
The Committee feels that the future of the country depends upon its technological
prowess and the research and development projects of the Department play an
important role in this regard. Any unjustified decrease in funds allocation would slow
the country’s progress. Hence the Committee reiterates that the Department should pursue
its demand for higher allocation with the Planning Commission more vigorously at R.E.
stage.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.3)
The Committee is pleased to know that a mechanism to monitor and review the ongoing
schemes has been put in place by the Department. The Committee hopes that the Department will
further strengthen this mechanism for effective implementation of its programmes.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department thankfully acknowledges the remarks of the Committee. The Department

2
will endeavour to put in place a transparent monitoring and review mechanism that will
enable it to implement its programmes as per the desired objectives and within the agreed time
frame.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 4.4)
The Committee takes serious note of such laxity on the part of the Department in utilization
of funds and recommends that the Department should make full use of its Project Monitoring
Committee so that funds could be utilized substantially for the purpose intended and within the time
frame set for the same. The Committee also recommends that optimum utilization of enhanced
budgetary allocation this year should be made by the Department.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department shares the concern of the Committee regarding non-utilization of the funds
allocated to it last year. The Department shall strive earnestly for optimum utilization of funds
allocated to it during the current year.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the Department will endeavour to fully utilize the funds
through its project monitoring committees.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 4.5)
The Committee is of the view that small and medium industrial units should be encouraged
to develop competitive technologies, as innovations are not necessarily dependent on huge
funding and many a times break-through comes from small and medium research units. What
is more required is the right guidance by the Department to researchers and their organizations
on commercial issues so that results of their efforts are not made use of by other market
predators.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department is in total agreement with the Committee’s view that small and medium
industrial units need to be supported for development of innovative technologies. For this reason,
the Department has initiated a programme aimed at small businesses viz. ‘Technology Development
and Demonstration Programme - Small Businesses’, during the Eleventh plan period.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the ‘Technology Development and Demonstration
Programme Small Businesses’ will be a success during the Eleventh Plan period and provide
the required impetus to the small and medium industrial units.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.3)
National Research Development Corporation (NRDC)
The budgetary allocations for 2008-09 for this programme is Rs.10 crores as

3
against Rs.8.00 crores during the year 2007-08. The Committee feels that this is a very useful
and important programme of the Department which encourages meritorious inventions
having commercial potential by giving awards, provide technical and financial assistance for
filing patents in India and abroad for protecting intellectual property and also helps in
dissemination of information on rural technologies and promotion of entrepreneurship among
rural masses. The Committee is of the opinion that this programme needs to be strengthened
and intensified. The Department should think in terms of providing more funds for this
programme.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department thankfully acknowledges the remarks of the Committee. NRDC, a Public
Sector Undertaking (PSU) of the Department, has been awarding meritorious inventions to
encourage inventors and keep their innovative spirit alive. During 2007-08, NRDC awarded
five meritorious inventions and also gave away a WIPO Gold Medal for best invention. During
2008-09, NRDC has invited applications from inventors in three categories, viz. innovation award
of the year, societal innovation award and budding innovators award. The department would
support NRDC in strengthening and intensifying this programme.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and recommends that besides National Research
Development Corporation, the other units of the Department should also initiate schemes
to provide encouragement through awards to meritorious inventions.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.4)
The Committee was informed that there is a rural show case centre of the Department
which helps in disseminating appropriate rural technology to the rural masses. The Committee is
of the view that rural showcasing of scientific achievements is of immense importance, as progress
in scientific research should be shared with the rural population and through an interactive process
local knowledge should be harnessed in the larger interest. The Committee would like the
Department to have more such rural show case centres to inculcate scientific temper among the
masses.
Action Taken by the Government
NRDC has set up a network of Rural Technology Demonstration-cum-T raining (R TDT)
Centres in association with NGOs for dissemination of appropriate rural technologies to the rural
masses and inculcating scientific temper among them. During the last year, NRDC established a
‘carpet weaving centre’ in Nagaland. NRDC has identified three sectors, viz. sericulture, dairy and
coir for technological interventions so as to assist the entrepreneurs/units in these clusters to
optimize production. As advised by the Committee, the Department would encourage NRDC to
establish a larger number of rural showcase and RTDT Centres to inculcate scientific temper
among masses.
Advanced Materials and Processes Research Institute (AMPRI), a constituent laboratory of
CSIR, has set up a ‘Rural Gallery’ in its campus at Bhopal. The gallery showcases products,
related to rural folks, developed by various CSIR laboratories. AMPRI is also planning to set-up
a Mobile Rural Gallery for efficient dissemination of S&T interventions in products and processes
for masses.

4
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Department would finalize a time
bound plan and set up RTDT centres to share the knowledge gained in the laboratories with
the rural masses.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.9)
Central Electronics Ltd. (CEL)
The Committee has been informed that the Department is striving to develop photovoltaic
cells through which solar energy could be used on a large scale. For this purpose it is working
on nano-material which has high absorption capacity per unit area and can go a long way in
making storage devices small and portable. The Committee observes that the Department should
explore the possibilities of exploiting more and more solar energy and in the long run it should aim
at replacing the conventional source of energy with solar energy. To achieve this objective it should
look for technologies like nano-technology to improve storage and conversion of solar energy into
usable energy sources. The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should make all out
efforts in this direction more so because conventional source of energy are regarded as adding to
the environmental pollution and global warming
Action Taken by the  Government
Presently, CEL is manufacturing crystalline silicon solar cells and modules in a plant of
10 MWp capacity. Besides crystalline silicon solar cells, thin film and nano technology based solar
cells with higher conversion efficiency are also available in the world market, although these have
a market share of just 15%. CEL, also plans to start R&D work for development of thin film dye-
synthesized/organic solar cells in association with research institutions.
The Department shares the concern of the Committee that the conventional sources of
energy are adding to environmental pollution and global warming and as advised by the Committee
would support CEL’s endeavours to develop and manufacture thin film/nano technology based solar
cells.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and recommends that the research and development
work in manufacturing cheap solar photovoltaic cells should be accorded top priority,
so that solar power can be provided to the poor sections of the society at an affordable
cost.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.9.1)
The Committee has also been informed that it is possible to run some water pumping sets
on solar energy. The Committee is of the view that such possibilities should be fully explored as
they have a great potential to solve irrigation problems and raise farm incomes particularly in the
rural areas where there is lack of electricity
Action Taken by the Government
CEL has so far supplied more than 2000 solar water pumping systems to various state nodal
agencies under Programmes of Ministry of New and Renewable Energy and Drinking Water
Mission Programme of Department of Rural Development. It will continue to supply more such
systems in rural areas for the purpose of irrigation.

5
Comments
The Committee feels that solar water pumping systems have a great potential,
keeping in view that India is basically an agrarian economy and, therefore, recommends
more such pumps may be supplied to the farmers across the country at subsidized
prices.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 10.3)
Solar power operated reverse osmosis desalination unit
The Committee is of the opinion that the Department in co-ordination with other
departments and agencies should also explore possibilities of converting saline water to drinking
water while working on technologies to convert saline water to irrigation water at low cost. The
Committee also feels that the network of such plan should be expanded so that more and more
people could be benefited by it.
Action Taken by the Government
CSIR, along with Department of Atomic Energy is formulating a project proposal at a cost
of Rs.742.00 crore (Rs.140.00 crore CSIR’s component and 602.00 crore DAE component). This
is to provide safe drinking water to a significant number of habitations and house holds in rural
areas through installation of desalination and water purification systems.
A lot of work has already been done in development of desalination plants using
various power sources including solar and even draught animals. The Department is conscious
of the enormity of the requirement to supply safe drinking water to maximum possible number
of persons.
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that sustained efforts are required for the
development and setting up of the desalinization plants across the country. The Committee
further urges that a time frame be worked out for implementation of the scheme.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 11.6)
Council of Scientific and Industrial Research
The Committee is aware that CSIR is involved in developing cost effective generic drugs
and standardization of Herbal and Ayurvedic drugs. It is also engaged in development of
commercially viable technologies for life saving drugs such as anti virals, anti cancer, anti bacterial,
cardio-vascular, etc. It is also designing and developing instrumentation for diagnostics
therapeutics and life supporting system among others. But the fact remains that diseases like
malaria, polio, diabetes, tuberculosis, cancer, etc. are still rampant in the country and are taking
a heavy toll. Treatment of some of these diseases are still beyond the reach of common man. The
benefit of scientific research is yet to percolate down to the society. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the department should seriously make an introspection and try to address these
issues on priority basis in a time bound manner.
Action Taken by the Government
The recommendation of the Committee is appreciated. V arious institutes of CSIR, like
CDRI, IMTECH, IIM, IGIB, IICT etc. are involved in the process of drug discovery and

6
development. So far, CSIR has been successful in developing 12 out of 17 drugs which have been
developed post independence within the country.
CSIR has initiated R&D programme on ‘New Development for parasitic diseases’,
development and commercialization of bioactive and traditional preparations etc. Under these
programmes expertise available within various laboratories of CSIR are networked and joint efforts
are being undertaken to discover and develop drugs for diseases like cancer, tuberculosis, malaria,
diabetes etc. A number of promising leads have been identified and are at various stages of drug
discovery and development process through these programmes.
CSIR has also formulated a programme for ‘Open Source Drug Discovery’, through which
development of drugs for tuberculosis is being taken up initially. The approach of this programme
is web-based and would involve scientists not only from CSIR but also experts from universities,
students, industry etc. within and outside India. It is presumed that this approach would be helpful
in reducing the cost of drug discovery and development process and ultimately help in making the
drugs available at a lower cost.
Comments
The Committee expresses its concern over the fact that the Department is yet to find
a complete solution to common man’s diseases viz. Malaria, Polio, Tuberculosis, etc., though
a lot of funds and effort has been invested for the purpose. The Committee therefore,
recommends that research and development efforts should be intensified to find medication
for these diseases at the earliest.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 11.7)
The Committee is also aware that the department is engaged in developing technology and
know-how for environment friendly conversion and utilization of bio mass to fuels. The Committee
has been informed that during 2008-09 the Department has targeted to make preliminary
experiments on bio fuels and has developed heterogeneous catalyst for bio diesel production,
process for production of bio ethanol from cellulose and starch. The Committee feels that fossil
fuel, besides being costly, is a major source of pollution in the environment. There is an urgent
need to seek an economically viable and environment friendly alternative. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the tempo of research and development should be accelerated so that the country
could reap the benefits at the earliest. The Committee, however, desires that while developing bio
fuels, care should be taken not to use edible cereals as it may affect the poor adversely.
Action Taken by the Government
CSIR has been executing projects, during Eleventh Plan, for development of environment-
friendly technology for conversion and utilization of bio-mass to fuels. None of the activity utilizes
edible cereals as a source of bio-fuel.
Comments
In view of the shortage of biofuel in the country and huge outgo of financial
resources due to their import, the Committee reiterates that Research and Development on
commercially viable biofuel should be given top priority.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 11.8)
The phenomena of Global warming and climate change are emerging as one of the greatest

7
and most formidable challenges of the 21st century which are stated to have the potential of
pushing the world to the brink of catastrophe. Adaptation and mitigation are the two possible ways
and means identified to counter their effects. But these are not so easy solutions as they appear
to be. Science has to play a pivotal role in this direction. Department of Scientific and Industrial
Research with its vast network of multi disciplinary laboratories and huge resource of competent
and expert man power has to take a lead role in galvanizing and dovetailing its activities and
resources to find viable solutions to provide relief to the nation and the world from this impending
calamity. It can address scientific issues relevant to atmosphere, geosphere, hydrosphere as well
as biosphere. It can assume the responsibility of standardizing all measurement of global change
with international traceability. The Committee is happy to note that CSIR is contemplating to initiate
a mega R&D programme on climate change covering various dimensions such as assessment,
adaptation, mitigation, alternate energy sources, etc. Since this has to be addressed on an urgent
and immediate basis, the Committee is of the opinion that the Department should start working on
this at the earliest without losing any time, in a time bound manner, efficacy of which may be
reviewed annually. Progress made in this regard may be brought before the public in the form of
a white paper or in any form the Department deems appropriate so that people could not only feel
assured but also come to know how real and formidable is the threat posed by global warming
and climate change. The Committee also feels that separate budgetary provisions for this purpose
could be made.
Action Taken by the Government
The recommendation of the Committee is well taken. CSIR has taken deep cognizance of
the PM’s National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) and is in the process of formulating
and restructuring programmes in tune with the expectations of NAPCC. Few recent initiatives
launched are “Sequestration of atmospheric CO
2 by Iron-fertilization”, “Assessment and
management of environmental pollutants”, “Eco-restoration and cleanup of contaminated sites”,
“Mapping of marine biodiversity along the Indian Coast” etc. with requisite budgetary support. The
outcome of these projects shall be appropriately disseminated. In order to meet above objective, all
the proposals required for patenting are thoroughly scrutinized and necessary suggestions are made
to laboratories to provide/generate required data for the above said purposes. Similarly, special
emphasis has also been laid on the projects arising out of public-private partnership and protection
of IP generated from such coordinated efforts.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the initiation of various projects by the department and
hopes that the outcome of these projects would result in formulation of plans on an urgent
basis to mitigate the problem of climate change.

National Laboratories
The Committee feels that the R&D activities can be undertaken only in laboratories and
unless we have a network of rich and sophisticated labs we can not make advancement in the field
of science. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the budgetary demands of the Department
for such crucial programme needs to be fully met and they should be provided enhanced funds

only 76 out of 94 priority projects under the National Laboratories Scheme, during 2007-08. The
Committee would have been happy if all the priority projects could have been initiated during the
period they were intended.

8

CSIR is taking all efforts to initiate all of its priority-l projects. In addition to 76
priority-l projects as reported earlier, eight more project have been initiated. Rest of the projects
are in the final stage of formulation. More funds needed to meet the project requirement.
Comments
The Committee hopes that all the Priority projects are initiated as per schedule and
the issue of additional funds requirement should be emphatically taken up with the
Planning Commission at the R.E. stage as recommended by the Committee earlier.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 12.6)
The Committee has been informed that some of the CSIR laboratories like those in Kolkata
and Hyderabad are facing difficulty in expansion as they are located in the heart of the city with
very little space. The Committee is of the opinion that these premier organizations need new
buildings for better functioning. The Committee suggests that the Department may take up the
matter with the concerned State Governments to acquire land for the said purpose.
Action Taken by the Government
Kolkata:  Department, through its constituent laboratory Central Glass and Ceramic
Research Institute, Kolkata has approached Government of West Bengal for allotment of land in
Baruipur, Kolkata. Response from Government of West Bengal is awaited.
Hyderabad: On request of the Department (through Centre for Cellular and Molecular
Biology, Hyderabad), Government of Hyderabad has given ‘in-principle’ approval for allocation of
150 acres of land in the Phase-III, Biotech Park, Karkapatla, free of cost  for setting up a world
class dedicated Institute of Translational Research in Hyderabad. However due to certain reasons
such as lack of medical hospitals, medical schools, land is far away from CCMB etc. Department
approached Government of A.P. to consider allocating alternate land. On the suggestion of
Government of A.P., Department approached Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, for allocating
150 acres of land in IDPL campus at Balanagar. IDPL has already given around 142 acres of land
to NIPER in that campus. Department is yet to receive response from the Ministry of Chemicals
and Fertilizers.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the issue of allotment of land is persued by the
Department with various agencies involved to ensure expansion of its institutes at the
earliest.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 13.3 AND 13.4)
Research Schemes, Scholarship and Fellowships
The Committee welcomes the enhancement in allocations for Research Schemes,
Scholarships and Fellowships. It also welcomes the fact that in the Eleventh Plan, emphasis is
being laid on promotion of R&D in Basic Sciences, Engineering Sciences and Inter-disciplinary
areas. It is of the opinion that paradigmatic breakthrough in science can be possible only if there
is progress in basic sciences which ultimately get translated to state-of-art technologies.

9
Human Resource Development is buzzword today in every sector of economy as it is of
paramount importance in the Nation building process and creating a knowledge society. But the
shortage of qualified, competent and dedicated manpower in the field of science is being felt not
only in India but the world over. This shortfall is such which cannot be bridged overnight. To
catch young students, nurture, groom and develop them into budding scientists, it is imperative that
a concerted and coordinated strategy is made on a long-term basis. Since CSIR has vast array of
labs and is one of the biggest public sector employer of scientists, it has to actively play a major
role in this effort. Merely by awarding some scholarships or awards, this daunting task is not going
to be accomplished. The committee, therefore, recommends that DSIR must come out with some
concrete proposals to trap, harness and retain human resource in the field of science by earmarking
sufficient fund for the purpose.
Action Taken by the Government
During Eleventh Plan, a long-term, comprehensive ‘Human Resource Development Scheme’
has been proposed to identify and nurture budding young scientific talent in the area of basic
‘science, engineering and inter-disciplinary science and to encourage them to take up science as
a career by way of granting necessary incentives in the form of fellowships. This process would
naturally entail reversing the present flow of talent away from science.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the ‘Human Resource Development Scheme’ would not
remain a scheme merely on paper, but would be implemented in letter and spirit to bridge
the acute shortfall in the requirement of scientific manpower.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 14.3)
Intellectual Property and Technology Management
The Committee feels that there is still a lot to be done to improve awareness about patents
in the country, especially among the scientist and other innovators so that the valuable intellectual
property earned by them is commercially protected. It is of the opinion that the Department should
make more efforts to increase the reach of such awareness programmes.
Action Taken by the Government
The suggestion of the Committee is well taken and the Department is regularly disseminating
through training programmes/Workshops the importance and need of IPR in our R&D programmes.
Some of the initiatives taken are:
• Training of interns and women scientists
During 2004-2008, 12 research interns/women scientists have been trained by CSIR in the
area of IP Management for a period of 1 and 2 years, under the scheme ‘CSIR Diamond
Jubilee Research Intern Awards’, and TIF AC-DST Scheme for the ‘women Scientist
Scholarship’. Many of these Interns/Women scientists now have been recruited by attorney
firms and Government departments.
• CSIR Diamond Jubilee Invention Award for School Children (CDJIA)
In order to enhance creativity and also to improve awareness of IP amongst school
children, CSIR announced 7th CSIR Diamond Jubilee Invention Award for School Children-

10
2008 on 26 April, WORLD IN TELLECTUAL PROPERTY DA Y . During the last six years,
2221 proposals have been received for this competition from school children from various
parts of the country and 41 inventions were selected for various prizes by a High Level A
ward Committee.
• Training programmes on IP
Human Resource Development Centre (HRDC), CSIR, Ghaziabad, organized a number of
training programmes on IP during the year 2007. The programmes were attended not only
by scientists from various CSIR labs/other R&D institutes/organizations but also from
corporate sector,
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that more number of training programmes/
workshops should be conducted at various universities/colleges/schools, to educate the
scientist about patents.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 15.4)
New Millennium India Technology Leadership Initiative
The Committee is pleased to learn that ‘industries which partnered NMITLI projects have
been highly benefited not only in terms of the objectives and achievements in the projects wherein
they participated but also in terms of other associated gains such as high quality technical inputs’.
It is also heartening to know that during the Tenth Plan the New Millennium Indian Technology
Leadership Initiative (NMITLI) has created a brand image and is viewed today as a benchmark of
Public-Private Partnership (PPP) schemes which is being emulated by various other Government
Departments. The Committee is of the view that the public-private partnership is the kind of
research model that can successfully work in today’s world where commercial viability of research
and its self-sustaining nature is as important as the research itself.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department is thankful for appreciation.
Comments
The Committee hopes that NMITLI projects would be taken up with the
various governments in the right earnest, so that all the States may benefit by this
programme.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 17.2)
The Committee, in view of the increased responsibilities and activities of the Department,
