CHAPTER I 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Earth System Science deals with all the five components of the Earth System, 
viz., Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, Cryosphere, Geosphere, and Biosphere, and their 
complex interactions. The Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) holistically  addresses 
all the aspects relating to the Earth System Science for providing weather, climate, 
ocean, coastal state, hydrological, and seismological services. The services include 
forecasts and warnings for various natural disasters like tropical cyclones , storm 
surges, floods, heat waves, thunderstorms, and lightning; alerts for Tsunamis and 
monitoring of earthquakes, etc. In addition, the ministry also has the mandate to 
develop technology for ocean surveys and exploration of living and non -living 
resources from the ocean and exploration of all three poles (Arctic, Antarctic, and 
Himalayas). The services provided by the ministry are being effectively used by 
different agencies and state governments to save human lives and minimize damages 
due to natural disasters. 
Objectives 
1.2     Some of the basic objectives of the Ministry are listed below: 
i. To provide Weather and Climate Services for various sectors like disaster 
management, agriculture, aviation, water resources, power, tourism, health, air 
quality, public safety etc. 
ii. To undertake the Science of Climate Change - Regional Climate Change 
Scenarios. 
iii. To provide Early warnings for Hazards such as Cyclones, Heavy Rainfall, 
Heatwave, etc. 
iv. To provide Ocean Services, which include Tsunami warnings, Potential Fishing 
zone advisories, Ocean State Forecasts, Oil Spill Advisories, Coastal Research, 
and Coastal Protection. 
v. Harnessing of non-living ocean resources - Water, Energy and Minerals 
vi. Ocean living resources mapping, Marine biodiversity, Marine Biotechnology 
vii. Survey and Exploration of Exclusive Economic Zone and Continental Shelf 
viii. Earthquake Detection and information, Deep Scientific drilling, Crustal 
Dynamics, Geoscience research. 
ix. Scientific exploration of the three poles (Arctic, Antarctic and Himalayas) and 
Cryosphere research. 
x. Develop deep sea technologies for sustainable use of ocean resources, and 
improve our understanding of deep sea. 

2 
 
xi. R&D and development of technology leading to weather intervention 
xii. To support various Extra -Mural R&D activities, Outreach and  Awareness 
activities and Capacity Building in the field of Earth Sciences. 
 
Structure of the Ministry 
1.3    The policies and program mes of the Ministry are being primarily pursued 
through its 10 Institutes viz., autonomous bodies, attached and subordinate offices. 
The institutions dealing with atmospheric sciences are the India Meteorological 
Department (IMD), National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting 
(NCMRWF), Noida and Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune.  The 
institutions related to ocean science and technology are National Centre for Polar and 
Ocean Research (NCPOR), Goa, National Institute of Ocean Techno logy (NIOT), 
Chennai, Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), 
Hyderabad, Centre for Marine Living Resources and Ecology (CMLRE), Kochi and 
National Centre for Coastal Research (NCCR), Chennai. The National Centre for 
Earth Science S tudies (NCESS), Thiruvananthapuram and National Centre for 
Seismology (NCS), New Delhi conduct research on solid earth and earthquake 
monitoring. Each Centre of the Ministry has a well -defined mandate to conduct 
research and development in a specific area of Earth Sciences. 
 
Creation of Earth System Science Council (ESSC) 
1.4 MoES has constituted the Earth System Science Council (ESSC) as part of a 
comprehensive reform process aimed at rationalisation, improved governance, and 
enhanced integration among it s autonomous institutions. The decision followed a 


3 
 
report submitted by the Department of Expenditure (DoE) under the Ministry of 
Finance (MoF), which reviewed the structure and functioning of autonomous bodies 
under MoES. The DoE recommended retaining the National Centre for Earth Science 
Studies (NCESS), the National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR), and 
the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) without structural change and 
merging the Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Serv ices (INCOIS) with 
the National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT). 
 
1.5  Considering these recommendations and the need for greater administrative 
efficiency, MoES proposed the merger of the individual Societies of all five 
autonomous bodies into a sing le overarching umbrella organisation, ESSC, as an 
autonomous society under MoES. Accordingly, ESSC replaced the separate Societies 
of the five institutions, and their respective Governing Bodies and Finance 
Committees were merged into a unified ESSC -Governing Board and ESSC -Finance 
Committee. A centralised administrative unit, the ESSC Secretariat, was established 
to coordinate and facilitate the execution of all Earth science -related activities. 
However, each of the five institutions continues to function under the leadership of its 
director and carries forward its specialised mandate and scientific programmes 
without any dilution of its functional or academic autonomy. 
 
1.6  The Ministry stated that t he merger seeks to improve coordination, reduce 
administrative duplication, and promote a more integrated approach to Earth system 
science, recognising the strong coupling among the atmosphere, oceans, cryosphere 
and geosphere. The unified governance struc ture enables uniform decision -making, 
promotes multidisciplinary research and development, and strengthens strategic 
planning across institutions. The rationalisation is also expected to result in efficient 
utilisation of manpower and financial resources, facilitate inter-institutional transfer 
of personnel where required, and reduce expenditure through consolidation of 
meetings and administrative processes. Further, ESSC provides high -level scientific 
guidance and supports coordinated implementation of nat ional missions such as the 
Deep Ocean Mission and Mission Mausam. Through improved institutional synergy 
and streamlined governance, the formation of ESSC enhances the delivery of 
weather, climate, ocean and disaster early warning services for societal ben efit while 
preserving the core mandates and autonomy of the constituent institutes.

2.7 The Committee notes that the Ministry of Earth Sciences  (MoES) had 
projected an outlay of ₹ 4371.54 crore to the Ministry of Finance under Demand 
No. 24 for the financial year 2026 –27. However, the Ministry of Finance 
approved an outlay of ₹ 3789.23 crore, which is about 86.67 % of the projected 
amount. The Committee is of the view that the pattern and extent of fund 
utilization during the current financial year significantly influence the allocation 
made in the subsequent year. A careful examination of the  Ministry‟s 
expenditure pattern over the years indicates that it has not been able to fully 
utilize the outlays sanctioned to it. For instance, the Ministry was allocated an 
outlay of ₹ 3658.08 crore at the BE stage in 2025 –26, which was subsequently 
revised downward to ₹ 3388.27 crore at the RE stage. Further, the Ministry has 
been able to incur an expenditure of only ₹ 2826.83 crore up to 31 st January, 
2026. This relatively slow pace of expenditure appears to have contributed to the 
reduction in allocation made to the Ministry in BE 2026 –27 vis -à-vis the 
projected outlay. The Committee also observes that the gap between the outlay 
projected by MoES to the Mi nistry of Finance and the allocation approved has 
widened over the years. The outlay allocated to the Ministry in BE 2023 –24 
constituted 99.43% of the projected outlay . In BE 2024–25, the allocation was 
107.90% of the projected outlay, while in BE 2025–26 it accounted for 97.69% of 
the projected outlay. However, the allocation for the forthcoming financial year 
represents only 86.67% of the projected outlay, indicating a comparatively lower 
level of budgetary provision against the projected requirement. In v iew of the 
above, the Committee urges the Ministry of Earth Sciences to take necessary 
measures to improve the rate of budget utilization and strive to achieve full 
utilization of the allocated funds in the coming financial year, so as to avoid any 
reduction in future budgetary allocations.

PRITHVI VIGYAN (PRITHVI) 
 
3.5 The Union Cabinet approved the PRITHVI scheme on 5 January , 2024 for 
implementation during 2021 –26. The major objectives of the overarching PRITHVI 
Scheme are as follows: 
 
(a) Augmentation and sustenance of long -term observations of the atmosphere, 
ocean, geosphere, cryosphere and solid earth to record the vital signs of the 
Earth System and change. 
(b) Development of modelling systems for understanding and predicting weather, 
ocean and climate hazards, and the science of climate change. 
(c) Exploration of polar and high seas regions of the Earth towards the discovery 
of new phenomena and resources. 
(d) Development of technology for exploration and sustainable harnessing of 
oceanic resources for societal applications. 
(e) Translation of knowledge and insights from Earth systems science into 
services for societal, environmental and economic benefit.

3.11 The Committee enquired whether NCPOR proposed to establish Maitri-II, a

the station to become operational.The Ministry submitted that NCPOR proposed the 
establishment of Maitri -II in Antarctica as a replacement for the existing Maitri 
Research Station. The present Maitri station, commissioned in 1989, has exceeded its 
designed operational life and is experiencing structural deterioration as w ell as 
limitations in waste -management and environmental compliance systems. The 
Ministry stated that, in view of safety considerations, operational sustainability, and 
evolving environmental norms under the Antarctic Treaty System, the establishment 
of a modern replacement station has become necessary to ensure the continuity of 
India‘s year-round scientific presence in Antarctica. 
 
3.12 The Ministry further submitted that Maitri -II is envisaged as a state -of-the-art, 
year-round, multidisciplinary research  facility equipped with modern laboratories, 
enhanced atmospheric, geological and geophysical observation systems, and 
advanced waste -management and treatment facilities in accordance with 
environmental regulations under the Antarctic Treaty. The proposed station will also 
have e1xpanded accommodation capacity to support a larger number of scientists and 
technical personnel, thereby strengthening India‘s research capabilities and sustained 
presence in Antarctica.

drinking water supply, it also notes that the desalination plants currently 
operate using electricity generated through diesel generator sets, which power 
the island systems. Considering the environmental sensitivity of island 
ecosystems and the broader national  commitment towards clean and renewable 
energy, the Committee is of the view that reliance on diesel -based power sources 

30 
 
should be progressively reduced. The Committee therefore recommends the 
Ministry to explore the feasibility of making t hese desalinatio n plants eco-
friendly by using sustainable power sources such as solar energy, Ocean 
Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC), or other suitable renewable energy 
technologies.  
3.19 The Committee also examined the proposal for establishing Maitri-II, a 
new research station in Antarctica to replace the existing Maitri station, which 
was commissioned in 1989 and has now exceeded its designed operational life. 
The Committee notes the Ministry‟s submission that the present station is facing 
structural deterioration and  limitations in waste -management and 
environmental compliance systems, making the establishment of a modern 
replacement station necessary to ensure safety, operational sustainability, and 
India‟s continued year -round scientific presence in Antarctica in ac cordance 
with evolving environmental norms under the Antarctic Treaty System.  
However, the Committee notes that the architectural and design consultant for 
the Maitri-II project has been selected through a global design competition and 
that the winning con sortium comprises Ramboll Deutschland GmbH and 
bofArchitekten, Germany. In this regard, the Committee recalls that India‟s 
first Antarctic research station, Dakshin Gangotri, was constructed with the 
involvement of the Indian Army, demonstrating indigenous  capability in 
undertaking complex operations in extreme polar conditions. The Committee is 
therefore of the view that greater consideration could have been given to Indian 
firms with relevant engineering and infrastructure expertise in cold -weather 
construction technologies. Such an approach could have contributed to cost 
savings while also providing impetus to the objectives of Make in India  and 
promoting greater self-reliance in specialized polar infrastructure development. 
The Committee recommends that, in future projects of this nature, the Ministry 
explore ways to ensure greater participation of Indian companies, either 

31 
 
independently or through collaborations, so that domestic capabilities in polar 
infrastructure and extreme-environment engineering are further strengthened. 
DEEP OCEAN MISSION 
 
3.20 As a Maritime nation with a coastline of about 11,098 km and around 30% of 
the country's population living in coastal areas, oceans play an important role in the 
Indian economy. The Government of India‘s Vision of New India by 2030, 
enunciated in February 2019, highlighted the Blue Economy as one of the ten core 
dimensions of growth. With a view to exploring the deep ocean for resources, 
developing deep -sea technologies for sustai nable use of ocean resources, and 
improving our understanding of the deep sea, the Government of India launched the 
―Deep Ocean Mission‖ at an estimated cost of Rs. 4077.0 crores for a period of 5 
years. This mission mode project will support various prior ity areas illustrated in the 
Blue Economy policy document of the Government of India. Ministry of Earth 
Sciences (MoES) is the nodal implementing Ministry for this multi-ministerial, multi-
institutional mission.   
3.21 The Deep Ocean Mission encompasses six verticals, which are: 
 
a. Development of Technologies for Deep-sea mining, Human Submersible, and 
underwater robotics. 
b. Development of Ocean Climate Change Advisory Services. 
c. Technological Innovations for exploration and conservation of deep -sea 
biodiversity. 
d. Deep-ocean survey and exploration. 
e. Energy and fresh water from the ocean. 
f. Establishment of Advanced Marine Station for Ocean Biology.  
 
3.22 The significant tangible scientific outcomes achieved under the Deep Ocean 
Mission (DOM) to date are as follows:  
 The design and system engineering of India‘s flagship human submersible 
MATSYA-6000, capable of reaching a depth of 6000 meters with three 
aquanauts, has been completed.  Its subsystems have been realized, and the wet 
tests were successfully conducted at L&T Harbour in Katupalli, near Chennai, in 
January-February 2025. Scientists at the National Institute of Ocean Technology 
(NIOT), Chennai, entrusted with the development of MATSYA -6000, have 
gained pilot experience with the French submersible NAUTILE in August 2025.  

32 
 
 The prediction of 100 -year return extreme sea level along the coas ts of the 
Indian subcontinent has been completed and associated coastal vulnerability 
maps have been prepared. To strengthen ocean observation, the Indian National 
Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), Hyderabad, has successfully 
completed 11 gli der missions along predetermined transects in the Arabian Sea 
(67°E) and the Bay of Bengal (89°E), and deployed 60 directional Wave Spectra 
Barometric Drifters and 92 physical and biogeochemical Argo floats in the 
Indian Ocean. 
 Nearly 1,845 deep -sea microb es have been isolated from water and sediment 
samples of the Indian EEZ, and rare deep -sea microbes previously not reported 
from the Indian Ocean have been discovered. A total of 25 seamounts 
(biodiversity hotspots) are surveyed by the Centre for Marine Li ving Resources 
and Ecology (CMLRE), Kochi, in the Lakshadweep and Andaman and Nicobar 
regions, documenting 195 deep-sea species (including 39 potential new taxa). 
 Two active and two inactive hydrothermal vents have been identified on the 
Indian Ocean seafloor through AUV (Autonomous Underwater Vehicle) surveys 
by the National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR), Goa, indicating 
potential locations of sulphide minerals. The contract for the construction of an 
all-weather multi-purpose research vessel was awarded to GRSE, Kolkata, under 
the Ministry of Defence in 2024.  
 A total of 142 collaborative research projects have been sanctioned under the 
Mission, resulting in capacity building across nearly 70 national government and 
private institutions in the fields of ocean sciences and engineering.  
 
3.23 The major milestone initiatives proposed for the scheme in the future are as 
follows:  
 Conducting 500 -meter shallow water demonstration trials and 6000 -meter

 Development of future climate projections for sea -level rise, cyclones, and

 Survey and mapping of ten additional seamounts for biodiversity assessment, 
followed by isolation, characterisat ion, identification, and inventorisation of

deployment for studies on deep -sea microbes. Establishment of a referral facility 
for deep-ocean biodiversity specimens‘ studies.  
 Continuation of the survey of an additional five hydrothermal sites for locating 
potential multi-metal deposits and completion and acquisition of the Ministry‘s 
all-weather multi-purpose research vessel.

 Studies on the generation of hybrid renewable energy (floa ting solar+wind) and

 Construction of Advanced Marine Station for Ocean Biology in Nemelli, 
Chennai, and continuation of expansion of capacity  building programs,

cooperation in marine sciences.

MISSION MAUSAM 
3.31    Mission Mausam (MM) was approved by the Union Cabinet in September 
2024. The scheme adopts a multi -faceted, transformative approach to make Bharat a 
―Weather-ready and Climate -smart‖ nation by developing cutting -edge weather 
surveillance technologies; implementing high -resolution atmospheric observations; 
deploying next -generation radars, wind profilers, satellites, and advanced HPC 
systems; enhancing understanding of weather and climate processes; developing 
advanced Earth system models and AI/ML -based methods; creating weather 
management technologies and protocols; establishing state-of-the-art decision support 
and last -mile dissemination systems; and strengthening capacity building and 
research collaborations. 
3.32  To achieve this, nine inter -related verticals have been established under the 
program, each with specific objectives and deliverables. 
 
(i) OBSERVEAll: Comprehensive weather monitoring and data collection for 
nowcasting, data assimilation, model development and decision support. All MoES 
institutions.  
(ii) DEVELOP: Development of next -generation Earth System Models (ESM) f or 
improved forecasts. 
(iii) IMPACT: Focus on high-impact weather forecasting and early warning systems and 
research to operations. 
(iv) FRONTIER: Establishment of advanced technologies for weather observation and 
measurement. 
(v) ATCOMP: Air quality monitoring and decis ion support systems for pollution 
management. 
(vi) DECIDE: Development of decision support systems for various service sectors. 
(vii) WeatherMOD: Protocols for weather modification strategies, including rain 
enhancement, hail/fog suppression 
(viii) LEAD (Leading Effort for Advanced Decisions): Capacity building and innovation 
in weather information dissemination. 
(ix) NEAT (National Enterprise for Atmospheric Technology): Establishment of a unified 
observational approach through public-private partnerships. 
 
3.33 Tangible scientific outcomes achieved under the scheme so far are as follows: 
 
 Nationwide strengthening of observational infrastructure with commissioning of C -
Band DWRs at Raipur and Mangaluru and X -Band dual -polarised DWR at 
Mahabaleshwar, improving real-time monitoring and nowcasting. 
 Indigenous 3D -printed AWS units developed by IITM to cost -effectively densify 
observation networks and enable hyper-local forecasting. 
 Bharat Forecast System (6 km) operationalised to deliver precise localized forecasts 
of extreme weather, supporting agriculture, disaster management, and public safety. 
 Mithuna Forecast System (12 km) operationalised as a coupled land –ocean–
atmosphere–sea-ice model, reducing biases and improving medium-range forecasts. 
 AI-based forecast tools integrat ed with numerical models for automated weather 
interpretation, bias correction, downscaling, and nowcasting. 

38 
 
 IITM-ESMv3 global Earth System Model developed, contributing to CMIP and IPCC 
AR7, with high-resolution climate projections for impact assessment. 
 Severe weather forecast accuracy improved by ~40%, with and better cyclone track 
and intensity guidance. 
 Block-Wise Rainfall Monitoring Scheme (BRMS) operationalised to provide high -
resolution real-time rainfall data at block level. 
 ―Mausamgram‖ platform launched for village and PIN-code level hyperlocal forecasts 
with hourly updates. 
 Indigenous Multi -Hazard Early Warning DSS developed by IMD and awarded the 
National Award for e-Governance 2025. 
 Heat Action Plans developed with NDMA and states to mitigate i mpacts of extreme 
heat events. 
 Aviation weather safety enhanced through expanded RVR and DCWIS deployment at 
airports. 
 Urban weather monitoring networks established, including the Delhi Urban Mesonet, 
with similar testbeds in Chennai and Visakhapatnam. 
 Cloud seeding protocols formulated and scientific understanding of aerosol -cloud-
precipitation processes strengthened through CAIPEEX. 
 Strengthened national severe weather monitoring through deployment of advanced 
Doppler Weather Radar systems under Mission Mausam. 
 Establishment of a nationwide Lightning Location Network under Mission Mausam, 
significantly strengthening lightning detection, early warning, and public alert 
services across India through real -time monitoring, research integration, and 
dissemination support to IMD and State Governments.

4.16 The Committee enquired whether IITM has undertaken any research under 
Mission Mausam to study the meteorological factors contributing to severe air 
pollution in Delhi during the winter months, and the details and key findings of such 
studies. In response, the Ministry stated that IITM has undertaken several initiatives 
under Mission Mausam to investigate Delhi‘s air pollution. The findings have been 
published in several peer -reviewed research papers. The studies indicate that very 
stable meteorological conditions during winter weaken atmo spheric ventilation, 
leading to the trapping of pollutants near the surface and a rapid escalation of air 
pollution to severe levels. Aerosol –radiation feedback further aggravates these 
conditions and intensifies pollution. Severe to very severe air pollut ion episodes are 
generally associated with such stable meteorological regimes. 
4.17 The Ministry further informed that another study examined the shift in the 
timing of stubble burning, which has shortened the interval between crop harvesting 
and sowing. T his shift increasingly overlaps with periods of stable synoptic -scale 

51 
 
atmospheric conditi ons. When combined with aerosol -radiative feedback, these 
factors suppress atmospheric ventilation and reduce boundary layer height, resulting 
in greater accumulation of PM2.5. Consequently, the frequency of severe air pollution 
episodes has nearly tripled, with around 40 per cent of the  increase attributed to 
aerosol-radiative feedback mechanisms.

4.21 The Committee inquired whether NCMRWF is undertaking any research or 
model development to improve the prediction of cloudburst events, particularly in 
vulnerable regions such as the Himalayas. The Committee also sought details 
regarding the dynamic models being used, the improvements achieved in forecast 
skill, and their application in early warning and disaster management.The Ministry 
submitted that NCMRWF is actively engaged in research related to cloudburst 
prediction, especially thro ugh the use of high -resolution numerical modelling for 
Himalayan weather events. In this context, NCMRWF has developed a High-
Resolution Rapid Refresh (HRRR)  forecasting system designed for short -range 
prediction of short-lived and rapidly evolving high-impact weather systems. 
4.22 The Ministry informed that the system is based on the Advanced Research 
Weather Research and Forecasting (ARW) dynamic core , coupled with the Grid-
point Statistical Interpolation (GSI)  data assimilation framework. It operates at 
convection-permitting resolutions of 5 km and 1km , with hourly data assimilation of 
conventional observations, satellite radiances, and Doppler Weather Radar data using 
a three-dimensional variational (3DVAR)  assimilation approach and hourly update 
cycles to improve model initial conditions. The 5 km domain covers the entire Indian 
region, while the 1 km domain  focuses on the north -western Himalayan region to 
better capture complex terrain and localized convec tive processes. The Ministry 
further stated that these models are currently under testing, and the generated data is 
regularly shared with the IMD for operational use by forecasters. 
4.23 It was further informed that recent upgrades include the introductio n of a 2 km 
HRRR domain covering the whole of India and neighbouring countries. Case studies 

53 
 
of intense rainfall events over the Himalayas indicate that the high -resolution HRRR 
system provides significant improvements in the spatial positioning and intens ity of 
heavy rainfall compared to the existing 5 km domain, thereby demonstrating better 
forecasting skill than coarser -resolution models. Objective verification using 
statistical scores and spatial verification techniques has also shown improved 
representation of rainfall patterns, highlighting the importance of higher resolution 
and frequent data assimilation. 
4.24 The Ministry added that the HRRR modelling system generates 18-hour 
forecasts every hour using updated initial conditions, making it highly suitable for 
nowcasting applications. Ongoing research efforts are focused on assimilating radar 
observations over the Himalayan region into the HRRR system, which is expected to 
further enhance the prediction capability for cloudburst events in the region. 
4.25   The Committee notes the important role played by the National Centre for 
Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF), Noida , in developing and 
operating advanced numerical weather prediction models for s hort-range, 
medium-range, extended-range and seasonal forecasts, which are widely used by 
various user agencies including the India Meteorological Department. The 
Committee appreciates the development of the High-Resolution Rapid Refresh 
(HRRR) forecasting  system, which is designed for short -range prediction of 
rapidly evolving weather systems, particularly in complex terrains such as the 
Himalayan region. Considering the increasing occurrence of extreme rainfall

ANALYSIS OF THE BUDGETARY TRENDS OF THE MINISTRY OF EARTH SCIENCES  
 
         The Committee notes that the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES ) had projected an 
outlay of ₹4371.54 crore to the Ministry of Finance under Demand No. 24 for the financial 
year 2026–27. However, the Ministry of Finance approved an outlay of ₹3789.23 crore, which 
is about 86.67 % of the projected amount. The Committee is of the view that the pattern and 
extent of fund utilization during the current financial year significantly influence the 
allocation made in the subsequent year. A careful examination of the Ministry‟s expenditure 
pattern over the years indicates that i t has not been able to fully utilize the outlays sanctioned 
to it. For instance, the Ministry was allocated an outlay of ₹3658.08 crore at the BE stage in 
2025–26, which was subsequently revised downward to ₹3388.27 crore at the RE stage. 
Further, the Ministry has been able to incur an expenditure of only ₹2826.83 crore up to 31 st 
January, 2026. This relatively slow pace of expenditure appears to have contributed to the 
reduction in allocation made to the Ministry in BE 2026 –27 vis-à-vis the projected outlay. The 
Committee also observes that the gap between the outlay projected by MoES to the Ministry 
of Finance and the allocation approved has widened over the years. The outlay allocated to 
the Ministry in BE 2023–24 constituted 99.43% of the projected outla y. In BE 2024–25, the 
allocation was 107.90% of the projected outlay , while in BE 2025–26 it accounted for 97.69% 
of the projected outlay . However, the allocation for the forthcoming financial year represents 
only 86.67% of the projected outlay , indicating  a comparatively lower level of budgetary 
provision against the projected requirement.  In view of the above, the Committee urges the 
Ministry of Earth Sciences to take necessary measures to improve the rate of budget 
utilization and strive to achieve full utilization of the allocated funds in the coming financial 
year, so as to avoid any reduction in future budgetary allocations.                                                                                                                                 
(Para 2.7) 
 
ASSESSMENT OF THE PATTERN OF BUDGET UTILIZATION BY THE MINISTRY OF 
EARTH SCIENCES 
The Committee notes with concern  the persistent underutilization of budgetary 
allocations by the Ministry of Earth Sciences over the past few years. An analysis o f the 
pattern of expenditure indicates that the utilization of funds has ranged between 83% and 
93% in recent years , and the Ministry has not been able to utilize the full budgetary 
allocation in any of the recent years. The Committee is of the view that such a pattern reflects 
a slow pace of expenditure and limited capacity to absorb the funds allocated , particularly 
under the Capital Head.  The Committee further observes that the reduction in allocation at 
the Revised Estimates stage , as seen in 2025 –26, h as been attributed by the Ministry to 
procurement delays, implementation challenges, site finalization issues, and other technical or 
operational constraints . While acknowledging that certain scientific and technological 
projects may involve complex proced ures, the Committee is of the firm opinion that it is the 
responsibility of the Ministry to anticipate such challenges and establish effective mechanisms 
to overcome them in a timely manner . The Committee also notes that the capital allocation 
has shown a declining trend in recent years, partly due to lower utilisation and the completion 
of certain major capital procurements. In the Committee‟s view, the persistent shortfall in 

73 
 
utilisation has also contributed to a comparatively lower allocation in BE 2026 –27. The 
Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry strengthen its planning, project 
management and financial monitoring mechanisms so as to ensure timely and efficient 
utilisation of allocated funds . The Ministry should institute periodic reviews of expenditure, 
particularly under capital-intensive projects, and take proactive steps to address procedural, 
technical and operational bottlenecks that delay implementation.                            ( Para 2.14)   
CHAPTER III 
ASSESSMENT OF THE SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY 
PRITHVI VIGYAN (PRITHVI) 
 
The Committee recommends developing advanced indigenous technologies for desalination 
plants that run on non -renewable energy. Deployed in coastal areas and cities, these plants 
would augment fresh water supplies to help address existing shortages.                                    
(para 3.17)  
The Committee acknowledges the significant role played by the PRITHVI (Prithvi 
Vigyan) Scheme  in strengthening India‟s capabilities in Earth System S ciences through its 
integrated focus on atmosphere, oceans, cryosphere, geosciences and societal applications. 
The Committee notes that several Low-Temperature Thermal Desalination (LTTD) plants  
have been established in the Lakshadweep islands under the PRITHVI-OSMART initiative to 
address the critical issue of potable water availability for island communities. While the 
Committee recognizes the importance of these plants in ensuring sustainable drinking water 
supply, it also notes that the desalination pla nts currently operate using electricity generated 
through diesel generator sets, which power the island systems. Considering the environmental 
sensitivity of island ecosystems and the broader national commitment towards clean and 
renewable energy, the Comm ittee is of the view that reliance on diesel -based power sources 
should be progressively reduced. The Committee therefore recommends the Ministry to 
explore the feasibility of making these desalination plants eco -friendly by using sustainable 
power sources  such as solar energy, Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC), or other 
suitable renewable energy technologies.              (Para 3.18)
  
The Committee also examined the proposal for establishing Maitri-II, a new research 
station in Antarctica to replace t he existing Maitri station, which was commissioned in 1989 
and has now exceeded its designed operational life. The Committee notes the Ministry‟s 
submission that the present station is facing structural deterioration and limitations in waste -
management and environmental compliance systems, making the establishment of a modern 
replacement station necessary to ensure safety, operational sustainability, and India‟s 
continued year -round scientific presence in Antarctica in accordance with evolving 
environmental norms under the Antarctic Treaty System. However, the Committee notes that 
the architectural and design consultant for the Maitri -II project has been selected through a 
global design competition and that the winning consortium comprises Ramboll Deutschlan d 
GmbH and bofArchitekten, Germany. In this regard, the Committee recalls that India‟s first 
Antarctic research station, Dakshin Gangotri, was constructed with the involvement of the 
Indian Army, demonstrating indigenous capability in undertaking complex o perations in 
extreme polar conditions. The Committee is therefore of the view that greater consideration

DEEP OCEAN MISSION 
The Committee notes that the Deep Ocean Mission , launched in 2021, with an 
approved outlay of ₹4,077 crore , for a period of five years, is a flagship initiative aimed at 
strengthening India‟s capabilities in deep -sea exploration, resource assessment, marine 
biodiversity research and development of advanced ocean technologies. While the Committee 
acknowledges the efforts made by the Ministry and its institutions in progressing with certain 
components of the Mission, it observes with concern that the overall progress achieved so far 
does not appear commensurate with the objectives and timelines originally envisag ed. The 
Committee further notes that key components of the Mission, including the Samudrayaan 
programme and the development of an integrated deep -sea mining system for polymetallic 
nodules, are still incomplete. It is also observed that against the approve d project cost of 
₹4,077 crore, only about ₹1,445 crore has been utilised so far, which constitutes roughly 35 
per cent of the total project outlay. In the Committee‟s view, the pace of progress under 
certain critical verticals of the Mission needs to be s ignificantly accelerated to ensure that the 
intended outcomes are achieved within a reasonable timeframe. In this regard, the Committee 
recommends that the Ministry establish clearly defined and measurable milestones under 
each of the six verticals of the Deep Ocean Mission. The Committee further recommends that 
specific timelines be fixed for the achievement of these targets, along with a structured 
monitoring mechanism to periodically review progress and address implementation 
bottlenecks.                                                                                                (para 3.30) 
MISSION MAUSAM 
 
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry under Mission Mausam, 
which aims to transform India into a “Weather-ready and Climate-smart” nation through the 
deployment of advanced observational systems, next -generation modelling, and improved 
weather forecasting and early warning mechanisms. The Committee notes the progress made 
in strengthening Doppler Weather Radar networks, operationa lizing advanced forecasting 
systems, developing AI -based weather interpretation tools, and establishing platforms for 
hyper-local weather information dissemination.  At the same time, the Committee expresses 
concern over the increasing incidence of extreme weather events, particularly cloudbursts, 
which in recent years have caused significant loss of life, damage to infrastructure, and 
disruption of livelihoods in several parts of the country, especially in mountainous and 
ecologically fragile regions. The C ommittee notes the Ministry‟s initiative to establish a state-
of-the-art cloud microphysics laboratory  at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology 
(IITM), Pune, and to deploy specialised observing systems in cloudburst -prone regions such 
as the North-Eastern States, the Western Ghats, and the Western Himalayas. The Committee 
recommends that these research efforts should be strongly oriented towards developing 
reliable forecasting capabilities and an effective early warning system for cloudburst events so 
that timely alerts can be issued to vulnerable communities and disaster management 
authorities.                                                    (para 3.41)
  
           The Committee further notes that ocean –atmosphere interactions play a crucial role in 
influencing weather and climate patterns, including the formation and intensification of 
cyclones and extreme rainfall events. In this regard, the Committee takes note of the proposal 
under consideration for the development of floating radar systems mou nted on buoys  to 

75 
 
enhance meteorological observations over oceanic regions where land -based radar coverage is 
limited. The Committee believes that such systems could significantly improve monitoring of 
cloud formation and rainfall dynamics over the seas and  strengthen cyclone detection and 
tracking capabilities.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to accord due 
priority to the development and deployment of floating radar observation systems and 
provide adequate financial and institutional suppo rt for this initiative under Mission 
Mausam.                                   (para 3.42) 
 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should coordinate with bodies like 
Central Water Commission and Survey of India to develop scientific models for analyzing 
riverine water flow. This initiative would enable accurate flood forecasting, thereby 
mitigating damage, particularly in the nation's flood-prone regions.                         ( para 3.43)

INDIAN METEOROLOGICAL DEPARTMENT (IMD), NEW DELHI 
           The Committee notes that numerous advan ced technologies, including Artificial

Department (IMD).                                                                                                         (Para 4.12)

implemented by the Centre for Marine Living Resources and Ecology (CMLRE), was

concerned that the ageing of the vessel has resulted in several operational limitations and that, 
in its current condition, the vessel may remain operational for deep-sea surveys for only about 
seven more years. In view of these limitations and considering the growing requirements of

Fishery and Oceanographic Research Vessel capable of supporting expanded scientific 
observations in deep and distant waters. Given that F ORV Sagar Sampada is already nearing 
the end of its operational life, the Committee recommends that the Ministry expedite the

construction of the new vessel. The Committee i s of the view that the new Fishery and 
Oceanographic Research Vessel should be built and commissioned at the earliest, preferably 
within the next two to three years, so that India‟s marine research programmes are not 
adversely affected due to the declining operational capability of the existing vessel.

NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF OCEAN TECHNOLOGY (NIOT), CHENNAI 
         The Committee notes that the National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT) has 
transferred around 40 technologies between 2020 –26, generating a total revenue of ₹1.64 
crore. The Committee expresses concern that these technologies are being transferred at 
relatively low prices compared to their commercial potential. It is o bserved that private 
partners often earn significant profits from these technologies, while the originating public 
institutes receive only a marginal share of the value created. The Committee understands that 
there is no robust mechanism to verify whether the benefits of these low -cost technology 
transfers reach the intended target users, and that the fees for renewal of licenses after expiry 
remain undefined. In view of this, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Earth 
Sciences should adopt a more competitive, market-aligned pricing framework for technology 
transfer. Licensing fees should reflect the true commercial value, uniqueness, and societal 
impact of publicly funded technologies. The Committee further recommends the 
establishment of clear pro cedures and norms for technology valuation and licensing, with the 
National Research Development Corporation (NRDC) and the technology developer jointly 
determining fees. To ensure transparency, accountability, and proper utilization of publicly 
funded technologies, all technology transfer agreements should be subjected to periodic third -
party audits, and the outcomes of such audits should govern future licensing and renewal 
terms.                                      (Para 4.58)
