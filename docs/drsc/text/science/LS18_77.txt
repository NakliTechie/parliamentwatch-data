R E P O R T
 
 
The Indian Space Programme, since its inception in the early sixties, is geared to achieve the goal of self-reliant use
 of Space Technology to ensure rapid progress in diversified fields of the country including social, economic, cultural and
 scientific for finding solutions to the problems of man and society. Even as India entered the new millennium, the space
 programme in the country has matured to a status where space has become an important element of the national
 infrastructure, especially in the areas of communication, broadcasting, meteorology, disaster management and resources
 monitoring. India is recognized the world over for its unique application driven space programme. Further, industry
 participation in the space programme  has been expanding, bringing economic benefits. The plan to further enhance and
 improve the space services by launching follow-on satellites in the INSAT and IRS series in coming years and to enhance
 the launch capability to place INSAT class of satellites in orbit through GSLV, the Indian Space Programme is well poised
 to play a stronger role in the country's march towards progress in the new millennium.
2.     Whereas the efforts of the Indian Space Programme in the early sixties were confined to getting familiar with space
 technologies and developing technical and organisational infrastructure in order to develop satellite launch vehicles, during
 the eighties, the space programme moved closer to realising the goal of self-reliant use of space technology for national
 development. The country entered the nineties with the launch of more ASLVs and PSLVs. With the launch of IRS-IB and
 INSAT 2A and 2B, the Indian Space Programme becomes fully operational through its own communication and remote
 sensing satellites. The Indian Space Programme is broad-based and it has an impressive array of achievements in putting to
 use space technology for vital applications - for telecommunications, TV broadcasting, weather watching, forecasting
 agricultural crop and forests wealth assessment, water resources management, flood mapping, drought forecasting,
 identification of marine resources, protection of the environment and rural literacy campaign etc.
3.     Self-reliance in space technology has been an important target of the programme from the very beginning.  Long-range
 plans have been drawn up, from the very beginning, for each decade, to steer the programme effectively, ensuring
 appropriate linkages among the application needs, satellite missions, launch vehicle capabilities and technology
 development. Over the last three-and-half decades, significant achievements have been made in country in the area of space
 technology (i) for building world-class satellites for communications and remote sensing, (ii) for launching the remote
 sensing satellites from India with our own launch vehicle and (iii) in reaching  the benefits of space technology to improve
 the quality of life at the grass root level, thus contributing to several areas of national development.  Necessary
 organizational and institutional mechanisms with users, industries and academia have also been established. In tune with the
 goal of self-reliant use of space technology for national development, the thrust areas of the Indian Space Programme are:-
(a)    Satellite-based communication for various applications;
(b)    Satellite-based resources survey and management and environmental monitoring;
(c)    Meteorological applications; and
(d)   Development and operationalisation of indigenous satellites and launch vehicles for
         providing these space services.

4.     The Department of Space, which was set up in 1972 to promote unified development and application of Space Science
 Technology for identified national objectives, is entrusted with the task of implementing the policies framed by the Space
 Commission.   The policies are carried out through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and the three Grant-in-
aid institutions viz., the National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) and the
 National Mesosphere-Stratosphere-Troposphere Radar facility (NMRF).
5.     Today, the space  sector world over, is poised for a transformation towards a matured, scientific and industrial sector,
 with multiple players and changing roles. Besides, it needs to adapt itself to the implications of a market driven economy
 and the formidable challenges arising out of increased societal needs, technological limitations, resource constraints and
 geo-political scenarios.   In India, while integrating the programmes with the international scenario, the national needs are to
 be addressed; the gains achieved so far have to be consolidated; new and potential areas of space technology utilization are
 to be identified and developed; organisational as well as managerial support have to be ensured to surge forward, harnessing
 national capability and capacity, ensuring synergy amongst the partners as well as space systems.
6.    The Committee observes India is one of the six countries with homemade satellites in orbit. Despite being a new entrant
 to the competitive global space club, which hitherto was an exclusive preserve of a handful of countries, India's overall
 track record both in the field of rocketry and satellites has been impressive. This, of course, does not overlook the fact that it
 had its share of failures too, which needless to say can be expected in any hi-tech project. To cite a few, one can recall the
 failure of the ASLV and of the PSLV.   But these failed missions only proved to be another step on the ladder to success.
 The subsequent flights were a resounding success. This endorsed India's claim to be growing space power. The Committee,
 however, would like the Department to keep the analysis of each failed mission in view so that corrective measures can be
 taken while executing future programmes.
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
7.     The Committee was informed that the total expenditure on Space research till the end of Eight Plan Period i.e. from the
 inception in 1962-63 to 1996-97 was Rs.7431.67 crores. During the Eight Plan Period most of the programmatic targets and
 missions have been achieved and the total expenditure incurred on these programmes was Rs.3928.53 crores (Plan
 Rs.3155.74 crores and non-plan Rs.772.79 crores).
8.     It has been stated that the Department of Space had asked for an allocation of Rs.11,000 crores for the Ninth Plan
 period to meet the programmatic requirements of the Department, but the allocation was restricted to Rs.6511.72 crores for
 the entire plan period from 1997 to 2002. This is however subject to review on annual basis by the Planning Commission. In
 the first three years of the 9
th plan, the Department has expended Rs.4,164 crores which is 54% of the total plan outlay.
9.     Analysing the performance of the Department on the basis of the information made available, the Committee found that
 during 1997-98, the Department had utilised the funds to the extent of 99.13 percent. In 1998-99, the fund utilization level
 was about 99.9 per cent. The provisional expenditure for 1999-2000 indicates utilization of funds by the Department. The
 Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department has made optimum utilization of the funds allocated to it during
 1997-98, 1998-99 and 1999-2000.
10.    The programme directions and proposals for Ninth Plan (1997-2002) have been formulated after extensive interactions
 and consultations with user Ministries. The guidelines provided by the Space Commission have been adopted in the process
 of formulation. The Committee was informed that consistent with the programme directions and targets envisaged for the
 Ninth Plan period, the specific activities/projects to be undertaken during the year 2000-2001 have been carefully prioritised
 while working out the B.E. for 2000-2001 which are in pursuance of the Zero Base Budgeting Methodology of the
 Department in formulating the process of annual budgets. The financial allocation for 2000-2001 works out to Rs.2019.22
 crores as against Rs.l725.86crores in the R.E. of 1999-2000 and Rs.1755.00 crores in the B.E. of 1999-2000, which is
 higher by Rs.293.36 crores over the revised estimates and Rs.264.22 crores over the B.E. of 1999-2000 (Annexure).
11.    The major landmark achieved by the Department during the year 1999-2000 is the successful launch of the Indian
 Remote Sensing Satellite, IRS-P4 (Oceansat) alongwith two foreign satellites, one belonging to South Korea and other to
 Germany. The successful mission has not only given the ISRO enough confidence to market its launchers abroad but also
 enhanced the space services to the nation in the areas of telecommunication, television broadcasting, meteorology, disaster
 management and resources survey.
11.1  The Committee observes that these achievements are a feather in the cap of the Department as the launch of three
 satellites in a single flight is a technological achievement and it marks India's entry in the commercial launch market.

 the largest domestic communication satellite systems in the world today which is entrusted with the task of providing
 services in the areas of telecommunications, television broadcasting, radio networking and meteorology including disaster

SATELLITE NAVIGATION
13.    Satellite navigation equipment has been made mandatory by the International Civil Aviation Organization on all
 civilian aircraft. It is expected to become operational in many advanced countries in the next few years. In India, the Airport
 Authority of India (AAI) is planning to make them adaptable to the Future Air Navigation System through the introduction
 of Indian Wide Area Differential GPS system. In its endeavour to conform to the standards set out by the International Civil
 Aviation Organisation, the AAI is planning to replace the existing navigational aids with satellite navigation equipment by
 the year 2010.
13.1   Department of Space is playing a vital role in establishing the satellite navigation infrastructure in the country. It has
 sent a preliminary project report on satellite based navigation system to the AAI and is working in close coordination with
 the National Steering Committee on Communications, Navigation and Surveillance Air Traffic Management for giving
 shape to the plan of action.
13.2  The Committee feels that in order to fall in line with international standards, the Department should consider
 establishing a satellite based navigation system as that would facilitate modern air navigation system. The Committee is of
 the view that the Airport Authority of India and the Department of Space should work in close coordination to achieve the
 objective.
INDIAN REMOTE SENSING SATELLITE (IRS)
14.    The Indian Remote Sensing (IRS) system, which was commissioned in 1988, constitutes an important element of
 National Resources Management System (NNRMS) providing continuously space based data related to land, water and
 ocean resources as well as atmospheric observations. The IRS system now comprising five satellites IRS-1B, IRC-1C, IRS-
ID, IRS-P3 and IRS-P4 (Oceansat), forms the largest constellation of remote sensing satellites in the world to undertake
 space based remote sensing. The recent launch of IRS-P4 has opened up new vistas for ocean remote sensing, while the
 next in the series IRS-P5 is exclusively devoted to cartographic applications and IRS-P6 targeted for resources survey.
 These are scheduled for launch in the next couple of years. The application of remote sensing data is being extended to
 more areas in agriculture, forestry, surface and ground water harnessing, geology, urban planning, land use and land cover
 mapping and ocean resources survey flood mapping etc.
14.1   The remote sensing applications in the entire country are carried out under the broad umbrella of National Natural
 Resources Management System (NNRMS). As the effective utilization of IRS database on an operational basis is possible
 with the involvement of the states in setting up appropriate mechanism to integrate the database for effective management
 of natural resources in the states, the idea of State Natural Resources Management System (SNRMS) was conceptualized,
 on the pattern of existing NNRMS. The Government of Uttar Pradesh took the pride of being first in establishing SNRMS.
 States like Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal and Maharashtra are expected to finalise the process of establishing SNRMS in
 their respective states, in the near future.
14.2  While the Committee appreciates the efforts being made by the Department in augmenting its remote sensing
 capability, what is important is not only the effective management and utilization of natural resources but locating more of
 such resources considering that such resources are on the verge of depletion in the times ahead. The Committee  therefore 
 observes  that  the  efforts  should  be  so  oriented  with emphasis  on mapping more of natural resources as also the
 optimum utilization of the resources already tapped.

INTEGRATED MISSION FOR SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT (IMSD)
15.    Integrated Mission for Sustainable Development (IMSD) was initiated in 1992 to generate action plan for sustainable
 development of natural resources in rural areas. The mission is an extension of the pilot studies taken up by the Department
 during 1988-90 in predominantly drought prone/desert districts. Under IMSD, local-specific plans for development of land
 and water resources are generated through integrated analysis of thematic maps generated using remote sensing,
 conventional and socio-economic data and also R&D inputs provided by research institutions.   These locale-specific
 developmental/action plans are generated on watershed/blocks basis, thus covering a geographical area of 84 million
 hectares.
15.1   Asked about the implementation of IMSD programme in problem districts of the country, the Committee was
 informed that the IMSD is extended to 247 areas in 175 districts, out of which 34 districts are covered in full.
15.2  IMSD is mainly carried out in the problem districts of the country and the scope of IMSD is expected to be enlarged in
 a phased manner after detailed review of the progress made so far. Recently, IMSD has been extended to 8 districts of
 Orissa. Plans are also being worked out, in consultation with users to extend IMSD for critical areas (IMSD Phase - III).
 The financial allocation ofRs.1.00 crores in B.E. of 2000-2001 is towards monitoring the implementation of action plans in
 selected watershed and for IMSD Phase - III.
15.3  The Secretary, Department of Space, informed the Committee that the Department was planning to start Development
 Programme under the IMSD scheme in some districts of Jammu & Kashmir.
15.4 The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation (68
th Report) that even though the programme was heading
 towards a right direction, the pace of the programme appeared slow. The Committee feels that the tempo of the programme
 needs to be geared up and it should have time bound targets.
 DISASTER MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
16.    The Disaster Management System (DMS) is visualised to provide the backbone support for management of disasters in
 near-real time and also for disaster zonation studies and effective initiation strategies. Space-based disaster management
 system provides valuable inputs when appropriately integrated with the conventional ground-based systems. In the Ninth
 plan period, the efforts are on to complete a system definition study of the DMS and also conduct pilot experiments for
 establishing a prototype DMS. Plot project on DMS has been initiated in some flood-prone districts of Assam.
16.1   With a view to ensure timely dissemination of weather warnings of impending disaster from approaching cyclones,
 specially designed receivers i.e. Cyclone Warning Dissemination Systems (CWDS) have been installed in the cyclone-
prone coastal areas to disseminate cyclone warning messages directly to the affected areas. . At present 250 CWDS have
 been deployed in various coastal states of India.
16.2  The Secretary, Department of Space, informed the Committee that the Department was reviewing its Disaster
 Management System. In this direction, the Department in cooperation with IMD, was planning to install New Generation
 receiver with Global  Positioning  System  to  track
early movements of cyclones.
16.3  The Secretary, further informed the Committee that after Orissa cyclone, the Department was assisting the relief
 agencies by providing them satellite maps of the area indicating the degree of damage. For flood management the
 Department is embarking on geographical information base. The Department, besides Orissa and Andhra Pradesh, is
 working with the Brahmaputra Flood Control Mission and creating geographical maps in Assam in order to identified flood
 prone areas.
16.4  The Committee is constrained to observe that the perfection in the functioning of the Cyclone Warning Dissemination
 Systems (CWDS) for predicting the contours of the cyclone is yet to be achieved. The country has witnessed the fury of
 weather Gods in the form of cyclones and other weather extremes resulting in tremendous loss of life and property in some
 states. There is thus an imperative need to perfect the system. The projects therefore need to be taken up with sense of
 urgency and, if necessary, in cooperation with other Departments and agencies. The Committee feels that the constraint of
 funds should not be allowed to stand in the totality of these projects.

CRYO-UPPER STAGE (CUS) PROJECT
18.   The Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS) Project, approved in 1994, was intended to achieve a stage carrying 12.5 tonnes of
 propellant and produce a thrust of 7.5 tonnes. It is expected that GSLV will put INSAT Class of satellites into geostationary
 transfer orbit. The objective of the CUS Project is to develop and qualify a restartable cryogenic stage for the upper stage of
 GSLV. A cryogenic engine which provides one and half times more specific impulse  than conventional space engines, is
 necessary for launching GSLV. Apart from being far more efficient, and enabling heavier payloads to be injected into
 farther orbits, cryogenic engines are cost effective  and are also environment friendly.
18.1   The Committee was informed mat the allocation for the project during the year 2000-01 has been reduced because
 certain materials required for the cryo stage development to be procured from foreign sources could not be realized because
 the tendering action did not result in acceptable quotations and hence had to be retendered. In view of the above, the cash-
flow requirement for the project for RE 1999-20000 had been reduced.
18.2   The Secretary, DOS, informed the Committee that out of approved project cost of Rs.335.89 crores, during the last six
 years i.e. from 1994 to 2000, Rs.251.65 crores have been spent on the development of the project and the Department is
 expected to achieve the first cryogenic stage by the year 2003.
18.3   The first indigenously fabricated cryogenic engine was put on test on 16 February, 2000 at Mahendragiri. While the
 engine was successfully ignited after meticulous preparation, the test was aborted at 11 seconds due to a leak in hydrogen.
 This is the first of  the series of qualification tests for Cryogenic engine. The recorded results will enable suitable corrective
 action to be identified.
18.4  The Committee observes that there is imperative need for the country to perfect its cryogenic capabilities. Sustained
 effort and perseverance should help in that direction. Added to this the analysis of past experiments would act as a guidance
 to the future.
DIRECT - TO- HOME (DTH) TV MISSION
19.    The basic concept of a Direct-to-Home Television Broadcasting Satellite System is a high power satellite operating in
 the KU-Band for direct broadcasting of TV programmes to homes. Responding to the Indian broadcasting industry's
 demand. Government permitted import of KU-Band equipment in 1996 but banned the import in the subsequent year. Since
 then the mission has not made any remarkable progress. The imperative for such a decision is stated to be the need to have a
 comprehensive broadcasting law on the subject. Further, due to decision of the Government not to fund the programme, its
 success depends on the participation of private investors, but this can be achieved only after the law in this regard has been
 enacted.
19.1   The Committee feels that the Government should clear the fog on the issue as early as possible or else its credibility to
 spread information technology is bound to suffer. DTH is a part of information technology and Government should clear the
 hurdles on the subject keeping the interest of the viewers uppermost while enacting any taw on the subject.

 IRS-P5(CARTOSAT) AND IRS-P6 (RESOURCESAT)
21.   IRS-P5 and IRS-P6 are the satellites with advanced state of art remote sensing payloads.  While IRS-P5 satellite is
 exclusively devoted to cartographic applications, IRS-P6 is targeted for resources survey. The sanctioned cost of the project
 is Rs.248.49 crores and Rs. 141.58 crores respectively. These satellites are planned to be launched by PSLV continuation
 flights in the next two years.
21.1   At present most of the procurement related activities relate to IRS-P5 particularly the materials, components and high

SPACE-INDUSTRY INTERFACE
22.    with a view to harnessing the industry's potential, the Indian Space Programme has been encouraging transfer of
 technologies to Indian industry to support various space objects to remote space applications and market development, and
 for disseminating spin-off space technologies to diverse economic sectors. Guided by the objective of achieving self-
reliance in space technology, partnership with industry has been nurtured through various initiatives and cooperative
 ventures. A number of technologies have been transferred to industry in the areas of electronics, communications,
 computers, optical  and remote sensing data utilization equipment. Besides, provision for technological consultancy and
 technology utilization has been promoted.   The association with the space programme has enabled industry to adopt
 advanced technologies and handle complex manufacturing jobs. So far, over 236 technologies developed under the space
 programme have been transferred to industries for commercialisation and more than 180 technical
consultancies provided in various areas of space technology. With Antrix Corporation, the commercial wing of Department
 of Space, having its presence in the global space market, Indian industries have begun participating in the fabrication of
 space hardware to meet the requirements of international customers also.
22.1   The Committee was informed that till date, ISRO has more than 100 patents, 8 trademarks and 5 copyrights to its
 credit and three applications have been granted patents in the last year. Patent awareness workshops, training programmes
 were also conducted last year to create a greater patent awareness. In the same year consultancy assignments such as
 development plan for Pimpari Chinchwad Municipal Corporation and the baseline mapping of Gir protected area have also
 been completed.
22.2  The Committee notes the position satisfaction.

 activities in space science, space applications and space technology in various national academic/research institutions
 through grants-in-aid, besides supporting conferences, symposia and publications. The budgetary allocation for this
 programme has been kept at Rs.5.50 crores during the year 2000-2001.

 projects. It should be the endeavour of the Department to complete the projects within the stipulated time frame.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS AT GLANCE
1.    The Committee observes India is one of the six countries with homemade satellites in orbit. Despite being a new entrant
 to the competitive global space club, which hitherto was an exclusive preserve of a handful of countries, India's overall
 track record both in the field of rocketry and satellites has been impressive. This, of course, does not overlook the fact that it
 had its share of failures too, which needless to say can be expected in any hi-tech project. To cite a few, one can recall the
 failure of the ASLV and of the PSLV.   But these failed missions only proved to be another step on the ladder to success.
 The subsequent flights were a resounding success. This endorsed India's claim to be growing space power. The Committee,
 however, would like the Department to keep the analysis of each failed mission in view so that corrective measures can be
 taken while executing future programmes. 
(Para 6)
2.  The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department has made optimum utilization  of the funds allocated to it
 during 1997-98, 1998-99 and 1999-2000.
(Para 9)
3.  The Committee observes that these achievements are a feather in the cap of the Department as the launch of three
 satellites in a single flight is a technological achievement and it marks India's entry in the commercial launch market.
(Para 11.1)
4.  The Committee feels that in order to fall in line with international standards, the Department should consider establishing
 a satellite based navigation system as that would facilitate modern air navigation system. The Committee is of the view that
 the Airport Authority of India and the Department of Space should work in close coordination to achieve the objective.
(Para 13.2)
5.  While the Committee appreciates the efforts being made by the Department in augmenting its remote sensing capability,
 what is important is not only the effective management and utilization of natural resources but locating more of such
 resources considering that such resources are on the verge of depletion in the times ahead. The Committee therefore
 observes that the efforts should be so oriented with emphasis on mapping more of natural resources as also the optimum
 utilization of the resources already tapped.
(Para 14.2)
6. The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation (68
th Report) that even though the programme was heading towards
 a right direction, the pace of the programme appeared slow. The Committee feels that the tempo of the programme needs to
 be geared up and it should have time bound targets.
 (Para 15.4)

 Systems (CWDS) for predicting the contours of the cyclone is yet to be achieved. The recent devastating cyclone in Orissa
 can be seen as an example, besides past experiences. The country has witnessed the fury of weather Gods in the form of
