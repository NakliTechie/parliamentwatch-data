REPORT 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment & Forests, 
having been authorised by the Committee to submit the report on its behalf, present the Thirty­
first Report, on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the Department of Science and 
Technology (Ministry of Science and Technology) for the year. 1996-97 (Demand No. 74). 
2. In its meeting held on August 8, 1996 the Committee discussed in detail the Demands for 
Grants of the Department of Science and Technology on the basis of documents made available 
to it and the submissions made before it by the Secretary and other officials of the Department. 
3. The Committee thanks the officers of the Department for replying to the clarifications 
sought by the Hon 'ble Members and placing before it the required material to enable the 
Committee to scrutinise the Demands for Grants of the Department of Science and Technology. 
4. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its meeting held on 23rd August, 
1996. 
5. In pursuance of the national objective of making scientific and technological progress 
and attaining self-reliance, Department of Science and Technology (DST) has been assigned 
nodal responsibilities for coordinating research and development in frontier areas of science and 
technology. Over the years the departments and autonomous institutions under Department of 
Science and Technology have been making consistent efforts to foster research and 
development (R & D) activities, resulting in development of infrastructure in diverse fields of 
Science & Technology, administration, human resource, etc. The department lays emphasis on 
planning process which emanates from the Panchayat Level through its Meteorology and Survey 
Departments. 
6. The Secretary, Department of Science and Technology, in his presentation before the 
Committee, gave an overview of the thrust areas, which the Department intended to take up 
during the year 1996-97, for which allocations were made in the Budget for the year 1996-97 for 
Department of Science and Technology. The mandate to promote frontline and emerging areas 
of science and engineering is being fulfilled through the Science and Engineering Research 
Council (SERC), which is an apex body consisting of eminent scientists from Universities, 
National Laboratories and Industry_ The Council is assisted by a "Peer Review System" which 
consists of a thorough assessment of each programme by a Programme Advisory Committee 
(PAC) and other Expert Committees. In addition, new and sophisticated research facilities and 
Core Groups/Units are set up to take up challenging research problems and to cater to the 
needs of scientists in the important areas of science and engineering. 
6.1 Over the years, the support under the programme has enhanced the vision of the Indian 
scientists at international level and the work of several scientists is gaining recognition. The 
Committee was informed that while the ongoing programmes would continue to have financial 
support during 1996-97, activities in some other important areas of science and engineering 
would be initiated. 
6.2 Important initiatives proposed in 1996-97 include a centre for excellence in engineering 
on Technical Acoustics and training of young engineers I scientists in the advanced 
manufacturing technology through SERC schools. The Committee was apprised that more than 
341 research and development programme costing Rs. 34.2 crores have been supported at 
various institutions through the SERC. Under the SERC Instrument Development Programme, 
priority was to transfer developed technology to industries for . commercial production. 
6.3 The Committee was further apprised of the support to basic and applied research in 
national institutions throughout the country and provision of minimum infrastructure facilities 

for testing and instrumentation which is entrusted to 15 autonomous high quality research 
institutions ranging from advanced medical research, materials to astronomy and four 
professional bodies supported by Department of Science and Technology namely Indian 
National Science Academy (New Delhi), Indian Academy of Science ( Bangalore), Indian 
Science Congress Association (Calcutta) and National Academy of Science (Allahabad). 
6.4 Specific mention was about two institutions, the Technology Information Forecasting 
Assessment Council, (TIF AC) and Vigyan Prasar and highlighted their achievements. 
TIF AC was in the news recently as Hon'ble Prime Minister referred to "Technology Vision 
2020" document which states the technology requirements of this country in the next few 
decades TIF AC looks forward in Technologies and energise action in select areas. Vigyan 
Prasar undertakes large scale science popularisation activities. 
6.5 The Committee was informed about initiation of multifaceted country~wtse 
astronomy popularisation programme, taking advantage of the opportunity provided by and 
built around the Total Solar Eclipse of October 24, 1995, with the combined effort of 
National Council for Science and Technology Communication (NCSTC) and Vigyan Prasar 
6.6 Department of Science and Technology activities also include support to ma,ly 
socially oriented ~cience and technology interventions for rural areas and weaker section~, 
assistance to the State Cuuncils on Science and Technology, Science and Technology 
Communicati01~ and Popularisation, SCience and Technology Entrepreneurship Development 
Programmes which aim to provide self-employment opportunities and Natural Resource!' 
Data Managemen~ System (NRDMS). Besides, the Department has also been successful in 
providing scientific services in terms of surveying and maps for Defence, External Affair<;, 
State Governments and several other development agencies through Survey of India (SOl) 
and National Atlas and Thematic Marping Organisation (NATMO). Meteorological Services 
for Agriculture, Water Re"iources Management, Disaster Warning, Civil Aviation as well ru: 
providing seismtcity data through Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) have been able 
to provide a certain direction to the Government for .enabling them to take major policy 
decic;iom. The Himalayan Seismicity Programme is expanded to provide seismic coverage m 
the Bihar Plains and support research projects in North-East. 
6.7 It was further states that the technology, projects in the mission mode on sugar 
technologies, advanced composites and flyash utilisation and the benefit accrueing thereof. 
The Secretary also informeu the Committee of the setting up of the Technology 
Development Board which would make available new technologies to the Indian Industry so 
that they became competitive in the international market. 
7. After listening to the presentation made by the Secretary, Department of Science and 
Technology, the Committee examined the budgetary allocations of the Department for the 
year 1996-97". While considering the Demands for Grants of the Department, the 
Committee also got an opportunity to review the work of various projects and programmes 
which fall within the mandate of the Department. 
8. The Budget Estimates of 1996-97 have registered an increase from Rs. 229.80 crores 
in the previous year to Rs. 242.55 crores on Plan side and from Rs. 192.05 crores to 
Rs. 241.50 crores on Non-Plan side. 
8.1 It was stated that the enhanced allocation on the Plan side was mainly due to the 
World Bank funded project, "Seismological Instrumentation in Peninsular Shield" and for 
"autonomous scientific institutions". The enhancement on the Non-Plan side is mainly due 
"For summary of the Demands for Grants see schedule. 

to allocation of Rs. 30 crores under Technology Development Board. The non-plan 
expenditure has risen in sharp contrast to the expenditure on plan side. The Committee 
expresses its concern on the rise in non-plan expenditure. 
9. On being asked about the reduction in the budget estimates for intensification of 
research work in High Priority Areas against a sum of Rs. 7.00 crores in the budget 
estimates for the year 1995-96 to Rs. 5.50 crores in 1996-97, the Committee was informed 
that the resource constraints have resulted in the reduced allocation. But the Department 
hoped to raise it to Rs. 7 crores at the stage of revised estimates. The Committee observes 
that the reduced allocation may hamper intensification of research work which is the main 
thrust of the Department . 
10. The Committee was informed that the constitution of Technology Development 
Board had been assented to by the President on 16th December, 1995. Inadequate allocation 
and delay in the finalisation of names on the Panel of the Board have resulted in the delay 
in the constitution of the Board. The Secretary assured the Committee that the notification 
for constituting the Board would be issued within a week's time. The Committee, observes 
that the dealy in the constitution of the Board should have been avoided . The Committee, 
however, notes the assurance and hopes that the constitution of the Board would help in 
making available new technologies to the Indian Industry in order to help them compete in 
the international market.

11.1 The Committee observes that the proper assessment and projection of requirements 
of funds was not done. Budgetary estimates should be prepared keeping in view all the 
t·clevant aspects so that surrender of funds is avoided to the extent possible. At the same time 
care should be taken to ensure that the funds are utilised in a best possible manner. Efforts 
should also be made to improve the budget management through modern methods like 
Management Information System (MIS), periodical control of expenditure, etc. 
12. Budget Estimates for 1996-97 provide the Drugs and Pharmac t!uticals research 
programme a sum of Rs. 6 crores which is at the same level as was allocated to the 
Department last year. During 1995-96, 4 research proposals and two national facilities were 
sanctioned involving industries like DABUR, RANBAXY, ZANDU Pharmaceutical Works 
Ltd. and the research institutes such as Centre for Bio-chemical Technology (Delhi), Indian 
Institute of Chemical Technology (Hyderabad), Regional Research Laboratory (Jammu), 
Central Drug Research Institute (Lucknow) and Indian Institute of Science (Bangalore). The 
overall expenditure on the research projects during 1995-96 was nearly Rs. 3 crores and 
Rs. 2.3 crores on the facilities supported under this programme. 
12.1 The Committee reiterates (Eighteenth Report, Para 9.1) that Department of Science 
and Technology should help Indian Drug Industry to be more competitive in the world 
market. This can happen only if greater thrust and higher priority is accorded to this area. 
The Committee feels that under the new Intellectual Property Regime, drugs and 
pharmaceuticals is one area which has been affected the r_nost. This calls for a determined and 
concerted thrust in terms of indigenous design and development. Seen against this background 
the allocation needs to be enhanced considerably so that the progress in the research 
programmes for development of drugs to combat diseases such as Tuberculosis, Leprosy, 
Malaria, Filaria, Diarrhoea etc. do not suffer. 
13. National Super-Conductivity Programme has been impl emented in two phases­
Phase- I, from April 1988 to September 1991, Phase- 11 from October 1991 to March 1996, 
involving 46 Basic Research Projects and 25 Application Projects were supported at 36 
Institutes/Organisati ons. The Committe~ is of the view that the programme should be revived 
with a greater thrust and higher priority. 

14. The Committee is conscious of the fact that new emerging areas like solar energy 
require greater attention. To that end the Department should initiate research on 
manufacturing of "amorphous silicon" which is stated to be a cheaper technology in the field 
of Solar Conductivity. This, the Committee feels, would be a significant contribution towards 
self-reliance in non-conventional energy sources. 
15. The Secretary informed that for some of the ongoing schemes aimed at developing 
technology based solutions for the upliftment of SO'ST and tribal population, women and 
the handicapped, the Department was dependent on other Ministries. DST's allocation for 
the year 1996-97 stood at Rs.5.75 crores. The Committee is of the view that the allocation in 
this area needs to be enhanced to ensure the benefits of modern technology to specific 
segments of the population. 
16. The Department, the Committee was informed, has been helping the State 
Governments to establish State Science and Technology Councils to enable them to make 
use of modern technological solutions to their development needs. Following considerable 
increased in the aspirations and empowerment at the grass-root level such as the Panchayats, 
Zila Parishads, etc. the Committee feels that the allocation of Rs. 2.50 crores to the state 
councils is grossly inadequate. 
17. The Committee was informed that there were a large number of Universities and 
institutions where very little research was being carried out as compared to internationally 
competitive research in several premier educational and research institutions. This was 
attributed to lack of enabling infrastructure and funds for research while the trained 
manpower available in these institutes have the capability to carry out scientific investigation 
in important areas. The Committee recommends that the Department should make use of the 
manpower as also its potential, promote research in such less endowed universities and 
institutes by chalking out a comprehensive plan of action. 
18. Seismicity has been part of the Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) system. 
Efforts of Indian Meteorological Department system (IMD) have been particularly 
concentrated in weather predictions and seismicity has not been paid adequate attention for 
a variety of reasons. The Committee notes with satisfaction that in the last couple of years the 
Department has attempted to strengthen the seismicity programme and a network to seismic 
stations is being contemplate£!, particularly in the Himalayan region. The Committee 
reiterates that what is important in all of this activity is not only the seismological network 
but a total plan for disaster mitigation; through proper zoning, building codes, strengthening 
of vulnerable points (e.g. telecom centres, hospitals, bridges, command structures, etc). In the 
budget estimates for 1996-97 a sum of Rs. 4.20 crores has been provided for R&D support for 
seismicity and Rs.13.00 crores under seismic observation (seismological instrumentation, 
upgradation and related geophysical studies in Peninsular Shield Areas). The Committee 
ohsenes that the demand for the year 1996-97 is inadequate. Keeping in view the importance' 
of the programme more funds should be provided for the upgradation of the National 
Seismicity Network. 
19. A sum of Rs. 2.90 crores h~s been earmarked in 1996-97 against a sum of Rs. 3.25 
crores in 1995-96 for the Programme for Young Scientists. The Committee feels that in view 
of the thrust of Department on two areas namely, the basic scientific research and 
dissemination of information to young scientists, the allocation under the head is inadequate. 
20. The Committee had recommended in its Eighteenth Report thai DST should 
,·igorously implement the Entrepreneurship Awareness and Development Programmes but no 

budgetary enhancement has been asked for in 1996-97 whereas in 1995-96 a sum of Rs. 4.25 
crores was allocated. The Committee is of the view that the DST should explore the possibility 
of implementation of these programmes through various schemes-jointly with other Ministries/ 
Departments . 
21. The Committee feels that Department of Science and Technology should be in a 
position to give an integrated picture of the ongoing scientific efforts in India. The 
Department should keep it self posted with various research programmes being conducted by 
other scientific departments and the R&D components of the Ministries/Departments of the 
Government which are not scientific per se. DST should endeavour to present a holistic view 
with regard to the scientific activities of the various Government departments, research 
institutions, Universities etc. Besides, it should also be in a position to give an outlook about 
the R&D component of Private Industries and its priorities. With the consequent liberalisation 
of Indian economy and rare linkages between Industries and scientific research institutions/ 
Universities there is a strong possibility that it may lead to exclusive priority to Research & 
Development relating to industry and to aspects which may lead to immediate results. This 
may possibly give rise to research in pure or basic science being relegated to the background. 
The Committee, therefore, strongly feels that DST may have to make concerted efforts to 
check the emergence of any such trend. 
22. After considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Science and 
Technology and assessing the progress of various important programmes and projects 
administered by it, the Committee recommends that the Demands for Grants of Rs. 481.05 
crores, as sought by the Department of Science and Technology, for the year 1996-97, under 
Demand, No. 74, may be approved.
