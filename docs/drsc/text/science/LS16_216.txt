REPORT 
  The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Atomic Energy on the recommendations 
contained in the Two Hundred and Seventh Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science 
and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Gran ts (2010-11) of the Department of Atomic Energy, which 
was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 23rd April, 2010. There were nineteen recommendations in the Two 
Hundred and Seventh Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received 
from the Department on 21st July, 2010. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken by the 
Department of Atomic Energy are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:–– 
Recommendation (Paras 4.3 and 4.4) 
 The Committee while examining the details of reasons provided by DAE for non-fulfillment of targets in Tenth Plan 
under R&D sector observes that as against the revised outlay of  Rs. 3501.35 crore, utilization of only Rs. 3199.45 crore could 
be done which works out to 91.37 per cent of the revised outlay and there is a short fall of about 8.63 per cent in terms of 
utilization. Similarly under Industries Sector, against the Budg etary support of Rs. 1539 crores , the utilization is Rs. 1241.2 3 
crores which means there is a short fall of Rs. 297.77 crores in terms of utilization.  Under power sector against the budgetary 
support of Rs. 13441.41 crores the utilization is only Rs. 10016.83 crares which is about 74.52 per cent of total of the budget ary 
provision. The Committee therefore, finds that despite the ela borate and comprehensive monitoring mechanism adopted by the 
DAE underutilization of funds under different sectors presents altogether a different picture because in certain cases the 
underutilization of budgetary support runs to almost 25.5 per cent which is not a healthy indication. 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department needs to further strengthen the mechanism of monitoring 
ongoing schemes for their effective implementation and eliminating any scope of surrender of funds at a later date due to under  
utilization. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The monitoring mechanisms have been developed by the Department to monitor and review the progress of the ongoing 
schemes/projects before formulating/finalising proposals for budgetary support. These mechanisms are being effectively used to 
eliminate the scope of shortage or surrenders of funds at later stage. 
 The recommendations of the Committee have been brought to the notice of all Head of Constituent Units/Autonomous 
Bodies and Public Sector Undertakings of the Department and also to all members concerned with the monitoring mechanisms 
with a request that all necessary efforts are taken at their en d to further strengthen the Monitoring System to achieve full 
utilisation of plan funds. 
Comments 
 T he Committee takes note of the action  taken by the Department  and hopes that monitori ng mechanism would be 
strengthened to achieve full utilization of plan funds in future. 
Recommendation (Para 5.8) 
 The Committee observes that the overall thrust of the Indian Nuclear Programme  during the Eleventh Five Year Plan 
(2007-12) is to sustain and strengthen the already established Nuclear based programmes which the Department has designed 
during past several years for socio economic development of the country. The Committee recommends that in addition to 
continuing and strengthening its already developed Nuclear Programmes and Technologies, the Department should also quest 
for mastering economical and more advanced reactor technologies, so that more viable  reactor technology could be developed 
in future. The Committee hopes that the Department would be able to achieve the goals set for itself during the Eleventh Five 
Year Plan period, despite the fact that the trend of utilization in the year 2007-08 is not upto the mark. The Committee finds that 

under Nuclear Power Schemes, the Department has been able to utilize only Rs. 1188.86 crores as against the plan allocation of 
Rs. 2449.15 crores. The Committee, however, notes that the trend of expenditure has improved during the last two years. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendation of the Committee have been noted to quest for mastering more economical and advanced reactor 
technologies and will pursue the same. Department is pursuing all its activities and will strive to achieve the goals set for i tself. 
during Eleventh Five Year Plan. 
 In respect of utilization of budgetary support In the year 200 7-08, the utilization of Russian credit for Kudankulam Project 
was Rs. 373.29 crore as against the allocatio n of Rs. 1021.97 crore in BE. This was due to delays in deliveries of shipments of  
components and equipment from Russian Federation due to developments in the Russian nuclear industry during the period. 
The credit utilization improved subsequently and the utilization in 2008-09 and 2009-10 has been 100% under the Nuclear 
Power Schemes. Further during 2007-08 an amount of Rs.536.00 crore could not be spent for the construction of new reactor 
(PFBR) at Kalpakkam on account of delay in getting raw material, delay in delivery of equipments etc. The expenditure during 
2008-09 and 2009-10 has beer substantially increased and attained 100% of the Budget Estimates. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Department and ho pes that all out efforts woul d be made to achieve the 
targets during the Eleventh Five Year Plan and establish India as a frontline state in Nuclear Science. 
Recommendation (Para 6.5) 
 The Committee notes that a major curtailment in funds has been  made under the Plan allocation for the Minerals Sector 
where against an initial amount of Rs. 2000.00 crores propos ed for ‘Acquisition of Uraniu m Assets from Abroad’ a meager 
amount of Rs. 1.00 crore has been approved for the time being as a provisional amount. The Committee is not at all satisfied 
with the explanation given by the department as mentioned above and feels that this token provis ions of Rs. 1.00 crore will not  
at all fulfill the purpose for which the same is being given. Th e Committee is of the opinion that if the Ministry of the Finan ce 
makes any token provisions of such low amount for any program me for which the initial demand is high, no scheme under the 
Department could realize its dreams and the entire token provisions may go waste. The Committee while expressing its serious 
concern over such drastic curtailment of funds recommends that  whenever any token provision is made in future for any 
scheme, the allocation made initially should be reasonably proportional to the original amount demanded and should not be 
granted in a causal manner as has been done  in the present case as such action may hinder the progress of strategic scheme of 
the Department in future. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Based on the developments with regard to the scheme for ‘Acquisition of Uranium Assets Abroad’ and the progress 
achieved at the time of finalization of BE 2010-11, decision was taken to keep only a token provision of Rs. 1 crore in BE 2010-
11. A decision on actual requirement of funds would be taken at the RE Stage for which. Government has assured availability of 
funds. The serious concern over providing token provision prop ortionately with reference to original demands by the 
Committee is noted in the Department for future course of action. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Committee and is hopeful that the De partment would take the 
concern expressed by the Committee seriously. 
Recommendation (Paras 8.2 and 8.3) 
 The Committee notes with serious concern that a drastic redu ction of about Rs. 340.00 crores have been made in the 
proposed allocation as regards the Research and Development sector. The Committee is of the opinion that reduction in the 
allocation towards Research and Development will slow down the initiatives that are proposed to be undertaken by the 
department and also the overall progress achieved by the country in this field. The Committee, therefore, recommends that such 
reductions should be avoided at all cost by the Planning Commission. 

 The Committee is of the opinion that the well thought ou t programmes and proposed demands of a crucial Department 
such as the Department of Atomic Energy should not face drastic cuts in funds allocation. The Committee is also of the opinion 
that reduction of funds for leading Institutions delays the endeavour of these institutions in achieving their main objective o f 
harnessing nuclear energy for the benefit of the society. The Committee recommends that to enable the Department to achieve 
its targets in the field of Atomic Energy, appropriate budgetary allocations should be made keeping in view the fact that Atomic 
Energy is a highly capital intensive sector and that the Department has been able to fully utilize the allocated provisions dur ing 
the last two years. The Committee also reco mmends that in future the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance would 
be extremely judicious while finalizing allocation for the Department. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendation of the Committee has already been brought to the notice of Ministry of Finance and Planning 
Commission. The Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission have noted the observations and recommendations of the 
Committee and have assured that they would consider departments proposal positively in Revised Estimates. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Department. 
Recommendation (Paras 9.5 and 9.6) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that th e economy of the country being primarily based on agriculture ‘with a substantial 
workforce of the country engaged in farming, the economic development of the country largely depends on agricultural 
development. The Committee is also aware that most of the farmers in the country do not have any access to the latest 
techniques to increase agricultural produce and also techniques  to prevent the loss in produce due to diseases and pests and al so 
due to lack of proper storage facilities. Therefore, increase in agricultural produce and processing of agricultural products in the 
country through scientific support, assu mes prime importance and the Department of Atomic Energy through its reputed 
institutions has a pivotal role to play in this regard. 
 The Committee is of the opinion that introduction of new varieties of disease and pest resistant crops in various parts of 
the country will help in increasing crop yields and give a boost to  agriculture and also help in tiding over shortage of food. The 
Committee is also of the opinion that irradiated food products wh ich have a higher shelf life will increase the export of farm 
products and thereby help in increasing th e agricultural incomes. While the Committee appreciates the initiatives taken by the 
Department in this regard, it feels that much more needs to be done further. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the 
department should intensify its research in the field of agri culture and popularize its technologies among farmers across the 
country, so that the benefit of research could percolates down to the poor and marginal farmers as well. 
Action Taken by Government 
 In the field of Nuclear agriculture genetic improvement of  crop plants is a continuous  endeavour. Success of a crop 
improverment programme depends on the availability of large genetic variability, which a plant breeder can combine to 
generate new varieties. In nature, occurrence of natural variability in the form of spontaneous mutations is extremely low 
(roughly 10-6), which can be enhanced to several fold (approximat ely 10-3) by using ionizing radiations or chemical mutagens. 
Radiation induced genetic variability in crop plants is a valuable resource from which plant breeder can select and combine 
different desired characteristics to produce better crop varietie s. Crop improvement programmes at Bhabha Atomic Research 
Centre (BARC) envisage radiation based induced mutagenesis along with recombination breeding in country’s important 
cereals (rice and wheat), oilseeds (groundnut, mustard, soybean and sunflower), grain legumes (blackgram, mungbean, 
pigeonpea and cowpea), banana and sugarcane. 
 Induction of modified traits and their incorporation in an ideal genotype could be achieved by a well planned and 
judicious use of induced mutation and hybridization techniques. Mutants or recombinants initially developed at BARC are 
evaluated in collaboration with the Indian Council of Agricultur al Research or State Agricultural Universities in multi locatio n 
trials for various agro-climatic zones. The promising ones after multi location testing in a given agro climatic zone/location are 
released for commercial cultivation. With the effective ble nd of mutation and recombination breeding, 39 new crop varieties 
developed at BARC have been released and Gazette notified by the Ministry of Agriculture, Government of India for 

commercial cultivation. These include 20 in oil seeds (l4-grou ndnut, 3-mustard, 2 soybean, 1 sunflower), 16 in pulses (8-
greengram (mung), 4-blackgram (urad), 4-pigeonpea (tur), 1-cowpea (chowli) and one each in rice and jute. 
 These improved Trombay crop varieties possess important agro nomic traits like. early maturity to escape end-season 
drought, to fit in different cropping systems and to cultivate in rice fallow system; optimum period of fresh seed dormancy to 
avoid in situ germination due to end-season rains; large seed for confectionery purpose; ideal plant type to fit in high density 
cropping; drought tolerance; disease and insect resistance; higher oil yields and improved nutritional properties. 
 In order to compliment current crop improvement programmes and to address future constraints arising out of climate 
change, newer research initiatives like mu tation breeding approaches for inducing genetic variability for drought, salinity, he at, 
mineral toxicity tolerance; multiple pest and disease resistance; improved grain quality and use of molecular marker assisted 
selection are being under taken in different crops.  
 For dissemination of research efforts of  BARC to the farmers, effective linkages have been established with Indian 
Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR), State Agricultural Departments, State Agriculture Universities, National and State 
Seed Corporations, State Farms Corporation of India, National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India Ltd. 
(NAFED), NGOs, National Institutes, Krishi Vigyan Kendras,  progressive farmers etc. Large scale production of 
nucleus/breeder seeds of Trombay crop varieties is undertaken  at BARC farms at Trombay, Gauribidanur, and also in 
collaboration with progressive farmers and Agricultural Universi ties. Breeder seeds are supplied to different National and Stat e 
Seed Corporations for multiplication into foundation and certified  seeds to reach farmers. ‘Paramanu Urja Agriculture Society’ 
has been established at Tarapur Atomic Power Station, Tarapur. This Society has an agriculture farm for demonstration to the 
neighbourhood agriculture products developed at BARC and also to produce quality planting material of BARC varieties. 
BARC also organizes/participates in various Kissan Melas (Far mers rally), exhibitions as well as awareness programmes 
through farmers federations, and through front-line demonstrations. 
 In the area of food preservation, BARC is  engaged in R&D work on the technology of preservation and hygienization of 
food by radiation for the past more than 50 years. The safety and wholesomeness of the technology was endorsed in 1981 by 
world bodies like World Health Organization, Food and Agricultural Organization, International Atomic Energy Agency, and 
by the Codex Alimentarius Commission that sets global standards for food, in 1983. In 1991, Atomic Energy Act was amended 
and Atomic Energy (Control of Irradiation of Food) rules, 1991 were established. Th ese rules were subseq uently amended in 
1996. In 1994, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare amended PFA Act rules to approve radiation processing of onion, potato 
and spices. The PFA Act’ rules were further amended in 1998 and 2001 to approve additional items of food. Ministry of Health 
and Family Welfare was approached by the department for approval of the food and agricultural commodities on wider 
generic/class-wise basis for radiation pro cessing, so that the radiation proeessing plants could process more agricultural 
commodities and be operated around the year for better economic gains. The draft notification for generic approval on class 
basis was published for public re view in May 2007. The Atomic Energy (Control of Irradiation of  Food) rules, 1996, under the 
Atomic Energy Act are also under review in the department with a similar intent. 
 In India commercial food irradiation could be carried out in a facility licensed to do so. The license could be obtained 
after fulfilling the requirements of the Atomic Energy Regulato ry Board (AERB). The department of Atomic Energy has set up 
two technology demonstration units in India. The Radiation Pr ocessing Plant at Vashi, Navi Mumbai, is a 30- ton per day 
capacity unit capable of delivering medium and high doses mainly for treatment of spices, dry vegetable seasonings like onion 
flakes, and pet foods. Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology  (BRIT) is operating the plant since January, 2000. Annually 
about 1500 tons of spices, dry vegetables and herbal products are irradiated in the plant. 
 Another unit, KRUSHAK (Krushi Utpadan Sanrakshan Kendra) at Lasalgaon near Nashik, is primarily a low dose 
irradiator capable of delivering doses less than 1 kGy to agri cultural commodities like onion, potato, mango, cereals, pulses a nd 
their products. In 2007, the plant was approved by the United States Department of Agriculture- Animal and Plant Health 
Inspection Service (USDA-APHIS) for export of mango to the US. A consignment of 157 tons of mangoes of different varieties 
processed at KRUSHAK was exported to US after a gap of 18 years. A breakthrough was thus achieved in demonstrating 
commercial feasibility of radiation technology in overcoming quarantine barrier to international trade and obtaining market 
access. This KRUSHAK facility is being commercially used by  Maharashtra Agricultural Mark eting Board under a tripartite 

MoU between BARC, BRIT and MSAMB. Since then more than 600 tons of mango have been processed at KRUSHAK and 
exported to USA. The facility has recently been upgraded to increase its products range. 
 In India radiation processing of food can be undertaken both for export and domestic markets. For export food could be 
processed for shelf-life extension, hygienization, and for overc oming quarantine barriers. India is well known for its export o f 
rice, basmati and non-basmati, seafood, spices, meat and poultry and to some extent onion. Radiation processing can be used for 
restructuring costs of bulk commodities in export markets, and for selling value added packaged commodities directly in retail 
markets. India has one of the world’s largest domestic markets.  Huge quantities of cereals, pulses, their products, fruits and 
vegetables, seafoods, and spices are procur ed, stored, and distributed throughout the length and breadth of the country. For 
domestic consumption radiation processing can be used for stor age of bulk commodity for primary processing, and packaged 
commodity for retail distribution and stocking. In order to fully realize the potential of radiation technology a change in 
conventional packaging and storage and practices is required. If we were to process even a fraction of our total production of the 
food commodities by radiation, it will require several new facilities to come up in future. 
 The department is encouraging private entrepreneurs for se tting up radiation processing facilities. The Board of Radiation 
and Isotope Technology has signed more than 24 MoUs with entrepreneurs, both in private and co-operative sectors, for setting 
up radiation processing plants.  
 Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology a constituent unit of the Department at Indore has taken an initiative in 
setting up a radiation facility to irradiate the agriculture produc ts of the local farmers in the Sabzi Mandi area of Indore. O nce 
the techno-economic viability of radiation processing of agricu ltural products by electron ac celerators is proven and the 
technology is accepted by the society, it will play a significant role in economy of our country. 
 The expanding activities as briefed above in the field of  agriculture will benefit the common man and farmers of our 
country. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses its satisfaction at the fact that 39 new crop variet ies have been released for commercial

 The Committee also expresses satisfac tion at the commercial food irradiatio n techniques which have resulted in

Recommendation (Paras 12.2, 12.3, 12.4 and 12.5) 
 The Committee notes that the Atomic Minerals Directorate (AMD) has been able to establish only 34,080 tonnes of 
uranium resource as against the target of 75,000 tonnes for th e Eleventh Five Year Plan period. The Committee expresses its 
concern over the fact that even after three years of the Eleventh Five Year Plan have lapsed, the Department has not been able to 
harness even half of the uranium resources targeted durin g the Eleventh Five Year Pl an. The Committee expresses its 
apprehension over the successful achievement of the set target . The Committee therefore, recommends that the obstacles 
coming in the way of achievements of the target set for the Eleventh Plan period need to be overcome in right earnest so that our 
dependence on imported fuel could be minimized.  
 The Committee has been given to understand that fuel shortage is coming in the way of generation of nuclear energy. 
Percentage of capacity utilisation of installed reactors has consistently gone down from 90 per cent in 2002-03 to 50 per cent in 
2008-09. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that to tide over this situation, the Department must do its utmost to 
procure uranium from indigenous, as well as, foreign sources. 

 The Committee takes note of the fact that despite sufficient allocation made during the Tenth Five Year Plan for 
expanding uranium mining activities under minerals sector, the Department could although have achieved reasonable success in 
areas where mining activities are already going on but it failed to achieve any major success at green field sites. The Committee 
has been given to understand that the main reason behind not achieving the desired success has been opposition to setting up 
uranium mines/milling activities from the local public. The Committee was also informed that uranium ore milling project at 
Turamdin was affected due to land dispute of tailing pond and water pipeline, while uranium ore mining and milling project at 
Lambapur and one at Kylleng Pyndengshohiong, Mawthaban are waiting mining lease from the State Governments of Andhra 
Pradesh and Meghalaya respectively. 
 While the Committee appreciates the fact that the Depart ment is engaged in sorting out the problems, however, it 
recommend to get these matters resolved at  the earliest because uraniu m resource is vital for the expansion of nuclear power 
programme. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Department is making its efforts to increase availability  of Uranium from indigenous service and also from foreign 
sources. The Uranium exploration activity in the country has been enhanced and is adequately supported by higher plan 
allocation. Several Inter Governmental agreements for cooperation in peaceful uses of atomic energy have been signed and 
these have enabled India to procure Uranium from foreign sources. Department is opening up new mines and efforts are on to 
overcome problems, including those arising out of public perception. 
 Atomic Minerals Directorate has established a total of 40,041 tonnes of  in-situ uranium resources which is 53% of XI 
Plan target of 75,000 tonnes. All efforts are in hand to achieve the envisaged target of Eleventh Plan. 
 In order to maximize the production of uranium from indige nous resources, Uranium Corporation of India Limited has 
fully utilized / mobilized its resources towards undertaking new projects, expanding the capacity of operating units (wherever 
possible) and planning for several new projects which are under different stages of implementation. The new projects 
commissioned during last seven years are 1) Commissioning of Turamdih Mine in 2003 to produce 550 tpd and later on 
expanded to produce 750 tpd. 2) Commissioning of Bagjata Mine in 2008 with capacity to produce 350 tpd (@70 capacity) 3) 
Commissioning of Banduhurang Mine in 2009 with capacity to produce 3500 tpd and 4) Commissioning of Turamdih plant to 
process 3000 tpd. 
 Further, the capacity of 1) ore production of Narwapahar mine augmented from 1000 tpd to 1500 tpd and, 2) Processing 
capacity of Jaduguda plant expanded from 2090 tpd to 2500 tpd during the last five years. 
 The projects under construction and expansion in hand ar e 1) Underground uranium mine of 3000 tpd capacity and a 
processing plant of matching capacity at Tummalapalle in Andh ra Pradesh. 2) Construction of Mohuldih underground mine in 
Jharkhand. 3) Expansion of Turamdih plant from 3000 tpd to 4500 tpd processing capacity. 
 In addition, following new projects and expansion projects are under different stages of implementation. 
(a)  Expansion of ongoing project at Tummalapalle from 3000 tpd to 4500 tpd  
(b)  Setting up a new mine and plant of 500 tpd capacity at Gogi in Karnataka 
(c)  Setting up mine and plant at Lambapur-Peddagattu in Andhra Pradesh 
(d)  Setting up mine and plant at KP M in Meghalaya  
 UCIL is also in the process of evaluation and feasibility of developing resources in other parts of the country as and when 
they are explored. UCIL through its steady efforts have been  consistently easing the mismatch of indigenous uranium 
production and requirement. On completion of the above on-goin g projects, it is hoped that UCIL shall meet the entire 
indigenous requirement of uranium. 

 The progress on uranium mining projects at Turamdih (Jha rkhand), Lambapur (Andhra Pradesh) and KPM (Meghalaya) 
are affected due to various reasons such as  - antipathy of the State Government for resolving the issues of land acquisition, 
public perception on uranium mining, law-and-order problem etc. 
 In spite of the pending issue on land acquisition at Turamdih, this project has been commissioned and production is being 
streamlined. Discussions are in progress with the concerned land ow ners at Turamdih and State au thorities for early acquisition 
of remaining land for sustained production. In Meghalaya and Andhra Pradesh, UCIL has been actively pursuing the discussion 
in different platforms involving people / local bodies / State Go vernment at different stages for resolving the issues at the 
earliest and initiate the project activities at respective sites. 
 The Kylleng Pyndengsohiang project (KPM) in Meghalaya is awaiting the approval of the Cabinet. The project at 
Lambapur in Andhra Pradesh has been approved by Cabinet to be implemented in two stages. As per the Stage - I activities, 
UCIL is in the process of acquiring the required land in the area. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the action taken by the Department, however, it is of the opinion that the Department should 
endeavor to increase its uranium explorat ion and production by amicably settlin g the issues regarding pending projects 
with the State Governments and at the sa me time try to dispel the wrong notion  about nuclear power from the mind of 
the general public, so that the country is able to achieve its target regarding Nuclear Energy Production. 
Recommendation (Para 13.2) 
 The Committee while acknowledging the need of the department to increase its Uranium sources through development of 
new mines, would like to stress upon the importance of avoiding the resulting health hazards and maintaining the ecology and 
environment of the areas near the mines. The Committee is also aware that in some regions of the country Uranium prospecting 
has led to public unrest due to resulting  health hazards and displacement of people. The Committee feels that the department 
should take preventive steps to ensure that there is no adverse effect due to the mining operations. Before mining operation 
starts the department should make consistent efforts for winning over the confidence of public by making aware of the factual 
information to all stakeholders of the project through extens ive public awareness programmes. The department should also 
ensure proper compensation for the displaced people to avoid public discontentment at a later stage and also undertake welfare 
measure programmes in the entire area by opening schools, hospitals, etc. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Uranium Corporation India Limited maintains a high standard of health safety and environmental standards around all its 
units. It is accredited with 1S0 9001:2008, ISO 14001:2004 and IS 180001:2007 certifications for quality assurance, 
environmental management system and occupational health and safety management system respectively. Well-equipped 
Environmental Survey laboratory are in operation at all its units to regularly monitor the status of different environmental 
matrices over an area of 20 km radius. Hospitals with many advanced facilities and equipment are maintained at company’s 
premises and are also extended to local population. The medical team regularly visits the near by villages providing medical 
assistance. Health study and demographic surveys, are carried out in the area involving eminent scientists, professionals, 
doctors, professors etc. No health effect due to uranium mini ng has been substantiated in any of UCIL units. Various welfare 
activities like education, health care, infrastructure developmen t, promotion of sports, cultural programme etc. are undertaken  
by UCIL around its units, specially intended towards the up-lif tment of local people. Operating Rural Medical centre for BPL 
people, Industrial training centre for local unemployed youth etc. have been opened at different units. The Department makes 
consistent efforts in different platforms to discuss with the local people on various aspects of the projects before 
implementation. Local bodies / NGOs are involved in discussion, base line data collection etc. Formal public hearing is 
conducted (mandatory) at project site to address the issues of  local people and clear the misconceptions, if any. Project 
displaced people are adequately compensated in line with the R & R policy. Some developmental works like construction of 
road, bridges etc. are taken up before start of the project in remote areas after discussion with local bodies. Welfare activities are 
also taken up considering the need of the people. For exampl e - an all weather bridge was constructed near Bagjata in 
Singhbhum before the start of the project. A 20 km road with three main bridges has been constructed as approach to the KPM 

site in Meghalaya. The Department through UCIL operates a Public Health Centre near KPM project site. Substantial assistance 
has been provided to educational institutions in the area. Seminars, workshops etc are regularly being conducted / sponsored to  
highlight the benefit of nuclear energy. Discussions are regularly being held at different platforms to clear the misgivings of  
uranium mining in Meghalaya. 
Comments 
 The Committee while appreciating the st eps taken by the Department to ensu re health safety and environmental 
conservation in the Units presently under operation reiterates its recommendation regarding the importance of avoiding 
the resulting health hazards from Uran ium mining and maintaining the ecology and environment of the areas near the 
mines. 
Recommendation (Paras 14.6, 14.7 and 14.8) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that Nu clear Energy with a huge potential is expected to play vital role in power 
generation in the country in future because it is a very clean source of energy, devoid of any environmental pollution and 
therefore recommends that conscious efforts should be made to produce the maximum amount of energy from Nuclear: sources. 
 The Committee feels that besides the present level of contribu tion of nuclear energy being dismal, the future projections, 
i.e., 6-7 per cent by 2020 and 10 per cent by 2032 are also not very encouraging. The Committee is conscious that the 
Department is also concerned about it. However, it recommends that the Department must devise some way out to enhance the 
power generation capacity. 
 The Committee is of the view that the Department of At omic Energy through its various research and development 
activities is striving very hard against all odds to uplift the standard of living in the country. The Committee is happy to no te 
that starting from a very modest beginning the Department has brought the country on the verge of becoming an export oriented 
country. Keeping in view the excellent work that the Department has done, the kind of competence it has exhibited and the kind 
of core and critical programmes it has envisaged for the future, the Committee strongly recommends that the Department of 
Atomic Energy should be properly and adequately funded and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. stage should be strictly 
avoided. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department agrees with the observations of the Committ ee that nuclear energy is a ve ry clean sources of energy. 
Efforts are being made continuously to enhance the share of nuclear energy from current level of around three per-cent 
contribution in electricity generation. Department plans to increase nuclear power plant installed capacity from 4560 MW to 
around 60 GW by 2032. This comprises of setting up of 7 GW  (10 X 700 MW) of indigenously designed Pressurised Heavy 
Water Reactors (PHWRs) which will be fuelled by indigenous Uranium; about 40 GW of Light Water Reactors (LWRs) in 
technical collaboration with foreign countries; another 7 GWe (10 X 700 MW) of indigenous PHWR which will be fuelled by 
recycled uranium pr oduced by reprocessing spent fuel from LWRs; about 3.5 GW of Fast Breeder Reactors (FBRs) using 
Mixed Oxide Fuel of indigenous origin; 300 MW Advanced H eavy Water Reactor (AHWR) using Thorium - Uranium fuel and 
about 1.6 GW of LWRs built with Indian technology. 
 As far as the funding towards the activities of the Depa rtment are concerned, the r ecommendation of the Committee has 
already been brought to the notice of Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission. The Ministry of Finance and Planning 
Commission have noted the observations and recommendations of the Committee and have assured that they would consider 
departments proposal positively at Revised Estimates Stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses satisfaction at the fact that the Ministry of Finance and Planning  Commission have 
noted the observations and recommendations of the Committee and have assured that they would consider the proposal 
of the department positively at Revised Estimates Stage. The Committee is of the opinion that a performing Department 
such as the Department of Atomic Energ y needs to be encouraged through suffi cient Budgetary support to achieve its 
pursuits in the field of nuclear energy.
