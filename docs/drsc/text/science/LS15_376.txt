INTRODUCTION 
The Department of Science & Technology (DST) is the nodal agency in the country for 
strengthening science, technology and innov ation, identifying gap areas in S&T sectors, making 
planning and policy to meet the societal needs, promoting new areas of S&T in view of future 
demands. DST also serves in connecting the science and technology sectors with different Government 
horizontals and verticals, academia, R&D labs /institutions and industries. With the objective to 
strengthen the national S&T capacity and capability, DST provides the largest extramural research and 
development support in the country to scientists cutting across institutions and disciplines through a  
competitive mode reinforcing our country’s education system, scientific and industrial R&D and the 
overall Science, Technology and Innovation landscape of the country.  
1.2 The Department has been continuing efforts towards strengthening the national STI ecosystem 
to emerge as a forerunner to play a critical role in science and technology for bringing positive 
transformations towards a safe, secure, better society, and be well prepared for future disruptions.  
1.3 The Department also administers 25 autonomous bodies and two statutory bodies, namely 
Science & Engineering Research Board (SERB) and Technology Development Board (TDB). The 
autonomous bodies include 16 research institutions, 4 specialized knowledge and S&T service 
organizations and 5 profe ssional bodies. These institutions, with long and cherished history and their 
variety of activities, occupy an important place in the S&T eco-system of the country. 
1.4 The Department -related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, 
Environment, Forests and Climate Change considered the Demands for Grants (2023 -24) of the 
Department of Science & Technology in its meeting held on 21st February, 2023.

3.  KEY ACHIEVEMENTS OF THE DEPARTMENT DURING 2022-23 
 
3.1 The Department of Science & Technology has four Central Sector Schemes/Project s, namely, 
Science and Technology Institutional and Human Capacity Building; Research and Development; 
Innovation, Technology Development and Deployment; and National Mission on Interdisciplinary 
Cyber Physical System (NM -ICPS). The Department implements its wide -ranging programmes under 
these schemes. The Department, through written replies and presentation during the meeting, submitted 
some of the key achievements across its various programmes during the year 2022-23, as below – 
- The Fund for Improvement of S&T Infrastructure in Universities and Higher Educational 
Institutions (FIST)  programme has been restructured to orient it towards  the goal of 
Atmanirbhar Bharat. During the year, 525 fresh proposals were received in seven subject areas , 
out of which 90 projects have been recommended. With support pro vided by the 15 
Sophisticated Analyti cal Instrument Facilities (SAIFs ) in the country , approximately 2400 
research papers were published and about 33,000 users across India utilized and be nefitted from 
these facilities. Under Synergistic Training program U tilizing the Scientific and Technological 
Infrastructure (STUTI) , a total of 192 training programs were organized and around 6,500 
researchers have been trained. Similarly, u nder State S&T Programme, 31 S&T Councils are 
supported currently. Two Policy Research Cells (PRC s) have also been supported and around  
19 DST-STI fellows received fellowship under the DST-STI fellowship programme.  
- Under Promotion of University Research and Scientific Excellence (PURSE) , four new 
Universities have been identified for support. The Department also announced a special call for 
the unserved regions in the country, to provide basic infrastructure for promoting R&D 
activities in new and emerging areas, in the universities of North Eastern Region, Jammu & 
Kashmir, Chhattis garh, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Bihar, Haryana, Telangana, Himachal 
Pradesh and Rajasthan. Under the special c all, 12 new universities have been identified for 
support.  
- Under Women in Science & Engineering - Knowledge Involvement in Research Advancement 
through Nurturing ( WISE-KIRAN), a total of 293 projects out of 990 proposals have been

selected in 5 different streams under Women Scientists Scheme – A (WOS -A), while under 
WOS-B grant has been released for 9 ongoing projects  and more than 30 publications  have

launched under the Women Scientists Scheme - Professional Development of Women in 
Science (WISE  - PDF). VigyanJyoti programme, which aims to encourage girls to pursu e

- Two national missions on climate change viz., National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for 
Climate Change (NMSKCC) and National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem 
(NMSHE) are being implemented  under the Climate Change Programme . Four new State 
Climate Change Cells (SCCCs) have been established, each in Goa, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh 
and Chandigarh (UT) resulting in the presence of SCCCs in 28 states/UTs across the country. 
- Under National Supercomputing Mission (NSM), five new installations of supercomputers have 
been successfully completed at IIT Roorkee, NIT Trichy, IIT Gandhinagar, IIT Guwahati, IIT 
Mandi. So far, 28 supercomputers have been installed while a n indigenously developed Rudra 
server is getting ready for use. 
- Under Technology Development Programme, around 170 project proposals were received 
during 2022, out of which 7 were sanctioned.  Several new dimensions were added to the Clean 
Energy Research Initiative (CERI) programme to  accelerate the pace of clean innovations to 
meet national needs. Similarly, several projects are being implemented to address water related 
issues through the Water Technology Initiative. 
- Under National Science & Technology Entrepreneurship Development Board (NSTEDB ), a 
new program called National Initiative for Developing and Harnessing Innovations (NIDHI) – 
Inclusive Technology Business Incubators (i-TBI) has been initiated to ensure inclusiveness of 
innovation and entrepreneurship across the country and to foster innovation and startup culture. 
A total of 30 institutions have been recommended for support  and 13 new i -TBIs have been 
established. A new Centre of Excellences ( CoE) at T -Hub, Hyderabad, Telangana has been 
established resulting in 8 CoEs under active support.  
- Several initiatives were taken up under Science for Equity for Empowerment and Development 
(SEED), such as Strengthening, Upscaling &  Nurturing Innovations for Livelihood (SUNIL), 
Scheme for Young Scientists and Technologists (SYST), Technology Intervention for Disabled 
and Elderly (TIDE) and S&T for Women. Over 75,300 students, 2,770 teachers and 330 schools 
benefited from the unique i nitiative 'Mobile Science Exhibition’ over its three phases across 
Gujarat. 
- Technical Research Centres (TRCs ) supported in 5 DST institutions since 2015 -16, have 
developed and transferred some significant technologies to industry during th e year – one such 
key achievement is  the Technology License Agreement between JNCASR and M/s. Hamsa 
Biopharma India Pvt. Ltd, New Delhi, which was entered on 28 th March 2022 for development 
a molecule (of TGR63) to effectively treat or prevent Alzheimer’s disease. 
- Several projects have been carried out under the National Geospatial Programme (NGP) 
programme. A National Network Programme involving three concerned Ministries and several 
partnering institutions has been developed, with the objective of exploring the fe asibility of 
monitoring total water storage variations by using data from the Gravity Recovery and Climat e 
Experiment (GRACE) satellite.  11 out of 140 R&D proposals received were supported. India 
hosted the Second United Nations World Geospatial Information Congress (UNWGIC) at 
Hyderabad from 10th to 14th October, 2022.

‘Visible Emission Line Coronagraph’ (VELC), one of the major space instrument s for Aditya –

time.  
- Pilot projects on banana fibre extraction and complete utilisation of banana pseudo stem were

Application & Reach (NECTAR), besides developing various products like handicrafts, banana

Base (HRNTDB) for the entire country, besides successfully carrying out drone survey of rural

‘Record of Rights’ to villagers. A Large Scale Mapping on 1:500 scale has been completed for 
several states and UTs, and is being carried out for Haryana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra 
Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands. A t otal number of 815 Continuously Operating 
Reference Stations (CORS) have been established covering about 80% of the country. 
- The Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB) has come up with several innovative 
programmes and schemes to identify potential scientists and support them for undertaking R&D 
in frontier areas of science and e ngineering. One of these is a Special Call on Wearable 
Electronics for Biomedical Applications. A new programme called State University Research 
Excellence (SERB -SURE) has been launched to augment the research capabilities in a 
structured way to create a robust R&D ecosystem in state universities and colleges . Similarly, 
Technology Development Board (TDB)  has signed 9 agreements for providing financial 
support to various industrial concerns, during the year. 
3.2 The Committee notes the achievements of the Department across its various schemes and 
programmes during the year, and appreciates its varied contributions for furthering scientific 
and technological progress of the country. The Committee desires that the Department should 
focus its resources on more projects and programmes which align with the nation al priorities. 
The Committee further desires that year -wise information/data pertaining to the targets and 
achievements in respect of each major head and the various programmes under them, should be 
maintained in a consolidated manner and updated whenever required, so as to enable a 
wholesome assessment of their performance across periods. 
4. NATIONAL MISSION ON INTERDISCIPLINARY CYBER -PHYSICAL SYSTEMS 
(NM-ICPS)

7. NATIONAL RESEARCH FOUNDATION 
7.1 On scrutiny of budgetary allocations of the Department for the financially year 2023 -24, it is 
observed that Rs. 2000 crore, i.e., almost 25 per  cent, has been earmarked for the head ‘National 
Research Foundation (NRF)’. The Committee sought details about the aims, objective and activities 
being planned under this head. In a written reply, the Department submitted that NRF is envisioned as a 
body with a mandate to set the overall research agenda at the national level and it will seed, grow and 
promote research and development (R&D) and foster a culture of research and innovation throughout 
India’s universities, colleges, research institutions and R&D laboratories. In addition, NRF will create a 
vibrant R&D framework for industries and other organizatio ns to work closely with academic and 
research institutions. Its objectives and goals include: 
- funding of competitive peer -reviewed grant proposals to indivi duals or groups of individuals 
across all S&T disciplines in all types of institutions;  
- assist in setting up research infrastructure and environment for scientific pursuit;  
- increase India’s role and participation in key areas of national and global importance;  
- coordinate across Ministries, State Governments, Industries, etc. to document and analyse 
expenditure on science research as well as their outcomes during each financial year; and 
- serve as a high -level think tank for the coordination and short -and long -term planning of 
research in the country  
 
7.2 The Department further submitted that NRF has a broad-based strategy to enlarge the scope of 
research funding and promote/facilitate growth in research in academic institutions across the country. 
Seeding research at State Universities and colleges at rural settings has been given top priority. 
Research Mentors (in the form of ‘NRF Professorships’) at Universities and Colleges where research is 
currently in a nascent stage, particularly State Universities, will be created by utilizing the expertise of 
serving or retired faculty. Bringing in young research talent will be key to developing research cultures 
at educational institutions. For this purpose, NRF will launch a large and prestigious system of 
postdoctoral fellowships to be used in our academic systems. Creating Centers of Excellence (CoE) & 
National Mission Projects are some other modes to develop and support areas o f  critical national 
importance, such as A rtificial Intelligence (AI) , Machine Learning, Environmental Science, Climate 
Change etc. Many of these  Centres will aim to work in an interdi sciplinary manner, while d edicated 
R&D platforms will be created in partnership with industry to support and improve linkages between 
R&D, academia and industry. 
7.3 The Department informed that the following programmes will be initiated during 2023-24: 
(i) Seeding Research at State Universities through NRF Professorships: One of the foremost aims 
of the NRF will be to seed research at Universities and Colleges where research is currently 
non-existent or in nascent stages, particularly State Universities, whe re over 90% of the 
country’s faculty and students work and study. 
(ii) Postdoctoral Fellowships: The NRF will initially select and fund Postdoctoral Fellowships to 
outstanding researchers across S&T fields. 
(iii) Centers of Excellence (CoE) & National Mission Projects: Each year, proposals will be invited 
for establishing Centres of Excellence/ National Mission Projects at academic institutions and 
National Labs in areas of critical national importance, such as Machine Learning, 
Environmental Science, etc. Many of these Centres will aim to work in an interdisciplinary 
manner. 
(iv) Funding of Grants: A major goal of the NRF will be to fund competent people with good ideas, 

13 
 
across fields, wherever they may be located. Proposals will be invited by the NRF from 
individuals and groups of individuals across institutions. The aim will be to increase the 
resource absorption capacity for high-quality research in the nation, over a period of time. 
7.4 Both domestic and International Projects will be undertaken under the Grant sy stem. Broadly 
these projects would have defining characteristics such as individual centric, competitive funding; high 
priority research around established research groups with complementary experti se from different 
departments/institutes; t ransformative a nd disruptive research concepts based on innovative and 
unproven hypothesis, possessing a high degree of uncertainty, yet having conviction to produce a 
lasting impact across discipline boundaries ; gender parity oriented; equity and e mpowerment focused; 
thematically oriented ; and State University/ College centric promotional competitive projects . In 
addition, there will be special window for addressing and funding industrial research engagement. 
Performing scientists will be recognized through National Fellowships. 
7.5  The Department submitted that the tentative physical targets for financial year 2023 -24 include 
- 10 NRF Professorships; 500 Postdoctoral Fellowships; a minimum of one CoE & National Mission 
Project; competitive funding of 1000 projects; Early  Career Research Support to 500 regular 
faculty/scientists; sanctioning of Homecoming Fellowship to  100 scientists from abroad; 100 Science 
Excellence Fellowships; 300 gender parity oriented programmes; 100 Special calls; and 50 Priority 
Research projects. 
7.6 The Committee desired to know as to what would be the nature of relationship between the 
Department of Science & Technology  and NRF, as also of other bodies under the Department such as 
the Technology Development Board (TDB) and Science and Engineeri ng Research Board (SERB) . In 
its response, the Department informed that NRF will be established as a statutory body through an Act 
of Parliament. While NRF will serve as a high-level think tank for coordination and short and long -
term planning of research in the country, funding of grant proposals to individuals, groups or 
institutions across all S&T disciplines in all types of institutions will be synergized with the EMR 
funding schemes of the Department . Further, considering that NRF has an expanded mandate covering 
activities over and above the activities of SERB, it is proposed SERB will be subsumed in NRF. 
7.7 The Committee takes due note of the visions, objectives and purpose of creation of the 
National Research Foundation. It believes that a cent rally established NRF that transparently 
seeds, funds, and coordinates research across institutions in the country - in all disciplines, in an 
integrated manner, with a special mandate to foster research and innovation in universities and 
colleges, includi ng inter -disciplinary research to provide practical solutions to societal, 
developmental, financial, techno -economic challenges of the nation,  is a much needed 
overarching mechanism. The Committee recommends that , in the process of implementing this 
programme, special consideration should be given towards under-funded as well as new and 
emerging areas of research, while also keeping under-served regions of the country in mind.
