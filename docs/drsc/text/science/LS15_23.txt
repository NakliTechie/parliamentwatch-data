REPORT
The Space programme in India which started modestly three and a half decades ago has now moved in a major way in the direction of
 developing of space applications for peaceful purposes, particularly cclating to telecommunications, remote sensing and radio/rV
 broad­casting, the technology for the design, fabrication and full utilisation of appropriate satellite systems needed for this and in rocket
 technology for launching these satellites.
2. The success of ASLV-D4ISROSS-C2 and PSLV-D2lIRS-P2 Missions in 1994 has amply demonstrated that the Indian Space
 programme is, indeed, marching towards self­reliance. Large components of IRS, INSAT, ASLV and PSLV programmes are being realised
 in a self-reliant manner. The Department of Space (DOS) has made considerable efforts to indigenise critical and sensitive technologies. The
 work on the development of the cryogenic upper stage for the GSL V on an indigenous basis, in addition to the procurement of a few stages
 from Russia is an example of the efforts being made by DOS towards achieving self-reliance in every sphere of its space activities.
3. The Secretary, Department of Space, in his presentation before the Committee, gave a brief account of the progress of the Indian Space
 Programme during the year 1994-95, and referred to the significant achievements of the ASLV-D4/SROSS-C2 Mission and PSLV-D2I IRS-
P2 Mission. The fourth development flight of ASL V was carried out successfully on May 4, 1994. SROSS-C2 satellite carried a Gamma Ray
 Burst Detector and a Retarding Potential Analyser and both these payloads have been functioning successfully. The Secretary informed the
 Committee that the ASL V Mission had the major objective of developing and proving certain important technologies which have relevance
 to PSL V and GSLV. The development of PSLV was initiated in 1982. The first development flight of PSLV which took place on September
 20, 1993 became sub-orbital and could not place IRS­IE Satellite into final orbit, though all the major rocket motors worked perfectly well
 in this case. PSLV-D2, the second development flight, lifted off on October 15, 1994 and successfully placed IRS-P2 remote sensing satellite
 in the desired orbit. PSL-D2, besides being successful, has also proved Indian capability for putting IRS class satellites of one tonne capacity
 into Polar orbit. IRS-P2 is giving fully satisfactory service. The Secretary, DOS informed that along with IRS-1A and 1B, IRS-P2 forms a
 unique constellation of these satellites for our remote sensing work. He also mentioned that IRS-IA has successfully completed seven years of
 its operation. The PSL V Missions have proved many new major technologies. One of the important outcomes of this programme has been
 the successful flight testing of the solid booster and liquid stage which are used for the. first two stages of GSLV. GSLV would be built by
 maximally utilising the modules developed for PSLV. It is configured by replacing the upper two stages of PSL V by a single cryogenic stage
 and the six solid strap-on motors by four liquid strapons derived from the PSL V second stage. GSLV will be capable of placing a 2,500 Kg.
 INSA T-2 class satellite into geosynchronous transfer orbit.
4. As regards programmes for the year 1995-96, the Committee was told that DOS is currently working on IRS-IC and ID. These
 satellites will have better spatial coverage, extended spectral bands with revisit capability, stereo viewing and onboard data recording
 facility. IRS-IC is scheduled for launch during the last quarter of 1995 and IRS-ID would be launched during 1997-98. The launch of IRS-P3
 onboard PSLV-D3 is scheduled during 1995­96. The launch of INSAT-2C is slated for 1995. It will be carrying three Ku band
 transponders, a mobile satellite service payload, expanded coverage of two normal C-band transponders, and seven normal C-band
 transponders with higher power in place of the meteorological and search and rescue payloads and one S-band TV transponder. INSAT-2C
 is being configured with double sided solar array to derive more power. The Secretary, DOS also briefly referred to the recommendations of
 this Committee made in its Fifteenth Report on remote sensing operations of DOS, and informed the Committee that the Department is
 trying to improve its capabilities in the agricultural yield prediction. He also informed the Committee about the Agro-aimatic Planning and
 Information Bank, the request for the setting up of which had come from the Planning Commission. The concept is to integrate information
 that is available through the remote sensing system, particularly on the Integrated Mission for Sustainable Development (IMSD). This
 Information Bank at a pilot stage will initially cover about 17 to 18 districts of Karnataka. Under IMSD programme, DOS has initiated
 remote sensing-based studies for sustainble development at micro-level. Under this programme 174 districts in the country have been
 identified and on the basis of detailed review 92 priority blocks have been identified by the Ministry of Rural Develop­ment for which
 action plans are being generated.

10.1 The Secretary, DOS informed the Committee that the Department follows a rigorous and systematic process for the formulation of
 the annual budget using the concept of Zero Base Budgetina. Detailed reviews are held. at various levels to arrive at the minimum essential
 flow for each project programme. The annual cash flow requirement of any project follows a pattern where the cash flow picks up slowly in
 the initial years, followed by larger requirement in the subsequent years and then tapers off towards the end of the project life. The
 Department also' looks at the total amount available to it Plan + Non-Plan, though for purposes of presentations, to conform t) standard
 Government practice, it shows its requirements under Plan and Non-Plan separately. As a project near completion, staff, space, and where
 possible equipment, is diverted to new projects that are starting up so that the requirements on the new projects get reduced.
11. The Committee wanted to know about the economics of the satellite programme in the field of communications and telecast. The
 Secretary, DOS informed the Committee that the expenditure towards the Space segment and its operation is included in the annual budget of
 DOS. The expenditure towards the ground segment is included in the budget estimates of the respective Ministries departments. The
 Government as a whole earns about Rs. 400 crores per year from the INSA T System. This revenue which can be attributed to the Space
 segment falls into four broad categories, namely, revenue earned by DOT by routing calls-Data through INSA T; revenue earned by DOT by
 leasing capacity to various closed user and private networks; share of Doordarshan advertisement revenues attributable to the space segment;
 and savinptevenue to AIR because of the executive satellite based radio networking. This apart, there are also large' non-quantified social
 benefits due to improved communications and educational broadcast. The benefits accruing due to improvement in meteorological services, if
 quantified, would also be substantial. It was made clear to the Committee that if lease charges of the total number of transponders in the
 INSAT system were to be computed on the basis of the prevailing international rates, the annual revenue would be to the tune of Rs. 200
 crores.
11.1 The Committee is happy to note that economic returns from the INSA T system are substantial. Large non-quantified local benefits,
 and Indirect benefits which result due to improves meteorological forecast, further underscore the importance of this programme.

 intensive interactive courses have given good inputs for the definition of the pilot project for GRAMSAT. The Committee was told that due
 to the complex nature of the issues involved and the preparations required, a full definition of the GRAMSA T system could be achieved in
 about two years .
