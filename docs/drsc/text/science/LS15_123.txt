 R E P O R T
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Space on the recommendations
 contained in the One Hundred-fourteenth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science &
 Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2003-2004) of the Department of Space which was presented
 to both Houses of Parliament on 2 nd May, 2003.  There were twenty-one recommendations contained in One Hundred-
fourteenth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the
 Department on 5th September, 2003.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the              Committee on the action taken by
 the Department of Space are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
INSAT SERIES
Recommendation
The Committee congratulates the Department for the successful launch of the INSAT-3A and records its appreciation for
 the abilities and talents of our scientists who have further enhanced the glory of our nation through this splendid performance.
 The Committee hopes that the other satellites in the INSAT series would be launched as planned without any cost escalation.
(Para 24)
Action Taken by the Department
After the launch of INSAT-3A on April 10, 2003, the spacecraft has been commissioned in orbit and put to operational
 use. The next satellite, INSAT-3E, is also now ready for launch. The spacecraft has been transported to the launch site French
 Guyana and the launch is scheduled for the third quarter of 2003. The work on INSAT -3D, an advanced meteorological
 satellite and also the last satellite in the INSAT -3 series, is in progress and the launch is targeted for 2004-05 onboard India's
 GSLV. The work on fourth generation INSAT-4A & 4B satellites has also begun. No cost escalation is foreseen in INSAT
 satellites at present.
 
Also, GSAT-2, an experimental communication satellite, was successfully launched on the second developmental flight
 of GSLV (GSLV-D2) on May 8, 2003 from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota. GSAT-2 has augmented the INSAT
 capacity with four C-band, two Ku band and One Mobile Satellite Service transponders.
Comments
The Committee congratulates the Department of Space and scientists of ISRO for the successful launch of GSAT-2 and
 expresses its best wishes for the success of all the future programmes.
DEVELOPMENT OF CRYOGENIC ENGINES
Proven abilities of our scientists have now to be used to develop the cryogenic technology which has been subjected to
 many experiments. The Committee feels that the indigenous development of cryogenic engines is critical to the development of
 our own geostationary satellite launch vehicles. It express full faith on our space scientists and hopes that they will accomplish
 the mission.

Action Taken by the Department
ISRO has made considerable progress related to the development of our indigenous cryogenic stage for use on Geo-
synchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV). The first two-engine hardware have undergone a total cumulative ground hot test
 firing duration of 4000 seconds. These tests have given confidence for the normal performance of the engine. Preparation for
 the stage test is in progress and stage tankages have been moved to the test stand for various tests before conducting the first hot
 test planned this year. The first set of flight version tanks have been realized. Based on a success-oriented schedule, it is planned
 to make a test flight of the indigenous cryogenic stage during 2004-05. The work on development of a higher thrust cryogenic
 engine (C-25) has also been initiated as a part of GSLV Mk III project.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the progress in the development of the indigenous cryogenic engines. However, it hopes that
 the progress in the development of higher thrust cryogenic engines (C-25) would be initiated as per schedule so that the projects
 like Mission to Moon, envisaged with cryogenic engines are not delayed.
GRAMSAT PILOT PROJECTS (GPP)
Recommendation
The Committee hopes that by the end of the 10
th Plan period the GRAMSAT programme would cover all the rural areas
 of India. The Committee also hopes that local bodies, district offices and all those involved in planning at the rural level would
 be given the data for their use. Since we have constitutionally guaranteed 33% reservation for women in Panchayat bodies, the
 Committee recommends that special focus should be given to women's development issues through GRAMSAT. Lest all this
 effort goes waste the Committee strongly urges the government to launch a scheme to equip every village of the country with
 the equipment necessary to utilize the service that will be given through GRAMSAT.
(Para 38)
Action Taken by the Department
GRAMSAT programme envisages provision of satellite transponders for developmental communications including
 education and training, health, women & child development, Panchayati Raj functionaries etc. Under GRAMSAT Pilot Projects,
 such networks have already been established in Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Goa and
 Rajasthan. The developmental programmes broadcasted under the network by State Governments address the women
 development issues also. In fact, the impact evaluation of the GRAMSAT network in Jhabua district of Madhya Pradesh
 indicates that the backward and tribal communities of the region, especially women are the main beneficiaries of the
 GRAMSAT programme. Work has already been initiated to build a satellite - EDUSAT (GSAT-3) -dedicated for spreading
 education and training at all levels covering a large area of the country including North East through a satellite based network.
Comments
The Committee observes that steps may be taken to cover all the states, particularly the hill states by the next phase of
 GRAMSAT project and hopes that EDUSAT satellite would cover wider regions.
LAUNCH VEHICLE INFRASTRUCTURE
Recommendation
The Committee commends the Department for making significant progress in completion of second launch pad and
 hopes that it would be ready for commissioning by the end of this year without any further delays.
(Para 40)
Action Taken by the Department
The Second Launch Pad is now ready to receive PSLV and the activities related to mock-up trials have been initiated.
 The Second Launch pad will be ready to receive     GSLV by end of the year after completing the installation and
 commissioning of           Cryo processing facilities.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the second launch pad would reduce our  dependence on foreign launching pads and besides,
 India could  offer its launching services to other countries.

SPONSORED RESEARCH
Recommendation
The committee recommends that apart from initiating the research projects in the academic institutions, the Department
 should develop a mechanism for constant monitoring of the performance of the projects to ensure their timely completion.
(Para 56)
Action Taken by the Department
The ISRO Sponsored Research Projects (RESPOND) are carried out at the Academic Institutions such as Universities,
 Colleges, National Institutions, IITs, Research laboratories under the supervision of experts at ISRO Centres. The Department
 has established a well-structured review system as below to monitor the progress of these research projects.
(a)        Each project has been assigned with a Focal Point (Expert) from the ISRO Centre who will interact with the
 project on a timely basis to ensure smooth progress of the project work. At the end of every year, the project
 submits an annual progress report which is reviewed by the Focal Point and other experts at ISRO Centres before
 recommending further renewal (continuation) of the project and release of grants for the next year.
(b)        Since last 5 years, ISRO has initiated an additional review mechanism, the Annual RESPOND Review meetings
 at ISRO Centres, where an exclusive ISRO Committee constituted and chaired by Director of each ISRO Centre
 will review the progress of the Projects and make suitable suggestions and mid-course corrections on the project
 work to ensure satisfactory completion of the project within the project period.
Comments
The Committee notes with satisfaction the initiatives taken by the Department for  review of the progress of  sponsored
 research projects.
 
TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER
Recommendation
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department for transfer of technologies to the Industry; however, the
 Committee recommends that the Department should acquire patents of the technologies transferred to prevent exploitation. The
 Department should assess such technology transfers from the commercial point of view and generate the maximum revenue out
 of it. The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department is bringing in more and more participation from industry in
 conducting the space programme. The Committee hopes that through greater transfer of technology, participation of industry
 will be further broadened.
(Para 62)
Action Taken by the Department
ISRO/DOS has been pursuing a Technology Transfer (TT) policy that has enabled the commercialization of ISRO
 developed technologies through Indian industries. Adequate measures are being taken to safeguard ISRO's interest through
 filing of appropriate patents, copyrights and trademarks. The technology transfer mechanism established by ISRO has resulted
 in more than 255 technologies being  licensed to the Indian industries in the small, medium and large scale sectors. Efforts are
 being made to widen the avenues and scope for industry participation with a view to maximally utilize the industrial capability
 and expertise.

INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION
Marketing of Space Services & Products
Recommendation
The Committee feels that India's expertise in space technology can be further shared with many other developing
 countries. The Department should explore opportunities for launching of more satellites and marketing of remote sensing data to
 many other countries.
(Para. 73)
Action Taken by the Department
ISRO has set up a Scheme "Sharing of Experience in Space (SHARES)" under which training in different applications of
 space science, technology and applications is provided to scientists from developing countries. Another important initiative in
 this direction is the establishment of the Centre for Space Science and Technology Education for Asia and the Pacific (CSSTE-
AP) affiliated to United Nations which offers specialized training in Remote Sensing & Geographical Information System,
 Satellite Communications, Satellite Meteorology, Global climate & Space / Atmospheric Studies and more than 300 scholars
 from about 40 developing countries have been benefited from this Centre.
The Department through Antrix Corporation (ANTRIX), the commercial wing of the Department of Space has mounted
 efforts towards promoting the market for launch services and marketing of remote sensing data to global customers. Concerted
 efforts have been made by contacting a number of potential customers in Europe (Germany, Sweden, Italy etc.), Canada etc., for
 provision of launch services, though the geo-political constraints play a dominant role in the global launch services market. On
 the marketing of remote sensing data, discussions have been held with international customers in South Africa, Yemen, Ecuador
 and Algeria etc., for the reception and dissemination of IRS data. Efforts are also on to provide value added services to
 Mauritius in the area of land Information System (LIS). Preliminary proposals have been submitted to the customer in this
 regard.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Development of low cost technologies 
Recommendation
The Committee feels that intense competition is on to capture the limited market of space industry and India can innovate
 design concepts for small as well as large payloads and by doing so can offer competitive prices to the interested countries for
 availing of India's technologies. The Committee recommends that the Department may explore the possibility of developing low
 cost space technologies which would be useful to developing countries.
(Para 74)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department with its unique expertise in space application, spacecraft and launching has been exploring the
 possibility of end-to-end services, which can be useful to developing nations.
With the GSLV capability improving to launch satellites of weight higher than 2000 kg; Department of Space is planning
 to launch satellites compatible with indigenous launchers and catering to the emerging markets such as DTH, TV broadcasting,
 long distance thin route communications, Ku-band satellites with multiple beams, Ka- band Regenerative payload etc. are being
 planned to develop low-cost space technologies suited to developing countries, Discussions are under way for providing
 satellite based communication systems for Vietnam, Malaysia etc.
Further, the concept of developing micro-satellites with appropriate payloads has been developed along with the Anna
 University, which also provides opportunities to develop low cost based technologies that could be of relevance and use to the
 developing countries.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

Telemedicine Kiosks
Recommendation
The Committee also hopes that in the next few years there will be telemedicine kiosks through the length and breadth of
 suburban and rural India. It is hoped that the government scheme to provide such kiosks will go hand in hand with the spread of
 telemedicine facility.
(Para 78)
Action Taken by the Department
The telemedicine technology demonstration projects have been operating in the State of Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh,
 Kerala, UT of Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands, Jammu & Kashmir including Ladakh, Assam, Sikkim and
 Tripura etc., connecting 27 hospitals (10 super speciality hospitals, 17 district/medical college hospitals) providing specialist
 consultation to remote hospitals located at rural and inaccessible areas.
The telemedicine system consists of customized medical software integrated with the diagnostic instruments like EGG,
 X-ray scanner which will be connected to VSAT for connectivity with the satellite. Similar VSAT and the medical software
 exists at the specialist end for viewing and analysis of the medical information. The tele-consultation and treatment are provided
 during the video conferencing between the patient end hospital and the specialist end.
The concept of a Tele-medicine Kiosk co-located with Information Kiosks can provide only limited Tele-medicine
 services out of different alternatives. Appropriate option in terms of technology, location, etc., which would maximize the Tele-
 medicine benefits to the remote areas will have to be adopted by implementing agencies.
Comments 
The Committee appreciates the Department’s projects under telemedicine system and feels that by expanding
 telemedicine facilities the objective of access to health care could be realized. The big hospitals in the States of Uttar Pradesh,
 Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Orissa and Bihar should also be linked with telemedicine projects as maximum poor people are
 living in these States.
 
 
DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT (DMS)
Recommendation
The committee feels that the remote sensing data is not being used to its full potential. With regard, to the landslide
 hazard zonation, the Committee feels that standardized landslide inventory maps would be helpful. Besides, accurate slop
 stability studies may be made by using space technology so as to understand the possible locations where landslides could
 occur. The Committee feels that special attention should be given to Northeast and Uttaranchal where landslides during rainy
 season block roads for days together. An Internet image transferring system may be of help for local bodies and relief agencies
 for emergency response in affected areas.
(Para 87)
Action Taken by the Department
Remote sensing data usage is coordinated under the auspices of the National Natural Resources Management System
 (NNRMS) with active involvement of various Central/State agencies as well as private entrepreneurs and academia. India is one
 of the very few countries in the world, which has effectively integrated remote sensing applications with developmental
 planning at various levels. However, considering that it is still a continuing process and there are newer applications and
 services in the light of advancement in technologies, the Department continues to spread the awareness through appropriate
 training and education programmes, which should enable further enhancement of remote sensing data usage in the future.
Initiatives have already been taken on landslide hazard zonation studies in the North East and the Uttaranchal. A network
 based information transfer and access system towards helping local bodies and relief agencies for emergency response in
 affected areas is being worked out.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

 NATIONAL NATURAL RESOURCES MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (NNRMS)
Recommendation
The Committee is of the opinion that a periodic census of the available natural resources would help assessing the
 utilization of such resources so as to formulate a plan for sustainable development.
(Para 91)
Action Taken by the Department
The NNRMS has identified the conduct of a Natural Resource Census (NRC) in the country as a major national activity
 from 10th Plan onwards. As pre-investment activity for the National Mission of NRC, a proto-type exercise has been taken up
 in 12 districts of the country. The Proto-type will generate national mapping standards for NRC and also help operationalise the
 mechanisms of the national mission. After completing the Proto-type activity during the year, the national programme on NRC
 will be initiated.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the National Mission on NRC would be taken up in all the districts of the country so that
 realistic census on the natural resources is drawn up for effective development planning.
NORTH EAST SPACE APPLICATION CENTRE
Recommendation
The Committee appreciates the Department for undertaking projects in the Northeast, which merits concentrated
 attention. The Committee hopes that bamboo resource mapping, erosion hazard assessment etc. would cover the whole of the
 Northeast so that the data could be used for meaningful planning for development.
(Para 93)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department through its North Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE- SAC) has initiated many remote sensing
 application projects covering the whole North East for providing inputs for developmental planning of the region.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the inputs provided by North East Space Application Centre would help in finalizing projects
 for industrial and agricultural development of the region.
Ground Water exploration
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that India is emerging as a water stressed country with millions of its people without
 access to safe drinking water. The Committee deeply appreciates that the Department has applied Space Technology to prepare
 1500 maps in respect of six States for the purpose of ground water exploration. While the Committee notes that the Department
 is mapping for few of the States, it recommends strongly that such efforts of the Department must be extended to all States,
 giving special priority to those, which have been severely affected by drought and shortage of rainfall.
(Para 94)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department has been working with the Department of Drinking Water Supply (Ministry of Rural Development),
 Government of India towards providing inputs to the Rajiv Gandhi National Drinking Water Mission (RGNDWM) for the
 purpose of ground water exploration as well as recharge. The Department will work closely with the RGNDW to extend the
 above activities to other States as suggested.
Comments
The Committee would like the Department to evolve an effective monitoring mechanism with Department of Drinking
 Water Supply (Ministry of Rural Development) to see the programme succeeds.
National Resource Information Systems
Recommendation
The Committee notes that the Department is proposing to prepare National Resource Information System in Nine States.
 Earlier the Department had set up such a system in 30 districts in different States. The Committee recommends that such
 systems be established as soon as possible in all the States of the country to enable the Government, people and larger civil
 society to have access to such information for national development.

Recommendation
            The Committee takes note of the large-scale developmental activities undertaken by the Government agencies throughout
 the length and breadth of the country. The Committee feels that Space Technology should not only be used as an input and a
