3.  COMMISSIONING OF PROTOTYPE FAST BREEDER REACTOR 
(PFBR) 
3.1 The Committee desired to know about the current status of commissioning of 
Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR) by the Department.   In response, it was 
submitted that the Department has been very cautious and extra vigilant given the use of 
liquid sodium as coolant.  The Committee was further informed by the Secretary that the 
Department expects to complete the task by October, 2022.   
 

7 
 
3.2 The Committee is acutely conscious of the extraordinary difficulties involved 
with the use of liquid sodium as coolant. It is also conscious of the fact that the 
PFBR is a pioneering venture in the world and that it is an essential intermediate 
step for the utilisation of the country’s vast thorium reserves for power generation. 
The Committee commends the DAE for all that h as been achieved as the PFBR is 
concerned and expresses the hope that it will be commissioned according to the 
schedule indicated by the Secretary, DAE. 
4. SEWAGE SLUDGE HYGIENISATION 
4.1 On being enquired about the action taken by the Department to popularise its 
radiation technology vis -à-vis sludge hygienisation, it was informed that Ahmedabad 
Municipal Corporation plant had become operational and the Department has also 
signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Pune Municipal Corporation and 
Indore Municipal Corporation is in a advanced stage of development.   It was brought to 
the notice of the Committee that the Department has also requested the Cabinet 
Secretary in the matter.  The Secretary of the Department, during his presentation before 
the Committee,  suggested that  if a mechanism is  worked out by providing some 
financial assistance , i.e. 50 -60% of the total cost of the plant by the Central 
Government, and the rest of money from the local Municipality,  it would be a good 
solution to furthe r populari se this technology  and to address the problem of sewage 
sludge. 
4.2 The Committee appreciates the Department for developing an indigenous 
technology for hygienisation of municipal sewage sludge.   The Committee, 
however, finds it surprising that t hough this technology is scientifically sound,               
it has not been promoted to the desired level and only a handful of Municipalities 
have shown interest in the technology .  The Committee is of the opinion  that this 
technology can be used on a larger scale by the other Municipalities in the country 
to tackle the problem of sewage sludge.  The Committee is of the considered view 
that the success in Ahmedabad may  be replicated across all the major 
Municipalities in the country , in a phased manner , and the Municipalities finding 
it difficult to deal with its sewage sludge should come forward and make best use of 
this technology.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that  the Department 
should write to all State/UT Governments, apprise them the benef its and efficacy 
of this technology and ask them to impress upon all the Municipalities facing the 
problem of sewage sludge to use this technology to solve the problem faced by 
them.  
5. EXPANSION OF CANCER TREATMENT FACILITIES 
5.1 On the issue of progress  achieved by the Department in the direction of Hub & 
Spoke Model of Tata Memorial Centre, the Committee notes that TMC has now 
expanded to six other hospitals located in Varanasi (two), Guwahati, Sangru r, 
Vishakapatnam and Mullanpur.  Responding to the qu ery about setting up of cancer 
hospitals in Goa, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and West Bengal, the Department has forwarded 

8 
 
the following suggestions, b ased on evaluation using the centres under the National 
Cancer Grid, and the prevailing incidence rates of various cancers in the country:  
a) For Chhattisgarh, there is an existing charitable cancer centre at Raipur 
(BALCO Cancer Hospital) for which technical support was pr ovided by Tata 
Memorial Centre.  It might be advisable to support this and upgrade 
infrastructure/ equipment at this hospital rather than come up with a new project.  
b) For West Bengal, to address the critical need for cancer care is in the northern 
part of West B engal (Siliguri/Cooch Behar ), t here is an existing cancer centre 
(Medical Cancer Hospital) in Rangapani (outskirts of Siliguri).   The TMC  
suggests that this hospital be upgraded rather than establish a new spoke here.  
c) For Odisha, there are existing cancer centres /departments in Cut tack (Regional 
Cancer Centre) and Bhubaneswar (AIIMS); in addition, a new 500 -bed cancer 
centre is being planned at Bhuba neswar by the state government.  It is  suggested 
that this hospital can provid e technical support from TMC rather than create a 
new cancer centre in Odisha.  
d) To address the  inadequate cancer care facilities in Goa and northern 
Karnataka, the creation of a hub at Karwar (a border town between Goa and 
Karnataka) is suggested, which could then serve the population of Goa as well as 
north Karnataka.  This could be a Greenfield project of DAE/TMC.  
e) In addition, while mapping existing cancer centres in India, there is a lack of 
access for cancer care in southern Rajasthan.  It would be advisable to have a 
hub in Kota/Udaipur to cater to the popu lation in this region. This would be a 
Greenfield project of DAE/TMC.  
5.2 The Committee notes the suggestion made by the Department and is of the 
considered opinion that the existing facilities available in Chhattisgarh,                       
West Bengal and Odisha may be upgraded in terms of required 
infrastructure/equipments and human resources to enable them to function as a 
‘Hub’ for the region. This can be recommended to the state governments, with 
TMC providing the technical support. As r egards to the greenfield projects in 
northern Karnataka and southern Rajasthan, the Committee is of the view that the 
Department through TMC should approach the concerned State Governments, 
sensitise them about the efficacy of having a Hub, given the incid ence of the cancer 
in these regions, and persuade the State Governments to implement their proposal. 
Further, the Department must impress upon the Ministry of Finance to allocate 
more funds to TMC to recruit additional administrative and technical human 
resources for the smooth implementation of the proposed greenfield projects.

6. MANAGEMENT OF DISUSED SEALED RADIOACTIVE SOURCE 
6.1 In response to the query of the Committee about the management of the disused 
sealed radioactive sources, the Secretary, D epartment of Atomic Energy informed the 
Committee that some such sources are lying in the custody of some of the Government 
organisation and universities.  It was informed that the Department is willing to take 
custody of all such disused sources currently  under the custody of all these institutions, 
if they face any difficulty with regard to their management. 
6.2 The Committee underlines the safety issues related with the disused 
radioactive sources and their management and it is, therefore, important that  all 
these sources must be brought under the physical custody of the Department of 
Atomic Energy to ensure proper and scientific management.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy should undertake 
an exercise and prepare a list of the organisations/universities which are struggling 
to manage these sources first, and then start the process of taking possession of the 
same. 
6.3 The Committee reiterates the fact that the country has abundant availability 
of Thorium.  The Com mittee urged the Department to find avenues for its 
consumption in the country as a substitute to the costly imported raw materials, 
through enhanced research and development. 
6.4 The Committee also appreciates the bullet -proof jackets developed by the 
Department.  It weighs less than the imported ones i.e. 6.8 kg as compared to 11kg.  
This would result in better maneuverability/agility of the forces and is almost 50% 
cheaper compared to imported jackets.  The jackets can be exported too and can 
be a good source of revenue generation. 
7.  CONCLUDING OBSERVATIONS 
7.1    Even with the most aggressive plans for renewable energy, India will still 
need a significantly accelerated and augmented nuclear power generating 
programme.  The Committee undersco res the fact that unlike coal, nuclear power 
does not lead to emission of greenhouse gases.  By 2040 at least 10% of the 
country’s electricity supply must come from nuclear stations.  It may seem to be an 
unrealistic target but all efforts must be made to ensure that it is achieved.

should write to all State/UT Govern ments, apprise them the benefits and efficacy 
of this technology and ask them to impress upon all the Municipalities facing the 
problem of sewage sludge to use this technology to solve the problem faced by 
them.                                                                                                                  (Para 4.2) 
EXPANSION OF CANCER TREATMENT FACILITIES 
 The Committee notes the suggestion made by the Department and is of the 
considered opinion that the existing facilities available in Chhattisgarh,                       
West Bengal and Odisha may be upgraded in terms of required 
infrastructure/equipments and human reso urces to enable them to function as a 
‘Hub’ for the region. This can be recommended to the state governments, with 
TMC providing the technical support. As regards to the greenfield projects in 
northern Karnataka and southern Rajasthan, the Committee is of the view that the 
Department through TMC should approach the concerned State Governments, 
sensitise them about the efficacy of having a Hub, given the incidence of the cancer 
in these regions, and persuade the State Governments to implement their proposal. 
Further, the Department must impress upon the Ministry of Finance to allocate 
more funds to TMC to recruit additional administrative and technical human 
resources for the smooth implementation of the proposed greenfield projects. 
            (Para 5.2) 
 MANAGEMENT OF DISUSED SEALED RADIOACTIVE SOURCE 
 The Committee underlines the safety issues related with the disused 
radioactive sources and their management and it is, therefore, important that all 
these sources must be brought under the physical custody o f the Department of 
Atomic Energy to ensure proper and scientific management.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy should undertake 

12 
 
an exercise and prepare a list of the organisations/universities which are struggling 
to manage these sources first, and then start the process of taking possession of the 
same.                                                                                                                    (Para 6.2) 
The Committee reiterates the fact that the country has abundant availability 
of Thorium.  The Committee urged the Department to find avenues for its 
consumption in the country as a substitute to the costly imported raw materials, 
through enhanced research and development.                                               (Para 6.3) 
6.4 The Committee also appreciates the bullet -proof jackets developed by the 
Department.  It weighs less than the imported ones i.e. 6.8 kg as compared to 11kg.  
This would result in better maneuverability/agility of the forces and is almost 50% 
cheaper compared to imported jackets.  The jackets can be exported too and can 
be a good source of revenue generation.                                                         (Para 6.4) 
  CONCLUDING OBSERVATIONS 
Even with the most aggressive plans for renewable energy, India will still 
need a significantly accelerated and augmented nuclear power generating 
programme.  The Committee underscores the fact that unlike coal, nuclear power 
does not lead to emission of g reenhouse gases.  By 2040 at least 10% of the 
country’s electricity supply must come from nuclear stations.  It may seem to be an 
unrealistic target but all efforts must be made to ensure that it is achieved.
