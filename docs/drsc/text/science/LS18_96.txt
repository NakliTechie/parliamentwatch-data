R E P O R T
 
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science & Technology on the recommendations
 contained in the Eighty-ninth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology,
 Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Department of Science & Technology which was presented to
 the Rajya Sabha on 20th April, 2001.  There are Seventeen recommendations in the Eighty-ninth Report of the Committee. Action Taken
 notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 14th September, 2001.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the Department of
 Science and Technology are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
SCIENCE & ENGINEERING COUNCIL (SERC)
Recommendation
The Committee has been given to understand that Science & Engineering Council (SERC) is one of the major R&D
 programmes of the Department, promoting multi-disciplinary research in Science and Engineering providing support to
 individual research scientists, establishing core groups and centers of excellence and setting up national R&D facilities. Further,
 the SERC has approved a large number of important programmes requiring additional funds. In view of the above and also to
 take care of the increased cost of sophisticated equipment, foreign exchange fluctuations, costly consumables and chemicals
 etc., the Committee recommends that the allocation needs to be suitably augmented.
(Para 10) 
Action Taken by the Department
Recommendation of the Committee has been noted. Budget allocation has been augmented for SERC during the current
 financial year from Rs.69.50 crore in the Budget Estimates 2000-2001 to   Rs.87. 10 crores in the Budget Estimates 2001-2002.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply with satisfaction.
TECHNOLOGY INFORMATION, FORECASTING AND ASSESSMENT COUNCIL (TIFAC)
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that in the wake of globalization and opening up of global markets, new technologies would
 flood Indian markets. Therefore, Technology Information, Forecasting and Assessment Council (TIFAC) must strengthen its
 forecasting/assessment of technologies so as to compete with the technologies coming from abroad. Besides, the technologies
 proposed to be developed should be cost effective. Efforts should also be made to make the scientists aware of the need for
 taking patents. Knowledge about Procedures for applying a patent as also other formalities required to be completed should be
 included, if  not  already available in the Department’s web page along with facilities for downloading
 of necessary forms. The
 web site should be updated periodically. The information about the patents applied must also be made available, if not already available,
 in the web sites. Sometimes, the web sites are not easily accessible. This needs to be looked into.
(Para 13)     
Action Taken by the Department
With a view to strengthening its forecasting assessment of technologies so as to compete with the technologies coming from abroad,
 TIFAC has conducted a long term Technology Forecasting exercise. This has been brought out as Technology Vision for India upto
 2020 Documents in 25 volumes covering 17 sectors. TIFAC reports, numbering more than 250, have led to several Technology
 promotional/developmental projects viz. Advanced Composite Missions/Fly Ash Mission/Sugar Technology Mission and Leather

 Mission (implemented by CLRI). The Technology Forecasting/Assessment of technologies is being continued and several new areas are
 being covered including fertilizers, cement, waste management, chemicals etc. The Committee's recommendation that the technologies
 proposed to be developed should be cost effective has been noted.
Patent Facilitating Centers (PFC) set up by DST has been very active in creating an awareness among the Indian Scientists on the
 need for obtaining Patents and other intellectual properties rights for them inventing work. This is being done through regular
 workshops held in the country. PFC also renders technical and financial help in this regard if inventions are emanating from publicly
 funded institutions and academic institutions. PFC bring out 2 CD ROMS every quarter on Indian Patents and has also created a website
 (www. lndianpatents.org) for accessing the information on patents, forms to be filled etc. Eight Patent information centers have been set
 up in various State Government Science & Technology Departments/Councils.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
JOINT TECHNOLOGY PROGRAMME
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that given the size of population and the use of ophthalmic glass, the, country must aim to achieve self
 sufficiency in glass manufacture under vision Technology, the time schedule should strictly be adhered to and tests should be conducted
 before they are transferred to be used on patients, as the cases of failure of cataract operations have made many patients reluctant in
 adopting new techniques. Laser technologies for curing cataract and other eye disorders must be strengthened and technologies
 developed should be available at affordable prices
(Para 15)
Action Taken by the Department
Ophthalmic glass: For Refractive Error correction, two types of glasses are generally used :       (a) Hard crown & (b) Flint.
The flint buttons are used in bifocal application. Flint buttons are manufactured in limited quantity by M/s Bharat Ophthalmic
 Glass Limited, Durgapur. There is no manufacturing facility at present to manufacture hard crown glasses. The main constraints in
 facilitating hard crown spectacle glass  manufacturing  facility in the country  are  the non-availability of  the  indigenoustechnology and
 the cost of importing the machinery. There is at present high custom duty on import of glass manufacturing machinery. The above
 factors coupled with dumping of cheap, inferior quality glass from certain countries normally dissuade the potential entrepreneurs from
 setting up such projects. This Department in association with some State Governments are making concerted efforts to create awareness
 amongst the entrepreneurs about the desirability of setting up such facilities in the country.  Taking into Consideration, the societal
 importance of this product, the Government policies may have to be reviewed in respect of Customs Duty on machinery meant for
 spectacle glass production.
Comments
In reply to unstarred question 2131 given in the Rajya  Sabha on 10
th December, 2001 it has been stated by Ministry of Health and
 Family Welfare that the general rate of import duty on glass making machinery falling under chapter 84 or chapter 85 of the Customs
 Tariff Act, 1975 is 25% (basic) plus 16% (additional) plus 4% (special additional duty).  It has also been stated that at present there is no
 proposal under Government’s consideration for review of the customs duty on glass making machinery.  The Committee feels that once
 the duty structure is simplified and brought down to a competitive level the entrepreneurs may come forward for setting up industries in
 this field.  The Committee is therefore of the view that the Department of Science & Technology should take up the matter with the
 Ministries of Health and Finance as such projects are important from health point of view.

SEISMOLOGY PROGRAMME
Recommendation
The Committee observes that the earthquakes of devastating magnitude likes the ones in Latur, Uttarkashi and Gujarat,
 which are in the recent memory, have once again underlined the need to penetrate in the Nature's otherwise impenetrable area
 which remains beyond human comprehension till date. Every cause has an effect. This simple reasoning should ultimately help
 us to understand Nature's temper in the field of seismology. Whether man's endeavour to play with the climate thus leading to
 what was known as Global warning has anything to do with the phenomenon of killer earthquakes, remains to be studied. This
 area of research needs to be accorded highest priority for the earthquakes have brought about devastation not only in this
 country but in several countries world over. The field of research was no less important than mysteries of human gene in which
 scientists seemed to be finding panacea for human ailments.
(Para 31)
Action Taken by the Department
 The Recommendations/observations of Parliamentary Standing Committee, have been noted. Necessary action on the  
 recommendations is being initiated in consultation with the experts. Also, the matter is under consideration of the Apex
 Committee of Seismicity Programme.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation
       The Committee further observes that the Department of Science & Technology should go all out in this field. To that end it was
 necessary that the Department first helped strengthen the mechanism of weather forecasting. The Committee has observed in its earlier
 reports that our weather forecasts often go awry. This needed perfection which might enable us to go a step further in understanding the
 mysteries behind killer earthquakes. The Department of Science & Technology therefore should concentrate its all out efforts in this
 direction.
(Para 32)
Action Taken by the Department
 For the effective further improvement in the field of  Weather Forecasting and Strengthening the mechanism therefor, consignment
 of two numbers of State-of-art technology Doppler Radars for Kolkata and Chennai have been received. One number indigenously
 fabricated Doppler Radar by ISRO is under test and will be installed at Sri-Harikota and commissioned shortly. Long Range Forecast
 capabilities are being upgraded during current year. Obsolete equipment on the eastern coast are being replaced by latest technology
 digital Cyclone Warning Disaster System and the High Wind Speed Recorders in phased manner to improve efficiency & accuracy of
 forecasts, warnings and their disseminations in relation to devastating Cyclonic Storms.
IMD would be establishing Seismological Observatories at Sihor, Bhavnagar and Gujarat to collect realtime basis earthquake data.
 Obsolete seismometers are being replaced by new State-of-art Technology Broad Band Seismographs in most parts in phased manner.
 Two numbers of Russian Seismographs will be purchased, installed & commissioned during current financial year to ensure
 improvement in observing system. Earthquake Risk Evaluation Centre will be established at Headquarters to go several steps further in
 understanding the mysteries of earthquake. Seismographs from world over have started carrying out several studies on the increased
 frequency of earthquakes to bring out possible reasoning of the phenomena.
Comments
While the Committee notes the reply with satisfaction, it, however, is of the view that since two numbers of Russian Seismographs
 are to be purchased, installed and commissioned during the current financial year it would have been better if the progress so far made
 in the acquisition of the seismographs had also been indicated for the benefit of the Committee.  The Committee would like to know the
 progress in this regard at the time of consideration of the Demands for Grants for the year 2002-2003.
INTEGRATED STUDIES RELATED TO GANGOTRI GLACIER
Recommendation
The Committee notes from the Annual Report of the Department for the year 2000-2001 that the Himalayan
 Glaciology programme has been undertaken by the Department with a view inter-alia to understand the behaviour of
 the glaciers. The Committee is also aware of the reports, which suggested the shrinking of the glaciers every year or
 every few years and possibly the Ganga River changing its course. While elaborate details of the programme have been
 given in the Annual Report, what has necessitated undertaking the programme does not seem to have been spelt out
 clearly. Nevertheless the Committee notes the immense potential which Himalayan Glaciers provide in recovering the
 short and long term records of the past environment and climate of Central Asia. Therefore, the Committee would like
 programme to be pursued with the enthusiasm and zeal it deserves.

file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20S%20and%20T,%20Env.%20and%20Forests/96th.htm[29-08-2018 16:20:37]
(Para 35)
Action Taken by the Department
Necessity for the Programme:
The necessity for undertaking the Himalayan Glaciology Programme has been felt by DST because the study of Himalayan
 Glaciers is important and significant due to the following factors:
(a)  Glaciers & snow fields are an important source of fresh water and their melt water support the flow of Indus, Ganga and
 Brahmaputra rivers and their tributaries. It is important to know the behaviour of glaciers for planning hydroelectric
 power projects, roads, and other utility services of national importance such as floods/avalanche monitoring and
 management. Glacier studies are being carried out all over the world for similar purposes.
(b) The climate and weather pattern of India is greatly influenced by Himalaya and Himalayan glaciers and snow fields.
 Glacier fluctuation has been identified as one of the Geo-indicators useful to assess the geological aspect of global
 climate change.  It was identified in Agenda 21 of the 1992 United Nations Rio Conference on the Environment.    
 Therefore, no country in the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river basins can afford to ignore the glaciers.
(c)  Glacier advance or recession is an important environmental parameter. Glaciers are sensitive barometers of climate
 change, growing and dwarfing in response to the changes in temperature and snowfall. So, the glacier fluctuations of
 past and present  serve as valuable source of  information for regional and global climate, melt water flow,
 environmental change in mountain region and their adjoining   plains. Being intimately connected with various
 geological processes, glaciers provide insight into the geology of Himalayas.
(d)  The valley glaciers and other related formations are part of the geomorphic pattern of the Himalaya.
DST’s Enthusiasm and Zeal for the Programme:
DST is coordinating/promoting studies on Himalayan Glaciers as also providing financial assistance for research and
 development activities so as to generate a strong R&D database for future studies  on the  subject.  DST has been  playing a
 pivotal  role to  provide a platform for  its
 sister organizations and academic/research institutions for pursuing research in
 identified priority areas and undertaking coordinated multi- institutional and multi-disciplinary research activities in selected
 major glaciers of national importance.  DST is very much keen to make all out effort to chalk out strategies so as to
 stimulate/encourage scientists and creating scientific temper which in turn may strengthen the Himalayan Glaciology
 programme.
Some of the important activities to be implemented by DST in the near future are as follows:-
In addition to on-going activities, DST has proposed a multidisciplinary and multi-institutional coordinated project to study
 the Dokriani Bamak Glacier. The project is now under consideration of DST funding. There are 8 research institutions involved
 in this project to study various aspects of the Dokriani Bamak Glacier.
DST has also proposed collaboration at international level to take joint studies and training of manpower. The proposal is
 being taken up by the International Division of DST with other Central Asian Region countries. ,
A project on "National Snow Science Workshop" to be organized by SASE, Chandigarh, is also under consideration of DST
 funding. The workshop will provide an opportunity to the scientists of the country to express their views and come out with
 future strategies in the field of glaciology.
As in the past, the emphasis will be laid on manpower development. Geological Survey of India (GSI) has trained a number
 of scientists in last five years involving DST support. Efforts will be made to organize field-oriented training programme for the
 promising young scientists who then could take up research activities.
Glaciological research has been pursued for long in the country with different levels of success. These studies were more or
 less carried out with limited objectives. Now, it has become very clear that the science of glaciology should be pursued in the
 country by a totally dedicated Centre. With this aim, a proposal to set up a National Centre for Field Operations and Research
 on Himalayan Glaciology (NCFOR-HG) has been prepared. The National Centre would strive to generate a strong in-house
 research base as well as a network of state-of-the-art laboratories mid field operation facilities which will also be available to
 researchers from other centers and Universities across the country for their investigations. The National Centre will bring
 holistic approach to the Himalayan glaciological research in the country and provide much needed data base for formulation of
 national policies on natural resource management in glacier regions of the country.
Efforts are on to disseminate information and to popularize the programme widely among scientists of the country. To
 achieve this target, a Brochure on Himalayan Glaciology is likely to come out shortly. A website on the programme is also
 being -planned to be accessed through INTERNET. Similarly, periodical publication of a News Letter on Himalayan
 Glaciology is under consideration.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply with satisfaction.

DRUGS AND PHARMACEUTICALS
Recommendation
 While the Committee appreciates that Department of Science and Technology is choosing herbs for development of
 drugs with a view to find a cure for many diseases affecting the society, the Committee is constrained to observe that not
 much is being done in the field of traditional systems of medicines in the country whereas some of the other countries
 have been in the forefront in adopting our system of medicine at mass scale. It was reported in the media recently that in
 order to cure the injuries of the victim of Cheronobyl explosion, an Indian System of medicine was found to be very
 effective. This paved the way for adoption of the Indian system of medicine in Russia. This is only one example. Efforts
 therefore need to be concentrated in adopting the traditional systems of medicine at mass scale by setting up
 laboratories, hospitals etc.
Another point which the Committee would like the  DST to bear in mind that the chances of bringing the private
 sector or, for that matter the industry on a common platform for research on any other form of collaboration, are
 indeed remote because the industry does not work from a welfare point of view. Rather profit is its motive. In fact the
 Committee is constrained to observe that the commercialization of human suffering diseases appears to be becoming the
 order of the day in private hospitals/clinics etc. This trends needs to be discouraged.
The Committee would like to reiterate that it would be indeed futile to merely add drugs to the ones already existing
 in the country. Efforts should always be aimed to develop drugs that tend to wipe out the diseases and not merely cure
 them.
(Para 38)
Action Taken by the Department
The research in Drugs and Pharmaceuticals sector is resource and knowledge intensive. Therefore, while formulating this
 programme initially in 1994-95, it was thought desirable to synergise the strength of public funded R&D institutions and
 pharmaceutical industry to develop new molecules both in modem as well as in traditional system of medicines. The drug
 industry has contributed significantly in the collaborative projects funded under the scheme. So far in the overall commitment
 of the order of Rs.46.00 crores, the Industry commitment is of the order of Rs.26.00 crores and Rs.20.00 crores of the
 Government. In line with the observation of the Committee, efforts were mounted to expand research programmes in traditional
 system of medicines by initiating research projects in standardization and validation of Asavas, Arishtas, Bhasmas and certain
 single plant formulation.
The Department has made efforts to initiate research projects not only to bring out new drugs/new processes but also to wipe
 out the diseases like Hepatitis-B and rabies through joint collaborative projects.
Hepatitis-B projects has been funded in Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore and Rabies programme in Tamil Nadu
 University for Veterinary and Animal Sciences. In view of the novelty of the research findings and their industrial use, 2
 patents for products and 6 patents for process have been filed in India and abroad. However, as observed by the Committee,
 efforts would be intensified to initiate more research programmes in the direction of wiping out diseases.
The commercialization
 of the human sufferings/diseases by the private hospitals and clinics and also adoption of traditional system of medicines at mass scale
 at the hospitals falls within the mandate of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. The observation of the Committee has been
 forwarded to the concerned Ministry for strict compliance.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the findings of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare will be communicated by the
 Department for the information of the Committee.

 projects/schemes which aim at strengthening research infrastructure including networking and computational facilities  in     the
 Universities/academic sector. While the Committee is satisfied that the scheme to provide fund for development of infrastructure and
 facilities for promoting R&D in new areas, it would impress on the research institutes that they should aim at generating their own
 sources of income by transferring its developed technologies to get royalty by collaborating with private sector industries.
(Para 45)
Action Taken by the Department
 Considering the lack of infrastructure facilities for imparting good quality higher education and conducting advanced
 research, a new Program "Fund for improvement of S&T infrastructures in universities and higher educational institutions
 (FIST)" has been approved by the Government for strengthening their infrastructure for post-graduate education & research in
 the Universities & related academic institutions. The Program was launched during financial year 2000-2001. The approved
 allocation of funds by the Government for the Program is Rs. 75.0 Crores (till 2002) having an allocation of Rs. 19.0 Crores in
 2000-2001 & Rs. 35 Crores in 2001-2002.
The Program was implemented by a two-tier Scientific Advisory Committees: Advisory Board of Experts on FIST
 (FISTAB) & Subject Expert Committees of six different subject e.g. Life Sciences, Physical Sciences, Chemical Sciences,
 Engineering Sciences, Earth & Atmospheric Sciences and Mathematical Sciences. During financial year 2000- 2001, out of
 1158 Proposals in two levels received from different universities and academic institutions, the FISTAB recommended 221
 Proposals (Level-1: 186 & Level- II: 35) at a total budget of about Rs.112 Crores for 5 years.
While the Program as approved by the Cabinet would support the Universities & related institutions for acquiring the basic
 infrastructure and enabling facilities and environment for promoting R&D in new and emerging areas and attracting fresh
 talents, the generation of income in the form of royalty etc. by transferring its developed technologies to industries may not
 arise under aforesaid Program Efforts are being made, separately to impress upon the institutions to generate resources.

VIGYAN PRASAR
Recommendation
The Committee feels that given the significance of Vigyan Prasar in popularizing Science & Technology the
 recommendations of the Finance Committee for the enhancement of allocation of funds to Rs.2.00 crores should not have been
 ignored. The Committee accordingly recommends allocation for Vigyan Prasar be increased to Rs. 2.00 crores.
The Committee is of the view that efforts should be made to strengthen publication activities and publications should
 be carried out in more & more Indian languages. Besides, in view of the role played by HAM radio during the recent
 Gujarat earthquake as well as Orissa cyclone, it should be popularized among people so that they are encouraged to
 develop and use it as hobby. Besides, equipment for HAM radio be made available at cheaper prices.
(Para 48)
Action Taken by the Department
 (a) Vigyan Prasar has already initiated efforts towards strengthening of the publication activities in various vernacular
 languages on relevant S&T topics and issues.
 
(b)  Vigyan Prasar has initiated efforts for popularization of HAM radio. In particulars, the activity will be initiated in States
 which are particularly prone to natural disasters, say Uttranchal, Orissa, Himachal Pradesh, U.P. and Gujarat. Vigyan
 Prasar intends to establish HAM radio club stations and run training programmes to help prospective hams to clear the
 Amateur station operator's certificate examination conducted by the Ministry of Communication. Passing this
 examination is a pre- requisite for obtaining a license for setting up a HAM radio Station. Efforts are also on to make
 HAM radio equipments available at affordable prices.
(c)  Vigyan Prasar Network (VIPNET) Science Clubs activity is expected to grow significantly into a movement for
 popularization of S&T especially in rural areas in the days to come.

 missions be made available to it.

Action Taken by the Department
Currently there are only four science wings abroad i.e, Germany, Japan, Russia & USA. Each Science Wing is headed by a
 Scientific Attaché with Personal Assistant and Junior Scientific Assistant as a supporting staff. So far, total budgetary support

 International Cooperation in Science & Technology for the Formulation of the Tenth Five Year Plan.
