REPORT
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research is one of the three Departments of the Ministry
of Science & Technology. The mandate of Department of Scientific and Industrial Research includes
promotion of industrial research for indigenous technology promotion, development, utilization and transfer.
1.1 The primary endeavour of DSIR is to promote R&D by the industries; support industrial units to
develop state-of-the-art globally competitive technologies of high commercial potential; catalyze faster
commercialization of laboratory-scale R&D; augment technology transfer capabilities; enhance the share
of technology intensive exports in overall exports; strengthen industrial consultancy and establish a user-
friendly information network to facilitate scientific and industrial research in the country.
1.2 The DSIR has two public sector undertakings viz. National Research Development Corporation
(NRDC) and Central Electronics Ltd. (CEL) and two autonomous organizations viz. Council for Scientific
and Industrial Research (CSIR) and Consultancy Development Centre (CDC). The Department also
provides host facilities and assistance to Asian and Pacific Centre for Transfer of Technology (APCTT)
as the focal point in the country.
1.3 The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment
and Forests considered the Demands for Grants (2015-16) of the Department of Scientific and Industrial
Research at its meeting held on 9th April, 2015.

4. RESULT FRAMEWORK DOCUMENT (RFD)
4.1 Result Framework Document (RFD) has been introduced by the Cabinet Secretariat's Performance
Management Division since 2009-10, wherein the project performance of all scientific departments of the
Government is evaluated by a third party, appointed by the Performance Management Division of the
Cabinet Secretariat. One of the most important features of RFD is that under it, schemes, programmes
and activities are defined in quantitative terms and the achievements of the projects are reviewed by the
Adhoc Task Force consisting of former Secretaries of Government of India.
4.2 In the year 2013, the Committee had noted decline in RFD performance of the Department for the
years 2011-12 and 2012-13. Department of Scientific and Industrial Research had intimated that
performance of the Department of RFD score was expected to improve with the approval of 12th Five
Year Plan schemes and implementation thereof. The Committee desired to know from the Department
the progress achieved in this regard and the RFD score of the Department during the last three years.

5. AUTONOMOUS INSTITUTIONS
Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)
5.1 The Council of Scientific & Industrial Research, constituted in 1942 is an autonomous body. The
detailed functions assigned to the Council are as follows:
(i) Promotion, guidance and coordination of scientific and industrial research in India, including
the institution and financing of specific researches;
(ii) Establishment or development and assistance to special institutions or departments of existing
institutions for scientific study of problems affecting particular industries and trade;
(iii) Establishment and award of research studentships and fellowships;
(iv) Utilization of the results of the researches conducted under the auspices of the Council
towards the development of industries in the country and the payment of a share of royalties
arising out of the development of the results of researches to those who are considered as
having contributed towards the pursuits of such researches;
(v) Establishment, maintenance and management of laboratories, workshops, institutes and
organizations to further scientific and industrial research and to utilize and exploit for purposes
of experiment or otherwise any discovery or invention likely to be of use to Indian industries;

8
(vi) Collection and dissemination of information in regard not only to research but also to industrial
matters generally;
(vii) Publication of scientific papers and journals;
(viii) To enter into arrangements with foreign scientific agencies and institutions for exchange of
scientists, study tours, training in specialized areas of science and technology, conducting joint
projects, providing technical assistance in the establishment of scientific institutions and for
other matters consistent with the aims and objectives of the Society;
(ix) To do all other such things as the Society may consider necessary incidental or conducive to
the attainment of the above subjects; etc.
CSIR has been striving to perform these functions in accordance with the priorities laid down in
Five Year Plans and perceived needs of the nation from time to time.
5.2 The Council of Scientific & Industrial Research is known for its cutting edge R&D knowledge base
in diverse S&T areas. Having pan-India presence, CSIR has a dynamic network of 38 national laboratories,
39 outreach centres, 3 Innovation Complexes and 5 units. CSIR's R&D expertise and experience is
embodied in about 4000 active scientists supported by about 7000 scientific and technical personnel.
5.3 CSIR covers a wide spectrum of science and technology from radio and space physics, oceanography,
earth sciences, geophysics, chemicals, drugs, genomics, biotechnology and nanotechnology to mining,
aeronautics, instrumentation, environmental engineering and information technology. It provides significant
technological intervention in many areas with regard to societal efforts which include environment,
health, drinking water, food, housing, energy, leather, farm and non-farm sectors. CSIR is the Nation's
custodian for Measurement standards of Mass, Distance, Time, Temperature, Current etc. CSIR has
created and is the custodian of Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) which is a powerful
weapon against unethical commercial exploitation of Indian traditional knowledge. CSIR maintains Microbial
Type Culture collection (MTCC) and Gene Bank.
5.4 CSIR published 5086 research papers during 2013 with an average impact factor per paper as 2.86
in SCI journals of national and international repute contributing to 8.72 % of Indian R&D literature.
Several research papers were in high impact factor journals like Chemical Reviews (IF 41.298), Lancet
(IF 38.278), Nature (IF 38.597), Science (IF 31.20), Chemical Society Reviews (IF 28.76) etc.
5.5 Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) is implementing following Ten Plan Schemes
to achieve its objectives of 12th Five Year Plan:
(i) National Laboratories;
(ii) National S&T Human Resource Development;
(iii) Intellectual Prope rty & Technology Management;
(iv) R&D Management Support;
(v) New Millennium Indian Technology Leadership Initiative;
(vi) Innovation Complexes;

8. Bus mounted Reverse Osmosis (RO) Water Filter
8.1 During the meeting of the Committee held on the 9th April 2015, the representatives of DSIR
informed the Committee about a bus mounted Reverse Osmosis (RO) water filter which houses RO
plant. The Committee desired to know the details of the said RO plant. The Committee was informed
by CSIR that CSIR- Central Salt and Marine Chemicals Research Institute (CSIR-CSMCRI), Bhavnagar
has designed and developed a bus-mounted Reverse Osmosis (RO) water filter which houses RO plant.
The plant runs on auto engine of the bus. It has also facility to install solar cells on the top of the bus.
This RO plant can process the water with salinity ranging from brackish upto sea water. The capacity
of the plant is 3000 Litre Per Hour in case of brackish and 1000 Litre Per Hour for sea water. The bus
also houses training and video facility to create awareness about quality of water. Mobile RO bus was
deployed in areas affected by natural calamities like floods in Bihar, West Bengal, Uttarakhand and Jammu
& Kashmir. Three persons including bus driver are needed to operate the system. The bus, costing
` 65.00 lakh (in 2008) has been modified to install the RO plant, costing ` 30.00 lakh. Along with some
other accessories, the total cost of the bus-mounted RO plant is approximately ` 105.00 lakh. The
Committee desired to know as to how many such units were available with CSIR. In response, the
Committee was informed that there was only one such mobile RO unit.
8.2 The Committee appreciates the bus mounted Reverse Osmosis water filter developed by
CSIR. However, the Committee is appalled to know that instead of commercialising such a path
breaking innovation at least to States/State agencies, at present there is only one such RO unit
with the Department. The Committee is at a loss to understand as to why, in the current climatic
scenario, when natural disasters are on the rise in the country, no thought has been given to
increasing the number of such mobile RO units or the commercialisation of this technology. At
a time when the country is making preparations for meeting the challenges of disasters, the
availability of such RO units has great relevance during disaster times.
8.3 The Committee is of the view that during natural calamities, the mobile RO Unit can be
of immense help in providing clean water to the affected people. These mobile RO units can also
contribute to the quality of life of the people by providing clean drinking water. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Department, in consultation with National Disaster Management
Authority, Ministry of Home Affairs, should take necessary steps for the commercialisation of
its mobile RO unit so that at least one such unit is available to each and every State/Union
Territory in the country which can be made use of in times of need.

15

aimed at building a 90 seater civil aircraft with state-of-the-art techniques developed with CSIR-NAL

agency for implementation of the programme even after a lapse of more than two years. Further, the

reduced allocations to CSIR, due to which the programme could not be approved. Now the programme
was proposed to be initiated in the format of a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) to be set up by Hindustan
Aeronautics Ltd. (HAL), Bengaluru in partnership with CSIR's National Aerospace Laboratories (CSIR-
NAL) and that had been agreed to in-principle by both Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd. and CSIR. However,
that has still not been implemented because all the required administrative approvals are yet to be obtained
and Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd. was doing the needful in this regard.
10.4 The Committee is extremely disappointed to note that a programme of great national
importance such as National Civil Aircraft Development Programme (NCAD) Programme has
made very little, rather no progress, at all during the last three years. The Committee is
constrained to state that although our country has mastered space technology, we are yet to
develop technology for developing a commercial aircraft. The Department has inter alia  indicated
financial constraints for non-approval of the programme during the last three years. The Committee
recommends that the administrative approvals for the fresh initiatives taken in collaboration
with Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd. may be obtained at the earliest so that the actual work on the
programme could be initiated in right earnest. The Committee also recommends that adequate
financial allocations be made for the above programme so that its execution is not delayed any
further. A comprehensive review of the causes of delay of the project needs to be undertaken
and responsibility fixed for the delay alongwith an action plan for successfully implementing the
project within a stated time-frame considering the strategic importance of the project.
11. CSIR-800 SCHEME
11.1 CSIR-800 was initiated as a new scheme during 12th Five Year Plan. The scheme aimed at bringing
in the desired S&T interventions for augmenting the income, removing drudgery and improving the
quality of life of people at the base of the economic pyramid, geographically spread across the country.
The Committee noted that the amount of ` 5.00 crore allocated for the above scheme in BE 2014-15
got reduced to ` 1.00 crore at RE stage and no allocation has been made under this scheme in
2015-16. The Committee desired to know the reasons therefor from the Department. The Committee was
informed that as per the direction of Government of India to rationalize the implementation of schemes,

16
CSIR reviewed the scheme and merged it with 'National Laboratories' scheme. Accordingly, no separate
allocation was made for CSIR-800 scheme in budget allocation of 2015-16.
11.2 Keeping in view the importance of the objectives of CSIR-800 scheme, especially its benefits to the
poor people of the country, the Committee desired to know the achievements of the CSIR-800 scheme
in 2014-15 and details of the programme/activities proposed to be undertaken by CSIR during 2015-16
to fulfill the objective of erstwhile CSIR-800 scheme in the new programme. In response, the Department
intimated that during 2014-15, CSIR continued to provide S&T knowledge base to villages to promote
employment generation and income augmentation. Some of there activities are as under:
(i) CSIR developed KrishiShakti, a small range (11.2 hp) diesel engine tractor for farmers with
small land holdings for effective tilling. The technology has been transferred and manufacturing
has been initiated in West Bengal. The tractor was launched in Delhi on 20th November 2014.
(ii) A food processing unit has been set up at CSIR-Centre for High Altitude Biology (CSIR-
CeHAB) in the remote tribal region of Lahaul and Spiti. A brining unit has been designed and
prototype developed for the preservation of locally and abundantly produced peas and
cauliflowers. The knowhow was showcased at the Tribal Fair at Keylong and training organized
for the benefit of farmers in the area of food and food processing: making novel products
from Buckwheat; and also brining of the peas which is a major crop.
(iii) CSIR introduced aesthetic appeal, standardisation and new ranges of leather products in North
East for higher profits to leather artisans. The handbags and other leather products crafted and
designed with the ethnic designs and materials of the North-East based on Ornaments, Dresses
& Artifacts adorned by tribal folks of the North East India.
(iv) CSIR introduced and commercialized Lilium cultivation in Lahaul and Spiti region of Himachal
Pradesh. CSIR played a catalytic role in the promotion of commercial floriculture in Himachal
Pradesh enabling enhanced income to flower growers of Himachal Pradesh.
(v) CSIR has been contributing in the area of healthcare. Some recently developed technologies/
interventions benefitting the common people include: nonClonableID technology for medical
product authentication; Diagnostic system for affordable, point of need testing to manage HIV
and TB; and eHealth Center (eHC), a platform for the fourth paradigm of science, data-
intensive discovery, while bringing affordable healthcare services to the doorstep of people).
11.3 The Committee was further informed that during 2015-16, CSIR technologies, useful for the
identified communities, will be implemented in a planned manner in partnership with government machineries.
CSIR will be providing its S&T knowledgebase for utilization in rural areas in form of relevant technological
interventions, skill development and up-gradation for targeted people at the base of economic pyramids
so as to promote creation of new micro enterprises through S&T interventions.
11.4 The Committee is at a loss to understand as to why a scheme launched to augment the
incomes and improve the quality of lives of one million rural citizens geographically spread
across the country, was merged with another scheme, the objectives of the new merged scheme
have no direct relation whatsoever with CSIR-800 scheme. The Committee hopes that with the
merger of the CSIR-800 scheme with the National Laboratories scheme, the objectives of the

17
original CSIR-800 scheme will not be lost. The Committee recommends that there should be no
let up in the activities of the Department on this front and adequate financial support should be
provided to the Department for achieving the objectives of the scheme in the new merged
scheme.
12. TREATMENT OF MUNICIPAL, URBAN WASTE etc.
12.1 During the meeting of the Committee held on 9th April 2015, the Committee was informed by the
Department about the CSIR's involvement and contribution in clean Ganga Mission. One of the points
highlighted by the Department was demonstration project on Phytorid Technology for treatment of
sewage to avoid pollution load to River Ganga. It was informed that the project was a single solution
to treat municipal, urban, agricultural and industrial effluents. Further, it was a low cost project with
facility for reuse of treated water.
12.2 The Committee desired to know whether the above project was successful in practical terms and
whether the technology had actually been applied to treat municipal and city waste. In response, the
Committee was informed that extensive research work on the project had been done at National Environment
Engineering Research Institute, Nagpur. In response to a further query as to whether the application of
the technology could be actually seen at the ground level, the DG, CSIR failed to provide a categorical
reply to the Committee.
12.3 The Committee is of the view that treatment of municipal, urban, industrial effluent etc.
is one of the major problems not only of municipal authorities but of Town Planners also, which
is also responsible for the increasing pollution load of our riveres. The Committee recommends
that the Department should take immediate necessary steps to test the Phytorid technology at
the ground level and, if required, further researches on the above technology should be carried
out on urgent basis to make it a successful model in a time bound manner.
13. ROBOTICS TECHNOLOGY
13.1 During its meeting, the Committee desired to know from the Department as to where do we stand
in the field of robotics. The Committee was informed by DG, CSIR that they had two laboratories in
the country, one was Central Mechanical Engineering Research Institute (CMERI), West Bengal which
was working on subsea or ground level robots and other was National Aerospace Laboratory, in Bengaluru
which was working on micro air vehicles.
13.2 The Committee feels that Robotics is the technology of the future as the future management
of human civilisation was going to depend heavily on robotics. Already, in countries like USA and
Japan, robots were being used for treating and taking medical care of the aged people. Compared
to these countries, India was way far behind in this critical field. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department should make an action plan to make rapid progress in the field
of robotics so as to be able to compete with advanced countries in this field in a given time frame.
The Committee also recommends that required budgetary support should be provided to the
Department to make rapid strides in this field.

2. At the outset, the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee; Secretary, Department of
Scientific and Industrial Research and his team of officers. He asked the Secretary to apprise the
Committee of key thrust areas of the Department during 2015-16, budgetary allocations for different
schemes/programmes, initiatives during the year 2014-15 and whether the allocation were sufficient to
implement the programmes of the Department. He also asked the Secretary to inform the Committee of
the steps taken to acquire a global leadership role in genomes, increasing publications by Indian scholars
in international scientific journals and increasing share of patented technologies. The Chairman also
desired to know whether any projects have been formulated to provide new seismic hazard map which
could be useful for coastal cities and nuclear power plants of the country.
3. The Director-General, CSIR made a power-point presentation on the various aspects of the Demands
for Grants of the Department during 2015-16 and the achievements during 2014-15. It was also informed
that the DSIR had done a lot of work in Swachh Bharat Abhiyan and Make in India programmes of
the Government. The Chairman observed as to how the Department could have achieved a lot in the two
programmes which had started only ten months back. The Director General, CSIR explained that they
had tried to categorise their contributions in these areas and that CSIR had been active in Make in India
since its inception.
4. The Committee was also informed about the Phytorid Technology for treatment of sewage to avoid
pollution load to River Ganga. It was informed that the project was a single solution to treat municipal,
urban, agricultural and industrial effluents. Further, it was a low cost project with facility for reuse of
treated water. The Committee desired to know whether the above project was successful in practical
terms and whether the technology had actually been applied to treat municipal and city waste. In

27
response, the Committee was informed that extensive research work on the project had been done at
National Environment Engineering Research Institute, Nagpur.
5. During the presentation, the representative of DSIR informed the Committee about a bus mounted
Reverse Osmosis (RO) water filter. The Committee desired to know as to how many such units were
available with CSIR. In response, the Committee was informed that there was only one such mobile RO
unit. The Chairman stated that in view of the enormity of the challenges of natural disasters in the
country, the country needed to have more such RO units. He advised the Department to take necessary
steps for the commercialisation of its mobile RO unit so that at least one such unit is available to each
and every State/Union Territory in the country.
6. The Chairman then desired to know about the support the Department was giving for developing
the future of the science and technology in the country. The Committee was informed that the Department
was in the forefront in terms of providing the highly competitive CSIR and UGC-Net Fellowships to pure
sciences, Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, Biology, Geo-sciences, etc. They also had nearly 2,000 plus
Fellowships from CSIR and an equal number from the UGC. They also had more than 8,500 research
scholars availing CSIR fellowships. Further, they also provided awards for recognizing excellence like
CSIR Young Scientist Award, Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award and G .N. Ramachandran Gold Medal.
7. The Committee then desired to know as to where the country stood in the field of robotics. The
Committee was informed by DG , CSIR that they had two laboratories in the country, one was Central
Mechanical Engineering Research Institute (CMERI), West Bengal which was working on subsea or
ground level robots and other was National Aerospace Laboratory, Bengaluru which was working on
micro air vehicles. Stating that the future management of human civilization was going to depend a lot
on robotics, the Chairman asked the Department to make an action plan to make rapid progress in the
field of robotics to be able to compete with the advanced countries in this field.
8. The Committee then raised various issues such as process for fabricating photonic crystal fibre;
technologies developed under Joint Technology Agreements; incremental innovations in material sciences,
publications of CSIR etc. The representatives of the Department also replied to some of the queries raised
by the Members. The Chairman then asked the Department to send written replies to all the queries raised
during the meeting to the Secretariat at the earliest.
9. The witnesses then withdrew.
