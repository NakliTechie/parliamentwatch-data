REPORT 
Necessity is the mother of invention. The history of mankind is full of examples where 
scarcities and shortages led to inventions. The men behind these inventions were not always 
intellectuals hailing from affluent families. Instead, they came from ordinary walks of life and in 
many cases were illiterate and so poor that they did not have even two square meals a day. They 
burnt mid-night oil in order to help the mankind with the benefit of their inventions. They 
followed the ideals enshrined in what Sir Winston Churchill described in following woi:ds:-
"Heights of the great men were not attained in a single flight, they came up toiling upwards when 
their companions slept in the night" 
The domain of inventions is thus not the property of any intellectuals or enlightened or 
affluent sections of the Society alone. It belongs to one and all. 
Shri P. Ramar Pillai who shot in the news sometime back comes from ordinary walk of life. 
He was reported to have invented what he described as "Herbal Fuel". This gave him a world­
wide publicity, for his experiment could open. new vistas for the worlq and save the mankind 
from the near crisis in the field of energy with which we are confronted today. It has often been 
projected and rightly so that the oil reserves were not going to last long. It is thus incumbent on 
all of us to conserve · the oil resources in the best possible manner and at the same time our 
endeavour should be to aim at inventing the alternative and new sources of energy. 
It was in this background, that Shri P. Ramar Pillai of Kamrajar district, Tamil Nadu, 
attracted attention world-wide about his production of herbal fuel. History tells us that to Dr. 
Isaac Newton, it was a rude jolt by a falling apple which made him ponder about earth's 
gravitational pull whereas young Ramar did not have to undergo such an ordeal with the nature 
when the green leaves burnt with a frenzy, he knew they belonged to an extraordinary genre. 
According to Shri Ramar Pillai, while taking part in an annual picnic to the Western Ghats 
with other students of Mamsapuram's Sivanthi Petty Nadar High School, he had noticed that in 
the rice they had just cooked, a spark fell on the leaves and they caught fire. While this set him 
thinking, his other classmates were not carried by the significance that Shri Ramar Pillai 
attached to the event. He drew his classmates' attention to it but they ignored him. It is stated 
that in the years that followed, the 'leaf-hunt' became such an obsession that Shri Ramar Pillai 
failed to clear the Xth standard. His wandering ways made follow.ed, the 'leaf -hunt ' became 
such an obsession that Shri Ramar to earn his bread, he started collecting firewood from the 
forest. Finally in 1987, he identified the plant and had a hunch that it could be converted into a 
fuel. None believed him except a school-mate of Ramar, Poonkani, who ultimately became his 
foster sister. Then began a series of trial and error experiments. They studied the effect of 
different materials on the herb after grounding it into a paste. Poonkani remembers that though 
they knew about the power of the herb all along, it was, however, on November 20, 1992, only 
that they made a definite breakthrough. When they mixed Sodium Chloride to a certain kind of 
fruit essence there was a reaction and a lot of smoke. They then mixed this with the herb and 
started heating , they got a semi-solid mixture, the precursor of the fuel that they claim to have 
made. By 1993, they had refined it to its present form resulting in the first official demonstration _ 
of the production of herbal fuel at the Indian Institute of Technology, Madras on 30th June, 1995. 
News agencies splashed that the ''herbal fuel" produced has been tested by Dr. V. Ganesan an 

expert at the Madras liT's Internal Combustion ·Engines Lab, who found that the oil is just 
as good, if not better, as a fuel for two stroke and four stroke engines. 
Dr. Ganesan, like some other scientists is stated to have wondered: From where does 
Ramar get the carbon to make his "Petrol"?" Ramar makes 400-750 ml. of his fuel from two 
litres of water using about 30 gm. of the herb. Even if the herb were full of carbon , and one 
gets maximum efficiency from it, it would take about 175 gm. of carbon to get so much fuel. 
While water, salt and lemon do not contain carbon, he uses just 5 gm. of the catalyst. 
Even the Secretary, DST was initially stated to have reacted to Ramar's experiment 
saying, "I. would neither believe it nor disbelieve it till I know the full details. But I would 
say that v':hat he showed was very interesting and significant and I have no reason to assume 
that he was doing any magic or fraud". A scientist whose scooter had run out of petrol 
decided to do the first verification outside the laboratory . After getting a refill of Ramar's 
fuel he m:1de several rounds on his campus and even rode home nine kms. away. This along 
with resu :ts of the test was telecast by Doordarshan. 
Then ·after, Shri P. Ramar is stated to have written a letter to the Secretary, 
Departme 1t of Science & Technology on 1st December, 1995 claiming to have developed a 
fuel from herbal mixture for two-wheelers. In order to substantiate his claim emission test 
reports of the Madras liT's IC Engine Laboratory were also stated to have been enclosed. 
He had a')pealed that he should be helped to set up production facility centres in his 
hometown in Tamil Nadu . 
Accordingly Shri P. Ramar was invited to demonstrate his production process again to a 
team of Scit-ntists and patent experts in Madras on 25th September, 1996. All the u"'""'~'"m

Technology dropped patent formalities as the demonstration could not establi sh the validity 
of Shri Ramar 's claim of "herbal fuel" and accused that Shri P. Ramar had used "dishonest 
means" to prod uce the herbal fuel. But Shri Ramar denied that he had used any "dishonest 
means" to produce his fuel an~ explained that he was prepared to prove his claim true. 
The Committee was informed that the Department of Science & Technology, however,

The Committee has been informed that the detailed analysis carried out by the Indian 
Institute of Petroleum on the solid and the liquid portions separated from the product 
sample obtained during the demonstration experiment for preparation of 'herbal fuel' on 
15th November, 1996 at Indian Institute of Petroleum, Dehradun clearly shows that it is a 
mixture of hydrocarbons boiling in kerosene range with dissolved camphor. 
The Committee has been further informed that the Indian Institute of Petroleum 
conducted a confirmatory experiment on 19th November, 1996 in order to further confirm 
the formation of the 'herbal fuel'. The steps followed were the same as in the demonstration 
experiment (Ramar experiment) carried out on 15th November, 1996. The data obtained in 
the confirmatory experiment as well as in the demonstration experiment of the 'herbal fuel' 
of Shri Ramar (15/11/96) are quite comparable in all respects including the change in the 
local volumes and burning characteristics. The Indian Institute of Petroleum has thus arrived 
at the following conclusions:-
(i) The 'Herbal Fuel' obtained in the demonstration experiment (Ramar experiment) 
was separated into the solid and liquid portions. This step became necessary since 
separation of solid wall observed when the 'Merbal Fuel' was allowed to cool to 
ambient temperature. Detailed analysis has been carried out on the solid and liquid 
portions. 
(ii) The laboratory analysi.s by liP established that the product produced during the 
Ramar experiment is a mixture of hydrocarbons boiling in the kerosene range with 
dissolved camphor. 
Going by the analysis of the Ramar's end product the expectations of finding an 
indigenous and economical solution to the energy crisis facing the mankind may 
have been belied for a while, the Committee nonetheless feels that it is not the end 
of the road. The Government should liberalise research and should extend all help 
to Scientists coming forward with their experiments. The Committee feels that there 
is no adequate infrastructure obtaining at the learning stages say at schools and 
colleges where Scientists/Officers/Researchers can conduct experiments on their 
findings. The Government should endeavour to provide the necessary infrastructure 
and throw it open to students and non-students in order to encourag e s cientific

2. The experiment was conducted by the liP scientists as per the procedure lay down by 
Mr. Ramar. The end product burned with a sooty smoke and strong camphor like smell. 
The end product was handed over to liP for detailed analysis. 
3. In any scientific experiment, the inputs and outputs are analysed and measured as 
follows: 
(i) The nature and detailed composition of inputs 
(ii) The quantity of inputs 
(iii) The nature and detailed composition of outputs 
(iv) The quantity of outputs

However , in this experiment the nature and detailed composition of inputs provided by

experiment) was separated into the solid and liquid portion s." This step became nece ssary

5. The laboratory analysis· by liP established that the product produced during the 
Ramar experiment is a mixture of hydrocarbons boiling in the kerosene range with dissolved 
camphor. 
6. Although, the nature of the inputs to the Ramar experiment were not allowed to be 
analysed , based on the fact that the solid input smelled strongly like camphor and the liquid

provided kerosene and camphor as inputs. Camphor dissolved in kerosene during the Ramar 
experiment with resultant increase in the volume of liquid.

confirmatory experiment where the same amount of camphor was mixed with the same 
amount of kerosene under the same experimental conditions leading to the same increase in 
volume with liquid having the same prope1 ty to burn as the so called herbal fuel produced in 
the Ramar experiment. 
8. It is finally concluded that the Ramar experiment carried out in liP was nothing other 
than mixing camphor, a combustible material, with hydrocarbons boiling in the kerosene 
range, also a combustible material, to obtain a solution of camphor in the hydrocarbons 

boiling in the kerosene range. This solution, claimed to be herbal fuel by Mr. Ramar, was 
obviously combustible. The herbal leaves used in the Ramar experiment had no role to play 
in the end product. 
We conclude that Ramar's claim of providing herbal fuel from herbal leaves and water 
is falacious.

S. Conclusions 
The detailed analysis carried out on the solid and the liquid portions separated from the 
product sample obtained during the demonstration experiment for preparation of what was 
described as herbal fuel on 15.11.1996 at liP clearly shows that it is a mixture of 
hydrocarbons boiling in kerosene range with dissolved camphor. 
Mr. Ramar, however , bad claimed that the so called herbal fuel obtained at the end of 
present experiment, if distilled, would yield petrol in the temperature range of 70--175 deg. 
C and diesel in the temperature range of 150--240 deg. C (see comments of Mr. Ramar in 
the report on the demonstralion on 15.11.1996 at liP). 
6. Confirmatory Experiment 
In order to further confirm the formation of the so called Herbal Fuel, a. confirmatory 
experiment was done on 19.11.96. The steps followed were the same as in thedemonstration 
experiment (Ramar experiment) carried out on 15.11.96. The steps are as foflows: 
1. A clean round bottom flask (2 litre) was washed with hot water. 
2. 750 ml water (corresponding to component A in Ramar experiment) was taken from 
Bisleri mineral water bottle in the flask. 
3. 230.1 gm of camphor (laboratory chemical) was weighed and transferred te the flask 
(corresponding to component C in Ramar experiment). 
4. Another 400 gm of camphor was weighed and transferred to the flask (corresponding 
to component B in Ramar experiment). 
5. 400 ml of kerosene fraction distilled from typical crude oil was measured and added 
to the flask (corresponding to component D in Ramar experiment, so called the essence). 
6. The flask containing all the components was closed With a stopper and heated in a 

mantle till the boiling started and continued for another five minutes intermittently openin~ 
the stopper to release excess pressure, total heating time was 28 minutes as was done in 
Ramar experiment. 
7. The flask was taken out from the mantle after the heating time and it was allowed to 
cool for some time. 
8. The contents of the flask were transferred to a separating funnel while they were stiR 
hot. While transferring the contents, 250 ml of Bisleri water was added simultaneously to the 
separating funnel. 
9. The two layers formed were transferred info two separate container s and their 
volume weights were recorded. The weight and volume of the upper liquid layer were 
720.1 g and 860 ml respectively as compared to 692.7 g and 850 ml for the so called herbal 
fuel obtained in the Ramar experiment. The weight and volume of the bottom layer in the 
confirmatory experiment were 998.0 g and 1010.0 ml as against 995.0 g and 1010.0 ml in the 
Ramar experiment (Table-11). 
10. Burning characteristics of the product obtained in the confirmatdry experiment were 
the same as that of the so called herbal fuel obtained in the Ramar experiment. 
The data obtained in the confirmatory experiment as well as in the demonstration 
experiment of what was described herbal fuel of Mr. Ramar (15.11.1996) are quite 
comparable in all respect s including the change in the liquid volumes and burning 
characteristics. 
7. Final Conclusions 
1. "Herbal fuel" obtained in the demonstration experiment (Ramar experiment) was 
separated into the solid and liquid portions. This step became necessary since separation of 
solid was observed when the so called herbal fuel was allowed to cool to ambient 
temperature. Detailed analysis has been carried out on the solid and liquid portions. 
2. The laboratory analysis by liP established that the product produced during the 
Ramar experime nt is a mixture of hydrocarbons boiling in the kero10ene range with disolved 
camphor. 
3. Although, the nature of the inputs to the Ramar experiment were not allowed to be 
analysed , based on the fact that the solid input smelled strongly like camphor and the liquid 
input (so called essence) smelled strongly like kerosene, it is most likely that Ramar 
provided kerosene and camphor as inputs. Camphor dissolved in kerosene during the Ramar 
experiment with resultant increase in the volume of liquid. 
4. The hypothesis in Shri Ramar's experiment above was verified by liP in a 
confirmatory experiment where the same amount of Camphor was mixed with the same 
amount of kerosene under the same experimental conditions leading to the same increase in 
volume with liquid having the same property to burn as the so called herbal fuel produced in 
the Ramar experiment.
