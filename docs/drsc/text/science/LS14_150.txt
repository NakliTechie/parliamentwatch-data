INSAT-4 SERIES
Recommendation
            The Committee hopes that INSAT-4 series would make India self sufficient in the areas of telecommunication,
 Mobile Satellite Services and acquisition of Meteorological, Hydrological and Oceanographic data.  It also hopes that
 these satellites would be successfully launched as per their schedule.                                                    
                                                                           (Para 22)
Action Taken by the Department
The INSAT system currently has about 140 transponders which are providing national services in the area of
 telecommunications, mobile communications, broadcasting, business communications and developmental
 communications in a self reliant manner. The INSAT-4 series of satellites have been planned to further augment the
 INSAT system with high power transponders to meet the growing demand.  The first satellite in INSAT-4 series,
 INSAT-4A has successfully undergone all the environmental tests and is planned for launch in second quarter of 2005-
06.  INSAT-4A will carry  high power C band (12 nos) and Ku band (12 nos) transponders.   This will be followed by
 launch of follow-on satellites INSAT-4B and 4C.   Further satellites in INSAT and GSAT series have been planned to
 meet the projected demand.  Also INSAT-3A & Kalpana-I are providing meteorological data on operational basis.  The
 work on Oceansat-2 has also been initiated to provide continuity of Oceanographic data (currently being provided by
 Oceansat-I).
Point raised by the Committee
It has been stated that the first satellite in INSAT-4 series, INSAT-4A has successfully undergone all the
 environmental tests and is planned for launch in second quarter of 2005-06. It is understood that the Satellite is yet to be
 launched. The reasons for the delay in its launch may be given and the likely date of its launch may be stated.
Clarification by the Department 
The launch service provider M/S Arianespace, was unable to provide a suitable launch window for the launch of
 INSAT-4A due to non-availability of suitable co-passenger satellite and also due to development problems.  However,
 the Department was continuously discussing this matter with the launch service provider to secure earliest possible
 launch.  The launch of       INSAT-4A is now fixed for the week starting from 21
st December 2005 and the spacecraft
 has already been transported to the launch site in Kourou.

            The Committee notes the reply.

Recommendation
            The Committee hopes that by this programme all the local bodies, district offices and all people involved in
 planning at the rural areas would be connected resulting into dissemination of vital information.  The Committee

 is taken care by the Associating Agencies.

TELEMEDICINE
Recommendation
The Committee is of the opinion that if better health services are to be provided to smaller towns and remote
 villages, there is an urgent need for establishment of telemedicine network in the country on a wider scale.  Moreover, at
 present the medical community is not fully aware of the development in the field of telemedicine.  The Committee,
 therefore, feels that there is a need for widespread effort to create awareness amongst the doctors and the public for
 adopting and reaping the benefits of this new advancement.  Apart from this, Telemedicine should be introduced as a
 topic at the graduate level.                                                                         (Para 33)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department has established 115 Telemedicine facilities in the country covering 88 remote district hospitals
 connected to 27 specialty hospitals in different States including far-flung areas like North-Eastern States, J & K,
 Andaman and Lakshadweep islands.  The Telemedicine network will be expanded in a phased manner   to cover more
 States.  As a part of creating awareness on use of Telemedicine, a regional workshop on Telemedicine for North East
 Region was conducted last year in Manipur wherein representatives of various hospitals in North Eastern States
 participated.  Further, to create national level awareness among doctors, health administrators and health-care providers,
 an international conference on Telemedicine – INTELMEDINDIA 2005 was organised which was attended by more
 than 800 delegates.  The conference also emphasized the need to introduce Telemedicine as a topic at the graduate level
 and some of the institutes like Sanjay Gandhi Post-Graduate Medical Institute have already taken initiatives in this
 regard.  To popularize Telemedicine, a Telemedicine society is in the formative stage and will have members from
 technology, medical profession and medical administration and ISRO is taking active part in this endeavour.  This
 matter is being actively pursued by ISRO.

 prepared for the Shillong – Silchar – Aizawl highway.  Plans are afoot to undertake information generation using
 satellite data for the priority multi-hazard prone districts of the country as part of the National Disaster Management

CHANDRAYAAN-I
Recommendation
            The Committee in its 129
th Report on Demands for Grants (2004-2005) had suggested for fulfilment of some
 scientific objectives as research perspective as a part of the Chandrayaan-I Mission.  The Committee would further like
 to recommend that in addition to the existing moon mission objectives of the Department of Space, possibility of
 extraction of Helium-3 from the Moon soil could also be examined.  It is expected to be the cleanest fuel for 21 st
 century fusion reactors because its reaction is efficient and at the same time produces low residual radioactivity.  Helium
 3 is deposited on the lunar regolith by solar winds.  So far as extraction of the Helium 3 from lunar regolith is
 concerned, which has been stirred by collision with meteorites, Helium 3 is available at several meters depth of the lunar
 surface.  The Committee has been given to understand that the greatest concentration of Helium 3 will be found on the
 lunar maria.  Since it is extremely potent, non-polluting, with virtually no radioactive by-products, the Committee feels
 that Helium 3 is the perfect fuel source for the current century.  Moreover, Helium 3 fusion energy may be the key to
 future space exploration and settlement.                           (Para 57)
Action Taken by the Department
The suggestion of the Committee on possible extraction of Helium
3 from lunar soil is noted.  Such an objective
 can be met by lunar lander/robotics/rover/sample return mission.  The Chandrayaan-1 mission is a remote sensing
 mission in lunar orbit primarily meant for high-resolution chemical and topographic mapping of the moon including
 detection of various mineral deposits on the lunar surface.  Thus, extraction of Helium3 is beyond the scope of this
 mission.  However, the Chandrayaan-1 data is likely to identify potential lunar areas for Helium 3 extraction through the
 detection of a specific titanium bearing mineral/ilmenite that can efficiently  trap solar wind Helium 3 incident on the
 lunar surface.  Ilmenite (Ti) rich areas will be ideal site for planning Helium3 extraction in future lunar landing missions
 as and when planned.  It may also be noted that fusion technology required for generation of clean and unpolluted
 energy from Helium3 is yet to be developed in the world.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.

SAFETY MEASURES AGAINST CRYOGENIC PROPELLANTS
            The cryogenic Propellants, especially liquid hydrogen is highly flammable and produces invisible cold flame. 
 Due to high liquid vapour expansion, it is liable to produce large volume of explosive cloud which is very sensitive to
 ignition.  The Committee, therefore, feels that before using cryogenic propellants very elaborate safety precaution is
 required to be taken since some of the earth storable propellants are toxic in nature and their exposure to human
 population can lead to serious environmental and biological consequences and, therefore, their level of exposure should
 be made limited and preventive steps should be taken against high level exposure because it has disastrous
 consequences.  The Committee, therefore, feels that adequate safety precaution during storage, handling and servicing
 of liquid hydrogen, needs to be taken in order to avoid any occupational hazard that may arise in
 future.                                                                 (Para 63)
Action Taken by the Department  (Paras 60, 61 & 63)
Well documented Safety procedures and precautionary measures are in place in Department of Space in terms of
 Storage, Handling and Transportation of Solid, Liquid, Auxiliary and Cryogenic propulsion systems.  These are
 periodically reviewed and updated.   Adequate safety measures have been taken into consideration to avoid
 environmental impact that may arise due to the nature of system being used for putting rockets into space.
Point raised by the Committee
The Department has mentioned that adequate safety measures have been taken into consideration to avoid
 environmental impact that may arise due to the nature of system being used for putting rockets into space. The details of
 these measures may be enlisted.
Clarification by the Department
Vapours and effluents generated during handling of earth storable propellants are treated in scrubbers and
 effluent plants to meet statutory requirements to avoid air and water pollutions before letting out to atmosphere or
 ground. Safety measures implemented include Fire fighting system, pollution monitors, use of personnel protective
 equipment, impermeable lining for storages, in-built safety devices for equipments & fluid circuits, well documented
 elaborate operational check lists, training to operational crew at regular intervals, safety auditing etc,.  
The principal hazards associated with the handling of cryogenic propellants are cold burn, asphyxiation, fire and
 explosion. Safety measures implemented include Automatic deluge system, Fire hydrant system, Hydrogen & oxygen
 detectors, Heat & smoke detectors, Electrical equipment as per code for hazardous area, static earthing, lightning

            The Committee is of the opinion that since the findings of the Space Physics Laboratory have a direct bearing
 over the quantum of environmental pollution due to aerosol eroding in the immediate atmosphere over the biosphere of
 the earth and also its influential impact over the predictions of the weather pattern and climate pattern in the atmosphere,

 pollution and predicting weather and climate changes in the near future.                          (Para68)
Action Taken by the Department
Under the ISRO Geosphere Biosphere programme (IGBP), the atmospheric aerosol optical depth over various
 bio-geographical zones across the country is studied to understand the aerosol radiative forcing. The salient findings of
 the study are discussed by an IGBP working group consisting of specialist scientists in the area of climate observations
 and modelling in the country including scientists from IMD.

AMMONIUM AND STRONTIUM PERCHLORATE POLLUTION AND CONTROL
 
Recommendation      
In this connection, the Committee would like to mention that although Ammonium Perchlorate (AP) and
 Storntium Perchlorate (SP) are necessary as oxidizer in solid motors and for injectant for Thrust Vector in control
 system of Launch Vehicles for rockets, necessary precautionary measures should be taken by Department of Space due
 to environmental pollution potential of AP & SP before using them as oxidizer and injectant in motors for rockets.  
 (Para 73)
Action Taken By The Department
Ammonium Perchlorate (AP) as an oxidizer in solid propulsion system and Strontium Perchlorate (SP) as
 injectant for Thrust control Vector in control system are being used in Launch Vehicles since 1970.  Adequate Safety
 and precautionary measures are being followed in Department of space in terms of Production, Storage, Handling and
 Transportation of the above chemicals to avoid environmental pollution before using them as oxidizer and injectant in
 motors for rocket.

Main environmental problem during production, storage and handling of Ammonium and strontium per chlorate
