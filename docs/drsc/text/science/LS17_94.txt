INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment &
 Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Ninety-fourth Report on the
 Action Taken by the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research on the recommendations/observations contained in the
 Eighty-sixth Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research.
 
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research Scientific
 & Industrial Research on the recommendations/observations contained in the Eighty-sixth Report of the Department-related
 Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of
 the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research  which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on 18
th April, 2001.
 
There are twenty-two recommendations contained in the Eighty-sixth Report of the Committee.  Action Taken Notes on
 the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 31st July, 2001.
 
In the meeting held on 28 th November, 2001 the Committee considered the draft Action Taken Report and adopted the
 same.
 
 
 
NEW DELHI;                                                                                       C. RAMACHANDRAIAH,

R E P O R T
  
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research
 on the recommendations contained in the Eighty-sixth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
 Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Department
 of Scientific & Industrial Research which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on 18 th April, 2001.  There are twenty-two
 recommendations contained in the Eighty-sixth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of
 the Committee were received from the Department    on 31st July, 2001.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by
 the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
 
Recommendation
      The Committee commends the steps being taken by the Department in augmenting its scientific and industrial
 capabilities towards the economic and social development of the Country.  The Committee however recommends that
 besides the on-going research projects, the Department should also support employment generating research such as
 research on Bamboo from which millions of people earn their livelihood worldwide.  This would help in generating
 employment among rural people.
(Para 10)
Action Taken by the Department
      The Steering Committee for formulation of Tenth Five Year Plan on S&T has assigned to the Department of Science &
 Technology the formulation of the mission on Bamboo.  CSIR would provide the requisite inputs for the mission.
Comments
            While the Committee notes the reply with satisfaction, it would like the Department to identify more of such
 projects which can generate employment without any investment on the part of the rural folk and thus help them

            The Committee conveys its appreciation for the efforts made to bring about reduction in the staff strength. 
 The Committee hopes that the Department would keep its word in reducing the staff strength to 20,000 by the end
 of 10th plan.

Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation
      The Committee feels that in order to make available best talent it is necessary that at least the minimum needs of
 employees are taken care of in this fast changing world.  It is all the more necessary because the monetary benefits given
 by the Fifth Pay Commission have to a large extent been neutralised by high cost of services and rising costs of living.  The
 employees, be they of Central Government or elsewhere are again on subsistence wage level with high component of
 personal taxation whereas according to Taylor’s concept of scientific management, a satisfied worker is an asset to an
 organisation.  The Committee therefore feels that every possible effort including adequate budgetary support should be
 provided for amenities like housing.
(Para 16)
Action Taken by the Department
            CSIR express its gratefulness to the Committee for its recognition and adequate budgetary support for Housing. 
 Government are also seized of the need to provide housing facilities to scientific and other staff.  Thus it has been making
 budgetary allocation to CSIR as per its requirements.  CSIR is making all possible efforts to raise the level of satisfaction
 at the metros and other towns where the housing conditions are difficult.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.
 
NATIONAL LABORATORIES
Recommendation
            While the Committee is aware that the year 2001-2002 being the terminal year of the Ninth Five Year Plan,
 the emphasis has to be given on completion of on-going schemes, the Committee would however like the DSIR to
 endeavour to bring about diversification in R&D work.  While the user services for the industry may continue to
 be provided, prioritization and diversification of research may help decrease the dependence on non-plan
 expenditure.  Efforts should also be made to secure research work from abroad to the extent possible.
(Para 18)

Action Taken by the Department
            Over the last decade and especially since 1995, CSIR has renewed its efforts to work closely with the user
 organisations to commercialise its technologies. This was with the view to help industry and users on one hand and
 supplement CSIR’s budgetary resources on other hand to meet the growing expenditures particularly the Non Plan.  CSIR
 today is meeting around 27% of its total expenditure from the contract R&D, Royalty, S&T services and thus lessening
 the dependence on non-plan expenditure.  While CSIR would make all efforts to secure more research work from abroad,
 its primary responsibility is to service and serve Indian industry & users.
Comments
            While the Committee notes the reply, it would like the Department to continue its endeavour to increase its
 earnings from the present level of 27% from Contract R&D, Royalty, S&T services etc.  At the same time, in order
 to bring about equilibrium between the resources available and the work force efforts may be made not to fill the
 vacancies caused by resignations, retirement etc. to the extent possible instead of taking recourse to harsh steps
 such as VRS etc.
 
Recommendation
            The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation made in 73
rd report and observes that there is a need
 to progressively modernise the CSIR Laboratories.  The Committee observes that the process of modernisation
 should be time bound.  The Department should also spell out precisely the areas of modernisation being
 undertaken/ proposed to be undertaken in the laboratories.  The Department should also ensure that the
 modernisation matches the international standards.  Also, the Committee should be apprised about the training
 being imparted/proposed to be imparted to the scientists for handling the upgraded/modernised laboratories.  The
 Committee, however, recommends that CSIR should continue its modernisation in the next plan period.
(Para 22)
Action Taken by the Department
            With the support and recommendations from the Parliamentary Standing Committee, the Modernisation Plan was
 implemented in a time bound manner during the Ninth Plan period.  The phase-wise modernisation plan for upgradation
 of equipment and R&D facilities at all the National Laboratories was approved by the Government and implemented.  By
 the end of the current financial year, this phase is expected to be over.  CSIR is seized with the matter of modernisation to
 continue and as recommended by the Committee, the next phase of modernisation proposed for the Tenth Five Year Plan
 relates to Infrastructure Renovation and Refurbishment.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.  It would, however, like to be apprised in brief about the modernisation
 brought about in the laboratories, giving details laboratory-wise so that the Committee could consider visiting any
 of these laboratories during its visit to those areas.  The Committee would also like the modernisation to be made
 an integral part of each of the laboratories notwithstanding the phases of recession like the one through which the
 Indian economy, like many other countries of the world, is passing through.
 
NATIONAL S&T HUMAN RESOURCES DEVELOPMENT
Recommendation
            The Committee observes that the Universities and Institutions will have to play a meaningful role to achieve
 widespread excellence in both teaching quality and high calibre human resources.  Therefore, there is a need for
 reviewing and strengthening/revamping the existing S&T supports systems in the universities and other
 educational institutions, be it in terms of infrastructure or quantum of funding as well as operational structures
 and mechanisms.  The issues related to the institutional mechanisms, policy guidelines, approval and clearance of
 procedures should be looked into with a view to simplify and make them user friendly.
(Para 26)
Action Taken by the Department
            The Scientific Advisory Committee to the Cabinet is seized with the matter of creating suitable mechanisms and
 policy directions to bring in together Universities/Institutions and S&T support systems for developing high calibre
 human resources.  The Steering Committee on Science & Technology for the Tenth Five Year Plan inter alia has also
 dwelt on the issue on interface between industry, R&D institutions and academia for S&T.  The recommendations of the
 Steering Committee are to be submitted to Planning Commission, which in turn, is expected to appropriately reflect the
 issues in its Report on Tenth Five Year Plan.
Comments
            The Committee notes the position.

Recommendation
            The Committee feels that CSIR should also consider introducing Scientific Research Scholarships to school
 leaving students in order to encourage them to pursue a career in S&T related areas.  National Women Scientific
 Research Award could also be introduced to the outstanding women researchers to encourage their partnership in
 the mainstream.  These initiatives would help in accelerating the pace of scientific & technological research for the
 development of a strong knowledge base and its application for the welfare of humankind.
(Para 27)
Action Taken by the Department
CSIR has already initiated a scheme known as CSIR Programme for Youth Leadership in Science (CPYLS)
 wherein top 50 students from each of the State Boards passing the X class exam are encouraged and motivated to take up
 science as a career.  They are invited for two days to nearest CSIR lab along with their teacher/parent, at CSIR cost, to
 experience and see the excitement and adventure of science, those who pursue science are assisted in the project work at
 the CSIR laboratory.  Those students passing XII class exam with 90% marks in science are further encouraged to pursue
 science at degree level by giving them the opportunity to work for 14 weeks/year for three years in any of the CSIR labs
 during vacations.  They are called ‘CSIR Students Associate’ and are given a stipend of Rs.500/- a week.  These Students
 Associates would also be provided support to pursue M.Sc.
The number of women scientists is maximum in life sciences, the Department of Biotechnology has accordingly
 introduced special awards for women scientists.
Comments
            The Committee commends the efforts made by the Department in promoting S&T culture in the Country. 
 It hopes that such measures will sustain the interest of  the young students in pursuing a career in S&T related
 areas.  In order to encourage more & more students, the Committee suggests that the experiences/initial
 observations of the students already availing the benefit of such schemes be obtained in writing/through their
 reactions and be made the basis/background of Newspapers/Electronic media ads with new visuals, pictures
 delivering messages.  This would help attract more students.
 
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
Recommendation
            The Committee commends the efforts made by the CSIR in maintaining its standards for Intellectual
 Property Movement by setting a formidable goal of achieving 1000 Patents and 500 foreign Patents by 2001.  The
 Committee, however, feels that the Department should create a Patent Facilitating Cell which should assist in
 writing, and completing of all formalities for filing of patents. Roving seminars should also be conducted at regular
 intervals to create  awareness on intellectual property rights and related issues.
(Para 29)
Action Taken by the Department
            The suggestion of the Committee to conduct roving seminars to create awareness on intellectual property rights and
 related issues are being initiated and it is expected that four such seminars would be conducted during the financial year. 
 It is proposed to prepare a CD based module that could enable an innovator to check the possibility of patenting an
 invention and also guide him step wise on formalities to be completed for filing of a patent application.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply with satisfaction and hopes that the steps would continue to be taken for
 creating awareness on intellectual property rights and related issues.

RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT BY INDUSTRY (RDI)
Recommendation
            The Committee observes that the socio-economic advancement of a nation demands a strong relationship
 between Industry and R&D laboratories, which unfortunately is not a reality in India.  Rather the wholesome
 involvement of national R&D with industry, which is a hallmark of the advanced industrial societies, is missing in
 India. The Committee, therefore, recommends that CSIR laboratories should put more emphasis  on  consolidating
its partnership with the industry so that the technologies developed by the laboratories can be commercialised and
 fully exploited.  More the commercialisation of technologies, more would be the confidence in the CSIR
 laboratories, providing further impetus to their R&D activities.
(Para 35)
Action Taken by the Department
            The CSIR is fully cognizant of the Committee’s concerns, however mere responsiveness of the CSIR and R&D is
 not enough.  The state of health and well being of the industry and conducive and enabling environment are needed for
 symbiosis of national R&D with industry.  These aspects of the innovation system are to be developed concurrently,
 which are beyond the ken of CSIR.  Nonetheless CSIR is making vigorous efforts to bring about such a system.
Comments
            The Committee observes that what has been stated by the Department is indeed true.  It is more so in
 periods like the one through which Indian economy is at present passing through with slump in economic activity. 
 But even in present periods competitiveness, cost controls and product innovations could be of help.  The
 laboratories must endeavour to make inroads in the industry with such qualities which help improve quality, cut
 down costs and product innovations.
 
NATIONAL INFORMATION SYSTEM FOR SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY(NISSAT)
Recommendation
            The Committee is of the view that dissemination of information can be facilitated through electronic
 network, as well as physical networks like libraries, booksellers, broadcasting companies and organization selling
 information in electronic form e.g. database available in the form of CD-ROM, DVD etc. Widespread
 dissemination of information depends upon availability of reliable and dependable networks.  Therefore, the
 Department should try to create appropriate delivery mechanisms.  Such delivery mechanisms would provide
 quality education, create a strong knowledge base and provide widespread access to information to the research
 community as well.

 System for Science and Technology (NISSAT) has been promoting information facilities on S&T by upgrading the
 existing centres, promoting resource sharing, providing access to international databases and imparting training for skill
 development, etc.
            With the development of internet technology, NISSAT is promoting and supporting developing of web-based
 information resources and information access and dissemination facilities.  NISSAT has established a number of web
 based information access facilities for the dissemination of information on specific sectors such as; iprlawindia.org,
 Indian-ocean.org, vigyan.org.in and calibnet.org. Indigenous database have been developed on various subjects like
 Directory of Indian Scientists and Database of Indian Chemical and Pharmaceuticals Industry.  These are made available
 on CD-ROMS and Vigyan server supported by NISSAT at IISc, Bangalore.  These are continuously being updated and
 efforts will be made to strengthen the delivery systems as recommended by the Committee.  
Comments

PROGRAMME AIMED AT TECHNOLOGICAL SELF-RELIANCE(PATSER)
Recommendation
The Committee notes with satisfaction the support rendered by the DSIR in the field of technology
 absorption and adaptation.  The Department should continue its efforts to interact with other Ministries for wider

 technologies.  An integrated and sustained approach would certainly help the Department in realising its projected

SCHEME TO ENHANCE THE EFFICACY OF TRANSFER OF TECHNOLOGY (SEETOT)
 
Recommendation
            The Committee is of the view that the need for transfer of technology in bringing improvement  in industrial
 productivity can not be underestimated.  The Committee is pained to observe that Transfer of Technology
 programmes have moved at snail’s pace.  It is necessary to identify the prevailing constraints and have insight of
 Industrial sector problems and then devise appropriate technologies to address the problems.  To that end, the
 Committee recommends that a multi-disciplinary team should work on priority basis for identification and
 solution of the problems being faced with regard to the technology.
(Para 48)
Action Taken by the Department
            The thrust of Committee’s observation is that it is necessary to identify & assess industrial sector’s problems and
 devise appropriate technologies to address the problems and evolve solutions through a multi-disciplinary team effort. 
 The Department considers, assesses, supports and reviews projects of industry and/or R&D organization and academic
 institutions, related to their specific problems in technology absorption, adaptation, development and commercialization. 
 This is done through inter-departmental and inter disciplinary Committees such as Technology Advisory Committees and
 Project Review Committees.  The Department through its various plan schemes promotes close linkages between the
 industry and R&D system, so as to encourage formulation and implementation of activities & programmes which are
 mutually relevant and beneficial to industrial units as well as R&D laboratories.  CSIR laboratories have been directly
 involved in conducting industrial research and are therefore, in a position to identify problems of industry in their
 respective areas of operation and work towards finding solutions for the same. 
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.

 the economy.  The Committee, therefore, feels that the scientists and all other working for transfer of technology
 should bear in mind all these points.
(Para 49)
Action Taken by the Department
            The thrust of the Committee’s observations is on careful planning and implementation of research projects, keeping
 in view the likely economic gains.  The programmes and activities of CSIR laboratories are assessed and prioritised by the

FOOD AND FOOD PROCESSING
Recommendation
            The Committee visited Central Food Technological Research Institute (CFTRI), Mysore a few years back
 and had the occasion to see some of the machines like large scale dosa making machine.  The Committee doubts
 whether there is demand for machines designed to cater large scale commercial requirement.  The Committee
 would therefore like to impress on all concerned to engage themselves in activities which are either against
 confirmed orders or can otherwise be gainfully exploited.
(Para 55)
Action Taken by the Department
            The Committee’s observations that activities should be taken up only which have either confirmed orders or can
 otherwise be gainfully exploited are well received. CFTRI efforts had always been to generate technologies that can be
 gainfully exploited, sometimes these are market led and sometimes lead the market. In respect of the dosa making
 machine CFTRI estimates the demand of 2000 numbers of dosa making machines, with 400 dosa per hour capacity, from
 large catering fast food establishments and social feeding centers.
Comments
            The Committee is not satisfied with the reply and feels that the recommendation made by the Committee
 was of general nature and was supported by an instance.  In case the recommendation was not based on just
 assumption, the facts in this regard should have been brought forward to clarify whether the instant case was
 against an order instead of giving a vague reply which did not lead the Committee to any conclusion.
 
Recommendation
            The Committee feels that in order to provide impetus to the technologies developed by the CSIR
 laboratories in food processing sector, the Department should take the initiative of interacting with all State
 Governments for providing sales and trade tax exemption, so that interest of the Industry can be sustained in
 extending the benefit of technologies to the larger sections of the society.
(Para 56)
Action Taken by the Department
            With reference to the suggestion of the Committee for taking initiatives with the State Governments for the Sales
 and Trade Tax exemption, a team of CFTRI Scientists have been formed to pursue these issues with the respective State
 Governments and CFTRI is making all efforts to bring in mechanisms for large adaptation of technologies by the State
 development departments/agencies.
 
Comments
            The Committee observes that the Department should continue its endeavours to impress upon the respective
 State Governments to waive Sales & Trade tax so that optimum exploitation of technologies can be ensured.
 
Recommendation

 pulses and oilseeds to develop marketable products and technology achieving highest recovery and top quality of
 the desired constituents/parts of the raw materials processed with minimum energy spent and no pollution created.
(Para 57)

 marketable products.  The technologies developed for primary processing of cereals, pulses and oilseeds give high priority
 to recovery with high efficiency of operation.  Nutritious and comparatively inexpensive foods have been developed
 based on oil seeds and their derived products.

 changing times.

Action Taken by the Department
            The NRDC was established to promote, develop and commercialise indigenously developed technologies.  The
 promotional activities carried out over the years through the Invention Promotion Programme and Technology Promotion
 Programme have increased sharply in recent times to assist the SMEs to face the challenges of global competition. 
 However, the budgetary support for this had remained same over the last three years.  As a result, the additional
 expenditure on these promotional activities have been met out of income from commercial activities.  The Technical

 organization to being a knowledge based, technology solutions and information providing company.  As a step in this
 direction NRDC has already launched marketing of Interactive Multimedia Training Package on Intellectual Property
 Rights.  All efforts would be made to turn around NRDC to make it more profitable.
