Introduction
 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the
 Committee to submit the report on its behalf, present the Eighth Report on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the
 Department of Biotechnology (Ministry of Science and Technology) for the year 1994-95 (Demand No. 73).
2. The Committee held three meetings on March 23, 24 and April 7, 1994 (Annexure). In the meeting held on March 23,
 while considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology, the Committee sought detailed
 clarifications on various development projects, programmes and schemes which fall within the mandate of the
 Department. On its sitting held on March 24, the representatives of the Department replied to the clarifications sought
 by the Hon'ble Members. The Committee wishes to express 'its thanks to the officers of the Department of
 Biotechnology for placing before it the required material and answering the queries-raised by the Hon'ble Members on
 various aspects of the Projects and Programmes administered by the Department to enable it to thoroughly scrutinise,
 the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology.
3. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on April 7, 1994

Biotechnology is an inter-disciplinary field in which rapid advances are taking place the world over. Applications of
 biotechnology can lead to the development of processes which can contribute significantly to agricultural production
 and industrial growth. In the medical field too biotechnology has many important and growing beneficial applications.
 There are many other sectors of economy which can benefit tremendously by the application of biotechnology. This

2. The Secretary of the Department of Bio-technology informed the Committee that the Department of Biotechnology
 has made steady progress· during the year 1993~94 in fulfilling its mandate. He particularly referred to basic research,
 product based R&D projects, demonstrations and transfer of technologies, manufacturing activities; manpower
 development and generation of new products and processes. He informed the Committee about the new initiatives taken
 up last year which would lead to sharply focused research based products and activities. The Committee was also
 informed about the proposed programmes of the Department for the year 1994-95 which were mainly concerned with
 research and development, experimental field demonstrations, manpower development, creation of. infrastructure
 facilities, activities of the autonomous institutions and public undertakings of the Department, international
 collaboration, bio-safety and programmes for the weaker sections.            
3. After listening to' the presentation made by the Secretary of the Department of Biotechnology, the Committee
 examined the budgetary allocations made for various programmes falling within the mandate of the Department.

While examining the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology, the Committee also got an opportunity
 to assess the progress of various ongoing programmes of the Department.
4. The budget estimates for the Department of Biotechnology for the year 1994-95 are places at Rs. 90.23 crores, 'of
 which 87.00 crores form the plan component and Rs. 3.23 crores form the non-plan component. The Committee is
 pleased that such a large part of the budget is under Plan, reflecting new initiatives that are monitored for useful and
 results, and not under non-plan which normally reflects only the running of an existing set-up. The budget estimates for
 the Department for the year 1993-94 were Rs. 88.10 crores, out of which the plan component was Rs. 85.00 crores and
 non-plan was Rs. 3.10 crores. Comparing the budgetary outlay of the Department. for the year 199~-95 with the outlay
 earmarked for the year 1993-94, 1992-93 and 1991 .. 92 the Committee found that the position with regard to the
 utilisation of the plan component of the annual allocation by the Department has greatly' improved. In the year 1991-92
 the approved plan outlay was Rs. 71.00 crores, while the utilisation was Rs. 61.34 crores. In the year 1992-93, the
 approved plan outlay was Rs. 80.33 crores and the Department could utilise Rs. 73.16 crores. In the year 1993-94 the
 situation further improved with regard to the plan component of .the budgetary allocation. Out of the approved outlay
 of Rs. 85.00 crores, the Department could utilise a sum of Rs. 84.95 crores. The Committee expresses its satisfaction
 over the narrowing down of the gap between the approved plan outlay and its utilisation and hopes that this- trend
 would be maintained during the current financial year as well.

 the Department.

7. On being asked why no provision has been made for the Oil Palm Demonstration Project in the budget estimates for
 the year 1994-95, the Committee was told that the Project taken up in the state of Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra and
 Karnataka has been completed. The Department did not propose to expand the project further, though several R&D
 projects on oil palm like tissue culture, pollination, oil quality, etc. would be taken up and these will be covered under
 the 'Basic and Product based R&D Projects'. The Committee is happy to note that the Oil Palm Demonstration Project
 has been completed but would like the Department to devote its efforts towards R&D pertaining to oil palm and give
 more attention to the developing of a package on feasibility and performance of oil palm under different climatic
 'conditions as this would help more farmers in the country.
8. The Committee is aware of the fact that biofertilisers cannot totally replace chemical fertilisers. Biofertilisers, by
 adding organic matter, help in maintaining the soil health. Their use can contribute to reduced requirement of chemical
 fertilisers in intensive agriculture. They can be of relevance to special crops and agroecological situations:'" As per the
 information furnished by the Department in the area of Demonstration with regard to Blue Green Algae (BGA) during
 the year 1993-94 there were 1500 demoristrations conducted and about 600 production ponds were made. 38 tonnes of
 BGA was produced and the increase in the paddy yield ranged from about 5 to 14 percent. As regards Rhizobium, 224
 field trials were done and three training programme were organised. In the area· of biological control of crop pests, the
 crops covered were sugarcane, cotton, chickpea, tobacco, tomato, groundnut, sunflowers, blackgram, green- gram,
 pigeonpea, sesamum, cauliflower and brinjal. Two production units were also set up. In view of the importance of
 biofertilisers and biopesticides which are. eco-friendly and safer, the Committee expresses its satisfaction over the' fact
 that the Department is having two separate Mission Mode Projects, one on Technology development and demonstration
 of biofertilisers and the other on the development, production and demonstration of biological pest control agents.
9. The Committee noticed that investment in the' Indian Vaccine Corporation Limited was made to the tune of Rs.
 148.00 lakhs, as per the revised estimates for the year 1993-94 but in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95, the
 allocation has come down to Rs. 10.00 lakhs only. The Department explained that this joint sector project with French
 collaboration is under re-negotiation to finalise the 'product mix'. Depending on the outcome of the negotiations with
 the French Company, the project would be re-defined and investment decision would be taken. Due to this a token
 provision of Rs. IO.OO lakhs has been made. The Committee, therefore, recommends that concerted efforts should be
 made to finalise the negotiations with a view to implementing the project. The Committee agrees that the use of Oral
 Polio Vaccine is very vital for control of polio in India and the Department should make every effort to ensure adequate
 reliable supplies of this vaccine to control and largely eliminate polio from India.
10. The Committee wanted to know the reasons as to why a provision of Rs. 64.50 lakhs has been made in the budget
 estimates for the year 1994-95 under Allowance (other than travel expenses) while in the revised estimates for 1993-94
 the sum under this category was Rs. 48.00 lakhs only? The Committee was informed that this increase is partly due to
 the expectation that the vacant posts in the Department would be filled up during the year 1994-95. At present there are
 52 posts lying vacant in the Department. The Committee is firmaly of the view that due to inadequate staffing the
 programmes undertaken by the Department may get adeversely affected and in this regard the Committee had in its
 Fifth Report already recommended for the filling up of the vacant positions in the Department. The Committee hopes
 that during the year 1994-95 most of the vacancies existing in the Department would be filled up. The Committee is
 firmly of the view that no time should be wasted on bureaucratic aspects in this regard. The Department was created out
 of the Department of Science and Technology and is part of the Ministry of Science & Technology, personal policies,
 recruitment rules, etc, already exist in the Department of Science & Technology and can be made use of without delay;

 scientists working in other countries to return to India what is important is that scientists working in the country should

11. The Committee pointed out that in the budgetary outlay for 1994-95 for creating infrast­ructural facilities the
 Department had demanded Rs. 10.00 crores, while it was given a sum of Rs. 7.90 crores. In view of this gap the
 Committee specifically wanted to know as to how the Department would be able to work in the area of creating infra
 structural facilities for its various programmes. The representative of the Department informed the Committee that the
 Planning Commission is of the view that the Department has already created several infrastructural facilities for
 collection, maintenace and supply of biotechnological material. Funds are also provided to the host institutions for
 undertaking various R&D programmes. Planning Commission is, therefore, of the view that efforts should be made to
 transfer some of these facilities to host institutions for maintenance of the facilities and Department of Biotechnology
 may provide research support on project-mode basis, and that it would not be appropriate to support such facilities for
 an indefinite period. The Committee was also informed that the Department of Biotechnology and Department of
 Science and Technology along with the CSIR and other Ministries are now making efforts to provide joint funding for a
 number of facilities. While the Committee agrees that joint funding of high-tech facilities involving large sum of money
 may be implemented, what is important is to ensure that such facilities are shared and fully utilized by the user
 Departments and all those in the country who need them and can benefit from them. The Committee is of the view that
 the facilities so set up should be made use of to the fullest extent; and it is the institutional set up for this to which
 attention needs to be devoted; since funding, whether through one Department or on a multi-institutional basis, is all by
 Government.
12. The Committee evinced keen interest in the immunological approaches to fertility. control and wanted to know
 whether any time-schedule has been fixed to arrive at some definite approaches. The Committee was informed that the
 project on immunological approaches to fertility control was launched in 1987 and has gone behind schedule, After
 completion of phase-II clinical trials, a decision was taken that there should be an extended phase-II clinical trials by
 the Expert Committee. With the approval of the Drugs Controller of India the Department has taken on these extended
 phase-II clinical trials. At the end of 1995, when the extended phase-II clinical trial enters the phase-III clinical trial, the
 Department would be able to give a time-schedule about the availability of vaccine in the market. The representative of
 the Department informed that the development of vaccine generally takes 10 to 15 years. The Committee shares the
 concern of the Department of Biotechnology in ensuring that the product which would ultimately result from the
 researches conducted by the Department should be totally 'safe and acceptable.
13. Under the project on semi-intensive prawn aquaculture a production of 10tlhalyr in two crops was demonstrated and
 the average production in the final crop was 5.49tA1a and production levels went as high as 6.7t1ha. The programme
1
 according to the Department, has given a great impetus to prawn farming by demonstrating techno-economic viability
 of the system. The Department is having a Missiun Mode Project on semi-intensive prawn aquaculture for freshwater,
 brackishwater and marine species. The Committee was also given to understand that the feed developed at the Central
 Food Technological Research Institute (CFTRI), Mysore, tested in a Semi-intensive prawn aquaculture system, gave
 results comparable to imported feeds. The cost of the feed comes to Rs. 25.00 per kg. as against about Rs. 35.00 per kg.
 of the imported feed. The indigenous feed developed at CFTRI, Mysore has been tried at Nellore in the semi­-
intensive aquaculture where the production level was 5t1ha/crop as against that of imported feed in which the average
 production level is 5.49t/ha/crop. The indigenous feed developed is still to be transferred to the industry for large scale
 production. The Committee observes that India has a vast coast line. There is, therefore, a great possibility to increasing
 prawn yields. lnstead of restricting to a few varieties, possibilities should be explored to develop many other varieties
 and this is an area which needs to be strengthened. The Department should make efforts to transfer the technology of
 producing feed to companies and ensure joint B&D industry work on this which would enable the country to save on
 the importing of feed. The Department should also devote itself to the developing of seed. To meet the shortage of
 prawn seed more hatcheries should be set up for developing seed and in this regard greater coordination is required
 among the Department of Biotechnology; the Ministry of Agriculture and the concerned Departments of the State
 Governments.

 wanted to know whether the Department has developed some indigenous tests which are less costly than the imported
 test kits. The Committee was informed that the kits currently used by the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO)

15. The Committee was told that more bio-technology based projects would be generated for the uplift of the SC/ST
 population and other weaker sections in different parts of the country. There is already an expert committee appointed
 to develop biotechnology based programmes for these sections of society,. The Committee has noted with satisfaction
 that in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 the allocation made for the biotechnology programmes for the
 Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes has increased from Rs. 50.00 lakhs made last year .to Rs. 100.00 lakhs this year
 and recommends that the new technologies developed by the Department should lay special emphasis on the needs of
 the women. The new technologies should aim at making them economically self-sufficient which would naturally
 enhance their social status. This programme also requires greater coordination among the socio-economic Ministries
 and con­cerned departments of the State Governments.
16. The Committee also wanted to know about the monitoring mechanism which the Department has evolved over the
 years to evaluate the efficiency and output of the projects administered by it; and the exact criteria which the
 Department adopts while assessing the efficiency of its programmes and the results achieved by it. The Committee was
 informed that the Department has been working with the expert advice of 13 Task Forces. 4 new Task Forces have been
 set up recently. These Task Forces help in monitoring the project through evaluation, written progress reports,
 presentations, site visits, and in-depth discussions with Project Inves­tigators. The monitoring mechanism is being
 further strengthened with more frequent site visits through Expert Committees in which generally a member 'of the
 Task Force is associated. An annual review is a regular feature. The criteria used for judging the performance of a
 project are as follows :
 
Progress vis-a-vis each objective
 Number of publications
(c) Ph.D. Thesis
(d) No. of trained  manpower
 (e) Patents
(f) New products or processes
(g) Technology package
(h) Societal impact
17. The Committee notes that this Department is the nodal department for biotechnology and the results stemming from
 its work have to be implemented through other Ministries of the Government of India and of the State Governments
 concerned with food, health, industry, etc. The latter have to ensure large scale field demonstrations, availability of
 inputs and final effective utilization. It is, therefore, essential to have appropriate high level institutional set up for
 coordination and effective project management. This issue and the related aspect of setting up a Biotechnology
 Commission will be considered by the Committee when it further examines the work of this Department. Meanwhile,
 the Department should give thought to these questions.
18. After making a detailed scrutiny of the Demands for Grants of the Department of Bio-technology and assessing the
 progress of various important programmes and projects administered by it, the Committee recommends that the
 Demands for Grants of Rs. 90.23 crores, as sought by the Department of Biotechnology for the year 1994-95, under
 demand No. 73", are in order and may be approved.

2. The Chairman at the outset informed the Members that the Committee had as on date (7-4-1994) completed a tenure
 of one year and was being reconstituted w.e.f. April 8, 1994. He thanked the Members for extending him their whole-
hearted co-operation in conducting the proceedings of the Committee smoothly and taking keen interest in its
 deliberations as a result of which it was possible for the Committee to present seven reports to both the Houses. He
 expressed the hope that since almost all the Members of the existing Committee had been re-nominated on the
 reconstituted Committee he would continue to have their co-operation as before and the Committee would function
 with renewed vigour.
3. The Committee then took up for consideration its draft Eighth and Ninth Reports on the Detailed Demands for Grants
 (1994-95) of the department of Biotechnology and the Ministry of Environment and Forests, respectively and adopted
 the same, without any amendments.
4. The Committee decided to present these reports to both the Houses of Parliament on the 18th April, 1994. The
 Committee authorised its Chairman and in his absence, Shri Ajit P.K. Jogi to present the Reports to the Rajya Sabha
 and Kumari Frida Topno and in her absence, Shri Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh to lay the Reports on the Table of the Lok
 Sabha.
5. The Committee thereafter took up further consideration of the Detailed Demands for Grants (1994-95) of the
 Department of Electronics and heard the replies of the Secretary and other representatives of the Department to the
 clarifications sought by the Members on various points relating to the budgetary allocations as proposed in these
 Demands for Grants.
6. The Committee concluded its deliberations on the Demands for Grants of the Department of Electronics.
A verbatim record of the proceedings was kept.
7. The Committee then adjourned at 5.05 P.M. to meet again at 11.00 A.M. on Friday, the 8th April, 1994.
