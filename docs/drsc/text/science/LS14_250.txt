REPORT
The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Ministry of Earth Sciences
on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Forty Third Report of the Department-
related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on
the Demands for Grants (2013-14) of the Ministry of Earth Sciences, which was presented to both
the Houses of Parliament on 8
th May, 2013. There were nineteen recommendations in the Report
of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee was received
from the Ministry on 26
th September, 2013. The Ministry has intimated that it has accepted all the
recommendations of the Committee. The Committee, on examination of Action Taken Notes,
however, is not satisfied with action taken by the Ministry in respect of six recommendations and
would like the Ministry to take further action in respect of such recommendations as indicated in
the Report.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee
on the Action Taken by the Ministry of Earth Sciences are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:–
Recommendation (Para 5.3)
The Committee feels that undue delay in devolution of appropriate financial and
administrative powers to the Earth Commission, which is responsible for formulating, overseeing
and implementing the policies and programmes of the Ministry, hampers the overall functioning of
the Ministry. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its recommendation made in its 226th Report that
immediate steps be taken to provide financial and administrative powers to the Earth Commission
to enable it to effectively carry out its assigned role.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry expresses sincere gratitude to the Committee for supporting the proposal on
administrative and financial power to Earth Commission. In this regard, the Ministry would like to
apprise the Committee that a draft Cabinet Note of the proposal on administrative and financial
powers to the Earth Commission has already been circulated to all the concerned Ministry/agencies.
Currently, their comments on the same are being obtained.
Comments
The Committee notes with displeasure that Ministry had furnished similar reply in
its Action Taken Notes for the Demands for Grants (2012-13). From the Action Taken Notes
of the Ministry, one could gather that no progress has been made on the issue since last
year. It also gives an impression that the Ministry had not made sincere efforts towards it.
The Committee accordingly reiterates its recommendation and directs the Ministry to
ensure that a decision on delegation of appropriate powers to Earth Commission is taken
at the earliest and in any case by the end of this financial year.
Recommendation (Para 7.3)
The Committee notes with happiness that the Ministry has been able to obtain high RFD
scores, in the years 2010-11 and 2011-12, which is indicative of the fact that the monitoring and
review mechanism adopted by the Ministry has been successful in timely implementation of

2
programmes. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would maintain the same level of performance
of its various schemes and even aim to secure a better RFD score, in the current fiscal.
Action taken by the Government
Thanking the Committee for appreciating the performance of the Ministry during the last
2 years, the Ministry would like to assure that every effort will be made to maintain the current
level of performance or more during the Financial Year 2013-14.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 8.2)
The Committee notes that the Ministry has been provided an increased Budgetary support
of R9506.00 crores during the Twelfth Plan, which is R2502.00 crores more than the amount of
R7004.00 crores provided during Eleventh Plan Period. It also notes that during the Twelfth Plan,
the Ministry has been successful in monitoring and implementing its programmes in a more
coordinated way towards achieving its targets, after the reorientation of its 76 projects into
14 major programmes. The Committee also hopes that with judicious planning and execution, the
Ministry would be able to utilize the increased allocation for the Twelfth Plan in a more efficient
manner and achieve its targeted objectives.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry has taken all the necessary steps for judicious utilization of funds allocated for
the current financial year to achieve the target for Twelfth Five Year plan. Currently, the half yearly
financial target has been accomplished for the year 2013-14. The same phase of expenditure is
anticipated for utilization of funds efficiently.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 9.2)
The Committee notes with concern the gross underutilization of funds by the Ministry over
the years 2009-10 to 2011-12, which is evident from the decreasing trend of percentage utilization
of allocated funds at B.E. stage, i.e. from 89.05% in 2009-10 to 74.85% in 2011-12. It also notes
that in the year 2012-2013, the B.E. outlay of R1668.00 crores was cut down to R1195.00 crores
at R.E. stage, due to resource crunch and the fact that the percentage utilization of B.E. 2012-13
was only 40% till September, 2012, which again points towards the underutilization of funds. The
Committee takes a strong view of the gross underutilization of funds and feels lack of proper
monitoring of projects on the part of the Ministry is the principal reason for underutilization of
funds. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should only seek funds to the
extent it can utilize and should meticulously monitor its spending from B.E. stage itself, so that
huge underutilization of funds could be avoided. The Committee further recommends that plan
outlay for the year 2013-14 should be adequately enhanced to offset reduction of plan allocation
made at RE stage in the year 2012-13 so that the total plan outlay of the Department for the
Twelfth Five Year Plan does not get reduced and the Department is able to achieve all of its plan
objectives for the Twelfth Five Year Plan.

3
Action taken by the Government
As recommended by the Committee, the Ministry has been continuously monitoring the
progress of various schemes to achieve the desired objectives. The fund allocation has been
carefully planned to meet the shortfall of last year requirement in certain programs and to avoid
underutilization of funds for the current financial year.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry and hopes that continuous monitoring
of the various schemes would help the Ministry in checking underutilization of funds.
Recommendation (Para 10.5)
The Committee is of the opinion that weather forecasting and its accuracy is of prime
importance, specially in view of the climatic changes which had taken place in the recent past and
the fact that agriculture in the country depends to a large extent on the monsoon and is happy to
know the accuracy of forecasting capabilities of the Department have increased significantly. The
Committee while complementing the Department on this achievement and taking note that the
Department has initiated modernization of IMD Phase-II programme with a view to further improve
the weather forecasting services, would like the Department to further improve its weather
forecasting services and make them at par with the best in the world, particularly in terms of
accuracy in prediction.
Action taken by the Government
Expressing the gratitude to the Committee for supporting the proposal “Modernization
Phase-II of IMD” it is submitted that the due to paucity of funds programme have yet not been
taken up. Memorandum for Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) and Detailed Project Report
(DPR) documents being prepared for further action. Ministry would endeavour to improve the
forecasting services by implementing modernization (Phase-II) proposal.
Comments
The Ministry has not stated whether Ministry of Finance/Planning Commission has
denied funds asked by it for the programme or it could not seek the funds due to
procedural delays. In any case, onus of seeking funds lies with the Ministry and Ministry
should not only submit its proposals properly and in time but should also convince the
Ministry of Finance/Planning Commission of necessity of making funds available for the
programme in larger national interest.
Recommendation (Para 10.6)
The Committee, keeping in view the large extent to which the Himalayas affect the weather
and monsoon pattern in the country and the need for developing deeper understanding of Himalayan
meteorology to obtain better accuracy in weather forecasting, welcomes the programme ‘Integrated
Himalayan Meteorology Programme’ undertaken by the Department.
Action taken by the Government
The Committee’s suggestions have been noted. For providing effective and user specific
Mountain Meteorological Services in holistic manner, Integrated Himalayan Meteorology programme
has already been proposed for Western, Central and Eastern Himalayas. Memorandum for

4
Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) of the project for Western and Central Himalayas has been
circulated and in the advance stage of approval from concerned authorities. Similarly “Integrated
Meteorological Services for Northeastern Region” submitted to Ministry of Development of North
Eastern Region (DoNER) for in principle approval.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 10.11)
The Committee notes with happiness that agromet advisory services have resulted in
increase in farm productivity resulting in increased availability of food and higher income generation
for farmers. The Committee further notes that agrometeorological services have brought a
substantial economic benefit of 
R50,000 crores per year, when only 10 to 15% of the total farmers
in the country were availing of its services and the same was expected to rise to
R2,11,000 crores if all the farmers avail the benefit of the agromet advisories for their agricultural
operations. The Committee is, therefore, of the view that agromet services launched by the
Ministry is a commendable initiative in the right direction and hopes that during the Twelfth Five
Year Plan, concerted efforts would be made to achieve the targeted plan of covering 10 million
farmers. The Committee also recommends that the proposal for increasing the outreach of the
agromet services from the present district level to block level, through discussions with the
Ministry of Agriculture may also be taken up in the right earnest and implemented at the earliest.
Action taken by the Government
Expressing gratitude to the Committee for appreciation, it is informed that at present
agromet advisories are being disseminated to the farming community in India through SMS and IVR
(Interactive V oice Response Technology) to farmers under Public Private Partnership mode. Under
PPP mode, IMD has so far partnered with Reuter Market Light (RML), Handygo, NOKIA, IFFCO
Kisan Sanchar Ltd. (IKSL), National Bank for Agriculture and Rural development (NABARD),
Maharashtra State Agriculture Department (Government of Maharashtra). In addition to the above,
a number of AMFUs in collaboration with other organizations like NIC, ATMA etc have also started
sending SMS to the designated farmers in the state. Besides, IMD has started sending the
advisories through recently launched KISAAN SMS portal by Ministry of Agriculture. Around
3.4 million farmers are benefited by this service. A number of organizations like Central Silk Board,
CABI International, Mahindra and Mahindra, ITC E-Choupal and others interested are in offing to
tie up with IMD for dissemination of agromet advisories. Thus with the inclusion of other firms
and more dissemination through Kisaan portal, best possible efforts will be made to reach 10 million
farmers in the country during the Twelfth Five Year Plan.
Secondly as per need of the users IMD on experimental basis will prepare block level
forecast and advisories in pilot mode. In consultation with Ministry of Agriculture 37 districts (348)
will be identified for generating service at block level. During Twelfthth Five Year Plan, District
Agromet Field will be established at 240 Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVK) in consultation with Ministry
of Agriculture.
Comments
The Committee is pleased to learn that a number of organizations are in line to tie
up with IMD for dissemination of agromet advisories. As recommended by the Committee,
the IMD on an experimental basis has decided to prepare block level forecast and advisories
in pilot mode. Besides this, in consultation with Ministry of Agriculture it has decided to

5
establish District Agromet Field at all Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs). The Committee hopes
with these measures the Ministry would be able to achieve its target of reaching 10 million
farmers in the country during the Twelfth Five Year Plan.
Recommendation (Para 11.4)
The Committee notes that the Ministry had engaged the services and participation of
research and academic institutions from India and abroad in collaborative research to improve the
monsoon forecasts. Further, 16 projects, eight from within the country and eight from abroad are
being funded under this Mission for improving prediction skills. The Committee recommends that
the Department take all necessary measures so that the programme achieves its objective of
improved capabilities for more accurate monsoon predictions.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry notes the recommendations of the Committee. The Department is taking all the
necessary measures to meet the targets of the Monsoon Mission, by encouraging more R&D
research organizations both from India and abroad to participate in the mission activities. At the
same time, we are encouraging all the MoES institutes to take necessary steps to improve the
capabilities of more accurate predictions. HPC resources for carrying out the mission activities
have already been obtained.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 11.8)
The Committee notes with satisfaction that the ‘Long Period Average’ (LPA) percentage of
accuracy attained in rainfall forecasting was almost equal to the actual rainfall occurred in the
country, every year from 2008 to 2012, barring the year 2009, when the actual rainfall was 78%
against the forecast percentage of 93. The Committee, however, observes that for generation of
accurate operational weather and climate forecasts, more research on improved models was
required, for which application of high performance computers with a computing facility of
2.5 Peta Flops was required. The Committee, therefore, feels that there is a genuine need for the
Ministry to acquire the High Performance Computers with around 2.5 Peta Flop and high
performance computing facility was essential for improving its forecasts capabilities and therefore
recommends that adequate funds for it should be made available to the Ministry at the earliest.
Action taken by the Government
The EFC for HPC (high at a cost of 
R567.16 crores has already been recommended.
Currently, the MoES is working towards approval of the Cabinet.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 12)
CLOUD MICROPHYSICS AND AIRBORNE PLATFORMS
The Committee feels that as June, 2013 is approaching, the Ministry should make all out
effort to complete the construction of the said lab as per schedule so that better understanding of

6
processes regarding interaction of clouds with aerosols is achieved and used for rain enhancement
purposes.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry appreciates the remarks made by the Committee. Necessary permission for
construction of laboratory has been taken. Ministry has taken action towards construction of
laboratory expeditiously.
Comments
The Ministry had given a categorical assurance last year that construction of the
cloud microphysics and airborne platforms would be completed by June, 2013. But till date
only permission for construction of laboratory has been sought and construction is yet to
start. The Committee finds no good reason for delay in construction of the Lab and
reiterates its recommendation.
Recommendation (Para 12.3)
The Committee notes that in view of the crucial role of airborne platforms in cloud micro
physics and aerosols interactions studies, the Committee in its 226
th report (Para 11.20) has
strongly recommended for a adequate budgetary support for such studies. The Committee
notes that due to the procedural bottlenecks, Ministry could not utilise funds allocated for it during
2012-13 and even in the budget of 2013-14 only a token provision has been made. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry should seriously pursue acquisition of Airborne Platforms so that its
studies aimed at better understanding of processes regarding interaction of clouds with aerosols and
using it for rain enhancement purposes are completed as per schedule.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry is pursuing the acquisition of the air borne platform for understanding the
aerosol inter action and cloud and precipitation process. EFC considered the proposal and
recommended the acquisition of the aircraft.
Comments
The Committee expresses its displeasure at the vague reply furnished by the
Ministry. Ministry has not explained why it has not been able to acquire so far Airborne
Platforms despite EFC recommending the acquisition of the aircraft. The Committee
accordingly reiterates its recommendation.
Recommendation (Para 13.3)
The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry was allocated only 
R 200.00 crores for
the year 2013-14, which is less than half of the amount proposed for the programme and this had
led to deferment of some important planned activities. The Committee is of the view that reduced
allocations may hamper the Polar Research Programmes of the Ministry and may have implications
on India’s stature amongst countries engaged in Polar Research, and feels that reduction in allocation
of this programme may be avoided to the extent possible. The Committee also hopes that the “Bharti”
station which had recently started functioning in the South Pole would be adequately funded to equip
it with the state-of the art scientific instruments for carrying out cryogenic research, so that data
regarding climate change in the polar region can be properly gathered and subsequently used for
better understanding of Indian climate and other societal benefits.

7
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry is deeply appreciative of the concerns of the Committee regarding reduced
budget allocations for the Indian Polar Programme and would like to assure the Committee that all
steps would be taken by the Ministry to ensure that the fulfillment of objectives by allocating
required budget to the extent possible there are no reductions in budget allocation for this
Programme at any stage. In this context, the Ministry would like to bring to the kind notice of
the Committee that during 2013-14, the anticipated expenses under the Polar Programme have
escalated to 
R285.36 crores, primarily on account of the recent FE increase and the proposed
initiation of the activities related to the construction of the polar research vessel.
As regards equipping the newly-constructed Bharati Station with state-of-the-art scientific
instruments, NCAOR, as the implementing Agency of the Indian Antarctic Programme has already
initiated steps to procure over 30 major and minor scientific instruments for installation at Bharati
during the forthcoming expedition (2013-14). This is in addition to equipping the station with the
requisite chemicals, reagents, glasswares etc. for carrying out the analytical work.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 13.4)
POLAR RESEARCH STATIONS
The Committee appreciates that a new research station i.e. Himadri had been established in
the arctic region of the globe which had paved the way for Indian researchers to conduct a gamut
of scientific observations in the Arctic or in North Pole. On the other hand, 3 stations were already
working in the Antarctic or in South Pole i.e. Dakshin Gangotri from 1983 and second research
establishment viz. Maitri since 1988 and the latest Antarctic station which was commissioned in
2012 i.e. Bharati.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry thankfully acknowledges the recommendation of the Committee that the
research being conducted at the Indian Stations in Antarctica should be properly co-ordinated so
that unnecessary duplication of research is avoided. The Ministry would like to assure the
Committee that all research activities by Indian Scientists in Antarctica are planned and co-ordinated
in such a way that they complement each other rather than being duplicative. In the same manner,
action has also been initiated to ensure that the research being carried out under the Arctic and
Antarctic Programmes supplement each other and is directed at addressing issues of more than
regional significance. One such programme that has been initiated aimed at an inter-hemispheric
comparison is the characterization of atmospheric aerosols over the Antarctic and the Arctic.
Another major effort in this direction being proposed under the aegis of the Belmont Forum seeks
to understand the past and present climactic variabilities in the Polar Regions vis-à-vis the Indian
monsoon and the forcing functions behind such tele-connections.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

Action taken by the Government
The Ministry has initiated necessary pre-project activities for replacement of vessels viz.,

Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 15.4)
Committee expresses displeasure at the slow pace with which procedural formalities are
being completed and even after lapse of more than one year, EFC approval for the project has not
yet been taken. The Committee recommends that all procedural formalities including approval of
EFC should be completed without any further loss of time and NCS set up within three years from
now.
Action taken by the Government
The EFC proposal for setting up of National Centre for Seismology was prepared and
circulated to the relevant Ministries/Departments for comments. The EFC meeting will be convened
shortly and the proposal will be placed for consideration of EFC.
Comments
The Committee expresses its strong displeasure over the snail pace at which the
Ministry is working on the project and lack of seriousness on its part to complete the
project within 3 years. The Committee once again directs the Ministry to expeditiously
complete the procedural formalities so that the centre is set up within the time frame
recommended by the Committee.
Recommendation (15.7)
The Committee welcomes the initiative of the Ministry to establish a location specific Bore-
Hole Geophysics lab at Karad (Maharastra) in the Koyna-Warna region to measure through bore-
holes, the properties of rocks before, during and after an earthquake. The Committee feels that
such a laboratory would help in providing knowledge and much needed data about the earthquake
processes, for aiding scientific investigations leading to better understanding about the mechanisms
of faulting, physics of earthquakes and also developing models for better earthquake forecast in
future and recommends that Ministry should ensure that the laboratory start functioning within
three years i.e. simultaneously or before the National Centre of Seismology is set up.
Action taken by the Government
The proposal for taking up deep borehole investigation in Koyna region including setting up
of a borehole geophysical lab at Karad has been cleared by EFC recently and a note for approval
of Cabinet is being processed.

9
Comments
The Committee notes that EFC has approved the proposal and hopes that the
Ministry would soon complete the procedural formalities and lab will become operational
within the time frame recommended by the Committee.
Recommendation (16.4)
The Committee notes that the Ministry proposes to undertake survey and exploration in the
deep oceanic realm along the mid-ocean ridges in the Indian Ocean for hydro thermal sulphide
deposits having high concentration of base and noble metals apart from continuing its Polymetallic
Nodules and Gas Hydrates Programmes from the funds allocated under Ocean Survey and Mineral
Resources Programme. The Committee understands that so far as its Polymetallic Nodules
Programme is concerned, it is still at exploration stage, while for Gas Hydrates Programme,
technology is yet being augmented for exploration purposes. Therefore, the dream of commercial
exploitation of these minerals or gas hydrates is still a distant dream. The Committee recommends
that the Ministry pursue with greater vigour its Mineral Resources Programme and make an
endeavor to produce concrete results from this programme in next few years.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry noted the suggestions indicated by the Committee and informs that the
focused initiatives were launched in undertaking surveys and exploration for multi-metal
hydrothermal sulphide mineralization in the Indian Ocean. Based on the results of the recent
scientific surveys carried out by it together with the available results of the studies by various other
scientific institutions, an area of 10,000 sq. km in the central and Southwest Indian Ridges has
been identified as having potential for further exploration. On the 26
th March 2013, the Ministry
filed an application with the International Seabed Authority (ISBA) for approval of a 15 years plan
of work for exploration in this area covering 100 blocks in five clusters enveloped in an overall
rectangular area of 3,00,000 Sq. km. The application is currently under consideration. Once the
application is approved, a 15-year exploration contract shall be signed with ISBA to explore
10,000 km
2 of seabed area in the Central and SW Indian Ridges.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 17.3)
The Committee is aware of the fact that rising HABs along the Indian coast had led to
serious eutrophication thereby killing a big section of fish population. The Committee had also
observed that HABs blooming was more prevalent along the west coast of the country than on the
east coast. The Committee is, therefore, of the opinion that entire scenario requires a deep study
particularly regarding the regular bloom occurrences and shell fish poisoning from algal toxin
causing human fatalities along the Indian coast. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
Ministry may take up a programme, in collaboration with other Ministries and national and
international Institutes or bodies, as may be necessary to address this issue.
Action taken by the Government
The Ministry noted the recommendation. Towards monitoring and prediction of Harmful
Algal Blooms (HAB) in the Indian EEZ, a National HAB programme was launched by Centre for
Marine Living Resources, Kochi a unit of this Ministry. This is a multi-institutional programme

10
implemented with the participation of other agencies such as the Indian National Centre for Ocean
Information Services (INCOIS), Hyderabad; IISER.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Ministry will closely monitor the
National HAB programme and ensure that HAB programme meets its objectives.
Recommendation (Para 18)
The Committee notes that the Ministry through its various schemes/programmes provides
a vast range of societal benefits such as meteorological, agromet services etc., besides being
responsible for exploring and exploiting the marine resources and conducting polar research. The
Committee after examining the various aspects of the Demands for Grants (2013-14) of the
Ministry of Earth Sciences, is of the view that Ministry should prudently utilize funds made
available to it, having due regard to recommendations contained in the report and efficiently
implement its various schemes and programmes in the year 2013-14.
Action taken by the Government
Thanking the Committee for approving the Demands for Grants (2013-14), the Ministry
would like to assure that every effort will be made to utilize the funds prudently and efficiently
towards implementation of various schemes/programmes in the year 2013-14.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
