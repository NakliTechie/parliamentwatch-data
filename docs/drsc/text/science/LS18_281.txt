INTRODUCTION
Department of Science and Technology (DST) strives to establish and foster India’s leadership and
excellence in the field of science and technology.  The Department serves as a robust facilitator and aligns
its initiatives with India’s developmental aspirations mainly to help optimise human, infrastructure and
financial resources through appropriate inter and intra-institutional mechanisms.
2. DST plays several roles which have evolved over time. DST develops S&T policies, strengthens
human resources and institutional capacities, enables development and deployment of technologies, create
opportunities for societal interventions through Sciences & Technology, establishes and engages in
mechanisms of cooperation, partnerships and alliances.  These approaches that reflect its mission ensure
a holistic systemic influence, immediate, medium and long term relevance/gains.
3. Mandate of the Department is as follows:-
z Formulation of policy statements and guidelines on Science and Technology (S&T);
z Promotion of new areas of scientific research;
z Co-ordination of inter-Departmental S&T efforts;
z Support basic and applied research in universities and other institutions;
z International S&T Co-operation;
z Support high quality research in autonomous bodies where specialization ranges from advanced
medical research materials to astronomy;
z Catalysing S&T interaction in societal programmes, and entrepreneurship development;
z Science popularisation, communication and S&T Management Information System;
z Promotion of Commercialisation of indigenous technology;
z Support to technology development programmes, technology information and assessment;
and
z Providing services in the areas of:
(i) Surveying & Mapping;
(ii) T echnology Development and Application.
4. OBJECTIVES
Main objectives of the Department of Science & Technology are as follows:
(a) Formulation of Policies relating to Science & Technology;
(b) Strengthening basic research and Expanding R&D base-Human capacity;
(c) Strengthening Basic research and Expanding R&D base-Institutional Capacity;
(d) Implementing Technology Development Programmes: from R&D to Commercialization;

2
(e) Societal interventions of Science & Technology; and
(f) International Science & Technology Co-operation.
5. ORGANISATION
5.1 The Department of Science & Technology is the nodal Department of the Ministry of Science and
Technology.  Broadly, the organisational set up of the Department has been divided into Autonomous
Institutes, Scientific Groups, Technology Development Board and Scientific Services besides the
Administration and Integrated Finance divisions. There are 19 Autonomous Institutions and 5 Science/
Engineering Professional bodies which are working in diverse areas for the promotion of Science and
Technology. In addition, one new autonomous body viz. National Centre for Molecular Material Research,
Thiruvanathapuram has been recently set up and National Innovation Foundation, Ahmedabad which was
earlier a ‘Corpus Funded’ Financial Institute has been taken over by the Department as fully funded
autonomous Research Institute. The Scientific Groups inter-alia  consists of the Science & Engineering
Research Council (SERC), National Council for Science & Technology Communication (NSCTC), National
Resources Data Management System (NRDMS), International Division, National Entrepreneurship Board
(NEB), National Science and Technology Management Information System (NSTMIS). The Scientific
Services have two subordinate offices, namely, National Atlas and Thematic Mapping Organisation
(NATMO) and Survey of India (SoI) under their administrative control.
5.2 The detailed budget allocation of the Department of Science & Technology for the year 2016-2017
was considered by the Department-related Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests
on the 31st March, 2016.

8. NEW INITIATIVES
8.1 The Department has taken some new initiatives in 2015-16.  The new initiatives conceptualised in
2015-16 will be made fully operational in 2016-17. Following are details of these schemes.
(a) National Supercomputing Mission
(b) DST-MHRD Collaboration in Impacting Research in Nanotechnology Products and Advanced
Materials (IMPRINT)

(c) DST-Ministry of Railways Joint R&D Initiative

8.2 Department of Science and Technology continued to implement its schemes and programmes during
2015-16 under six broad categories viz. Human Capacity Building in S&T; Institutional Capacity Building;
Technology Capacity Strengthening; S&T Competitiveness through Alliances, Partnerships and  R&D
Missions; Societal Contract of S&T;  Science, Technology and Innovation Policy.
8.3 The Department of Science and Technology, being the nodal Department of Science and Technology
related matters, is also contributing in a big way to the flagship schemes of Government of India such as
“Make In India” Programme, “Swachh Bharat Abhiyan” (Clean India Initiative) and “Swasth Bharat
Abhiyan”.
8.4 Some of the new initiatives started are as follows:-
(i) Technology Development for Waste Management (e-waste, biomedical, Plastic)
(ii) Advanced Manufacturing
(iii) Collaboration with Department of Heavy Industry on National Mission for Electric Mobility
(iv) Innovation and Incubation at National Institutes of Importance (Joint initiative with MHRD)
(v) Scheme for funding High Risk-High Reward Research
(vi) Early Career Research Award
(vii) National Postdoctoral Fellowship (N-PDF) scheme
(viii) Science and Technology of Y oga and Meditation (SATYAM)
(ix) North Eastern Centre for Ethno Medical Research
(x) Leveraging International S&T Cooperation:-
(a) Launch of India Science & Research Fellowship (ISRF) for scientists from neighboring

(b) A new programme with Russian Science Foundation for cooperation in Basic Sciences
(Physics, Chemistry, Earth Sciences, Biology (Medical and Agriculture), Mathematics);
(c) Launch of Indo - US Fulbright-Kalam fellowship programme on Climate Research.
8.5 DST-MHRD Collaboration in Impacting Research Innovation and Technology (IMPRINT)
Project -
Department of Science & Technology (DST) has joined hands with Ministry of Human Resource
Development (MHRD) to implement IMPRINT projects. IMPRINT projects will address major societal
and developmental needs such as healthcare, information and communication technology, energy, sustainable
habitat, Nano technology, water resources and river systems, advanced materials, security and defence,
and environment and climate.

9
8.6 The Committee welcomes the joint collaborative steps taken by the DST in association with
Ministry of Human Resources Development to implement Impacting Research Innovation &
Technology (IMPRINT) Projects and hopes that this collaboration will help much needed societal
and developmental needs.
8.7 DST-Ministry of Railways (MoR) Joint R&D Initiative
Department of Science and Technology (DST) and Ministry of Railways (MoR) worked together
for shaping a joint R&D initiative to address research problems of immediate and direct relevance to MoR
by taking up suitable projects within time span of 3-5  years with well defined deliverables. Collaboration
between MoR and DST are in the area of fuel efficiency and emission control technologies, alternate
fuels, fuel conservation in diesel traction etc. and to derive synergy based on mutual strengths. This
collaboration would add value in Swachch Bharat Mission of the Government.
8.8 Technical Research Centres
Five Technical Research Centres (TRCs) in the existing autonomous institutions of the Department
of Science & Technology have been set up. TRCs are expected to further enhance translational research
being carried out by these autonomous institutions and bring out innovative products.
8.9 Industry-relevant R&D
Recognizing the need to facilitate increased Public Private Partnerships, the Science and Engineering
Research Board (SERB) has approved a scheme that provides an opportunity for collaborative research
between academic institutions and industry. This scheme aims to bridge the gap between public funded
research and industrial R&D. The new scheme seeks to target solution driven research to address industry
specific problems.
8.10 Technology Development for Waste Management
With the objective to contribute towards “Swachh Bharat Abhiyan”, a new programme under the
Technology Development Scheme has been initiated to come up with technological solutions for the
problems faced by the country in waste management. Areas under this include hospital waste, plastic
waste, e-waste, etc.
8.11 Collaboration on National Mission for Electric Mobility
Department has formalized collaboration with Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) on working
jointly for implementation of R&D Component of  DHI’s National Mission for Electric Mobility. A  Joint
Technology Platform for Electric Mobility (TPEM) is being set up in the Department of Science &
Technology to implement the programme.
8.12 Advanced Manufacturing
Aligning with the “Make in India” agenda of the Government, the Department has initiated a programme
to promote development of advance manufacturing technologies in the country. The areas include robotics
and automation, Nano-materials, precision manufacturing, etc. The Department has informed that Call
for proposals have already been given.
8.13 The Committee welcomes the initiatives taken to make India clean, healthy and global
manufacturing hub. The Committee also values the partnership of the Department of Science &

10
Technology with the Ministries of Railways and Human Resources Development, Departments of
Heavy industry and Electronics and Information Technology  and hopes that such efforts would
give much needed philip to the Railways and provide much needed capacity building in Human
resource Sector. The Committee hopes that these initiatives will help India become cleaner and
healthier.  The Committee also hopes that adequate budgetary provisions will be made for these
ambitious schemes.
8.14 The Committee hopes that “Make In India” Programme will help India attain leadership in
global manufacturing. The Committee recommends that considering the significance of the
initiative, the Department should make every possible effort in assisting the lead Ministries for
the success of the Programme. The Committee also recommends that allocated outlays to the
Department for the Programme are fully utilised to make the Programme a success.

11. MEGA FACILITIES FOR BASIC RESEARCH
11.1 The basic research in the country is dependent on mega and capital intensive facilities created by
other countries and this practice has led to asymmetries in credit sharing. Further, Indian expertise to
build advanced scientific instruments and devices does not get fostered outside the strategic areas of
research where technology denial forces prevent the building of capacities. The DST along with Department
of Atomic Energy (DAE) has identified several areas where an effective partnership of the two departments
can bring about an effective capacity building in the academic sector for building mega facilities for basic
research. For the same purpose, a Memorandum of Understanding was signed between the DST and the
DAE.
11.2 The Mega  Facilities  for  the Basic Research Programme has twin objectives:
(i) to  enable  Indian  scientists to participate in building very large international facilities which
are manifestly multinational efforts and gain access to the same for scientific studies, and
(ii) to  create  some  facilities  of  international  standards  in  the  country. India’s participation in
the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), FAIR, the Thirty Metre Telescope (TMT), etc. falls in the
this category.
11.3 When the Department was asked the extent to which “Mega Facilities for Basic Research Programme”
is helping to create facilities for basic research in the country.  It was replied that projects under the
“Mega Facilities for Basic Research Programme” are such projects that not only require huge financial
resources but also sophisticated scientific knowledge at the very frontiers. According to the Department,
such projects also push the global technology frontiers and such projects are set up at the best possible
locations in the world and with the best possible global know-how.  However, India’s participation in
such projects worldwide is certainly helping in setting up a few such projects for basic research in the
country. For example, the India-based Neutrino Observatory (INO) project in Tamil Nadu will use Resistive
Plate Chambers as the detector element which has been pioneered by CERN, Geneva and Indian scientists
have participated in this development there as part of the CERN-India Collaboration. Participation of
Indian scientists in neutrino experiments at Fermi lab (United State)will also help in doing science at

14
India-based Neutrino Observatory. Indian scientists have made significant contributions towards the LHC
Computing Grid and established two Tier 2 Grid Computing facilities in the country for analyzing the
CERN data. This has initiated Grid Computing activities in the country, which has much wider scope of
application. The segmented mirror technology that is being learnt by the Indian astronomy community
will pave the way for establishing a large (8-10 m class) optical telescope at a suitable site in the Himalayas
at an appropriate time in future.  Starting with the hardware engagement at CERN in the 1990’s, India’s
involvement in various Mega Facilities has started feeding back into the system now. Creation of such
huge research infrastructures in the country is manifestly a multi-agency task. DST and DAE have
shown cooperation and coordination in creating such infrastructure in the country. Most of the projects
(national and international) planned for the 12th Plan period have been initiated. The National Large Solar
Telescope (NLST) could not start so far because of the uncertainty in the availability of the Merak site in
Ladakh. The permission from Ministry of Defence has been received very recently. The new roadmap for
creating such infrastructures will emerge as part of the next Plan exercise. All such projects will be
national (and also possibly international) collaborative ventures, and not solely a venture of the Department
of Science & Technology.
11.4 The Committee takes note of the initiatives taken and infrastructure developed under Mega
Facilities for Basic Research in collaboration with national and international agencies. However,
since permission of Ministry of Defence has now been received for availability of the Merak site
in Ladakh, the Committee hopes that new roadmap for creating infrastructure will be drawn at
the earliest and the National Large Solar Telescope will established at the earliest.  The Committee
also recommends that necessary budgetary provision should be made for creating such Mega
facilities.

12. LASER INTERFEROMETER GRA VITATIONAL-WA VE OBSERV ATORY (LIGO)
12.1 Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) is world’s largest night time Astronomy
Telescope to be set up in USA by several international partners.
12.2 LIGO Project facilities are located in the USA. It has been informed that a new advanced Laser
Interferometer Gravitational-wave Observatory (LIGO) will be set up in India. This will be the third such
detector in the world, the other two being in the United States. Recently, the Union Cabinet has accorded
in-principle approval for the same. 37 Indian scientists have contributed towards the recent discovery of
gravitational waves. Steps have been taken by the gravitational wave community of the country for
forging international scientific collaborations and developing human resource in this field. For this, a pan-
Indian consortium, Indian Initiative in Gravitational-wave Observations (IndIGO) was formed consisting
of researchers from premier institutions and universities of the country. IndIGO consortium has
representation in the Gravitational Waves International Committee (GWIC) which works to facilitate
international collaboration and cooperation in the construction, operation and use of the major gravitational
wave detection facilities world-wide. Indian scientists are participating in the international LIGO Science
Collaboration (LSC), through IndIGO.
12.3 As regards benefit to India, it has been informed by the Department that LIGO-India will be an
important project in the field of fundamental sciences in the country. The Observatory will be among the
most precise scientific apparatus on the Earth, with technical capability of measuring displacements
which are a billion times smaller than the size of an atom. This will help develop national capabilities in
high-technology areas such as control systems, lasers and optics and vacuum technology, well beyond
the currently available expertise in the country. This will help train highly skilled scientific and technical
manpower. The analysis of the measurements for obtaining science results will involve very sophisticated
computational and data handling infrastructure. Such a front-ranking science experiment on Indian soil
will be a source of inspiration for young and budding scientists. This will help to  launch an entirely new
discipline of gravitational-wave astronomy in the country.
12.4 From the Indian side, LIGO-India is the joint responsibility of three lead Indian institutions, Inter-
University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA)-Pune, Institute for Plasma Research (IPR)-
Gandhinagar and Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT)-Indore. There are 61 researchers
in the international LIGO collaboration and about 40 scientists and technical staff are involved in LIGO-
India project.
12.5 As regard budgetary allocation, initial budgetary allocation to the project to the tune of ` 9.7 crores
is being made for preparing a Detailed Project Report for the project, site selection and some prototyping
work.
12.6 The Committee believes that the proposed LIGO-India project has tremendous potential to
unravel the myriad mysteries of the universe. It is likely to become a tool for the Indian astronomy
in keeping up the level of excellence in research in astronomy and astrophysics of the country.
The Committee hopes that the LIGO-India project would help the country to embark on world
class international science collaborations and help India become a force to reckon with in this
field.

posed by Climate Change. The Committee also feels that there is a need for crowd management
in a better way in the science express and more such trains should be introduced so that viewers
get more time for viewing. (Para 14.6)
EFFORTS FOR RETAINING RESEARCH TALENT IN THE COUNTRY
The Committee notes the initiatives taken by the Department of Science & Technology that
have resulted in brain-gain of 235 accomplished scientists to India. The Committee recommends
that further measures should be taken to attract Indian scientists to comeback to India. For that
purpose, the Department should further increase the scholarship/stipends provided to researchers
and incentivise performance based research. (Para 15.5)
MECHANISM TO MONITOR AND REVIEW THE ONGOING SCHEMES
The Committee notes the mechanism devised by the Department of Science & Technology
to monitor and evaluate the progress of various schemes.  However, the Committee feels that
had the monitoring mechanism of the Department been so robust, underutilisation would
not have taken place under some schemes particularly in Modernisation of Mapping
Organisat ions. (Para 16.2)
The Committee is of the view that there is a considerable scope for improvement in the
performance of the D epartment and in the light of adverse audit observations made by the
Controller & Auditor General particularly on Drugs & Pharmaceutical Research Programme,
the Department needs to strengthen its monitoring mechanism so that misuse of funds could
be prevented and  timely completion of projects and optimum utilisation of funds could be
ensured. (Para 16.3)
It is high time that the Department of Science and Technology strives to be on par
with international standards  in the long run in terms of the speed and quantum of scientific
and technological breakthroughs in order to expeditiously realise  India’s rightful place as
a global leader in various domains of science and technology including particularly
supercomp uting. (Para 16.4)
