PREFACE
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and
Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the
Report on its behalf, present this One Hundred and Ninety-sixth Report on the Action Taken by
the Department of Bio-technology on the recommendations contained in the One Hundred and
Ninetieth Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2008-09) of the Department of Bio-
technology.
2. The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-
technology on the recommendations contained in the One Hundred and Ninetieth Report of the

the Committee. Action Taken Notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from
the Department on 16th September, 2008.

REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-
technology on the recommendations contained in the One hundred and Ninetieth Report of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the Demands for Grants (2008-09) of the Department of Bio-technology, which was
presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 29th April, 2008. There were sixteen recommendations
in the One Hundred Ninetieth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations
of the Committee were received from the Department on 16th September, 2008.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.2)
The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should be provided with additional
fund at the RE stage so that the gap between the projected demand and the actual allocation can
be bridged so as to enable the Department to establish new autonomous institutions. The committee
is also of the view that the well thought out projected demands of scientific departments should
be honored and accepted by the planning commission, more so when the department has the
capacity and the readiness to utilize the additional funds.
Action Taken by the Government
The recommendation of the Committee was brought to the notice of Planning Commission
during Annual Performance Review meeting held in April, 2008. The Planning Commission assured
provision of additional funds at the RE stage depending upon the progress of expenditure.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the Department would pursue with the Planning
Commission for providing additional funds at RE stage and expects that in future atleast the
scientific departments would be spared from routine cuts in funds allocation as they are
responsible for the country’s technological progress.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.3)
The Committee also strongly recommends that the Department should draw a reasonable
time frame for establishing new autonomous institutions and devise a mechanism to monitor their
progress periodically so as to avoid time and cost overruns.
Action Taken by the Government
During the Eleventh Plan, establishment of 8 new institutions in frontier areas of
biotechnology have been taken up. Out of this, approvals from Expenditure Finance Committee
(EFC) and Cabinet have been already obtained in case of 3 institutions and implementation of
activities has already begun. EFCs have been circulated in case of 2 other institutions for comments
and the same are under preparation in case of 2 more institutions. Further, the Cabinet Note for
establishment of UNESCO centre is in pipeline as per procedure. In all these cases, the timeline
for implementation are clearly worked out for monitoring from time to time.
Comments
The Committee feels that administrative delays should be avoided by the Department
in the establishment of new scientific institutions.

2
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.5)
The Committee is pleased to know that the current trend of expenditure indicates that there
are no budget heads of the schemes under which the funds will be underutilized. The Committee
is of the opinion that the Department should continue its good efforts for optimum utilization of
funds.
Action Taken by the Government
The department notes the appreciation made by the committee and sincere efforts will be
made to maintain the trend.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.7)
The Committee desires that the reorientation of some of the above programmes, which
could not be completed, should be given a sharper focus and more efforts should be made for their
completion so as to avoid wastage of scarce resource owing to unnecessary delays.
Action Taken by the Government
The programmes dropped during the Tenth Plan have completed the objectives, then set for
their project duration. One of the schemes namely ‘Biotech product and process development’ has
been reoriented with a sharp focus based ‘on the research trends /leads and is being implemented
during Eleventh Plan as a scheme “Bio-processing and Scale-up”.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 3.9)
The Committee is pleased to know that the Department has been successful in meeting its
targets set for the Tenth Plan. Growth rate of 40% is encouraging and thus the Committee desires
that the Department should continue the good work during the next plan period also.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department noted the appreciations of the committee and efforts will be made to
maintain the trend.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the targets for the future plans would also be met
successfully.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 4.5)
The Committee is of the view that remodeling/ reengineering of university departments of
Life Sciences is of utmost importance in order to remain globally competitive. The Committee
expresses its concern over the lack of infrastructure for R&D in molecular modeling, protein

3
engineering, drug designing, immunological studies, pre-clinical studies, clinical trials, etc. as
without a strong R&D base success of Department will remain short-term and would fail to
sustain, itself in the long run. Therefore, the Committee recommends that infrastructure for R&D
needs to be built up adequately to achieve the desired targets fixed in the Eleventh Plan period.
Action Taken by the Government
Keeping in view the observations of the Committee, the department had advertised the
scheme inviting proposals from universities on certain parameters of reengineering of Life Science
departments. About 12 universities responded with letter of intents. The process of selection for
funding and support is ongoing based on the merit of the proposal and vision of the university to
transform life science research for making it globally competitive. Department will support every
effort made in upgrading the infrastructure of R&D base of Life Sciences Department in such
universities.
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that the department should strive towards the
upgradation of research and development facilities both at the graduate and undergraduate
levels.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.2)
The Committee is of the opinion that State Agriculture University should be given required
support to start interdisciplinary translational research centres so as to emerge as solution providers
for local agricultural needs. The Committee recommends that the Department engages itself in
exploring the possibilities for the use of barren land for agricultural or horticultural purposes with
the help of bio-technology, as this would help bring a vast unutilized area under productive
cultivation in the country.
The Committee would be happy to see progress in potential application of
nano-biotechnology in agriculture, medicine and environment.
Special emphasis needs to be paid for use of technology in preserving our environment
which has seen large scale degradation. This would go a long way in conservation of lakes which
are getting polluted every day.
The Committee would also like the Department to establish specialized virus research
centres to address viral biology, pathogenesis, biomarkers, etc. within a reasonable time-period.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department has evolved a scheme to support State Agriculture Universities strengthen
research in interdisciplinary areas. Four agriculture universities have been provided programme
based support for the purpose. Dialogue with more agriculture universities through site visits by
experts have been undertaken for assisting them to prepare suitable proposals. To address the needs
of the degraded lands with the salinity, alkalinity and heavy metal toxicity, several programmes have
been undertaken for developing crops resistant to these abiotics factors. Several leads are already
available.
The Department during Eleventh plan has a separate allocation for promoting the application
of nano-technology in agriculture, medicine and environment. So far, about 70 translational R&D
proposals have been approved for implementation for a period of 3-5 years. A special experts
committee has been constituted for technical appraisal and review. Opportunities for utilizing the

4
research leads in these programmes through public-private partnerships have also been put in place
through separate schemes and mechanisms.
The Department has a major programme with separate budgetary allocation and special
expert Task Force for addressing problems of environmental degradation and biodiversity
conservation. Six thrust areas have been identified namely biodegradation of Hazardous pollutants;
bioremediation of polluted sites as well as degraded eco-system; biopolymers/biodegradable plastics
from industrial waste; bioenergy/fuel from industrial waste; Biosensors/Biomarkers; Biodiversity
conservation of endangered/threatened/plant and animal species and Mitigation technology for
climate change. The R&D programmes addressed development of technology packages, tools,
techniques and processes for use by public and private sectors. Already few technologies packages
are available and few of them transferred to users.
The department has supported a special programme under Centres of Excellence for design
and development of anti-virals for Hepatitis ‘C’, vaccines research platform for HIV/AIDS, invitro
screening laboratories for microbicidal compounds. Proposals are also approved for antiviral drug
discovery programme and development of genome based diagnostics methods as a part of the
activities in the new institutions of DBT to be established in the Eleventh plan.
Comments
The Committee notes the efforts undertaken by the Department and is of the
opinion that sustained efforts are required to use technology as a tool to ensure
development in agriculture and its widespread use by farmers at the ground level.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 5.3)
The Committee would also like the Department to focus and channelize its R&D activities
on programmes for using algae and jatropha as a source of biodiesel and producing ethanol from
cellulose because the sources of fossil fuel are depleting fast but yet constitute the major source
of energy. This should be done in a targeted manner so as to make early breakthroughs.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department of Biotechnology is supporting a major programme on Energy Bioscience
focusing on Biodiesel and Bioethanol from varied feed stocks. Quality planting material of Jatropha
from more than 2,500 accessions has been collected from across the country and characterized at
chemical and molecular level. The superior accessions have been multiplied and field planted over
and area of nearly 800 hectares for multi-location trials at 10 locations across the country. A
coordinated programme is also being supported on Bioethanol production from cellulosic biomass.
Leads have been obtained at the laboratory level, for agriculture and forestry waste. Recombinant
microorganisms for efficient ethanol recovery have been identified, the technology is now being
scaled up. In case of Algae feedstock for biofuel projects have been shortlisted for screening and
collection of strains from across the country, genetic improvement of strains for desired lipid levels
and developing photobioreactor systems for efficient production of the superior strains on mass
scale.
Comments
The Committee feels that research and development activities on finding a substitute
for fossil fuel has become the need of the hour, in view of the outgo of huge amounts of
foreign exchange for purchase of fossil fuels from abroad and, therefore, Committee is of
the opinion that research in this field should be accorded top priority.

5
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 6.4)
The Committee would like the Department to disseminate proven and field tested
technologies to help the rural population in their skill development, employment and income
generation in the field of agriculture, sericulture, etc. This has a potential not only to provide self-
employment and raise farm incomes but also to check migration to the cities, which are already
under stress. Self Help Groups along with other civil society groups need to be roped in big way
to make such programmes reach tribal, women and other weaker section of the society.
Action Taken by the Government
As on May 2008, the department has supported 2800 projects under progarmmes for
promoting biotechnology applications for the benefit of women, SC/ST and rural population. These
projects have been implemented benefiting about 2.5 lakhs people, which are implemented through
Krishi Vigyan Kendras, NGOs, and self help groups with technical support from central/states R&D
institutes, universities/colleges and research foundations. Progrmmes include projects for self
employment, additional income generation and improving health and agriculture practices. Efforts
are also being made to launch major programmes having impact at national level through
appropriate linkages with other ministries and their programmes.
Comments
The Committee hopes that projects promoting bio-technology applications for the
benefit of the rural masses should be popularized through proper coordination with various
State Governments, so as to ensure its widespread use.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 7.2)
The Committee has been informed that the country produces lesser number of PhDs, in
comparison to other developing countries like Brazil and China except in engineering. This has
grave implications for Scientific Research in future. The Committee recommends that this situation
be rectified so as to create a good research base in the country and that the Department must play
a pro-active role in this regard. The number of PhDs is bound to less unless and until students
are initiated in the subject at a young age. The Department should evolve a suitable long-term
mechanism for the purpose. But the Committee feels that this would not be possible as the
allocation under this programme is Rs.30 crores as against Rs.70 crores proposed. The Committee
is of the opinion that adequate resources should be earmarked for Human Resource Development.
Action Taken by the Government
In so far, as the department is concerned within its mandate, 4 majors progarmmes/
strategies have been developed for addressing the issue of producing more number of quality PhDs
in Life Sciences:
(i) About 200 Ph.D. fellowships are supported through biotechnology entrance tests on
annual basis. The budgetary requirements are met out of the scheme “Human
Resource Development”. Keeping view of the suggestions of the committee, the
budgetary requirements will be revised at RE stage based on expenditure;
(ii) Under a separate scheme “Upgradation /reengineering of Universities”, selected 2-3
universities per year will be provided with adequate budget for increasing number of
PhD scholars/fellowships alongwith expenditure support for additional faculty and
research infrastructure of globally competitive nature. This programme is expected to
generate about 1000 Ph.Ds;

6
(iii) Under another separate scheme “Reentry Fellowships for early, medium and senior
career scientists of India”, resident in foreign countries shall be provided with salary,
infrastructure and laboratory support for taking up positions in universities/institutions
in India. In this context, a joint programme with WELCOME trust, UK and DBT has
been approved recently for a period of 5 years. This programme is also expected to
widen the research base; and
(iv) Under each of the 8 new institutions to be established/implemented by DBT in the
Eleventh plan, an estimated number of 800 Ph.D. fellowships will be made available.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and reiterates that a long term mechanism should
be evolved by the department to generate an interest towards science, right from the school
level.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 8.2)
The Committee is of the opinion that financial and technical support should be given to
individual innovators as good innovations many a times come from the unorganized sector. The
Committee is of the opinion that the Department should do all needful in spreading the culture of
knowledge based entrepreneurship.
Action Taken by the Government
The department would like to clarify that the biotechnology entrepreneurship is research
intensive and requires minimum expertise in subjects related to biological sciences. However,
programmes under the scheme “Biotechnology based programme for societal Development”
addresses selective needs of entrepreneurship development in unorganized sector among women,
SC/ST population and rural areas. The suggestions of the committee shall be implemented through
this programme in a more focused manner. In the formal/organized sector, the department has
already launched two major programmes namely (a) Innovative Young Biotechologist Awards’ for
young researchers below the age of 35 years for research in frontier areas, about 25 awards
already been given as on date; (b) A programme Young Entrepreneurs Scheme (YES) in
collaboration with Government of UK was implemented by the department as one time activity. The
programme aims to encourage biotechnology entrepreneurship in young post-graduate and doctoral
students by exposing them to issues involved in commercialization of bio-science. A one-time team
of 5 members each present a commercial idea in the realm of biotechnology have been successfully
selected. Based on the suggestions of the committee, a similar programme is evolved for
implementation by the department from 2008-09 onwards.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the Department through its various programmes is able
to generate entrepreneurship development especially in the rural areas.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 9.2)
The Committee welcomes the idea of spreading the scheme to Universities by setting up of
university-incubators in selected universities based on previous performances in life sciences. This
will not only bring the universities in the ambit of professional research but also mobilize contract
assignments from small and medium industries and promote academia-industry interaction thus
helping universities to get more exposure for good research.

7
Action Taken by the Government
As a follow up, the department has initiated proposals for establishment of university-
Industry incubators at University of Hyderabad, Hyderabad and University of Agricultural Sciences,
Bangalore through brain storming workshop involving stakeholders. Techno-economic feasibility
reports have been commissioned for the purpose.
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that more number of universities across the country
require high quality scientific equipments for research purposes and the department with its
resources can play a pivotal role in this regard.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 10.2)
The Committee desires that the Centre should continue its research work in the areas of
Hepatitis, Malaria, recombinant gene product, plant molecular biology, plant resistance, plant
transformation, etc., which help for the development of Biotechnology in our country. The
Committee is of the opinion that there is a lot to learn from the developed counties in the field
of genetic engineering to address the health needs of our country. The Institute can play a pivotal
role in coordinating scientific and technological exchanges with the west so as to obtain vital inputs
for our health research.
Action Taken by the Government
As suggested by the committee, measures have been initiated for continuous support to
research work carried out by International Centure for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnolgy
(ICGEB) with international and national linkages. Proposal for acquisition of new powerful tool to
identify new drug targets in national drug development programmes has been approved.
Comments
The Committee feels that the Department should coordinate with the foreign scientific
institutions on a regular basis so as to garner knowledge gained by them and ensure faster
development of biotechnology in the country.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 11.2)
The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should take serious and sincere steps
to make project/schemes for benefit of North Eastern Region and Sikkim successful by not just
allocating funds but also creating capacity to absorb these funds. The Committee feels that this
scheme should be seen as an opportunity to involve local communities and provide them
employment.
Action Taken by the Government
As a follow up, the department has established a special cell for North Eastern Areas to
implement targeted programmes. The cell with adequate technical and supporting staff would assist
these areas generation of projects of viable nature benefitting in capacity building and monitoring
for timely implementation of projects both in terms of technical and financial goals. The projects
shall implement with overall goal of improving quality life sciences education, research and benefit
to local communities besides employment generation and entrepreneurship development.

8
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that the North-Eastern region of the country
requires special support as regards development in scientific field and all out efforts are
required to make the schemes a complete success, which would result in economic
development of the region.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 12.2)
The Committee has been informed that while 7000 patents were filed globally, there were
only two patents from India. The reason being lacunae in our institutional design and in our
strategy for getting cutting-edge scientists. The Committee emphasized the need to improve the
incentive structure so as to attract good scientists and make efforts for the same. The Committee
is of the view that post-doctoral fellowships are very low and need to be increased to retain our
scientists in the country.
Action Taken by the Government
Keeping in view of the suggestions of the committee, adequate attention has been paid for
technology management in all the new institutions and ongoing institutions as well as major projects
funded. A programme for reentry fellowships has been launched to be implemented by the
department in collaboration with WELCOME Trust, UK for attracting young Indian scientist from
abroad to take up position in Indian Institutions/Universities in the cutting edge areas of research.
The matter of increasing of post doctoral has been taken up by the department with the Ministry
of Finance. Accordingly, the fellowships have been increased from Rs.11000/- (1st year)—
Rs.12000/- (3rd year onwards) to Rs.l6000/- and Rs.18000/- respectively w.e.f. 01.04.2007.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the increase in the amount of Post Doctoral Fellowships and
feels that this is a step in the right direction for providing incentive to the young scientist.
RECOMMENDATION (PARA 12.3)
The Committee is further of the view that in a dynamic and competitive environment, quick
decision-making is the key to success. Owing to involvement of several ministries in decision-
making process, initiation and implementation of schemes gets considerably slowed down in our
country. It is, therefore, required that a better coordination at different levels of decision-making
is established so that opportunities are not lost due to undue delays.
Action Taken by the Government
Recognizing the need for coordination for implementation of national biotechnology
development strategy formulated by the department as well as for the programmes for the Eleventh
Plan, the department has constituted an inter-ministerial committee involving all the concerned
ministries and departments. The first meeting of the committee was also held in May 2008. This
step will ensure prompt coordination and decision making for implementation of schemes.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and reiterates that without proper coordination
amongst the various ministries which are involved in the decision making process, faster
implementation of the schemes will not be possible.
