General Background 
  
1.         The purpose of the Indian space programme  is to make India self-r eliant in regard to the 
applications of space technology thereby ensuring ra pid progress in socio-economic sectors: The space 
programme is broa d-based and its activities range from development of space launch systems,
sophisticated satellites, their o perations in the orbit to the conduct of extensive a pplications oriented

2.         Under the existing space programme s, space services in the areas of communication,
meteorology, resources surveys and. management, incl uding the development of  associated satellites,
launch vehicles, and ground system s are being looked after by th e Department of 8pace. The
Department of Space and the Space Commission were set up in 1972. 
 
3.         India has, by now, successfully launche d 16 satellites starting wi th Aryabhatta in 1975. INSAT
satellites have virtually revoluti onised telecommunications and TV and radio broadcasting in India,
enabling more than 75% of the country population to  have access to educat ional and entertainment
programmes on TV. The applic ation of IRS series of satellites is for carrying out remote sensing from
space for providing vital inputs for management-of land and water resources, and for enabling integrated
development of strategies at micro level for overall development of the nation. 
4.         A major achievement in India's space ca pabilities was demonstrated through the first successful
launch of SLV-3 in July, 1980. Th e successful launch of the Augm ented Satellite Launch Vehicle 
(ASLV) on May 20, 1992 is a very si gnificant milestone in the deve lopment of indigenous launch
capability. It has not only validated all the modifi cations carried out, base d on the detailed failure
analysis of the previous two un successful ASLV flights, but also  has proved, in flight, a number o f
important advanced, technologies needed for the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) and the
Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV). 
5.         Despite some failures, our space pr ogramme has achieved new heights with the successful
launch and operationalisation of the indigenously built INSAT-2A whic h has enabled the Indian Space 
Programme to provide uninterrupted and enhanced services in the vital area of communications,
television, broadcast and meteorology including disaster warning. INSAT-2A was originally intended to 
be a test spacecraft as it was w holly developed and built in the country. However, on launch it has
proved to be so totally successful that it has now become a part of the INSAT operation system. 
6.         The Committee applauds the objective of  self-reliance in the space  programme and feels happy 
over the fact that the Department ha s learnt from its past  failures and carried out modifications for its
future system. The Committee has noted in particular, that the De partment of Space has evolved a
mechanism to go into various aspect s of the failure of the missions undertaken by it earlier and would
take suitable corrective measures while formulating future programmes.

was told that Demands for Grants for the year 1993-94 were essentially for meeting the programmatic 
needs of the approved time bound projects like IRS, INSAT, PSLV. GS LV, related technology
development, establishment of infrastructure and promotion of a pplications. The Committee then

10.       To augment its resources so as to impr ove its financial viability, the Committee feels that the
Department of Space may tap certa in areas. The Committee has noted that the technology development,
activities are being carried out in the various establishments of  1SRO. Important achievements have
been made in the field of solid  propellant technology, polymers and chemical s. Some of these
technologies have big commercial scope . In view of the changed econom ic scenario of the country, the
spin-off applications of the ne w technological development achieved  by the Department need to be 
exploited commercially. The Commit tee, therefore. recommends th at a suitable mechanism may be
created within the Department of  Space which should explore the possi bility of selling the spin-of f 
applications of the new technologi cal developments on a commercial basis. The Committee notes with
satisfaction that the Government has taken some in itiative in this direction by setting up in September,
1992 M/s. Antrix Corporation Ltd., a company regist ered under the Companies Act, 1956, to market a
wide variety of space products a nd services developed in the various ISRO units and centres. This
company aims at entering the globa l market for space serv ices and products and also promoting ISRO-
Industry interface activities to enab le the Indian industries to meet the rising and complex requirements
of Indian Space programme.

hasten the process of indigenisation of the Indian space programme.

18.       A point was raised in the course of th e deliberations of the Committee about the location o f
many of the activities of the ISRO in the southern region of the count ry. The clarification given by the
representative of the Department of Space in this regard was as follows: 
"The Centres/Units of ISRO are set up at various  places after considering technical feasibility,
geographical parameters etc. As for example, T humba near Thiruvanan-thapuram was chosen as 
rocket launching station way back  in 1962 considering that the site lies on magnetic equator.
Similarly, the rocket and satell ite launching faci lities were located in Sriharikota in Andhra
Pradesh after considering the a dvantages of nearness to ge ographic equator, the technical
requirement of a large un-inhabi ted area from the safety angle and other such considerations.
Also considering the need for the launch to be done towards the east  to take advantage of Earth's
rotational velocity. Satellite Centre is locat ed in Bangalore, since a number of electronic
industries are available there. Space Applications Centre,was es tablished at Ahmedabad, since
there was space applications e xpertise available in Physical Research Laboratory set up by late
Dr. Vikram Sarabhai. NRSA at Hyderabad was originally esta blished by DST and subsequently
transferred to DOS. ISTRAC a nd MCF stations are located c onsidering the el ectro-magnetic 
interference aspects and also the nearness to spacecraft design centre. 
The Department has established Regional Remote Sensing Service Centres at various places llke
Dehradun, Jodhpur, Nagpur and Kharagpur, in addi tion to Bangalore, and has also located Solar
Observatory at Udaipur, Earth Station at Delhi, Ground Stat ion at Lucknow, Meteorological
Rocket Station at Balasore and Down Range St ation at Car Nicobar. The Department has also
supported establishment of Remote Sensing Centres in all States." 
19.       The Committee enquired from the Departme nt about the reasons for failure of the 1NSAT-1A 
and INSAT-1C missions and wanted to know about the losses suffered on account of these failures and 
whether any effort was made to recover the losses. The Committe e was informed that 1NSAT-1A 
launched in 1982 and INSAT-1C launched ill 1988 had failed. In case of INSAT-1A the insurance 
amount received by the Department wa s US $ 64.9 million as against the cost of the satellite which was
US $ 58.95 million. In case of INSAT-1C the insurance amount received was US $ 68 million as against
the cost of US $ 96.67 million. In cas e of INSAT-1A the satellite was fu lly insured in the International 
insurance market and the total claim of US $ 64.9 million was fully paid by the Insurance Consortia.
However, in the case of INSAT-1C  the satellite was operational for a bout 16 months and, therefore, the 
insurance company admitted the clai m only to the extent of US $ 68 million. This was also due to the
fact that the insurance coverage was not total in view of the very high rate of premium payable.

Telecommunications, TV, Radio, Meteorology, Disaster Manageme nt, Education, Management o f
Natural Resources and Monitoring of Environment are very vital for the progress of' India. Afte r
examining the demands for grants of the Departme nt of Space and reviewi ng the progress of various
space-related projects, the Committee recommends th at the demands for grants of Rs.718.07 crores, as
sought by the Department for the year 1993-94 are in order and nay be approved.
