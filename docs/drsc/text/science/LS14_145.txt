2.         The programmes being pursued by DAE emanate from the following mandate:
 
Ø      Increasing share of nuclear power through deployment of indigenous and other proven technologies, along
 with development of fast breeder reactors and thorium reactors with associated fuel cycle facilities. The
 three-stage Nuclear Power Programme is achieved through-
 
(i)         Setting up of pressurised heavy water reactors (PHWRs) and associated fuel cycle facilities;
 
(ii)
                Setting up of fast breeder reactors (FBRs) backed by reprocessing plants and plutonium-
based fuel fabrication plants and
 
(iii)
               The third stage that would be based on the thorium-uranium-233 cycle where Uranium-233
 is obtained by irradiation of thorium.
 
Ø      Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes and carrying out radiation
 technology applications in the field of medicine, agriculture and industry;
Ø      Developing advanced  technologies such as accelerators, lasers, supercomputers, advanced materials and
 instrumentation, and encouraging transfer of technology to industry;
Ø      Support to basic research in nuclear energy and related frontier areas of science; Interaction with universities
 and academic institutions; Support to research and development projects having a bearing on DAE’s
 programmes, and International cooperation in related advanced areas of research; and
Ø      Contribution to national security.
3.         The atomic energy programmes comprise three sectors, namely, ‘Nuclear Power Sector’, ‘Industries & Minerals
 Sector (I & M Sector)’ and ‘Research & Development Sector(R & D Sector)’. The Plan and Non-Plan outlays of the
 Department are formulated under power sector, industries and minerals (I&M) sector and Research and Development
 sector. The financial supports for the activities of the Department are provided by budgetary support. The public sector
 undertakings, namely Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd.(NPCIL), Mumbai, Indian Rare Earths Limited (IRE),
 Mumbai, Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL), Jaduguda and Electronics Corporation of India Limited (ECIL),
 Hyderabad and Bhartiya Nabhikiya Vidyut Nigam Ltd.(BHAVINI), Kalpakkam of the Department provide Internal and
 Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) in addition to budgetary support to the capital and infrastructural developments.       
4.         The Nuclear Power Sector of the Department deals with design, construction and operation of commercial power
 reactors with associated safety in all its phases.  This comprises building of Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR) and
 development of Fast Breeder Reactors (FBR) and Thorium Reactors on commercial scale.  Associated waste management
 and environment monitoring and technology development relating to operation and maintenance of the reactors also form
 part of the programmes.
5.         Industries & Minerals Sector is involved in industrial application of technologies developed in the R&D facilities and
 includes- programmes related to nuclear fuel cycle covering design, construction and operation of industrial plants for
 refining ores, fabrication of fuel, production of heavy water, instrumentation and control, etc. needed for sustained operation
 of the power reactors; and applications of radioisotopes, radiation, laser and accelerator technology for development in
 industry, medicine, agriculture and food preservation.
6.         Research & Development Sector provides R & D support to the Nuclear Power Programmes of the Department.  The
 R & D efforts of DAE are in multidisciplinary high technology areas.  Significant achievements have been made in building
 technical capabilities in the design, construction and operation of Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR); exploration,
 mining extraction, purification and conversion of nuclear materials; production of fuel element for nuclear reactors;

CHOICE OF FUEL FOR FAST BREEDER REACTOR (FBR)
 
25.       The Committee has been given to understand that Department of Atomic Energy  (DAE) is planning to use metallic
 fuel for future Fast Breeder Reactor (FBR).  The metallic fuel has high breeding ratio i.e. high electricity generating
 capacity through breeding process and shorter doubling time.
25.1     The Committee welcomes the idea and feels that this advanced fuel will reduce the cost of production of electricity
 through FBR and not only that by reducing the capital cost of production of electricity the plant life span will also increase
 by incorporating better safety features for these reactors.  Faster the breeding, quicker will be the rise in the FBR’s capacity.
 Other nuclear fuels for FBR and PFBR (Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor) like oxide, nitride and carbide fuels may now take
 the back seat for being used as fuels for FBR.
25.2     The choice of fuels for breeder reactors, the Committee feels, is a choice between doubling time versus the cost of
 production. The Committee, however, feels that oxide fuel with a ratio of 30% plutonium oxide and 70% uranium oxide is a
 well-proven fuel world over.  In India, however, mixed oxide fuel (MOX) could be used for prototype Fast Breeder Reactor
 (PFBR).
25.3     The Committee, on the other hand, feels that carbide fuel is very difficult to fabricate on a large scale as it is highly
 pyrophoric and is highly susceptible to oxidation and hydrolysis.  Moreover, reprocessing of spent carbide fuel is difficult
 as it is difficult to dissolve in nitric acid and leaves behind organic complexes.  Apart from this, the carbide fuel  has low
 burn up value in comparison to oxide fuel. The fuel ‘burn-up’ in a nuclear reactor refers to the amount of energy extracted
 from the fuel before it is discharged for storage or reprocessing.  Higher the burn-up, lesser will be the fuel cycle cost.
25.4     The Committee understands that cost of power production comes down with increased burn up value.  Also oxide
 fuel is easy to fabricate as it is not pyrophoric and hence needs no inert atmosphere during fabrication.  The Committee is,
 therefore, of the opinion that use of oxide fuel would be more vital for making a foray into fast breeder power plants.
25.5     The Committee understands that breeder technology works on the principle of self-sustenance and ability to produce
 more plutonium than it actually consumes to produce power.  The breeding ratio, which is 1.1 in case of oxide fuel, is 1.2 –
 1.4 in case of carbide and nitride fuels.  While it is maximum in case of metallic fuel i.e. 1.4 – 1.5 and hence doubling time
 (i.e. surplus plutonium produce to start a new reactor) is shortest in case of a metallic, carbide and nitride fuels respectively 
 in comparison to oxide fuel.
25.6     The Committee, therefore, is of the opinion that doubling time becomes paramount when the country goes on
 commissioning new reactors.  Hence, the stress on using advanced fuel is necessary.  The Committee feels that making
 technology available for producing power at cheaper cost may gain more public acceptance, which is vital to breeder
 programme.
25.7     The Committee is of the opinion that lower burn up by using carbide fuel is not economical since fabrication and
 reprocessing would increase cost of power production.  However, the Committee feels that long-term strategy calls for fuels
 with shorter doubling time.  Sometimes it is felt that nitride fuel seems to be more preferable due to its easier reprocessing
 and improved performance.
25.8     The Committee recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy may like to consider the following choices:-
 
i)                    High breeding ratio and shorter doubling time

FOOD IRRADIATION
 
33.       It is well-known that incidence of food born diseases can be reduced by food irradiation techniques.  It can also
 inhibit post-harvest sprouting in potatoes and onions – disinfect fruits and vegetables, delay ripening in fruits, can eliminate
 outrightly the food borne  pathogens which can be kept for a long time without refrigeration before substantial
 consumption.  In addition, the irradiation technology can be used for eliminating pests nuisance etc.
34.       Although irradiation technology could have   outright advantages, there could be  certain health risks that might be
 involved in the application of the technology of the following nature:
·
        Food irradiation can result in loss of nutrients.
 
·        When food is exposed to high doses of ionising radiation, the possible changes in chemical composition and nutritional
 content of food damage  DNA in humans.
 
·
        The food irradiation does not necessarily inactivate dangerous toxins which have already been produced by bacteria
 prior to irradiation.
 
·
         Irradiated food products like chicken meat, etc could mislead consumers into thinking that they are biologically safer for
 consumption.
 
35.       The Committee therefore feels that some precautionary measures should be taken by the Department of Atomic
 Energy while irradiating foods so that the possible health risks involved before consumption of irradiated food could be
 effectively dealt with. In this connection, Radiological Safety, Microbiological Safety, Nutritional adequacy and
 Toxicological Safety measures may be kept in view.
36.       The Committee also feels that long term effects of irradiated food should be studied thoroughly from the point of
 view of the fact that how  ionizing radiation alters chemical structure of food material.  This could be managed if a

 would be seriously affected, besides it would lead to a reconsideration of their research activity. Therefore, the Committee
 recommends that the aided institutions should review and strengthen the budgetary mechanism and planning process so as
 to ensure full utilization of their allocated amount.
(Para 21)
6.         The Committee appreciates the effort of the Department in achieving criticality of TAPP-4 within five years from the
 first pour of concrete. The Committee hopes that the gestation periods of Kaiga 3&4 and RAPP 5&6 projects that are being
 constructed with a tight schedule of 60 months and 66 months for the first and second units respectively from the first pour
 of concrete to commercial operation doesn’t escalate over. Similarly, the Department should make all-out efforts to reduce
 the gestation period of Kudankulam -1  (KK-1)  and complete it within the Xth plan period itself, so that it is completed in
 five years from first pour of concrete to commercial operation.
(Para 23)
7.         An allocation of Rs.82.80 crore has been made during 2005-06 for VECC, Kolkata However, the Committee is
 constrained to note the slow execution of this project during 2004-05 due to which the allocated fund  could not be utilized,
 resulting into reduction of RE.  The Committee recommends that since SPECT isotopes are presently imported from abroad,
 setting up of Medical Cyclotron in VECC, Kolkata would considerably reduce our dependence on import. Once the Medical
 Cyclotron becomes operational, production of radioisotopes and radio pharmaceutical (SPECT & PET) for advanced
 medical diagnostic purpose would become easily available therefore this programme may be accorded high priority for
 early completion. The Committee hopes that the enhanced allocation in 2005-06 will be fully utilized in installing the
 Medical Cyclotron Facility and other projects at VECC, Kolkata.
(Para 24)
8.         The Committee welcomes the idea and feels that this advanced fuel will reduce the cost of production of electricity
 through FBR and not only that by reducing the capital cost of production of electricity the plant life span will also increase
 by incorporating better safety features for these reactors.  Faster the breeding, quicker will be the rise in the FBR’s capacity.
 Other nuclear fuels for FBR and PFBR (Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor) like oxide, nitride and carbide fuels may now take
 the back seat for being used as fuels for FBR.
(Para 25.1)
9.         The choice of fuels for breeder reactors, the Committee feels, is a choice between doubling time versus the cost of
 production. The Committee, however, feels that oxide fuel with a ratio of 30% plutonium oxide and 70% uranium oxide is a
 well-proven fuel world over.  In India, however, mixed oxide fuel (MOX) could be used for prototype Fast Breeder Reactor
 (PFBR).
(Para 25.2)
10.       The Committee, on the other hand, feels that carbide fuel is very difficult to fabricate on a large scale as it is highly
 pyrophoric and is highly susceptible to oxidation and hydrolysis.  Moreover, reprocessing of spent carbide fuel is difficult
 as it is difficult to dissolve in nitric acid and leaves behind organic complexes.  Apart from this, the carbide fuel  has low
 burn up value in comparison to oxide fuel. The fuel ‘burn-up’ in a nuclear reactor refers to the amount of energy extracted
 from the fuel before it is discharged for storage or reprocessing.  Higher the burn-up, lesser will be the fuel cycle cost.
(Para 25.3)
11.       The Committee understands that cost of power production comes down with increased burn up value.  Also oxide
 fuel is easy to fabricate as it is not pyrophoric and hence needs no inert atmosphere during fabrication.  The Committee is,
 therefore, of the opinion that use of oxide fuel would be more vital for making a foray into fast breeder power plants.
(Para 25.4)
12.       The Committee understands that breeder technology works on the principle of self-sustenance and ability to produce

(Para 25.6)
14.       The Committee is of the opinion that lower burn up by using carbide fuel is not economical since fabrication and
 reprocessing would increase cost of power production.  However, the Committee feels that long-term strategy calls for fuels
 with shorter doubling time.  Sometimes it is felt that nitride fuel seems to be more preferable due to its easier reprocessing
 and improved performance.
(Para 25.7)
15.       The Committee recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy may like to consider the following choices:-

ii)
                   High burn up value and less fuel cycle cost
iii)                 Faster breeding and quicker capacity of fast breeder reactor
iv)                 High burn up value and low production cost of power; and between

 choosing the most  ideal fuel for its FBR and PFBR programme.
 
(Para 25.8)
16.       The Committee feels that without choosing the best options from among the above series, the choice of fuel for
 future reactors of the Department of Atomic Energy may not be optimally fulfilled unless maximum efficiency in power
 production is derived at least possible opportunity cost.
(Para 25.9)
17.       Although the Committee feels that TMC is providing state-of-the-art treatment against this killer disease, it would
 still like to recommend for a new line of research in the field of oncology. The Committee feels that Gene Therapy
 experiment is the most important therapy for finding a permanent solution against the disease.  In  Gene Therapy  process,
 the healthy copy of the missing gene is inserted into the cell using a virus known as Adeno-Associated Viral Vector that is
 no longer harmful and as such it could be termed as a disabled virus.  Cells that have been successfully changed by this
 gene therapy process are separated out, grown and returned to the patient’s body to fight against the disease.  In other cases,
 disabled viruses are used to deliver healthy gene directly to cells within the patient’s body.
(Para 27)
18.       The Committee is also of the opinion that following fields in Cancer Research apart from the above needs research
 and deep attention.

      Role of angiogenesis inhibitors in Cancer treatment;
b)      Role of Tumour Suppressor Genes like pRB and p53 in Cancer Control and
c)      Role of Farnesyl Transferase Inhibitors (FTIs) – Tumour Necrosis Factor(TNF)
 and Endothelial Growth Factors Receptors(EGFs) in promoting neoplastic

(Para 28)
