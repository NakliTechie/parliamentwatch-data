INTRODUCTION
The Ministry of Science and Technology was established in 1971 to formulate and implement Science
and Technology policies, identify, and promote “frontline” research in science and technology infrastructure.
The Ministry has three Departments, namely Department of Science and Technology, Department of
Biotechnology and Department of Scientific and Industrial Research. Through Department of Science and
Technology, the Ministry coordinates intra-governmental and international cooperation and provides funding
for domestic institutions and research programs. The Department of Scientific and Industrial Research is a
technology transfer organization, and the Department of Biotechnology, runs a number of developmental
laboratories.
2. The following constitute the mandate of DST:-
z Formulation of policy statements and guidelines on Science and Technology (S&T);
z Promotion of new areas of scientific research;
z Co-ordination of inter-Departmental S&T efforts;
z Support basic and applied research in universities and other institutions;
z International S&T Co-operation;
z Support high quality research in autonomous bodies where specialisation ranges from advanced
medical research materials to astronomy;
z Catalysing S&T interaction in societal programmes, and entrepreneurship development;
z Science popularisation, communication and S&T Management Information System;
z Promotion of Commercialisation of indigenous technology;
z Support to technology development programmes, technology information and assessment;
and
z Providing services in the areas of:
(i) Surveying & Mapping;
(ii) T echnology Development and Application
3. OBJECTIVES
Main objectives of the Department of Science and Technology are as follows:
(a) Formulation of Policies relating to Science and Technology;
(b) Strengthening basic research and Expanding R&D base-Human capacity;
(c) Strengthening Basic research and Expanding R&D base-Institutional Capacity;
(d) Implementing Technology Development Programmes: from R&D to Commercialisation;
(e) Societal interventions of Science & Technology; and
(f) International Science & Technology Co-operation

2
4. ORGANISATION
4.1 The Department of Science and Technology is the nodal Department of the Ministry of Science and
Technology. Broadly, the organisational set up of the Department has been divided into Autonomous Institutes,
Scientific Groups, Technology Development Board and Scientific Services besides the Administration and
Integrated Finance divisions. There are 19 Autonomous Institutions and 5 Science/Engineering Professional
bodies which are working in diverse areas for the promotion of Science and Technology. In addition, one
new autonomous body viz. National Centre for Molecular Material Research, Thiruvanathapuram has been
recently set up and National Innovation Foundation, Ahmedabad which was earlier a ‘Corpus Funded’
Financial Institute has been taken over by the Department as fully funded autonomous Research Institute.
The Scientific Groups inter-alia consists of the Science & Engineering Research Council (SERC), National
Council for Science and Technology Communication (NSCTC), National Resources Data Management
System (NRDMS), International Division, National Entrepreneurship Board (NEB), National Science and
Technology Management Information System (NSTMIS). The Scientific Services have two subordinate
offices, namely, National Atlas and Thematic Mapping Organisation (NATMO) and Survey of India (SoI)
under their administrative control.

was considered by the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology,
Environment and Forests in its meeting held on the 14

the Department are in most of the cases procedural and administrative in nature i.e. delays in
approval of scheme. Surrendering of a grant at the end of the year is not only a reflection of fiscal
mismanagement but an imprudent practice of denying funds that could have benefited some other
deserving schemes.

7. MECHANISM  TO  MONITOR  AND  REVIEW  THE  ON GOING  SCHEMES
7.1 The Department informed the Committee that it has a sound mechanism for monitoring and
evaluation of progress of various schemes. Schemes are being monitored by various expert groups and
committees constituted by the Department. Feedback mechanisms including stakeholder consultations,
wherever necessary, have been put in place during the implementation of some of the schemes to fine
tune the programs. Separate reviews have also been done during the year for several schemes of the
Department. Monitoring of targets and financial progress is also undertaken in the Department at
Secretary level.
7.2 The Committee notes the mechanism devised by the Department of Science and Technology
to monitor and evaluate the progress of various schemes. However, the Committee is of the view
that had the monitoring mechanism of the Department been so robust, underutilisation would
not have taken place under some schemes particularly in Supercomputing Mission and Survey of
India. The Committee recommends that the Department needs to further strengthen monitoring
mechanism.

15. MEGA  FACILITIES  FOR  BASIC  RESEARCH
15.1 The Mega Facilities for Basic Research Programme, or the Mega Science Programme, as it is
popularly known, aims at creating Mega Science facilities and launch Mega Science projects in and out of
the country to improve access to such state-of-the-art facilities for the Indian scientific community,
especially from the academic sector. Such projects, being technically extremely complex and requiring
huge funds and other resources, are mostly, and naturally, multi-agency, multi-institutional and most
often, international in character. Most of the projects currently under implementation under this programme
in the areas of nuclear physics, high energy physics, astronomy and astrophysics etc., have been jointly
funded and overseen by the Department of Science and Technology (DST) and the Department of Atomic
Energy (DAE). DST and DAE have a long-standing Memorandum of Understanding on joint funding,
implementation and monitoring of such projects. Several important developments took place during the
year under this Programme.
15.2 The M ega Facilities for  the Basic Research Programme has twin objectives:
(i) to enable  Indian  scientists to participate in building very large international facilities which are
manifestly multinational efforts and gain access to the same for scientific studies, and
(ii) to create  some  facilities  of  international  standards  in the country. India’s participation in the
Large Hadron Collider (LHC), FAIR, the Thirty Metre Telescope (TMT), etc. falls in this
category.
15.3 The Department was asked about the performance of Mega Facilities for Basic Research Programme
in last 3 years and steps taken for creating Mega facilities. It was replied that projects under the “Mega
Facilities for Basic Research Programme” are such projects that not only require huge financial resources
but also sophisticated scientific knowledge at the very frontiers. In fact, such projects also push the global
technology frontiers. Such projects are set up at the best possible locations in the world and with the best
possible global know-how.

20
15.4 The Committee was informed that India’s participation in such projects worldwide is certainly helping
in setting up a few such projects for basic research in the country. For example, the India-based Neutrino
Observatory (INO) project in Tamil Nadu will use Resistive Plate Chambers as the detector element which
has been pioneered by CERN, Geneva and Indian scientists have participated in this development there as a
part of the CERN-India Collaboration. Participation of Indian scientists in neutrino experiments at Fermilab
will also help in doing science at INO. Indian scientists have made significant contributions towards the
LHC Computing Grid and established two Tier 2 Grid Computing facilities in the country for analyzing the
CERN data. This has initiated Grid Computing activities in the country, which has much wider scope of
application. The segmented mirror technology that is being learnt by the Indian astronomy community will
pave the way for establishing a large (8-10 m class) optical telescope at a suitable site in the Himalayas at an
appropriate time in future.
15.5 Responding to the query of the Committee about collaboration of DST and Department of Atomic
Energy, it was informed that creation of such huge research infrastructures in the country is a multi-agency
task. DAE and DST have shown excellent cooperation and coordination in creating such infrastructure in
the country. Most of the national and international projects planned for the XII Plan period had been initiated
by the Department.
15.6 The Committee desired to know the progress made in the areas identified by the Department in
collaboration with the Department of Atomic Energy for building effective partnership in creating mega
facilities. It was informed that the ongoing projects were in the areas of nuclear physics, high energy
physics, astronomy and astrophysics, macromolecular crystallography and materials science. Scientific
deliberations were in progress for new initiatives under Mega Facilities for Basic Research programme
which included establishing a new generation synchrotron radiation source facility in the country. For
identifying other areas, a Vision meeting was planned shortly.
15.7 The Committee commends the infrastructure developed under Mega Facilities for Basic
Research in partnership with national and international agencies particularly with the Department
of Atomic Energy and recommends that adequate budgetary provision should be made for marching
ahead in avant-garde science.

16. NATIONAL  SUPERCOMPUTING  FACILITY  AND  CAPACITY  BUILDING
16.1 National Supercomputing Mission is driven by the aim to consolidate and build synergy in various
ongoing efforts in supercomputing across the country.
16.2 Some of the mission oriented departments such as Defence and Research Development Organisation
(DRDO), DAE and other few leading academic institutions were involved in the use of supercomputers.
However, in the recent times, many more departments and research laboratories in government, financial
sector, telecom  industry, e-governance, Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI), education, and
intelligence agencies have also come into the fold of users of supercomputers. Private sectors such as

22
pharmaceutical industry, automotive industry, online gaming industry, entertainment industry, online services
with Indian language contents have also joined the field.
16.3 Supercomputing has potential applications in the fields of climate modeling, weather prediction,
engineering including CFD, CSM, CEM, Agriculture Research and Education, Computational Biology, Materials
and Chemicals., Molecular Dynamics, Atomic energy simulations, Security and defence applications, financial
and Big Data Analytics. Additionally, it can also be used in the disaster simulations and management, discoveries
beyond Earth (Astrophysics) and Information Repositories and Government Information Systems.
16.4 The major objectives of the Mission are:-
(i) Make India a world leader in High Performance Computing (HPC) and to enhance the national
capability in solving grand challenge problems of national and global relevance;
(ii) Empower scientists and researchers with state-of-the-art compute facilities for their cutting-
edge research in respective Domains;
(iii) Reduce redundancies and avoiding duplication of efforts and investments; and
(iv) Attain global competitiveness and self-reliance in HPC.
16.5 The Mission is partnered by the DST, the Department of Electronics and Information Technology in
consultation with the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru and Centre for Development of Advance Computing
(C-DAC), Pune. These organisations are preparing the strategies for implementing the Programme. In view of the
inter-ministerial nature of the proposed mission, National Supercomputing Mission follows six principles namely
ONE Mission, ONE Goal, Mission First, Centralised Planning, Decentralised Implementation, Clear Division of
Roles, Responsibilities and Costs, Harmonisation, Coordination and Sharing; and Federated Governance Structure.
16.6 Financial aspects
Total budget allocated for the seven years for Supercomputing Mission is ` 4, 500 Crore out of which
DST will be contributing ` 2,740 Crore; whereas ` 1,760 Crore will be contributed by DeitY . The project is to
be completed within seven years. Some of the key deliverables of the project are 70+ HPC installations, HPC
Grid, Million-core cloud, development of HPC applications, 20000 HPC-aware manpower, R&D for next-
generation Exa-Scale HPC. The total cost of this seven years mission is ` 4500 Crore with following breakup.

19. POPULARISATION  OF  TECHNOLOGIES  DEVELOPED
19.1 The Committee understands that Scientific Departments of Government of India have developed
a number of technologies that are ready for commercialisation. Departments of Atomic Energy and
Space have also developed many spin off technologies which have the potential to serve the society in
a number of ways, for instance providing potable water, checking adulteration in milk, keeping the
environment clean and green and providing healthcare at affordable cost. However, the Committee is
concerned with the slow pace at which these socially useful technologies developed by various Scientific
Departments are trickling down to the targeted groups in particular and society in general. This is
primarily because Scientific Departments have not made any proactive combined effort to create a
dedicated agency and portal for popularisation of socially useful technologies and other technologies
ready for commercialisation. Though some individual efforts like Board of Radiation and Isotope
Technology (BRIT) of Department of Atomic Energy have been created but they are inadequate and are
like a drop in the Ocean.

28
19.2 The Committee is of the opinion that the pace of dissemination of fruits of technology to the
end user needs to be expedited. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Science
and Technology, being the nodal department of scientific department responsible for consideration
and policy formulation, in collaboration with other scientific Departments, should consider developing
an institutional mechanism exclusively for marketing and popularization of technologies developed
by various scientific departments, so that the benefits of these technologies are actually utilised by
the people for whom those are intended.

process of India becoming an Associate Member of CERN, the funds under Mega Facilities for Basic
Research would now be optimally utilised by Department of Science and Technology and desired
output will be achieved. (Para 15.12)
NATIONAL  SUPERCOMPUTING  FACILITY  AND  CAPACITY  BUILDING
The financial performance of the Programme during the last five years makes it clear that
the performance of the Programme has been extremely poor. (Para 16.8)
The Committee observes that there has been undue delay in the operationalisation of National
Supercomputing Programme. The inordinate delay in holding of meeting of National Empowerment
Board is inexplicable as it resulted in undue delay in the operationalisation of Mission, practically
ruling out the possibility of making any significant headway for realising the Mission.
(Para 16.11)
The Committee, therefore, expresses its strong displeasure that the Department has not even
been able to fully utilise funds meant for operationalisation of Supercomputing Mission and
recommends that the Department should ensure full utilisation of allocated resources and should
ask for additional funds only when it is in a position to utilise additional funds asked for by it. The
Committee is also of the considered view that the procedural aspects of the National Supercomputing
Mission could have been handled in a better way and a proactive approach should have been adopted
to get the desired result which sadly is lacking. (Para 16.12)
The Committee also finds that India was a leader in Supercomputing till 2005 but it has now
slipped down in the global ranking. The Committee is of the view that boosting indigenous efforts in
Supercomputing capability is in strategic interests of India, more so when Information Technology
capabilities of the country are recognised worldwide. The Committee recommends that considering

34
the significance of Supercomputing, the Department should make every possible effort to execute
the programme in a mission mode, with a view to avoid any delay. (Para 16.13)
THE NATIONAL MISSION ON NANO SCINECE AND TECHNOLOGY
The Committee notes with satisfaction that India holds the third rank in terms of publications
of papers on Nano science and technology/Materials area. The Committee takes note of the various
initiatives taken under the National Nano Science and Technology Programme such as thematic
projects, Nano Application and Technology Development Programme and hopes that the programme
will achieve the targets set within stipulated time. (Para 17.11)
The Committee finds that fund utilisation under the Mission has improved under the
Programme in the last three years. However, the Committee recommends that the Department
should inject the qualitative parameters in terms of utilization of resources of the National Mission
on Nano Science and Technology and set regulatory framework for Nano Science and Technology
expeditiously. (Para 17.12)
EFFORTS  FOR  RETAINING  RESEARCH  TALENT  IN  THE  COUNTRY
The Committee takes note of the schemes run by the Department that have resulted in
arresting brain drain of accomplished scientists to India particularly Visiting Advanced Joint Research
Scheme and Overseas Visiting Doctoral Programme and hopes that these initiatives will create
conducive environment for doing R&D in the country at par with international standards and yield
desired results. (Para 18.4)
POPULARISATION  OF  TECHNOLOGIES  DEVELOPED
The Committee is of the opinion that the pace of dissemination of fruits of technology to the
end user needs to be expedited. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Science
and Technology, being the nodal department of scientific department responsible for consideration
and policy formulation, in collaboration with other scientific Departments, should consider developing
an institutional mechanism exclusively for marketing and popularization of technologies developed
by various scientific departments, so that the benefits of these technologies are actually utilised by
the people for whom those are intended. (Para 19.2)
UTILISATION  OF  SOCIALLY  USEFUL  TECHNOLOGIES  READY  FOR  COMMERCIAL  USE
DEVELOPED  BY  SCIENTIFIC  DEPARTMENTS  WITH  SCHEMES/PROGRAMMES
OF  MINISTRIES/DEPARTMENTS
The Committee welcomes the joint collaborative steps taken by DST in collaboration with
Department of Heavy Industries, Ministries of Railways and Urban Development and hopes that
these collaborations will fill the critical gaps.  The Committee is of the view that the Department
should leave no stone unturned in assisting the lead Ministries/Departments for the success of
these programmes. (Para 20.2)
The Committee feels that yet another effective way of gainfully utilizing the socially beneficial
technologies ready for commercial use could be to integrate them with the related schemes/
programmes being run by various Ministries/Departments of Government of India. All that the
Department of Science and Technology needs to do is to reach out to the concerned Ministries/

35
Departments and to impress upon them to integrate into their programmes/projects, these proven
technologies. For example, providing potable drinking water in rural and urban areas has turned
out to be a formidable challenge for the Ministries of Drinking water and Sanitation and Urban
Development during natural Disaster which has affected country in a big way in recent past. After
such disaster, problem of drinking water affects people in a big way. CSIR has developed Reverse
Osmosis mounted bus. This technology can be of great help to affected States facing natural disaster.
Similarly, Department of Atomic Energy has proven technology for water purification sludge
hygienisation and water desalination, which costs as less as 6 Paisa per litre. This technology can
easily be replicated at all those places facing safe potable water problems or where underground
water is contaminated with arsenic or fluoride, etc. both in urban and rural areas of the country
by getting it linked with Rural Water Mission and in the urban areas with the Urban Local
Government Bodies. (Para 20.3)
Similarly, Department of Science and Technology can help power deficient states with
transferring solar LED bulbs, Surya Jyoti which saves power. Milk adulteration checking technology
developed by Departments of Science and Technology and Scientific and Industrial Research can be
shared with State Governments and Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. The Committee believes
that by doing so the Department would be able to disseminate socially beneficial technologies ready
for commercial use in a big way and their benefits would also easily reach to the people and the
society. This will also help the Ministry of Science and Technology earn creditability and goodwill.
(Para 20.4)
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Science and Technology
being the nodal Department for Science, Technology and Innovation should act on the lines being
suggested by the Committee and initiate interaction with the concerned Ministries/Departments
to convince them of the relevance and efficacy of technologies developed by Scientific Departments
within a given time-frame. The Committee also recommends that the Department should take
all possible measures to take the technologies to the people as technology lying in the labyrinth
of laboratory is good for nothing. Its real success lies in its application for the benefit of the
poorest and the weakest. (Para 20.5)
