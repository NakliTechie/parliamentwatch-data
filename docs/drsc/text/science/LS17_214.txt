PREFACE 
  I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment 
and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Two Hundred fourteenth 
Report on the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Fifth 
Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2010-2011) of the Department of Bio-technology. 
2.   The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology on the recommendations 
contained in the Two Hundred Fifth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and 
Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-2011) of the Department of Bio-technology which was 
presented to the Rajya Sabha on 22nd April, 2010. 
3.   There were sixteen recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Fifth Report of the Committee. Action Taken Notes 
on the recommandations of the Committee were received from the Department on 2nd August, 2010. 
4.   In the meeting held on 8th December, 2010, the Comm ittee considered the draft Action Taken Report and adopted the 
same.

REPORT 
  The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology on the recommendations 
contained in the Two Hundred Fifth Report of the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and 
Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-2011) of the Department of Bio-technology, which 
was presented to both the houses of Parliament on 22nd April,  2010. There were sixteen recommendations in the Two Hundred 
Fifth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the 
Department on 2nd August, 2010. 
  The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken by the 
Department of Bio-technology are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: - 
Recommendation (Para 4.3) 
  While the Committee appreciates the above goals set by the Depa rtment for the Eleventh Five Year Plan 2007-12, it noted 
with concern that the trend in expenditure incurred so far during the Eleventh Plan period is not very encouraging. During the 
last three financial years, i.e. 2007-08, 2008-09, 2009-10 around 43 per cent of the total budgetary allocation has been spent by 
the end of the March, 2010. Thus, the Department will have to spend the remaining 57 per cent of the total Eleventh Five Year 
Plan allocation in the next two years. The Committee, therefor e, recommends that there must not be any laxity in utilising 
resources allocated to it and the trend in utilization of resources should be uniform and consistent during the entire Plan period. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The department during the last three financial years made a total projection of 67% of the Eleventh plan allocation of 
Rs.6389.00 crores. Every year as against th e proposed outlay, the budgetary allocations have been at lower level taking into 
account the B.E. of 2006-07 or last year of the Tenth plan as base year. However, it may be noted that while the B.E. of 2007-10 
period was 40% of the Eleventh plan total outlay, the department has spent 43% of the allocation in the last three years of the  
Eleventh Plan with increases at R.E. stage. With the programmes/projects planned for the years 2010-11 and 2011-12, there will 
be no laxity in utilising the resources allocated. 
Comments 
  The Committee takes note of the reply of the Department . However, the Committee feels that the progress of the 
ongoing schemes needs to be monitored a nd reviewed at regular intervals so as to ensure that the budgetary allocation 
for a financial year is properly utilized. This will also en sure uniform and consistent utilization of resources during the 
entire plan period. 
Recommendation (Paras 5.5, 5.6, 5.7 and 6) 
  The Committee expresses its dismay over the fact that despite the track record of utilisation being as high as 98.55 per 
cent, 90.15 per cent and 99.82 per cent during the last three years respectively, the Department has to face mandatory cut of 1 0 
per cent of B.E. under most of the programmes. The Committee is of  the view that such routine cuts should not be effected in 
scientific Departments as it adversely affe cts not only research and development, but also scientific and technical advancement  
of the nation. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Bio-technology should be exempted from such 
routine cuts and the entire allocations as requested by the Depart ment for the year 2010-11 at B.E. stage should be released at  
the R.E. level also. The Committee also recommends that the Department should vigorously take up this matter with the 
Planning Commission so as to ensure that no routine reduction is resorted to at the R.E. level. 
  The Committee expresses its serious concern over the drastic cut in the plan allocation for the Department. Even in case 
of allocation for priority areas where realistic projections were  made by the Department, there has been a cut which has forced  
the Department to reprioritize and reassess the expenditure on new initiatives. The committee is of the view that unless there is 

substantial increase in the allocation, the Department will not be able to translate their dream of making India to take a gian t 
leap innovation and research because growth is closely linked with investments made. 
  As regards the minimum requirement of Rs.200.00 crores  more for the research and development, public-private 
partnership and infrastructure sectors; the Committee suggests that the Department should take up the issue emphatically with 
the Planning Commission and give a comprehensive note providing cogent reasons and details of the funds required under each 
sector. 
  The Committee feels that as against the backdrop of such a brilliant performance by the Department of Bio-technology, it 
is sheer injustice with the Department if Finance Ministry fa ils to provide total budgetary support at least for strategic 
programmes being undertaken by the Department. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that brilliantly performing 
Department should be rewarded and encouraged by providing total budgetary support as per their demand. The Committee, 
therefore, also recommends that the financia l requirements of the Department must be met by the Ministry of Finance at the 
R.E. level. 
Action Taken by the Government 
  The Department has communicated the views of Parliamentary Standing Committee alongwith detailed justification for 
appropriate allocations matching with the programmes and trend of expenditure to Planning Commission and Ministry of 
Finance. It was informed that the increased allocation can be considered at R.E. stage during September-October of the current 
financial year depending upon the expenditure as on date. 
Comments 
  The Committee hopes that the Department would succe ssfully pursue with the Pl anning Commission and the 
Ministry of Finance, the case for incre ased allocation at R.E. stage. The Committee also reiterates its recommendation 
that the axe of routine cuts should not fall on the scientific departments, as it affects their performance adversely. 
  The Committee also hopes that the minimum requirement of Rs. 200 crores more for Research and Development, 
Public Private Partnership and Infrastructure sectors sh ould be impressed upon the Planning Commission as the 
country is in its nascent phase so far as growth of Bio-technology is concerned  and requires substantial investment in 
infrastructure. 
Recommendation (Para 7.3) 
  The Committee in its 190th report on the Demand for Grants (2008-2009) had strongly recommended that the Department 
should draw a reasonable time frame for establishing new autonomous institutions and devise a mechanism to monitor their 
progress periodically so as to avoid time and cost overruns. However, the Committee is constrained to note that the six new 
institutions are yet to be set up. The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction at the undue delay in the obtaining of the neces sary 
Government approvals and reiterates its recommendation for expediting the setting up of the proposal six institutions, so that 
they could be completed as per schedule and the new institutions could start functioning at the earliest and become centres of 
excellence.  
Action Taken by Government 
  The six institutes have been approved for implementation during the Eleventh plan. Following a procedure of preparation 
of detailed project report, approval through EFC and Cabinet, the institutes have been approved at different time intervals 
during the last three years. The Translati onal Research in Health Science and Technology Institute, Faridabad was approved in 
November 2007; UNESCO Regional Centre for Education, Training and Innovation in Bio-technology, Faridabad in November 
2008; Institute for Stem Cell Science and Regenerative Medicine, Bangalore in November 2008; National Agri-Food Bio-
technology Institute and Bio-processing Unit at Mohali, Punjab in May 2008; National Institute of Bio-medical Genomics, 
Kolkata in February 2009; and National Institute of Animal Bio-technology, Hyderabad in May 2010. In all the institutions, the 
time schedule of various activities during implementation has been worked out. Accordingly, the implementation on ground so 
far has been as per schedule. In all the institutions, Directors or Officers on Special Duty (OSD) have been appointed, 

architectural, plan has been finalised and construction has begun, (except in case of National Institute of Animal Bio-technology 
– approved recently) an d interim laboratory facilities have been establishe d for undertaking research activities as per the 
mandate. 
Comments 
  The Committee notes with concern that  procedural requirements for the setting up of the six new institutions, such 
as preparation of detailed project Repo rt, approval through EFC and Cabinet, etc. are ta king unduly long time. The 
Committee reiterates its recommendations for expediting setting up of these institutes. 
Recommendation (Para 7.4) 
  The Committee also recommends that the Department should focus on research and development in bio-energy and bio-
fuels, particularly, the green sources of energy which is badly required today in view of the climate change and global warming . 
The network programme on biobutanol and green diesel should, therefore, be launched at the earliest, so as to expedite the early 
breakthrough in this regard. 
Action Taken by Government 
  R and D, demonstrations, product development and commercia lisation are major thrust areas of bio-energy and bio-fuels 
programme launched during 2007-08. The first energy bio-scien ce centre, established at Univer sity Institute of Chemical 
Technology has already developed technologies for cellulosic ethanol. The programmes on biobutanol and green diesel have 
peen initiated during the month of June 2010. 
Comments 
  The Committee is aware that a consid erable amount of precious foreign exch ange has been spent on import of 
fossil fuels from abroad. The Committee th erefore reiterates that the Department sh ould accelerate its R and D in bio-
energy and bio-fuels and find an  economical alternative to fo ssil fuels so as to reduce the country’s dependence on 
import from abroad. 
Recommendation (Para 8.3) 
  The Committee feels that quality human resource forms the back bone of research activities in a country and as such the 
Department need to accord greater priority to lapping, nurturi ng and involving talented students in scientific pursuits. The 
Committee is to the opinion that the Department must playa pro-active role in this regard. The Committee appreciates the 
efforts being made by the Department in creating capable human resource in the bio-technology sector and extending support to 
students at the college level through fellowships, short term training courses, providing industrial training to 
B.Tech/M.Sc/M.Tech bio-technology students and hopes that such schemes would be encouraged and expanded to 
universities/colleges in all the states, however, it recommends  that this programme should be further intensified and 
strengthened and more funds should be earmarked for this programme. 
Action Taken by Government 
  In order to expand and strengthen this programme, the Department had launched a scheme to redesign /upgrade university 
department of life sciences to start an interdisciplinary pr ogramme on life sciences for adva nced training, education and 
research. Under this scheme, universities are supported based on site visits and assessment of past performance, state-of-art post 
graduate teaching laboratories, centralised facilities with major equipments and seed money for research in emerging areas, etc . 
About 25 universities will be supported and the scheme has been already operational at a total cost of Rs.25 crores per year. 
Comments 
  The Committee notes that the Departme nt has not adequately addressed the recommendation of encouraging and 
expanding schemes of building quality human resource in universities/colleges in all the states. The Committee therefore 
reiterates its recommendation that the Department must ensu re that universities/colleges in all the states must be 
covered under such schemes. 

Recommendation (Para 8.4) 
  The Committee also recommends that the Star College scheme  which aims towards strengthening of life science, bio-
technology education and training and provides experience to bio-technology students in the cutting edge-technologies needed 
for modrn biological studies at undergraduate level in life sciences should be supported to the maximum extent through funds 
allocation and hopes that as a result of such experiences, more students will take up life science as a career. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The Department has initiated scheme ‘Star College’ to provide hands-on exposure to students in life sciences at 
undergraduate level, faculty improvement programmes, strengtheni ng of infrastructure in laboratories, library, computer labs 
etc. The programme has completed one year. Under this programme - 37 colleges have been supported so far from all parts of 
the country. The programme has provided the desired impetus which is evident from enhanced enrollments, better university 
result, better cut-offs, increased hands-o n experience, faculty improvement and opportunities for interaction for faculty and 
students with leading experts in the field. It is planned to create common standard teaching, materials, films, etc. for sharin g 
with all colleges supported under this programme. Further, more colleges will be supported for which adequate funds are made 
available under the scheme Human Resource Development of the Department. 
Comments 
  The Committee notes the reply.  
Recommendation (Para 9.4) 
  The Committee appreciates the Department in taking initiatives towards promotion of new R and D projects across 
various streams of Bio-technology. The Committee is also of the opinion that the economy of our country is basically based on 
agriculture and for the development of the country and for providin g food security to the masses, agricultural yields have to b e 
increased many folds. The Committee theref ore recommends that the Department should  concentrate its R and D activities on 
agriculture Bio-technology to develop new variety of higher yielding, pest and disease resistant crops. The Committee also 
recommends that the Department should engage itself in exploring the possibilities for the use of barren and desert land for 
agricultural or horticultural purposes with the help of bio-techno logy, as this would help bring vast unutilized tracts of land  
under productive cultivation in the country and also prevent desertification of the productive land in the country. 
Action Taken by Government 
  Agricultural Bio-technology is an important priority area for R and D for the department. The department has been 
supporting research projects on various crops to improve thei r productivity and value addition through pests and diseases 
resistance, tolerance to a biotic stresse s like drought, salinity, submergence, rising atmospheric temperature and nutrient 
contents. The department has also undertaken a major programme on Accelerated Produc t Development making use of 
molecular markers already developed for important traits in crops viz. rice, wheat, maize, chickpea and soybean grown in soil at 
different fertility level and soil structure. Proposals for network projects on improvement of agricultural productivity of coa stal 
region and developing heat and drought tolerant for wheat genotypes using molecular markers are also being funded during 
2010-11.  
Comments 
  The Committee notes that the Depart ment has not responded to the recommend ation on use of barren and desert 
land for agriculture. The Committee, theref ore reiterates its recommendation regardi ng the exploration of possibilities 
for use of barren and desert land for agricultural use, with the use of agriculture bio-technology. 
Recommendation (Para 9.5) 
  The Committee also appreciates the initiative of Department to start a public-private partnership with the International 
Crops Research Institute for Semi-Arid Tropi cs (ICRISAT) for development of new variet ies of crops and feels that better and 
faster results in the field of research in  agricultural bio-technology can be obtain ed only if the expertise gained by the 

Government and private sector are pooled together. The Committ ee therefore recommends that such public-private partnership 
projects should be encouraged and sufficient funds should be allocated to initiate the projects across the country. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The Department has established a technology platform for product development and commercialization of biotech crops 
at ICRISAT. The facility is accessible through agriculture universities, ICAR institutions and small and medium seed 
companies. The promising research products are identified and financial support through public-private partnership is provided 
under special schemes Small Business Innovative Research Init iatives and Bio-technology Industry Partnership Programme. 
During the Annual Plan 2010-11, a total allocation of Rs.118 crores has been provided for this purpose compared to Rs.60 
crores in 2009-10. 
Comments 
  The Committee notes the reply of the Department. 
Recommendation (Para 10.3) 
  The Committee is of the opinion that the benefits of science and technology should percolate to the lowest strata of 
society and help in upliftment of rural masses and women. The Committee is also of the view that popularization of proven and 
field tested technologies will help the target rural population in their skill development, employment and income generation in  
the field of agriculture, sericulture, production and manufacture of biopesticides and biofertilisers. In health sector too, su ch 
projects result in creating awareness on nutrition includ ing traditional food and health care. The Committee therefore 
recommends that such programmes which involve local people throug h self help groups and result in overall societal benefit 
should be encouraged by the Department and efforts should be made to ensure that benefits of such programmes reach the 
various parts of the country. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The department has been implementing bio-technology based programmes for Societal Development to benefit rural areas 
particularly women and SC/ST populations. These programmes will continue to be supported and expanded during 2010-11 and 
accordingly budget has also been increased from Rs.l 0 crores in 2009-10 to Rs.15 crores in 2010-11. 
Comments 
  The Committee notes the reply.  
Recommendation (Para 11.5) 
  The Committee is aware that global re search towards discovery of vaccines a nd medicines for diseases afflicting the 
western countries find precedence over the diseases which are commonly found in the developing countries such as Cholera, 
Typhoid, Rabies, Malaria, Dengue, Tuberculosis. It is therefore the responsibility of the scientific departments to concentrate on 
drug discovery and development for the aforesaid diseases which afflict millions of people across the country and also make 
them available to the poor people at affordable prices. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department should 
accelerate the development of vaccines and medicines thro ugh its Vaccine Grand Challe nge Programme and ensure 
participation of both premier government scientific and the private sector research institutions in this regard. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The department recognises the observations made by the committee and the importance of development of indigenous 
drugs and vaccines in healthcare. The Vaccine Grand Challenge Programme has already become operational for development of 
vaccines such as Choler a, Typhoid, Rabies, Malaria, Dengue, and Tuberculosis. Public sector institutions and private sector 
hospitals are all engaged in the programme. A separate management unit has been setup with a chief executive officer to 
coordinate the programme. In addition, a National Development (Clinical) Service Agency has also been established to promote 
pre-clinical and clinical development of health technologies a nd products generated; in terms of providing expertise for pre-

clinical services, clinical trials and regulatory advices through Indian partnership with global entity for SMEs, public funded  
institutions and not for profit organizations. The main objectives of NDSA is to provide low cost and quality services for 
evaluating vaccines bio-tech products in a clinical trial set up upto 10 faculty positions name have already been filled. 
Comments 
  The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Depa rtment in promoting the ‘Vaccine Grand Challenge 
Programme’ and expresses its satisfaction over the fact that it has become operational for development of vaccines such 
as Cholera, Typhoid, Rabies, Malaria, De ngue, and Tuberculosis, involving both the Public sector institutions and 
private sector hospitals. The Committee hopes that the efforts of the Department will bear fruit and lead to discovery of 
drugs afflicting the common man. 
Recommendation (Para 12) 
  The Committee while appreciating the efforts made by the Department so far in this field, recommends that the 
Department should make concerted efforts to involve more and more Indian industries and encourage young entrepreneurs to 
give a boost to the area of bio-technology which has already emerged as promising and fast growing sector for feeding the 
economic development of country. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The Department is already implementing a public-private partnership scheme namely Small Business Innovative 
Research Initiatives to promote R and D and entrepreneurship in small and medium enterprises. Besides, schemes on 
Entrepreneurship Development Programme and Young Entrepreneur s Scheme are also launched with the aim of encouraging 
young postgraduate and doctoral students in developing bio-technology entrepreneurship and exposing them to issues involved 
in commercialization of bio-science. Schemes have also been designed for scientist entrepreneurship programme to increase 
number of start-up companies. 
Comments 
  The Committee hopes that the trend of encouraging the en trepreneurs in the area of bio-technology should be 
given full budgetary support. The scheme besides promotin g employment of our youth would also help in the 
development of the country. 
Recommendation (Para 13.4) 
  The Committee is of the opinion that mandatory 10% allocation of total budget to North-Eastern Region would be more 
beneficial for the Region. The Committee therefore recommends th at instead of thinking of limiting the annual allocation of 
Rs.80.00 crores, the Department should make efforts towards creating of skilled human resource and setting up institutional 
resources of the Department to ensure that  the funds allocated are fully utilized, specially when the region is in dire need of  the 
funds for its development. The Department should also plan towards cooperation with other scientific departments to ensure the 
much needed growth in this region. 
Action Taken by Government 
  The Department of Biotechnology has earmarked 10% of its total Annual Budget amounting to Rs.120.00 crore to North 
Eastern States for the year 2010-11. It has already identified se veral new areas to be formulated/ implemented in the areas of 
Life Sciences and Biotechnology towards Strengthening of Human Resource Development Programmes, Establishment of 
Outreach Research Centre in Sikkim, Establishment of new Bioinformatics Centres, Electronic Journal Access Facility, 
National and Overseas Fellowships, Telemedicine and Communi cation for Health Care Services, Establishment of Biotech 
Hubs and Biotechnology Centres, etc. The Department is also implementing several agriculture related programmes in 
association with ICAR. A Biotechnology Park is being established at IIT Guwahati. 
 
 

Comments 
  The Committee notes the steps taken by the Department to ensure developm ent of bio-technology in the North 
East Region of the country. 
Recommendation (Para 14) 
  The Committee is of the view that the Department of Biotechnology through its various research and development 
programmes is striving to uplift the standard of living in the country. Keeping in view the excellent work that the Department 
has undertaken, the kind of competence it has exhibited and the kind of core and critical programmes it has envisaged for the 
future, the Committee strongly recommends that the Department of Biotechnology should be properly and adequately funded 
and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. stage should be strictly avoided. 
Action Taken by Government 
  Noted and the department would continue to perform in terms of physical, financial and technical achievements. 
Comments 
  The Committee hopes that the Department of Bio-technology would strive towards better performance and 
productivity and ensure that the benefits of science reaches the common man.
