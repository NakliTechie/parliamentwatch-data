REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science and
Technology on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred and Ninety-sixth Report of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the Demands for Grants (2017-18) of the Department of Science and Technology, presented
to both the Houses of Parliament on 31st March, 2017. There were 29 recommendations/observations
in the Two Hundred and Ninety-sixth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations
of the Committee were received from the Department on 4th August, 2017.
The Committee's recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the
Action Taken by the Department of Science and Technology are set out in the following paragraphs:–
TWELFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN OUTLAY AND ITS UTILISATION
Recommendation
The Committee notes that during the financial year 2016-17, total plan BE allocation of 
` 4000.00
crore was slightly reduced to ` 3981.00 crore at RE stage whereas Non-Plan allocation of ` 496.32 crore
was increased to ` 512.83 crore at RE stage. However, the Department has been able to utilise only
` 2951.75 crore under Plan Head and ` 400.37 crore by 31st January, 2017 which implies that the
Department is yet to utilise 25% of the Budgetary support with less than two months left in the
completion of financial year, the Committee is afraid that the Department will not be able to fully utilise
the amount allocated. (Para 5.6)
Action Taken
Department of Science and Technology (DST) during the financial year 2016-17 has total Plan BE
allocation of ` 4000.00 crore which was slightly reduced to ` 3981.00 crore at RE stage whereas Non
Plan allocation of `496.32 crore was increased to `512.83 crore at RE stage. The Department has utilised
` 3834.87 crore of the Plan allocation and ` 490.26 crore of the Non-Plan allocation by 31st March, 2017
which implies that the Department has utilised 96.32% of Plan RE and 95.60% of Non-Plan RE.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

2012-17 of the Department has not been up-to-the-mark. The approximate actual expenditure of the

MECHANISM TO MONITOR AND REVIEW THE ONGOING SCHEMES
Recommendation
The Committee notes the mechanism devised by the Department of Science and Technology to
monitor and evaluate the progress of various schemes. However, the Committee is of the view that had
the monitoring mechanism of the Department been so robust, underutilisation would not have taken place
under some schemes particularly in Supercomputing Mission and Survey of India. The Committee
recommends that the Department needs to further strengthen monitoring mechanism. (Para 7.2)
Action Taken
DST notes the concerns of the PSC. While initiation, review and closure of projects under a scheme
are monitored through programme monitoring and Programme Advisory Committees under various
Divisions, DST agrees that coordination and time-bound intervention are areas to be further strengthened.
This is to be addressed specifically in the ongoing appraisal/approval of the schemes/programmes for
continuation beyond the 12th Plan period to the 14th Finance Commission cycle. In case of Supercomputing
Mission, since Executive Board (EB) has started its activities, full utilization of the budget ( ` 83 crores
for 2017-18) would be ensured. Supercomputing Mission even for the financial year 2016-17 utilized
100% of its RE allocation. In case of Survey of India, while Plan and Non-Plan budgets now have been
merged; it would be ensured that the amounts allocated for activities under erstwhile Plan schemes do
not remain unutilized.
Comments
The Committee is hopeful that with co-ordinated effort and timely intervention at the
highest level, the Department will meet the targets in respect of the above two programmes.

to any other agency of the Department which is competent enough to do justice with the task of setting

Recommendations
The Committee is constrained to observe that the malady of underutilisation of funds by Survey of
India is not new. The Committee notes that in the year 2012-13 and 2015-16 also the Department had
failed to fully utilise funds made available to it under this programme. The allocations of the Department
were also drastically reduced at RE stage in 2013-14 and 2014-15. In this context, the Committee would
like to reiterate the recommendation made in its 240th Report on Demands for Grants 2012-13 of the
Department which is reproduced below:-
"Modernisation of infrastructural facilities and upgradation of data capturing and processing
capabilities and technical skills of these organisations are essential for efficient exploration and
mapping of the country's domain and to ensure that all resources available in the country contribute
their full measures to the progress, prosperity and security of our country. The Committee, therefore,
expresses its displeasure that the Department has not even been able to fully utilise funds meant for
upgradation of infrastructure of its two important organisations and recommends that the Department
should take all necessary measures to ensure early and successful implementation of this programme."
(Para 11.13)
The Committee notes that this year also, the grounds furnished by the Department for underutilisation
of funds are not convincing. The Committee feels that underutilisation of scarce funds by Survey of India
defeats the objective of allocation of funds to scientific organizations with high hopes of reaping their
benefits for larger interest of masses. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department must
make a firm resolve and unflinching commitment to fully utilise the resources allocated to the Department
this year and accomplish the task of modernisation of mapping organisations. (Para 11.14)
Action Taken
Efforts are being made to complete the infrastructure projects in time but due to various reasons
utilization of funds has not improved significantly.
To fully utilize the resources allocated, the Survey of India has undertaken revamp of the IT
infrastructure and Geodetic infrastructure of country, modernization/upgradation of data capturing and
processing capabilities and technical skill development with objectives of efficient preparation, maintenance
and updation of National Topographical Data Base (NTDB) of the country.
Comments
The Committee expects the Department to give details of reasons for underutilisation of
funds. The Committee, while taking note of the reply, would like to be apprised of the progress
achieved in revamping of the infrastructure periodically.

NATIONAL SUPERCOMPUTING FACILITY AND CAPACITY BUILDING MISSION
Recommendation
The Committee finds that in total ` 2740 cr. were earmarked for Supercomputing in Twelfth Five
Year Plan. However the Programme has not taken off till date. Under the programme, total actual
utilisation from 2012-13 to 2016-17 has been only 
` 82.64 cr. against total B.E. allocation of ` 2740.00
cr. in five years. In 2016-17 total allocation was ` 120.00 cr . which was reduced to ` 90.00 cr . at R.E.
but actual utilisation of funds by 31st January, 2017 was only ` 76 lakhs. The financial performance
of the Programme during the last five years makes it clear that the performance of the Programme has
been extremely poor. (Para 16.8)
Action Taken
The National Supercomputing Facility and Capacity Building Mission was approved by the Cabinet
on 25.03.2015 with the administrative approval issued by D/o Electronics and Information Technology
(DeitY) on 09.04.2015. The expenditure under the project started only from financial year 2015-16 after
the approval of the Cabinet. The expenditure against RE allocation has been close to 97.5% in the first
year of implementation 2015-16 and about 99.92% in 2016-17.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply.
Recommendation
The Committee observes that there has been undue delay in the operationalisation of National
Supercomputing Programme. The inordinate delay in holding of meeting of National Empowerment
Board is inexplicable as it resulted in undue delay in the operationalisation of Mission, practically ruling
out the possibility of making any significant headway for realising the Mission. (Para 16.11)
The Committee, therefore, expresses its strong displeasure that the Department has not even been
able to fully utilise funds meant for operationalisation of Supercomputing Mission and recommends that
the Department should ensure full utilisation of allocated resources and should ask for additional funds
only when it is in a position to utilise additional funds asked for by it. The Committee is also of the
considered view that the procedural aspects of the National Supercomputing Mission could have been
handled in a better way and a proactive approach should have been adopted to get the desired result
which sadly is lacking. (Para 16.12)
The Committee also finds that India was a leader in Supercomputing till 2005 but it has now slipped
down in the global ranking. The Committee is of the view that boosting indigenous efforts in
Supercomputing capability is in strategic interests of India, more so when Information Technology
capabilities of the country are recognised worldwide. The Committee recommends that considering the

significance of Supercomputing, the Department should make every possible effort to execute the programme

Action Taken
In the 2nd year of its implementation, the Expert Groups in all four verticals namely Infrastructure,
Research and Development, Applications Development and Human Resources were constituted in April,
2016, under the guidance of NSM-Technical Advisory Committee (NSM-TAC). Subsequently, the Expert
Groups met together first in April, 2016 and then had independent meetings.
Based on the Expert Group on Infrastructure recommendations and subsequent discussions in the
NSM-TAC, the NSM-TAC reviewed and approved the Mission Implementation Strategy Document and
Preliminary Design Review (PDR) document for the various specifications/ capacities of supercomputers.
Subsequently, in the 2nd meeting of NSM-Executive Board (NSM-EB) in December, 2016, based on the
recommendations of NSM-TAC, it approved providing 6 supercomputers with data centres (3 in build
mode and 3 in buy mode) to 6 Institutions by the end of 2017. The Institutions have been informed and
visited by the teams of IISc and CDAC who have guided them in making the infrastructure ready for
installation of supercomputers.
The NSM-EB also approved development of Applications in few areas in Phase 1 which include the
following:
(i) Drug Development Platform
(ii) Predictive and Personalized Medicine
(iii) Materials and Computational Chemistry
(iv) Climate, Weather and Disaster Prediction
(v) Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) for Engineering Applications
(vi) Digital Agriculture
Based on the approval of the EB, short-term and medium-term courses on High Performance
Computation (HPC) System Administration have been started by C-DAC at IIT-Mumbai.
The expenditure during the financial year 2016-17 was 99.92% against RE.
The Department is making every effort in executing the NSM along with Ministry of Electronics
and Information Technology.

POPULARISATION OF TECHNOLOGIES DEVELOPED
Recommendation
The Committee is of the opinion that the pace of dissemination of fruits of technology to the end
user needs to be expedited. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Science and
Technology, being the nodal department of scientific department responsible for consideration and policy
formulation, in collaboration with other scientific Departments, should consider developing an institutional
mechanism exclusively for marketing and popularization of technologies developed by various scientific
departments, so that the benefits of these technologies are actually utilised by the people for whom those
are intended. (Para 19.2)
Action Taken
The invaluable views and suggestions of Hon'ble members of the Committee are well taken and will
form the basis of the endeavours of the DST in future. SEED Division is taking requisite steps to
customize and deliver need based technologies at users level by engaging S&T knowledge institutions and
field agencies for effective technology delivery at the grassroots level.
DST is taking appropriate steps to collaborate with other scientific Departments and other agencies
to popularize and commercialize technologies developed by them to facilitate actual utilization of such
technology packages for the benefit of our people, particularly the poorest, weakest and underprivileged.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply. The Committee is hopeful that with necessary
support of the Department, the lead Ministries/Departments will popularise technologies developed
for benefit of the poorest, weakest and underprivileged people of the country.
