REPORT 
 
  INTRODUCTION 
1.  The Department of Atomic Energy that made a modest beginning in the year 1954 
with the primary objective of harnessing nuclear energy to meet the energy requirements 
of the country, has in its about  fifty eight years of  journey, mostly so litary dotted with 
international sanctions and technology denials, carved out a niche for itself in the arena of 
Atomic Energy.  Starting with a specifi c mandate of energy generation, it has 
successfully diversified and multiplied its activi ties in a number of socially relevant and 
useful programmes, projects and disciplines.  The current mandate of the Department, on 
which its programmes are based extends to:- 
 Increasing share of nuclear power through deployment of indigenous and 
other proven technologies, along with de velopment of fast breeder reactors 
and thorium reactors with associated fuel cycle facilities; 
 Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes 
and carrying out radiation technology appl ications in the fi eld of medicine, 
agriculture and industry; 
 Developing advanced technologies  such as accelerators, lasers, 
supercomputers, advanced materials a nd instrumentation, and encouraging 
transfer of technology to industry. 
 Support to basic research in nuclear energy and related frontier areas of 
science; interaction with universities and academic institutions; Support to 
research and development projects ha ving a bearing in DAE’s programmes, 
and international cooperation in related advanced areas of research; and 
 Contribution to national security.   
Some of the significant deliverables from Department of Atomic Energy include:  
 Energy Security 
 National Security 
 Food security 
 Water security 

 Health security 
 Bringing-in benefits of Nuclear and Ra diation Technologies for the growth of 
Indian industries 
 Cancer research 
 National capacity building through pr omotion of science education and 
research  
2.  While, we have enough reasons to cheer the impressive and commendable 
achievements and feel proud of the advancem ents made by the Department of Atomic 
Energy, we need to guard ourselves from the danger of becoming complacent by basking 
in the past and the present glory. The Depart ment of Atomic Energy has miles to go and 
many milestones to achieve.  It is yet to re alize its vision to empower India through 
technology, create more wealth and provide bett er quality of life to  its citizens, make 
India energy independent, contri bute to provision of sufficien t, safe and nutritious food 
and better healthcare to our people through de velopment and deployment of nuclear and 
radiation technologies and their applicatio ns. Even after, over four decades of 
commercial operation of nuclear power plants, the Department of Atomic Energy is yet to 
redeem the dream of three stage nuclear pow er programme conceived by Dr. Bhabha, the 
precursor of atomic energy in the country.  We have hitherto been able to master only the 
first of the three stage nuclear power programme.  The share of nuclear in total electricity 
generation is mere a dismal 3.7 per cent (approx .) with a total inst alled capacity of 4,780 
MW.   
3. All this, notwithstanding, the greatest st rength of Indian Nuclear Programme lies 
in its major strides towards self-reliance a nd indigenous research and development both 
in power and non-power application being the outcome of multi-disciplinary, multi-
institutional and multi-dimensional prog rammes undertaken by the Department of 
Atomic Energy in an era marked by international sanctions and technology denials.   
4.  The Department of Atomic Energy, over th e years, has established a vast network 
of institutions and evolved a broad based organisation to support,  sustain and carry 
forward its avowed objec tives and activities.  It has, so far, esta blished 107 installations 
spread across several States of the country.  The Organisations/Institutions under the 
ambit of Department of Atomic Energy, category-wise, are as follows:- 
 Research Centres:  Bhabha Atomic Research  Centre (BARC)– Mumbai; 
Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Re search (IGCAR)– Kalpakkam; Raja 
Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT)– Indore; Variable 
Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC)– Kolkata; and Atomic Minerals Directorate 
for Exploration and Research (AMD) – Hyderabad; Global Centre for Nuclear 
Energy Partnership (GCNEP), Gurgaon. 
 Industrial Organisations:  Heavy Water Board (HWB) – Mumbai; Nuclear 
Fuel Complex (NFC)– Hyderabad; a nd Board of Radiation and Isotope 
Technology (BRIT)– Mumbai.  
 Service Organisations:  Directorate of Purchase  & Stores (DPS)– Mumbai, 
Directorate of Construction, Services  and Estate Management (DCSEM)– 
Mumbai; and General Services Organisation (GSO)– Kalpakkam.   
 Public Sector Undertakings:  Nuclear Power Corpor ation of India Ltd. 
(NPCIL)– Mumbai; Bharatiya Nabhiki ya Vidyut Nigam Ltd. (BHAVINI); 
Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu; Uranium Co rporation of India Ltd. (UCIL)– 

Jaduguda; Indian Rare Earths Ltd. (IREL)– Mumbai; and Electronics 
Corporation of India Ltd. (ECIL)– Hyderabad.  
 Aided Institutions:  Tata Institute of Fundame ntal Research (TIFR) – 
Mumbai; Tata Memorial Centre (TMC) – Mumbai; Saha Inst itute of Nuclear 
Physics (SINP) – Kolkata; Institute of Physics (IOP) – Bhubaneswar; National 
Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER) – Bhubaneswar; Harish-
Chandra Research Institute (HRI) – A llahabad; Institute of Mathematical 
Sciences (IMSc) – Chennai; Institu te for Plasma Research (IPR) – 
Ahmedabad; and Atomic Energy Education Society (AEES) – Mumbai. 
 DAE through Board of Research in Nu clear Sciences (BRNS) and National 
Board for Higher Mathematics (NBHM) Promotes research in nuclear and 
allied fields and mathematics, respectively.   
 Academic programmes run by BARC, IGCAR, RRCAT, VECC, SINP, IPR, 
IOP NISER, IMSc, HRI and TMC come under the fold of Homi Bhabha 
National Institute, a deemed University.  TIFR is also a deemed university.  
5.  Grants for Department of Atomic Energy
 
5.1  There are two Grants, i.e., Grant No. 4 (Atomic Energy) and Grant No. 5 (Nuclear 
Power Schemes) under which Demands for Grants  of the Department of Atomic Energy 
are made.  Grants for six research Centres, three industrial Organi sations, three Public 
Sector Undertakings; Aided In stitutions; three Service Orga nisations; Atomic Energy 
Regulatory Board (AERB), etc. are covered under Grant No. 4.  Grant No. 5 comprises of 
Budgetary allocations mainly for Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd. and BHAVINI 
besides Bhabha Atomic Research Centre fo r Waste Water Management facilities and 
Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research for Prototype Fast Breeder Test Reactor, etc.

8.  Utilisation of XI Five Year Budgetary Outlay  
8.1  It is pertinent to  point out here that the approved outlay for a Five Year Plan does 
not in any way guarantee that the Department  would actually get and utilize the entire 
allocated amount during the span of five financ ial years.  On an analysis of the overall 
financial summary of the Department of Atom ic Energy pertaining to XI Five Year Plan 
reveals that out of the total approved ou tlay of Rs. 46431.13 Crores, the Actual for the 
first 4 years, i.e., 2007-08 to 2010-11 remained  at Rs. 21916.94 Crores.  Even in the last 
year, it was tentatively expected that the expenditure/ utilisati on would extend to Rs. 
31811.49 Crores during the entire XI  Plan period.  But finally, it got restricted to Rs. 
29173.00 Crores.  If the total Budgetary allocati on of the XI Five Year Plan i.e. Rs. 
46431.13 Crores is distributed evenly among the five financial years, the average 
allocation per year works out tentatively to  Rs. 9000.00 Crores.  The actual utilization 

being restricted only to the extent of Rs. 29173.00 Crores suggests that the Budgetary 
resources adequate to meet the allocation fo r two financial years remained unutilized.  
Thus out of the five years, it can also be said  that there was nil or zero utilisation for two 
financial years. Going by this rate of utilisa tion, it could not be claimed that the outlay of 
Rs. 107186.73 Crores approved for the XII Five Year Plan would be fully utilized.

10. Budgetary Support in 2013-14 vis-à-vis 2012-13  
10.1  On a comparison of the budgetary ou tlay for 2013-14 with that of the 2012-13, 
the total allocation both Plan and Non-Plan  under Grant No. 4, i.e., Atomic Energy has 
gone up from Rs. 9852.33 Crores in the 2012-1 3 at the BE Stage to Rs. 11153.44 Crores 
in the year 2013-14, thus showing a net incr ease of Rs. 1301.11 Crores.  The Budgetary 
Support component has increas ed from Rs. 9388.33 Cores to Rs. 10749.64 Crores.  The 
component of IEBR has gone down from Rs . 464.00 Crores to Rs. 403.80 Crores.  The 
Budgetary Support component has thus increased by Rs. 1361.31 Crores under Grant No. 
4, while, the IEBR component has declined by Rs. 60.20 Crores.  The Committee finds a 
reverse trend under Grant No. 5, i.e., National Power Schemes.  While the Budgetary 
Support both Plan and Non-Plan has been br ought down from Rs. 4521.27 Crores to Rs.

 12. Trend of Utilisation of approved outlay 
12.1  On scrutiny of BE and RE allocations vis-à-vis Actuals for the financial year 
2010-11 and 2011-12 and BE & RE for the financ ial year 2012-13 of the Department of 
Atomic Energy, it is found that underutilizatio n of approved outlay particularly under 
Plan Head has become a recurring  problem with the Department.   
12.2  Let us take BE/RE/Actuals for 2010-11 a nd 2011-12 first.  In the financial year 
2010-11, the total BE allocati on including Plan and Non- Plan was Rs. 14841.23 Crores, 
which got reduced to Rs. 14574.42 Cr ores at the RE Stage, wh ile the actual utilisation 
came down to Rs. 12220.24 Crores.  Simila rly, the total BE outlay of Rs. 22895.64 

Crores for the financial year 2011-12 was although projected to be Rs. 23142.47 Crores 
at the RE Stage, the actual utilisation remained at Rs. 20482.83 Crores.  On close scrutiny 
of the trend of utilisation, it is found that the Department has been able to utilize the Non-
Plan outlay optimally and even in certain cases exceeded the approved outlay.  But, the 
Department has failed to make optimum utilis ation of allocated outlay under Plan Head.  
So, if only the trend of utilisation of appr oved outlay under Plan Head is considered, the 
gap between the approved outlay and actual utilisation made, becomes wider and more 
prominent.  For example, while the Plan  allocation BE for the year 2010-11 for the 
Department was Rs. 8226.00 Crores, the actu al utilisation remained at Rs. 5452.39 
Crores leaving a gap of Rs. 2773.61 Crores.  Likewise, while Plan allocation for the year 
2011-12 at BE level was Rs. 10012.00 Crores, the actual utilisation was to the extent of 
Rs. 7256.79 Crores only – a gap of Rs. 2755.21 Crores.   
12.3  The trend of underutilization of approved BE outlay is perceptible in all the 
sectors be it Research & Development, Mine rals, Industries or Power Sector.  In the 
R&D Sector, while the Plan allocations for the year 2010-11 and 2011-12, were Rs. 
2084.86 Crores and Rs. 2735.00 Crores respectively, the actual utilizations were to the 
tune of Rs. 1817.07 Crores and Rs. 2193.88 Crores  respectively. Under Industries Sector, 
while the Plan allocations for the years 2010-11 and 2011-12 were Rs. 819.14 Crores and 
Rs. 1074.00 Crores, the actual utilisation came to Rs. 605.45 Crores and Rs. 695.69 
Crores respectively.  Similar is the case with the Minerals Sector, where in the year 2011-
12, as against the BE allocation of Rs. 622.00 Crores under Plan Head, the actual 
utilisation remained at a dismal Rs. 213.97 Crores.  The total unutilized amount thus 
works out to Rs. 408.03 Crores. 
12.4 In the Power Sector, the Committee finds that the actual utilis ation of allocated 
resources was far less than the approved allocation.  While in the year 2010-11 and 2011-
12, the Budgetary Support al location under Plan Head was Rs. 1848.00 Crores and Rs. 
1609.00 Crores respectively, the actual utilisation in the corresponding years stood at Rs. 
708.24 Crores and 1378.03 Crores respectively.  It  is surprising to note that BHAVINI 
which is entrusted with the task to plan, ex ecute and operate an in tegrated programme of 
Fast Breeder Reactors – the second stage of the Nuclear Power Programme, could utilize 
a motley amount of Rs. 315.00 Crores as ag ainst the approved outlay of Rs. 1400.00 
Crores under Plan Head, thus leaving a whopping Rs. 1085.00 Crores underutilized in the 
year 2010-11.  The situation slightly improved in the year 2010-11 where the Department 
could utilize Rs. 875.00 Cror es as against the approve d Plan outlay of Rs. 1025.00 
Crores.  A comparison of the BE, RE and Ac tuals of the last two years, i.e., 2010-11, 
2011-12 reveals that the Actuals always remain  far less than the RE  suggesting that RE 
allocation does not present a realistic picture of the likely utilisation of allocated 
resources, although, the Department claims that  the approved outlay is likely to be fully 
utilised by the end of the financial year.   
12.5 Based on the above, it can be safely conc luded that the reduced Revised Estimates 
in comparison to the Budget Estimates are na tural outcome of poor trend of utilisation 
during the year.  A cursory glan ce at the BE vis-à-vis, RE ou tlays of the Department for 
the year 2012-13, reveals that while the Plan allocation for Grant No. 4 consisting of both 
Budgetary Support and IEBR was Rs. 5065.73 Crores, it came down to Rs. 2784.55

rd

14.1 The Committee being concerned about some of the projects of the Department of

steps taken and Programme, if any, chalked out to deal effectively with the situation.  The 
Committee was informed that the DAE carri ed out a number of public awareness 
activities at a mission mode in a structured manner and through a multi-pronged 
approach.  A number of public awareness programme pursued by the Department over 
the last 5 years consisted of organizing/ pa rticipating in 100 exhibitions in different 
regions of the country; conducting some 145 seminars/workshops for students/ academy, 
general/ rural public, etc.; making of over 15 video films and multimedia presentations, 
organizing all India essay contest in nucle ar science & technol ogy and several essay 
contests and quiz competitions for high-school and college level students, publishing over 
45 publications and about 12 advertisements/ articles in variou s popular magazines 
mostly in various official la nguages, etc.  The Department also informed that it had 
recently chalked out a public outreach programme that included:- 
• Regular advertisements in TV channels & print media 
• Media workshops 
• Fifteen public outreach centres at DAE facilities ( during the XII plan period ) 
One such centre to be established at the DAE Head Quarters, Mumbai. 
• Displaying positive messages about Atomic Energy on all departmental vehicles 
• Hoardings to be put up in its premises and residential complexes 
• Exploiting the social media (FB, Twitter, mass e-mailing, mass messaging) 
advertisements, etc. 
14.2  While the Committee appreciates the e fforts made and the steps taken by the 
Department to reach the public in the r ecent past to promote awareness among them 
about the peaceful usage and societal benefits  of atomic energy, it feels that the public 
apprehensions on the safety of nuclear installa tions, particularly, nuclear power plants as 
also possible fears of adverse impact on th eir traditional means of livelihood like fishing, 
biodiversity, health of work ers of Uranium mines and t hose living in the vicinity, 
environmental impact, etc. are strong and deep seeded.  These fears and apprehensions in 
the minds of the people arise mostly due to lack of communication, ignorance, gossips 
and rumour mongering.  Some amount of fear also comes out of the reported or perceived 
ill effects of radiation on th e people living nearby the nucl ear installations which are 
often exaggerated and blown out of proporti on by the media.  Fukushima accident, has 
further added to this fear psychosis and heightened the level of apprehensions.   
14.3  In such a complex scenario, what th e DAE needs to focus on, is to allay the 
apprehensions and misconceptions, harbour ed by the people against nuclear power 
projects by suitable, sustained and effective campaign with as wider reach as possible.  
Merely, organizing exhibitions and conducting seminars and producing some video films 
to cater to only a few lakhs of people would not have the desired impact.  The fact that 
during the last 40 years of commercial operati on of nuclear power plants in the country, 

not even a single, small incident has taken place needs to be emphatically and vigorously 
emphasised to the people.  If at all there are some genuine, legitimate concerns of the 
people as projected in the media from time to  time, the Department must instead of 
simply denying or ignoring them, proactivel y allay those fears a nd apprehensions by 
explaining to people in their own language a nd in a manner they would understand as to 
why their apprehensions or f ears are unfounded and that utmo st care has been taken to 
safeguard the interests of the public.

18. Popularization of Spin off Technologies  
18.1 The Committee is aware that Departme nt of Atomic Energy has developed a

technologies but the Department  in its Action Taken Note le t the matter re st by saying

details of the activities undertaken for thei r popularization.  The Committee is happy to

19. Integration of Spin-Off Technologies  with Related Schemes of Ministries
 
19.1 The Committee feels that yet anothe r effective way of successfully and 
adequately utilizing the socially beneficial technologies could be to  integrate them with 
the related programmes/schemes being run by di fferent Ministries/ Departments of Govt. 
of India.  All that the Department needs to do is to reach out to th e concerned Ministries/ 
Departments and to impress upon them to inte grate into their programmes/projects, these 
proven technologies.  For example, providing potable drinking water in rural and urban 
areas has turned out to be a formidable cha llenge for the Ministries of Rural and Urban 
Development/ Local Administra tion, etc.  Department of  Atomic Energy has proven 
technology for water purificati on and water desalination, which costs as less as 6 Paisa 
per liter.  This technology can easily be replicated at all those places facing safe potable 
water problems or where underground water is contaminated with arsenic or fluoride, etc. 
both in urban and rural areas of the country by getting it linked with Rural Water Mission 
and in the urban areas with the Urban Local Bodies.  Similarly, Department of Atomic 
Energy can help Ministry of Water Res ources draw a map of water hydrology by

role in the JNNURM Programme run by the Ministry of Urban Development, which has 
a component of sewage treatment.  The technology on food irradiation could be 
integrated with the programmes of the Minist ry of Food Processing Industries.  By doing 
all this, the Committee feels the Department w ould be able to disseminate its socially 
beneficial technologies in a big way and their benefits wo uld also reach easily to the 
people and the society.  This will also help the Department earn creditability and good 
will in the larger populace and clear the apprehensions/ misconceptions in the minds of 
the people towards atomic energy.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the 
Department must act on the lines being suggested by the Committee and initiate 
interaction with the concerned Ministries  to convince them of the relevance and 
efficacy of its technologies within a given time schedule.  It also recommends the 
Department to take all possible measures to take the technologies to the society as a 
technology lying in the labyrinth of laboratory or an institution is useless and 
meaningless.  Its real success lies in its wider & wider applications and use.

ultimately whatever available within the coun try was available to us  forever and so long 
as we were able to get it, we should do so.   
23.3  While the Committee agrees with the contention of the Department to the extent 
that the import of Uranium is economical it being cheaper, however it feels that domestic 
development of uranium mines as well as processing of Uranium ores by UCIL must also 
go side by side not only to enable faster development of the nucle ar energy sector, but 
also to keep ready to brace any eventualitie s of disruption or denial of Uranium by 
international community at any given stage.  The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that the Department of Atomic Energy should not ignore or go slow on the 
indigenous prospecting exploration mini ng and processing of Uranium simply 
because of the fact that imported Uraniu m is much cheaper or is, presently, being 
made available by the international communit y.  Priority must continue to be given 
to the development of domestic Uranium and devising of means of cheaper and 
more efficient extractions of Uranium from the ores and adequate financial 
resources must be made available to Atom ic Minerals Directorate and Uranium 
Corporation of India Ltd., more so, because the gestation period of mining typically 
requires 10 years and any incidence of su dden disruption or de nial of imported 
Uranium can put us in precarious situation.    
24.  Nuclear Agriculture 
 
24.1 Vagaries of monsoon, poor precipitat ion, scattered rainfall, diminishing 
underground water table, faltering irrigation fac ilities, receding ecological flow of rivers, 
etc. caused due to climate change have put tremendous pressure on our poor and marginal 
farmers particularly of the wheat & paddy gr owing States of the country.  In such a 
situation, it becomes imperati ve to develop such varietie s of wheat & rice which could 
withstand biotic as well abiotic stress.  Although, the Department of Atomic Energy, 
under its Project Nuclear Agriculture is work ing in this area and has tested a rice 
involving mutant which was found to be high yielding and has also tested early mutants 

of Cv. C306 in wheat, the Committee desires that research & development activities in 
this area need to be further intensified so as to come out with viable solutions at the 
earliest.  Yet another area, in which, the Co mmittee would like the Department to focus 
its attention on, is preservation & prolonging the shelf life of onion and potato through its 
irradiation technology to provide relief to fa rmers belonging to different parts of our 
country – Punjab, Maharashtra, West Bengal,  Bihar, etc. who are often heard to be 
throwing their onion and potato products on roads because they do not have access to any 
technology to keep them for longer period nor do they get remunerative price during the 
peak season.  The Committee recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy 
should explore the possibilities to start wo rking in this area, in a mission mode and 
come out with viable solutions in a time bound manner.  
24.2  The Committee hopes that the Departme nt of Atomic Energy would give due 
considerations to the observations and recommendations of the Committee and try 
to do its best to take such actions/ steps required to given them the practical shape.  
The Committee is confident that as outlin ed in its outcome budget the Department 
of Atomic Energy would make XII Five  Year Plan more oriented towards 
programme with objectives rather th an spending efforts in building 
infrastructure/equipment facilities that may exist elsewhere.  The Committee also 
hopes that the delivery of output by the Department during the current Five Year 
Plan would be more than what is commensurate with the resources utilized.
