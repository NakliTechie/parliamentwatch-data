7.         The Committee inquired from the Secretary, Department of Shipping  about the shortfall in the utilisation of the outlay
 in BE/RE 2005-06 and wanted to know the reasons for the mismatch in physical and financial parameters, the Secretary, while
 agreeing to the shortfall deposed before the Committee as under :
"Sir, it is the fact that we have not spent the money which has been allocated to us, year after year...... When a
 plan is projected to the Department, the ports and other organisations become very ambitious. Without doing the
 preliminary groundwork, they say, 'we want to do this kind of a thing, this kind of capital raising, this kind of
 investment', and they make a proposal to the Ministry. Then, money is provided. The question is, why is money
 provided and why does the Department back them? It is done because the general trend is, if the Budget
 Estimates is 'X', the Revised Estimates will be 'X-minus', it will not be 'X-plus'. So, they want to project a
 certain level of allocation which on account of their inadequate preparedness they are not in a position to spend.
 That is the first reason.  Sir, the second reason is, which is partly true and partly also depends on our own
 failure, there are procedural rigidities. Suppose, the Shipping Corporation of India wants to acquire vessels.
 This acquisition of vessels has to be approved at various levels. They will first make a proposal, which will be
 approved by the Board. Then, it will come to the Ministry for getting the PPIB approval. When the PPIB
 approval is communicated to them, then only the order will be placed for acquisition of vessels. In the
 Government system, when our intention is not only to spend money but also to ensure accountability, these
 things, these twin things, and, at times, contradictory objectives, are enshrined in our procedural stipulations.
 That is the reason sometimes they miss the bus on account of procedural rigidities. The third reason, sometimes,
 in implementing the project, which is related to the first point, not only the groundwork is not done but also the
 logistical coordination is not done. Suppose, a project is to come up in the Andaman & Nicobar. For that
 purpose, the contract will be awarded for bringing the material from outside, etc., which takes a lot of time. But,
 all the three together would only amount to zero. There is no explanation or justification to support this kind of
 a thing. We are now trying to deal with the situation in a little better way."
 
8.         The Committee notes that in the annual plan 2005-06, the outlay of Department of Shipping  was reduced from Rs.
 2419.66 crore to Rs. 2333.71 crore at RE stage.  The reduction had been both in the Gross Budgetary Support as well as IEBR
 component.  As far as the reasons for under utilisation of funds is concerned, the Committee finds that inadequate preparedness
 on the part of executing authorities and procedural rigidity for approval of scheme are main culprits for under utilisation of
 funds.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that Department of Shipping should not only have to do away the procedural
 hurdles leading to delay in processing and approving the scheme but also have to adopt ­proactive approach while preparing
 the scheme.  In BE 2006-07, an outlay of Rs. 3263.19 crore has been provided which consists of a Gross Budgetary Support of
 Rs. 740.88 crore and IEBR of Rs. 2522.31 crore, which is almost 40% over and above the amount of Rs. 2419.66 crore
 allocated in BE 2005-06.   The Committee expects that the increase in the plan outlay will have a positive effect on the
 schemes/projects of the Department of Shipping.  The Committee is of the view that complete utilization of the enhanced
 allocation will help effective implementation of the schemes/performances undertaken by the Department of Shipping.  The
 Committee recommends that the Government should increase the outlay of Department of Shipping  and if need be,
 supplement the allocation by increasing the Gross Budgetary Support to the Department of Shipping.

CAPACITY ENHANCEMENT IN MAJOR PORTS
16.       The capacity enhancement in major ports is an on-going process keeping in view the demands of maritime trade.  To
 cope with the surge in cargo traffic resulting from the liberalization of the economy since the early 1990s, the Government has
 focussed on enhancing the capacities in major ports through construction of additional berths, expansion of existing berths,
 modernization of cargo handling equipments and floating craft and other back up facilities.  The Government has formulated a
 scheme for private sector investment including foreign direct investment in major ports, with special emphasis on capacity
 augmentation. Foreign direct investment upto 100% under automatic route has been permitted for construction and
 maintenance of ports and harbours.  As a result of the efforts, the aggregate capacity in the 12 major ports increased by 124.40
 million tonnes per annum (MTPA) during the Ninth Plan period and reached a level of 343.95 MTPA as on the 31
st March,
 2002.  The aggregate capacity has reached a level of 408.60 MTPA as on 31 st March, 2006.   As against the aggregate
 capacity, the major ports handled a total traffic of 383.75 million tonnes during 2004-05 and 310.80 million tonnes in 2005-06
 upto December, 2005.  By the end of 10 th Plan, aggregate capacity in major ports is anticipated to reach 470.60 million tonnes
 per annum .
17.       In the context of India’s growing overseas trade, the Department of Shipping has formulated the National Maritime
 Development Programme which aims mainly at modernizing and upgrading the facilities in the major ports to levels which are
 globally comparable and doubling their aggregate capacity to 800.41 MTPA by the year 2011-12.   A total of 276 projects
 covering the entire gamut of activities in the 12 major ports, namely, deepening of channels, construction/upgradation of
 berths, modernization of equipment, improved rail/road connectivity and other associated activities have been identified under
 the Programme to be taken up for implementation by the year 2011-12.
18.       The Committee wanted to know the steps taken by Department of Shipping to encourage Private Sector participation for
 development of infrastructure services in the Port Sector and the details of policy guidelines, if any, laid down for involvement
 of Private Sector participation in the major ports, the Department of Shipping  replied to the Committee that the Major Port
 Trusts Act, 1963 permits private sector participation in port development. With a view to improving productivity and
 efficiency and promoting competition, the Government has put in place a scheme for private sector participation in major ports
 including container terminals, specialized cargo berths for handling bulk, break bulk, multipurpose and specialized cargo,
 warehousing/storage facilities, dry docking and ship repair facilities. The comprehensive guidelines for private sector
 participation in major ports and formation of joint ventures by major ports, issued by the Government, inter-alia, indicate the
 areas of private sector participation including through joint ventures, modalities and general terms and conditions for such
 participation, bidding procedures and evaluation and selection criteria. The preferred route for selection of the private operator
 is through open competitive bidding and the mode of participation is on BOT basis, with a concession period not exceeding 30
 years. To make the projects bankable, Government has prescribed model license agreement which takes into account, inter
 alia, investor concerns. Bidding, evaluation and selection procedures have also been standardized. To ensure transparency,
 selection of the private operator for BOT projects is based on a single variable, namely, the party offering the highest revenue
 share to the port is declared the successful bidder. Where the BOT operator takes over an existing port facility, the port labour
 rendered surplus as a result is also required to be taken over by the private operator on terms which are not inferior to what was
 available to them in the port.
19.       The Committee notes that the Department of Shipping is implementing the schemes for enhancing the capacity and
 productivity of all major ports and to encourage the private sector participation for the development of ports infrastructure and
 accordingly, comprehensive policy guidelines have been issued.  The Committee also notes that the Government has allowed
 cent percent FDI for construction and maintenance of ports in order to mobilise substantial resources as well as to improve
 efficiency, productivity and quality of services of Ports.  The Committee, however, observed that as against the capacity of
 408.60 million TPA, the major ports handled a total traffic of 310.80 mt. upto December 2005.  This way, the aggregate

PORT LABOUR REFORMS
20.       In view of the changed scenario of liberalization and globalization, the Government has been keeping a close watch and
 taking initiatives in the sphere of labour reforms in Port Sector. One of such initiatives is allowing private sector participation
 in Major Ports. It has been observed that the major expansions required in the port infrastructure sector in the country need
 substantial resources. To mobilize such large scale resources and to deploy the available resources for developmental activities
 in other areas/sectors, it has been decided by the Government. of India to go in for private sector participation for development
 of port infrastructure projects too. This aspect of private sector participation has been the concern for labour federations/unions
 also as they had been fearing loss of employment opportunities after enhancement of private sector participation. Therefore,
 the guidelines for Private Sector participation, inter-alia provide that before tendering out an existing Port facility, the
 concerned Port should identify the labour which has to be necessarily taken over in the facility and clearly bring it out in the
 tender document so that the intending tenderers are aware of the liability. This however, is not justified in the case of BOT
 operators who have to incur huge expenditure in building the facility first and then operate it for a fixed period. Therefore, it
 has also been clarified that in case of new facility built by the private operator, the option of employing the Port labour will rest
 with the BOT operator. However, the private operators have also to abide by the existing labour laws of this country.
21.       The Committee  notes that in the changed fiscal regime the Government is committed for labour reforms  and desires to
 allow private sector investment for creation and augmentation of  capacity building in the port infrastructure. The Committee
 also notes with concern that enhancement of private sector investment for development of port infrastructure may deter the
 interest of employees unions/federations. Therefore, the Committee feels that the port labour reforms are necessary before
 exposing the labour sector to open market mechanism.  In view of this, the Committee recommends the Department of
 Shipping to de-regulate and de-centralize the commercial environment for collective bargaining to improve the productivity
 levels at ports while taking adequate care of labour interests.
PORT CONNECTIVITY
22.       At present, all Major Port Trusts are connected through a network of roads.  Capacity enhancement of the infrastructure
 including roads is an on-going process depending on the requirements and augmentation of road connectivity is undertaken on
 a case to case basis depending on the traffic projection of the Ports.  The time taken by the containers depends on a number of
 factors, including the initial and terminal points of destination and, therefore, it is not possible to compute the average time
 taken for a container to return from/to the Port.  At present, projects involving 111.73 Kilometers for improving road
 accessibility to Ports have been completed and projects involving 313.85 Kilometers of road length for improving road
 accessibility to Ports are at various stages of implementation. 
23.       The Committee observes that the lack of proper port connectivity has hampered the development of ports. The
 Committee also notes that projects involving 117.73 km. for improving road accessibility to Ports have been completed and
 projects involving 313.85 kms. of road length are under implementation. The Committee is of the view that development of
 other modes of transport viz. railways, highways and inland water transport is also essential for improving the accessibility to
 ports and to keep pace with the growing requirements of overseas trade. The Committee feels that the availability of such
 modal mix will result in considerable saving in cost and time for cargo containers assessing these ports. The Committee
 therefore, recommends the Department of Shipping  to take steps for better road and rail connectivity infrastructure in all the
 major ports for the growth of the container cargo. The cost of connectivity could be shared in public sector by the strategic
 partners or alternatively,  the private public partnership (PPP) or joint venture partnership (JVP) could also be explored.
CONTAINERIZATION IN MAJOR PORTS
24.       To cope with the surge in cargo traffic resulting from the liberalization of the economy since the early 1990s, the
 Government has focused in a sustained manner on enhancing the capacities in major ports through construction of additional
 berths, expansion of existing berths, modernization of cargo handling equipments and floating craft and other back up
 facilities. As regards bringing in improvement in cargo handling including container handling facilities in major ports, the
 Department of Shipping informed that it is an on-going process keeping in view the demands of maritime trade and the
 Government has formulated a scheme for private sector investment including foreign direct investment in major ports, with
 special emphasis on capacity augmentation. Foreign direct investment upto 100% under automatic route has been permitted for
 construction and maintenance of ports and harbours.  As a result of the efforts, the aggregate capacity in the 12 major ports
 increased by 124.40 million tonnes per annum (MTPA) during the Ninth Plan period and reached a level of 343.95 MTPA as
 on the 31

SETHUSAMUDRAM SHIP CHANNEL PROJECT
28.       Sethusamudram Ship Channel Project envisages dredging of a ship channel in the shallow portion of sea to connect the
 Gulf of Mannar and Bay of Bengal through Palk Bay so that the ships, depending on draught required, moving between east
 and west coast of India could have a continuous navigable sea route around the peninsula within India's own territorial waters. 
 This will save up to 424 nautical miles (one nautical mile is equal to 1.852 kms.) of distance and up to 30 hours of sailing time
 for ships between east and west coast. The project will also enhance the national security and world lead to overall economic
 development of the region. 
29.       As regards allocation and utilization of outlay, the Department of Shipping  informed that total allocation for
 Sethusamudram Ship Channel Project in BE 2006-07 is Rs. 304 crore comprising Rs. 204 crore budgetary grant and Rs.100
 crore internal and extra-budgetary resources(IEBR). The budgetary grant component is towards the payment of equity share by
 the Government to Sethusamudram Corporation Limited (SCL), the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) for implementation of the
 project. The IEBR component is towards payment of equity share to SCL by the other partners in the SPV, namely Chennai
 Port Trust, Tuticorin Port Trust, Visakhapatnam Port Trust, Paradip Port Trust, Ennore Port Limited, Shipping Corporation of
 India Limited and Dredging Corporation of India Limited. The amount is likely to be utilised for carrying out the dredging and
 other associated works for the project.  
30.       The Committee pointed out about the environmental impact of the project and wanted to know about the details of study
 undertaken for the purpose, the Department of Shipping  submitted that taking into consideration the environmentally sensitive
 nature of the project, in view of its location and also the main component of work, namely, dredging and disposal of dredged
 material, an initial Environmental Examination of the project was made through National Environmental Engineering Research
 Institute (NEERI) Nagpur.  The final report of NEERI was received in 1998 in which they had suggested a new alignment of
 the channel, prima facie, acceptable from environmental considerations. The alignment of the channel now proposed is
 completely in the sea and thus the term 'channel' instead of 'canal' is being used in respect of this project.  A presentation of the
 Initial Environment Examination Report was made to the Ministry of Environment & Forests in April, 1999 for 31 ft. draft.

 benefits have been withdrawn from them in Financial Bill 2004-05 without extending tonnage tax benefit to them.  The
 rationale for extending Tonnage Tax benefit to this sector is to unfold the infrastructural potential of these sectors which will
 ultimately benefit Indian economy much more than the tax which can be realized with the present taxation structure.  The

69.       The Committee pointed out that the entrepreneurs are not flocking in to take advantage of the incentives being provided
 to IWT sector and wanted to know the reasons from the Department of Shipping. The Department of Shipping informed the
 Committee that Inland waterways require regular development and maintenance for becoming/ remaining a competitive mode
 of transport in the dynamic situation of economic development. However, Inland Water Transport sector remained neglected
 for a long time in the country,  while the other modes namely rail and road developed substantially. There also has been
 significant improvement in technology of providing and maintaining infrastructure in rail and road sectors both for creation of
 basic facilities as well as rolling stock.    However, due to the neglect, IWT sector not only remained dormant but actually de-
generated and lost its significance in  many areas. Due to its inherent advantages in terms of fuel efficiency, lower cost of
 transportation and environment friendliness, this sector still remained important in some areas where it continued to remain
 competitive to the other modes.  Such of these areas are Goa, West Bengal, Assam, Kerala, Orissa,  Mumbai etc.  When the
 IWT mode is also developed like the other two modes, this mode also would play its role in the overall transport mix of the
 country as an alternative and supplementary mode of inland transport.

 drove away many operators and shippers who had no alternative but to utilize road and rail modes.  Bringing them back to
 IWT sector requires many actions including up-gradation of basic IWT infrastructure, fiscal incentives, vessel building subsidy
 as well as freight subsidy etc.  While some of these have been put into place, it will take some more time for these activities to
 develop to a level where the private sector feels confident to invest in the sector.  It may be noted that while Government may
 provide the basic infrastructure namely fairway, terminals and navigational aids, the ownership and operation of cargo and
 passenger vessels have to be primarily in the private sector for long term success of this mode.  We are hopeful that by
 providing basic IWT infrastructure on various waterways significant cargo can be shifted to IWT mode.  We have already seen
 substantial increase in transportation of cargo on Indo-Bangladesh routes in 2-3 years where it has gone up from the level of 2-

71.       As regards the proposal to give institutional framework for development of IWT in the country the Department of

 dismayed to note that an outlay of Rs. 150.00 crore was allocated in BE 2005-06 for the IWT Sector  but it was reduced to Rs.
 101.25 crore at RE stage and an expenditure of Rs. 47.87 crore was made only upto February, 2006. The Committee notes that
 Inland Water Transport is one of the oldest, economically, environmentally sustainable mode of transport but the potential of
 Inland Water Transport remains largely unexploited due to the detrimental policies of the Government.    The Committee
 observes that although the Department of Shipping is committed to step up the share of IWT in the country's total trade still
 this sector remains under developed at present for want of requisite infrastructure and therefore, is not viable for commercial
 operations.  The Committee feels that inadequate policy support, interest and investments are some of the factors which are
 aggravating the problems of the Inland Water Transport and this sector is loosing a competitive edge with other modal mix in
 the country.  The Committee is of the view that growth of the Inland Water Transport sector will entirely depend upon the
 quantum of investments, made through public-private partnership and a multi-dimensional approach for creating the required
 infrastructure primarily through Government Support.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department of Shipping to
 provide some institutional framework for creating a vibrant Inland Water Transport sector in the country and  IWT should be
 included under the National Maritime Development Programme for augmentation of IWT fleet, creation of inter-modal
 linkages and upgradation of cargo handling technologies etc. (Para 73)
