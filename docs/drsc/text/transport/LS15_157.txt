REPORT  
  India, being one of the oldest civilizations occu pies an important place on the cultural map of 
the world. Culture plays an important role in the d evelopment agenda of any nation. Firstly, it 
contributes immensely to economic growth by creatin g vast employment opportunities. Secondly, it 
provides goals in terms of quality of life and mean ingful existence as an end to economic growth 
itself. Culture and creativity manifest themselves in almost all economic, social and cultural activities. 
The role of culture as a vehicle for national integ ration cannot be overemphasized in the context of 
India’s multi ethno-cultural society. Development o f culture in fact, works as a catalyst to the 
economic development. As such, culture cannot be confined to the boundaries of any particular sector. 
However, it could be recognized that there are cert ain sectors wherein presence and contribution of 
culture and creativity are much more dominant and v isible. Elements of culture encompass media, 
,films, music, handicrafts, visual arts, Performing  Arts, literature, heritage management, cultural an d 
creative industries etc. The cultural development inter-alia  includes areas of culture both in tangible 
and intangible fields. The functional spectrum of t he Ministry of Culture is rather wide, ranging from  
generating cultural awareness at the grassroots lev el to promoting cultural exchanges at an 
international level.

ELEVENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN  
3.  Ministry of Culture informed that against the p rojected demand of Rs.4965.99 crore for the XI 
Five Year Plan, Planning Commission allocated an am ount of Rs.3524.11 crore. The Ministry 
informed that they would require additional Plan allocation for the new schemes/programmes initiated 
in the last 2-3 years such as celebration of 150th Birth Anniversary of Guru Rabindranath Tagore, 
setting up of a National Mission on Libraries, sett ing up of National Centre for Performing Art, 
National Archives for Audio Visual Materials, restr ucturing and strengthening of ASI including the 
activities/programmes proposed and deferred by vari ous cultural organizations on account of a 
substantial cut in Plan allocation under XI Plan. 
4.  The Secretary, while appearing before the Commi ttee stated that: 
“This is a small-budget Ministry of Rs.700 crores o r thereabouts. Sir, the entire Budget of the 
Ministry comes to 12 paise per hundred rupees that Bharat Sarkar  spends; not even one rupee. 
Twelve paise per hundred rupees is the figure that we have placed before Parliament — and we 
are representing again and again — out of which the  core concern of India’s cultural heritage 
monuments get four paisa, that is, 0.04 per cent”.  
5.  The Committee was further informed that Archaeo logical Survey of India, which is a flagship 
organization of the Ministry of Culture was engaged in conservation, preservation and maintenance of 

3675 Centrally Protected Monuments. Unless a substa ntial allocation was made to Archaeological 
Survey of India towards undertaking the massive tas k of maintenance and conservation of our 
heritage, ancient monuments and historical sites, t he heritage preservation and promotion of ancient 
cultural heritage could not be achieved in a sustai ned manner. Unfortunately, this aspect of heritage 
preservation/promotion in the country has received very little attention it deserves. This is due to t he 
meager Plan budget of the Ministry of Culture, whic h acts as a constraint to step up its activities 
including that of Archaeological Survey of India. F urther, to aggravate this situation of inadequacy o f 
funds, every year Ministry of Finance apply a cut i n the total budgetary allocation, especially under 
Plan, at the Revised Estimates stage, the effect of which invariably percolates down to Archaeological 
Survey of India also.

ANNUAL PLAN 2010-11  
7.   Demand No. 19 containing the details of the bu dget allocation of the Ministry of Culture for the 
financial year 2010-2011 was considered by the Depa rtment-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture at its meeting held on the 7th April, 2010. The 
Committee heard the Secretary, Ministry of Culture and other officials of the Ministry and 
organizations during the meeting. 
8.   Ministry of Culture projected a demand of Rs.1 186.62 crore for the Annual Plan 2010-11 on the 
basis of the proposals received from various cultur al Organizations and Divisions under the Ministry. 
The Planning Commission has allocated a sum of Rs.7 35.00 crore for developmental activities of the 
Ministry of Culture for the Annual Plan 2010-11, thereby reducing the proposal by Rs.451.62 crore on 
account of resource constraints. On being asked, th e Ministry informed that this cut would affect 
adversely almost all activities of the Ministry inc luding organizations such as Archaeological Survey 
of India; Zonal Culture Centres; Akademis; Asiatic Society, Kolkata; Indian Museum; Salarjung 
Museum; Victoria Memorial Hall; National Library; R aja Rammohun Roy Library Foundation; and 
Plan Schemes (including Celebration of 150th Birth Anniversary of Guru Rabindranath Tagore and 
Setting up of a National Mission on Libraries) of the Ministry. 
9.   As against Revised Estimates of Rs.630.00 cror e in 2009-10, Plan allocation of Rs.735.00 crore 
have been made in 2010-11, thereby resulting in an increase of Rs.105.00 crore. Ministry of Culture 
informed that this increased allocation would be ut ilized for developmental activities of National 
Museum, Celebration of 150th Birth Anniversary of G uru Rabindranath Tagore, Celebration of 150th 
Birth Anniversary of Swami Vivekananda, Development  of Sardar Vallabbhai Patel Memorials, 
National Gandhi Heritage Site Mission, Setting up o f a National Mission on Libraries, Sahitya 

Akademi, National Council of Science Museums, Indian Museum etc.. The Secretary also emphasized 
on the changes in the budgetary aspect this year:- 
“ .......centenaries and memorials are essential pa rt of the nation’s celebrations. These have 
been dealt with in an ad hoc manner and from the no n-Plan budget. The logic was that 
sometimes these decisions are taken in the beginnin g of the year or in the middle of the year. 
Since these are not predicted in the Plan period, w e spend out of the non-Plan. This as a policy 
decision has been shifted from non-Plan to Plan. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Memorial has a long-
standing demand. They have been demanding some amou nt of corpus or some amount of 
support. This will be under Plan. Because of the un certainty and because of the time-lag that is 
involved and because of the fact that some of these will be starting in the middle of the year, we 
have kept it at Rs.735 crore. The Planning Commission has also recorded it. An assurance given 
to us is that if we manage to spend or if we requir e to spend this amount and proceed at the end 
of the first half, they will take a view of it. 
10.   The Allocation in 2009 was Rs.700.00 crore, b ut the same was reduced at RE to Rs.63000 crore. 
The Committee was informed that reduction was made due to slow pace of expenditure during first six 
months of the financial year. The expenditure upto 31st March, 2010, is Rs.533.40 crore out of the 
allocation of Rs.630.00 crore. 
11.   The Ministry assured the Committee that all C ultural and other Organizations have taken timely 
initiatives and action to achieve at least 60% Plan  expenditure of by the end of September, so that th e 
precious fund allocated to the Ministry for implementation of various programmes and schemes under 
Art and Culture sector were not surrendered at RE s tage. In order to ensure that these targets were 
achieved review meetings are held at regular fortnightly/monthly/quarterly intervals. 
12.   The Committee underlines the need for more allocati on to the Ministry of Culture lest 
many of its important activities should suffer. For the year 2010-11, the Ministry of Culture has 
been allocated an amount of Rs.735 crore, which comes to less than 12 paise per hundred rupees 
being spent under the General Budget. However, the Committee is surprised to find that during 
the first three years of the XI Five Year Plan, the  Ministry as in the past, has not been able to 
utilize even the reduced allocation. Underutilizati on of funds by the Ministry of Culture is a 
recurring phenomena year after year. During the yea r 2009-10, the allocation of Rs.700 crore 
was reduced to Rs.630.00 crore at RE stage, however , the Ministry could utilize only an amount 
of Rs.533.40 crore. The Committee recommends that t he Ministry should identify the 
bottlenecks and make corrective measures to utilize  the allocations as per the norms laid down 
for the purpose.  
13.   Upon recognizing the fact that the Plan expen diture of the Ministry of Culture and its various 
organizations, particularly of Archaeological Surve y of India, picks up only after September of each 
year, the Department-related Parliamentary Standing committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture in 
its 137th report on Demands for Grants, 2008-09 of the Ministry of Culture, recommended that 
Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance should reschedule the present budgetary procedure and 
practices as far as Ministry of Culture is concerne d, so that the final Plan allocation were conveyed to 
the Ministry by December every year, as ASI activities were seasonal in nature and started picking up 
only after the end of monsoon. 
14.   On the basis of the above, Culture Ministry h ad taken up the issue of deferment of exercise of 
finalization of RE for the Ministry of Culture to D ecember from the month of September, with the 

Planning Commission and Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance. Further it also requested 
to consider the matter and issue necessary instruct ions, so that the Revised Estimates for the Ministr y 
of Culture was decided, based on expenditure at the end of December, instead of September. 
15.   In response to the recommendation of the Parl iamentary Standing Committee and the 
suggestions given by the Ministry of Culture, the Ministry of Finance stated that the completion of RE 
exercise by November is necessary, as the preparati on and compilation of Budget document starts 
from December. The review of Budget allocation at R E stage is done as thoroughly as possible after 
considering all aspects, to ensure that provisions were made to meet all the anticipated expenditure for 
the remaining part of the year. While doing so, car e is taken to avoid any situation leading to non-
utilization of funds. Due to these reasons, Ministr y of Finance has declined to accept the suggestions  
made by Ministry of Culture. 
16.   The Committee notes the comments of the Ministry of  Finance. However, the Ministry of 
Finance/Planning Commission has not offered any sol ution to the problem of the Ministry of 
Culture which the Committee still appreciates. The Committee deplores the “one size fits all” 
attitude of the Ministry of Finance and recommends that the non-utilization of funds due to the 
hostile weather and season may be given a special s tatus to ensure that allocation is not reduced 
at RE stage. Till there is no respite on this accou nt, the Ministry of Culture will have to make 
efforts to maximize its expenditure, before the review of the RE allocation begins.  
17.   The Ministry should also explore new methodology for conservation and preservation with 
the help of Scientists/Universities which can be do ne in all weather conditions. The Committee 
observes that the fund allocated to ASI need to be given a special status to make it non-lapsable.  
18.   The Committee once again recommends to the Ministry  of Finance and the Planning 
Commission to understand the needs of the instituti ons working under the Ministry of Culture 
and the limitations of those institutions to spend the money during the first six months of the 
financial year. 
ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA  
19.   The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), an attached office of the Ministry of Culture has a 
network of 24 circles throughout the country. ASI p rotects 3675 monuments, which include 21 
properties included in the World Heritage List by U NESCO. The major activities of ASI are: 
maintenance, conservation and preservation of centr ally protected monuments/sites and remains. In 
addition to the above activities, it conducts archa eological exploration and excavation and underwater 
archaeology regularly. ASI is also engaged in developing epigraphical research; setting up of new site 
museums and reorganization of existing site museums , imparting training programmes in 
Archaeology; strengthening of the Chemistry Branch for development of chemicals for preservation. 
20.   Archaeological Survey of India has undertaken  more than 1500 schemes (works) for structural 
conservation, chemical preservation and horticultural operations based on the priorities, commitments, 
available manpower and financial resources. 
21.   Archaeological excavations and explorations i s one of the oldest and most important activities 
of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI). The AS I conducts various operations and programmes 
like Village-to-Village Surveys, Problem-Oriented S urveys, Excavations of archaeological sites and 
mounds, documentation of antiquarian remains and sc ulptures, architectural survey of temples and 

colonial buildings throughout the country, through its various field offices, Excavation and Pre-history 
Branches, Temple and Building Survey Projects as well as Circles. 
22.   Ministry of Culture informed that in the pres ervation and conservation of monuments 
participation of universities, other academic insti tutions and research bodies in academic work is 
ensured by extending matching Grants-in-aid and Fin ancial Assistance to eligible institutions. 
Documentation of antiquities and sculptures stored in various field offices and sculpture sheds is als o 
one of the activities identified and the various field offices have been directed to undertake this work. 
23.   Further, it as informed that greater thrust i s being given by ASI to collaborations with the 
corporate sector such as the Taj Group of Hotels, W orld Monuments Fund etc. for formulation of 
Master Plans and for implementation of integrated d evelopment of some important monuments. In 
order to encourage private-public partnerships in t he area of conservation and preservation of 
monuments, ASI functions in association with Nation al Culture Fund (NCF) with regard to projects 
sponsored by private sector organizations. 
24.   The Committee notes various activities undertaken b y ASI in the field of culture and 
appreciates the efforts of ASI.  
25.   Ministry of Culture informed that the six Exc avation Branches undertook large-scale excavation 
and exploration of sites/regions with simultaneous conservation, while some of the circles had 
undertaken the small scale excavation and explorati on for the years 2007-08, 2008-09 and 2009-10. 
During 2010-11 also ASI proposes to undertake these excavation and exploration activities. 
26.   In the area of Underwater Archaeology, ASI un dertook works pertaining to excavation in 
Dwarka (Gujarat), which will be continued during 2010-11 also. Further, during 2008-09 and 2009-10 
ASI had to undertake excavation and exploration wor ks in Lakshadweep Island, which was started in 
2007-08 and will be continued during 2010-11.

CULTURAL  AWARENESS  PROGRAMME  
31.   Ministry of Culture informed that publication  programme of ASI encompassing the fields such 
as academic, informative and awareness generation i s one of the important activities of ASI. This 
programme will be strengthened further during the y ear 2010-11 so that more publications relating to 
archaeology could be brought out for the general public, particularly for students. 
32.   The Committee is of the view that there is a need f or publications in archaeology for 
general awareness in all the major Indian languages , besides some foreign languages. These 
publications should aim at creating interest in the  public about our ancient monuments and 
archaeological sites as well the importance of pres erving these as our cultural heritage. Such 
awareness in the general public would facilitate better protection/maintenance of these sites.  
33.   Further, there is need for generating interest amo ngst general public, specially children, 
by putting some special programme such as puppet sh ow, documentary etc. Moreover, the 
display boards should be made bilingual and it shou ld have all the relevant information about a 
particular site. Brochures should be made available  in local language also. NGOs/college 
students may also be roped in to act as volunteers/guides to ensure the local participation.  
34.   Archaeological Survey of India has 47 site mu seums. During 2007-08, 2008-09 and 2009-10, 
ASI commissioned site museums at various places including Fatehpur Sikri and Shivpuri. Further, the 
shifting and reorganization of museums at Chanderi and Khajuraho were also done. ASI also proposes 
overall upgradation/modernization of all site museums during 2010-11 in a phased manner. 

35.  The Committee hopes that upgradation/modernization of all site museums will be 
completed in time.  
36.   Under the scheme of Cultural Awareness Progra mme, the funds are being provided to the 
various circles/branch offices for holding Heritage Days during the years 2007-08, 2008-09 and 2009-
10. During 2010-11 also ASI proposes to undertake s imilar programmes. On Heritage days various 
programmes like, exhibitions, workshops, seminars, essay/drawing/quiz competitions are organized 
for school/college/university students in addition to arranging visits to monuments, conservation sites, 
etc. for the targeted groups. In order to spread th e cultural awareness among the public, publication of 
archaeological literature, production of short docu mentary films on monuments giving focus to the 
thematic subjects, region-wise would be given due emphasis during 2010-11. 
37.   The Committee notes the steps taken by the ASI in involving school children in their effort 
to popularize and preserve our monuments. The Commi ttee recommends that the local 
community and the students of local colleges and ed ucational institutions may also be involved 
in the various activities/efforts of ASI.

PROTECTION OF MONUMENTS  
42.   The Committee was informed that there are 367 5 monuments/sites declared as of national 
importance across the country by Government of Indi a. The conservation, preservation, maintenance, 
development of environs and creating tourist relate d amenities ( e.g.  drinking water, toilet blocks, 
facilities for physically challenged, pathways, cul tural notice boards/signage, vehicle parking, cloak  
rooms, etc.) at the centrally protected monuments/s ites are the regular activities which the ASI 
undertakes as per needs and availability of resourc es. Besides providing security with limited 
manpower resources, the ASI protects and preserves the monuments, declared as of national 
importance, on the basis of the requirements assess ed by the concerned officers annually. The annual 
plan is drawn for undertaking the required works in  accordance with the feed back received from the 
field level and the recommendation of supervisory a nd executive level officers. Likewise, State 
Governments are responsible for protection and mana gement of the State protected monuments. 

Ministry of Culture informed that the details in re gard to steps taken by the respective State 
Governments for protection of monuments under their control are not known to the ASI. 
43.   The Committee was informed that the Ministry of Culture did not share resources with the 
States. It has no Centrally-sponsored schemes. It h as only central sector schemes. Until the 13th 
Finance Commission, the States were given massive a ssistance by the Finance Commission for 
archaeological preservation. The Secretary, Ministry of Culture said: 
“... the Planning Commission has told us that please go in for Centrally-sponsored schemes. We 
may have to go completely for a different regime of  going in for partnership with the State 
Archaeology Departments, State Archaeology Director ates so that their monuments, which are 
also a part of our national heritage, not because they are listed in the Central List, could be given 
assistance and working models for their preservatio n, both technical and financial. So, this is 
another area. If this comes up this year, there wil l be an expansion of the Budget that would 
have to come in along with it.” 
44.   The Committee also agrees in-principle to such a co nvergence between the Centre and 
States as far as development and protection of our ancient heritage is concerned. Under the 
existing system, due to lack of sharing and close c oordination, there is complete confusion 
between ASI and the States Archaeology Departments.  As a result, hundreds of our heritage 
sites are either not identified and notified and have been demolished or encroached upon. Such a 
sharing would, the Committee feels, go a long way i n protecting, pressing and developing our 
ancient sites.  
45.   The Committee is concerned at the lack of coordinat ion between the Central and State 
Governments. So much so, the ASI was not aware of t he steps taken by the State Governments 
to protect their monuments. The monuments under the  control of State Governments also need 
to be preserved and protected. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Culture should 
ensure that the monuments under the control of Stat e Governments are also protected and 
maintained. If necessary, the Ministry should also explore the possibility of giving adequate 
finance to the State authorities in this regard. Th e Committee is of the view that Government 
needs to take effective steps to coordinate and fac ilitate the monitoring of protection of 
monuments by State Governments.  
STAFF STRENGTH OF ASI  
46.   The Archaeological Survey of India has deploy ed regular/temporary staff or persons on daily-
wages for attending to these works on regular basis  at the protected monuments and ancient sites 
depending upon the requirement. 
47.   The Committee was informed that the existing staff strength of the ASI is inadequate in view of 
the number of protected monuments, archaeological s ites and remains under its control and other 
functions. Further, the inadequacies of the manpowe r strength vis-a-vis  the duties and responsibilities 
have been highlighted at various forums including t he Parliament. Various Review Committees 
constituted in the past to look into this aspect ha ve also pointed out the constraints of the manpower  
structure of the ASI and had recommended the streng thening at various levels. However, so far, no 
substantial result has come out. As late as March, 2010, Moily Committee, constituted by the 
Government, has strongly recommended for the streng thening of the ASI. The proposal for 
augmenting the manpower structure of the ASI is in formulation stage. 

48.   The Secretary in his presentation deliberated  on the issue of shortage of manpower in ASI and 
stated inter-alia : 
“The ASI’s Bill, the AMASRA Amendment Act, has been  a historic amendment to which all 
political parties across the board agreed. There wi ll be certain sacrifices that we would have to 
make as we move along but it has been the first momentous change 106 years after Lord Curzon 
made his first Act for the preservation of India’s’  monuments. Hundred and six years later, the 
nation makes a choice and what started as a Commonw ealth Games face-saving project became 
a major exercise. For the next hundred meters of an y protected monument, there can be no 
construction, no construction, no construction! We will have to go through it. Beyond 100 
meters, for the next two hundred meters, you cannot  have high rises blocking the view of a 
monument. So, there will be building restrictions; building heritage bye laws will be made. 
These bodies have to be set up. The entire process of administration, of putting up competent 
authorities, legislative groups, the National Monum ents Authority, would be coming but, more 
so, at the ground level, it is the ASI which has pu t it on record, in writing, that it is unable to 
man 2500 of India’s 3675 monuments. We don’t have a ny manpower. Now, 2500, an 
approximate figure, out of the 3675 monuments that we have, is what you have pointed out now. 
Why is the ASI’s capacity being kept at dwarfish le vel? Why is the ASI not being ramped up? 
Now, that is a question I would also like to ask. B ut now we get on to what is practical. Since 
the Parliament has passed this amendment and since the President has assented, it is now 
incumbent on the nation to keep a watch and ward at  every monument. Ten thousand is the 
number of people that we have projected. Some of them could be Ex-Servicemen, some of them 
could be local volunteers; we are yet to find out. India is too complex to be determined across 
the table. This additional expenditure on ASI has been projected at about Rs.300 crores per year. 
ASI’s establishment doubles, thanks to the amendment of Parliament!” 
“It may not double this year; it will ramp up in sl ow degree. Everybody has his problem. They 
have the procedure of selecting, tendering, etc. Let us assume that out of Rs.300 crore projected 
for ASI, Rs.100 crore is spent this year.” 
49.   The Committee is of the view that ASI has spread it self thin and a severe shortage of 
technical personnel has hamstrung the conservation efforts. The Committee will like to invite 
the attention to the recommendation of the R.N. Mir dha Committee that a minimum of 9,000 
monument attendants would be needed to provide secu rity to the existing structures. The 
Government of India needs to rethink its ‘fence and forget’ approach towards the monuments.  
50.   The shortage of staff and monument attendants is a serious problem affecting the safety, 
security and upkeep of various monuments adversely. The monuments need to be protected just 
like any other assets of nation. The Committee, the refore, recommends that the Government 
should make efforts on a war footing to ensure that  staff strength as recommended by various 
Committees may be increased and personnel are posted at the earliest.  
51.  The Committee has come to know that there are hundr eds of attendants who are 
protecting the monuments for 20 years or more, have  not been regularized till date. As a result, 
their basic pay is static and no other allowances a re being paid to them. It is also learnt that 
some of them had gone to Court, but could not get any relief. It is a strange situation when there 
is a huge backlog at different levels including that of monument attendants, yet the existing staff 
are not being adjusted. The Committee, therefore, s trongly recommends that Ministry of 

Culture should adopt humanitarian approach and take steps to increase their wages taking into 
account their length of service and rate of inflation.  
52.   The current framework operates at two levels - Cent ral, that looks after nationally 
important monuments, and the State-level that is re sponsible for State protected monuments. 
There is an urgent need of activating a third level  of institutional and legal arrangement 
involving local bodies and all these levels need to  work with a very close coordination and well 
defined partnership, legal, financial, etc..  
53.   The Committee recommends that for preservation of o ur monuments activities should be 
decentralized. The historical monuments can be bett er protected by the involvement of local 
people. The Ministry should come out with a decentr alized and integrated mechanism that 
would fill the gaps existing today in this regard. The Committee has already expressed its 
serious reservation about the proposed National Heritage Commission that it will not be able to 
address the major issues currently faced in identif ying, developing, protecting, etc. of our rich 
heritage scattered all over the country from Delhi to Talukas and remote villages.

ENCROACHMENTS AND REHABILITATION  
59.   The Committee was informed that in order to c ontain encroachments of the centrally protected 
monuments/sites, the Superintending Archaeologists of the Circles have been vested with the powers 
of Estate Officer to issue eviction notices/orders to the encroachers under Public Premises (Eviction of 
Unauthorised Occupants) Act, 1971. They are also de legated with the powers to issue notices under 
the provisions of Ancient Monuments and Archaeologi cal Sites and Remains Act, 1958 and Rules 
1959 followed by an order to the District Collector/Magistrate by Central Government to remove such 
encroachment under section 19 (2) of the AMASR Act and AMASR Rules 38 (2). Assistance in 
containing and removing encroachments is also sough t from the respective State Government/police. 
In case, such measures do not bring fruitful result s, action is also initiated against the encroachers  by 
even filing cases in the court of law. 
60.   The Committee was informed that various kinds  of formulae were being worked out to take care 
of the encroachment including the resettlement and rehabilitation aspect of the encroachers. In the 
State of Karnataka, the State Government had worked  out a rehabilitation and resettlement plan by 
taking into consideration the concerned local peopl e who have encroached on the monuments etc. 
They agreed to relocate it, the State Government ha d now approached for sharing the expenses by the 
Government of India and the Government of Karnataka on a 50:50 basis. 
61.  The Committee notes that despite there being strict  rules under the Ancient Monuments 
and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958, the problem of encroachment of monuments 
could not be checked. The Committee was informed that the 35 monuments, all managed by the 
Central Government, could not be traced. Delhi, the  headquarters of Archeological Survey of 
India, tops the list with 12 untraceable monuments.  They now exist only in memory and in 
history books. The Committee would like to invite t he attention of the Government to the plight 
of the older Forts in the country. Many of those Fo rts are made up of dry sandstone on 
foundations of clay, sand and rock. These forts are  facing the problem of encroachments also. 
These forts are ill-maintained and in a dilapidated  condition. The growth of tourism and the 
large number of visitors are putting unbearable pre ssure on the structures. One example is the 

Jaisalmer Fort also known as ‘ Sonar Qila’ or ‘Jewel of Desert ’ which is reported to have been 
listed among the most endangered sites of the World  Monuments Fund. It is reported that the 
waste water has seeped through decayed drains and penetrated the walls of the Fort resulting in 
cracks in various structures of the Fort. The Fort is also facing the problem of encroachment.  
62.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Minis try of Culture should take up the 
matter with the concerned State Governments and oth er authorities to ensure that the Forts 
which are part of our cultural heritage are not fur ther damaged due to human action. The 
Committee also recommends that the Ministry should take effective steps to protect the forts 
from neglect and destruction.  
63.   The Committee feels that since in the removal of en croachment, the major role has to be 
played by the State Government, their stake has to be properly recognized. The Ministry should 
initiate consultation with State Government and wor k out a plan for rehabilitation. Financial 
assistance for heritage protection needs to be increased. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
for a separate allocation for dealing with illegal encroachments in ASI monuments and 
rehabilitation to solve this perennial problem. 
NATIONAL MISSION ON MONUMENTS AND ANTIQUITIES  
64.   The National Mission on Monuments and Antiqui ties, which is functioning under the umbrella 
of Archaeological Survey of India, in a full-fledged manner proposes to undertake major activities like 
documentation of antiquities spread over site museu ms of ASI, museums under the control of State 
Governments, Union Territories and Universities; compilation of data on unprotected monuments and 
sites; digitization of archival drawings of various  architectural institutions and organizations; 
arranging training programmes to the State Departments, local bodies, NGOs, Universities, etc. as part 
of the capacity building programmes; and organizing  various outreach programmes in order to 
sensitize the common people about the historical an d cultural aspects of various heritage sites and 
antiquities. During 2010-11, the programmes under the Mission will be strengthened. 
65.   As per the decisions of the III Mission Monit oring Committee, the templates on built heritage, 
sites and antiquities have been prepared. About 50, 000 listing containing information on unprotected 
built heritage, sites and about 1,20,000 antiquities from different sources have been documented as per 
the format of NMMA. 
66.   The Committee was also informed that many Sta tes have already set up Implementations 
Committees and documentation centers and work has b een initiated on a large scale. Training 
programmes for officials working on the project are  being organized and a large work force is being 
engaged for timely completion of the project. 
67.   The final product of the documentation will b e an interactive website which will give a 
comprehensive picture of all protected and unprotec ted heritage structures in the country. A software 
is being developed to enable the public to provide inputs, if there are heritage structures or sites t hat 
have gone unnoticed by the Mission, the general public can point it out through this platform. 
68.   Currently there are 3,675 protected monuments  under the ASI and 3,500 under the state depart-
ments of archaeology, and the NMMA estimates that there are around seven lakh unprotected heritage 
structures. 

69.  Along with the heritage structures, a national  register of antiquities is also being formulated. The 
sources for this will be the ASI, national and stat e museums and the general public will also be 
encouraged to inform if they posses or discover antiquities. 
70.   All heritage structures-ancient, medieval, mo dern and even residential which match the 
definition of an ‘ancient monument’ under the Ancie nt Monuments and Archaeological Sites and 
Remains Act, 1958, will be enlisted. For antiquitie s, the definition under the Antiquities and Art 
Treasures Act, 1972 will be followed. 
71.   The Committee was also informed that National  Mission on Monuments and Antiquities 
(NMMA) has started an initiative to document and li st all heritage structures, protected as well as 
unprotected, across the country. The project has be en sanctioned a budgetary allocation of Rs. 90 
crore and a stipulated time period of five years fr om 2007-12 for completion. Along with the heritage 
structures, a national register of antiquities is also being formulated. 
72.   The Committee appreciates the setting up of Nationa l Mission on Monuments and 
Antiquities which will bring in record all the cult ural and heritage properties and antiquities 
available in the country. The documentation of the heritage sites and antiquities is a gigantic 
task for which the Government of India have sanctio ned a budgetary allocation of Rs.90 crore 
and the project has to be completed within a period  of five years from 2007-12. The Committee, 
in view of urgency of the work, calls up the Minist ry to complete the work in a time bound way 
and make available more funds if required for the p urpose. The Committee is also of the view 
that Mission has to do a huge task which cannot be completed without the involvement of 
general public. The Committee recommends that Gover nment should take steps to sensitize the 
masses and take assistance of the NGO already working in this area.

73.   The seven Zonal Cultural Centres, encompassin g all the States and Union Territories of the 
Country, were set up to extend local creative suppo rt to folk and traditional artists and artisans of 
India. Each Centre functions as an autonomous body,  with the Governor or the State where it is 
located serving as the Ex-Officio Chairman. The mai n objective of these Centres, inter alia , include 
preservation, innovation, promotion and disseminati on of the various art forms covering the broad 
disciplines of music, dance, theatre, visual arts, literary activities and craft traditions with speci al 
focus on folk and dying art forms within the consti tuent states with a view to reinforce the spirit of  
“Unity in Diversity” as a hall mark characteristic of Indian Culture. Plan allocations is also made to  
Zonal Cultural Centres for their ongoing activity a s well as for taking up new activities. “Octave” - a 
North East Festival involving artists from the Nort h East States as well as from other States, are 
organized by ZCCs every year in major cities of the  country. The variation in allocation and 
expenditure during 2008-09 and 2009-10 under the scheme are due to increased expenditure for North 
East activities by ZCCs.

75.   The Committee notes the efforts of Zonal Cultural C entres in preserving the Indian 
culture. However, the Committee is not happy that p lan allocation of Rs.14 crore for the year 

2009-10 was reduced to Rs.12.75 crore at RE stage. Such reduction are not unusual but in cash 
starved Department, efforts should be to utilize th e plan outlay for some other similar purpose, 
if organizing folk dance festival in Delhi was not possible. The Committee feels that there need 
to be more publicity of the events being organized by Zonal Cultural Centres. Without reaching 
the masses, the Zonal Cultural Centres cannot achie ve their objectives. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that concerted efforts should  be made for publicity of the events at 
school and college levels also.

ANTIQUITIES AND ART TREASURES ACT, 1972  
135.   The Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 enacted by Parliament, came into force from 
5.4.1976. The primary purpose of the Act is to regulate the export trade in antiquities and art treasures, 
to provide for the prevention of smuggling of and f raudulent dealings in antiquities, to provide for the 
compulsory acquisition of antiquities and art treasures for preservation in public places. 

136.   Amendment of the Act has been proposed, to u pdate some provisions and make it more 
stringent and in pursuance thereto, a review of various provisions of the Antiquities and Art Treasures 
Act, 1972 is under consideration. The Secretary informed that: 
“Sir, the Antiquities Act was promulgated in 1972 t o stop certain priceless jewels or other 
jewellery from leaving India. The prologue of the A ct mentions that it was meant for stopping 
Indian treasures from going abroad. In reality, the system that was set up is said to be a little bit 
cumbersome. Every antiquity would mean to be regist ered and all that. We are told that the 
provisions of internal movement within India could be revisited and eased up in terms of 21st 
century like where instead of filing up a form phys ically, you do it by e-mail, or, you buy an e-
ticket. Right now, . there is a strong bi-polarity in our approach. On the one hand, the outgoing 
DG has given a strong suggestion for strengthening the Act in the sense of putting up physical 
inspectorate. He lacks manpower, and, so, he will ask for it. On the other hand, there is a feeling 
that while absolutely no compromise can be made abo ut antiquities leaving India, if there is a 
movement within the republic, there would be econom ic stimulus of art and antiquities, and, 
then, the republic and the people would stand to ga in. We are caught between the two view 
points, and, we may be heading for a committee disc ussion. We cannot take a unilateral view. 
DG’s part is over. He said, give me so many people, I will do the job. The Antiquities Act needs 
to be looked into. Maybe this Committee could find time and give us the benefit of its views at a 
separate session.” 
137.  The Committee is of the view that amendments in the proposed Act are the need of ho ur and it should be done in a time bound manner. The apprehension of the public who are scared to show antiquities should be removed. There should be some mechanism of one time voluntary disclosure and registration of articles as there is a boom in art 
market and with the amendment in the Act, the trade can be done within the country, lawfully 
benefitting the country.

care be taken to ensure that the time schedule pres cribed for each of the excavation work is 
adhered.     (Para 29)  
  The Committee recommends that ASI should explore new scientific methods to preserve 
and conserve the heritage sites. The major research and scientific institutions may be roped in to 
explore newer technology and scientific knowledge for preservation and conservation of heritage 
sites.      (Para 30)  
CULTURAL AWARENESS PROGRAMME 
  The Committee is of the view that there is a need  for publications in archaeology for 
general awareness in all the major Indian languages , besides some foreign languages. These 
publications should aim at creating interest in the  public about our ancient monuments and 

archaeological sites as well the importance of pres erving these as our cultural heritage. Such 
awareness in the general public would facilitate better protection/maintenance of these sites. (Para 32)  
  Further, there is need for generating interest am ongst general public, specially children, 
by putting some special programme such as puppet sh ow, documentary etc. Moreover, the 
display boards should be made bilingual and it shou ld have all the relevant information about a 
particular site. Brochures should be made available  in local language also. NGOs/College 
students may also be roped in to act as volunteers/guides to ensure the local participation. (Para 33)  
  The Committee hopes that upgradation/ modernizati on of all site museums will be 
completed in time.   (Para 35)  
  The Committee notes the steps taken by the ASI in  involving school children in their effort 
to popularize and preserve our monuments. The Commi ttee recommends that the local 
community and the students of local colleges and ed ucational institutions may also be involved 
in the various activities/efforts of ASI. (Para 37)  
FUND ALLOCATION FOR ASI 
  The Committee notes that inspite of cuts/reductio ns in all the heads, ASI was provided 
with increased allocation from the scarce resources  to meet their expenditure. However, the 
Committee is concerned to note that ASI was able to  incur expenditure only to the tune of Rs. 
110.07 crore. ASI should ensure their spending of f unds in an effective way so that the entire 
allocated fund is utilized within the stipulated ti me frame specified. The Committee may be 
apprised about the reasons for non-utilization of funds. (Para 40)  
  During the Commonwealth Games it is anticipated t hat a large number of tourists would 
visit various monuments in and around Delhi under t he protection of ASI. There is a need for 
common entry ticketing system alongwith availabilit y of tickets on Internet. The Committee 
feels that unifying the entry system to the various  monuments under the control of ASI as well 
as the tourists booking their entry tickets. throug h Internet would be of great help to tourist. 
The common entry ticket system need to be introduce d before the Commonwealth Games 2010. 
The Committee recommends that the ASI should coordi nate with the Ministry of Tourism to 
ensure that the tourists had a pleasant and hassle free experience during their visit to various 
monuments. The Committee further recommends that cl eanliness and other basic facilities may 
be ensured at all the monuments.   
 
PROTECTION OF MONUMENTS 
  The Committee also agrees in-principle to such a convergence between the Centre and 
States as far as development and protection of our ancient heritage is concerned. Under the 
existing system, due to lack of sharing and close c oordination there is complete confusion 
between ASI and the States Archaeology Departments.  As a result, hundreds of our heritage 
sites are either not identified & notified and have  been demolished or encroached upon. Such a 
sharing would, the Committee feels, go a long way i n protecting, pressing and developing our 
ancient sites.                                                                                                                         (Para 44)  
  The Committee is concerned at the lack of coordin ation between the Central and State 
Governments. So much so, the ASI was not aware of t he steps taken by the State Governments 
to protect their monuments. The monuments under the  control of State Governments also need 

to be preserved and protected. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Culture should 
ensure that the monuments under the control of Stat e Governments are also protected and 
maintained. If necessary, the Ministry should also explore the possibility of giving adequate 
finance to the State authorities in this regard. Th e Committee is of the view that Government 
needs to take effective steps to coordinate and fac ilitate the monitoring of protection of 
monuments by State Governments.    
STAFF STRENGTH OF ASI 
The Committee is of the view that ASI has spread it self thin and a severe shortage of technical 
personnel has hamstrung the conservation efforts. T he Committee will like to invite the 
attention to the recommendation of the R.N. Mirdha Committee that a minimum of 9,000 
monument attendants would be needed to provide secu rity to the existing structures. The 
Government of India needs to rethink its ‘fence and forget’ approach towards the monuments. (Para 49)  
  The shortage of staff and monument attendants is a serious problem affecting the safety, 
security and upkeep of various monuments adversely. The monuments need to be protected just 
like any other assets of nation. The Committee, the refore, recommends that the Government 
should make efforts on a war footing to ensure that  staff strength as recommended by various 
Committees may be increased and personnel are posted at the earliest. (Para 50)  
  The Committee has come to know that there are hun dreds of attendants who are 
protecting the monuments for 20 years or more, have  not been regularized till date. As a result, 
their basic pay is static and no other allowances a re being paid to them. It is also learnt that 
some of them had gone to Court, but could not get any relief. It is a strange situation when there 
is a huge backlog at different levels including that of monument attendants, yet the existing staff 
are not being adjusted. The Committee, therefore, s trongly recommends that Ministry of 
Culture should adopt humanitarian approach and take steps to increase their wages taking into 
account their length of service and rate of inflation.   
 
  The current framework operates at two levels - Ce ntral, that looks after nationally 
important monuments, and the State-level that is re sponsible for State protected monuments. 
There is an urgent need of activating a third level  of institutional and legal arrangement 
involving local bodies and all these levels need to  work with a very close coordination and well 
defined partnership, legal, financial, etc..    
  The Committee recommends that for preservation of  our monuments activities should be 
decentralized. The historical monuments can be bett er protected by the involvement of local 
people. The Ministry should come out with a decentr alized and integrated mechanism that 
would fill the gaps existing today in this regard. The Committee has already expressed its 
serious reservation about the proposed National Heritage Commission that it will not be able to 
address the major issues currently faced in identif ying, developing, protecting, etc. of our rich 
heritage scattered all over the country from Delhi to Talukas and remote villages. (Para 53)  
HORTICULTURE BRANCH OF ASI 
  The Committee was informed that the proposal for restructuring the Archaeological 
Survey of India has been prepared as per the guidelines of Department of Personnel & Training 
and Ministry of Finance, Department of Expenditure taking into account the requirements of 
each Wing/Branch of the Archaeological Survey of In dia including Horticulture Wing. The 
proposal has been submitted to the Ministry of Cult ure for further action in the matter. The 

Committee is constrained to note that the proposal for restructuring of the Horticultural Branch 
is pending for a long time and till date no decisio n has been taken. The Committee recommends 
that the Government should take speedy action on th e proposal of restructuring of Horticulture 
Branch of ASI so that they can take additional activities. (Para 57)  
  The Committee realizes the need for green environ ment and gardens around the 
monuments. The Committee notes that there is severe  shortage of gardeners and posts need to 
be filled up. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry of Culture should take 
immediate steps to fill up the vacant posts. (Para 58)  
ENCROACHMENTS AND REHABILITATION 
  The Committee notes that despite there being stri ct rules under the Ancient Monuments 
and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958, the problem of encroachment of monuments 
could not be checked. The Committee was informed that the 35 monuments, all managed by the 
Central Government, could not be traced. Delhi, the  headquarters of Archeological Survey of 
India, tops the list with 12 untraceable monuments.  They now exist only in memory and in 
history books. The Committee would like to invite t he attention of the Government to the plight 
of the older Forts in the country. Many of those Fo rts are made up of dry sandstone on 
foundations of clay, sand and rock. These forts are  facing the problem of encroachments also. 
These forts are ill-maintained and in a dilapidated  condition. The growth of tourism and the 
large number of visitors are putting unbearable pre ssure on the structures. One example is the 
Jaisalmer Fort also known as ‘ Sonar Qila’ or ‘Jewel of Desert ’ which is reported to have been 
listed among the most endangered sites of the World  Monuments Fund. It is reported, that the 
waste water has seeped through decayed drains and penetrated the walls of the Fort resulting in 
cracks in various structures of the Fort. The Fort is also facing the problem of encroachment. (Para 61)  
  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Min istry of Culture should take up the 
matter with the concerned State Governments and oth er authorities to ensure that the Forts 
which are part of our cultural heritage are not fur ther damaged due to human action. The 
Committee also recommends that the Ministry should take effective steps to protect the forts 
from neglect and destruction.   
 
  The Committee feels that since in the removal of encroachment, the major role has to be 
played by the State Government, their stake has to be properly recognized. The Ministry should 
initiate consultation with State Government and wor k out a plan for rehabilitation. Financial 
assistance for heritage protection needs to be increased. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
for a separate allocation for dealing with illegal encroachments in ASI monuments and 
rehabilitation to solve this perennial problem. (Para 63)  
NATIONAL MISSION ON MONUMENTS AND ANTIQUITIES 
  The Committee appreciates the setting up of Natio nal Mission on Monuments and 
Antiquities which will bring in record all the cult ural and heritage properties and antiquities 
available in the country. The documentation of the heritage sites and antiquities is a gigantic 
task for which the Government of India have sanctio ned a budgetary allocation of Rs.90 crore 
and the project has to be completed within a period  of five years from 2007-12. The Committee, 
in view of urgency of the work, calls up the Minist ry to complete the work in a time bound way 
and make available more funds if required for the p urpose. The Committee is also of the view 
that Mission has to do a huge task which cannot be completed without the involvement of 

general public. The Committee recommends that Gover nment should take steps to sensitize the 
masses and take assistance of the NGO already working in this area.  (Para 72)  
ZONAL CULTURAL CENTRES (ZCC) 
  The Committee notes the efforts of Zonal Cultural  Centres in preserving the Indian 
culture. However, the Committee is not happy that p lan allocation of Rs.14 crore for the year 
2009-10 was reduced to Rs.12.75 crore at RE stage. Such reduction are not unusual but in cash 
starved Department, efforts should be to utilize th e plan outlay for some other similar purpose, 
if organizing folk dance festival in Delhi was not possible. The Committee feels that there need 
to be more publicity of the events being organized by Zonal Cultural Centres. Without reaching 
the masses, the Zonal Cultural Centres cannot achie ve their objectives. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that concerted efforts should  be made for publicity of the events at 
school and college levels also. (Para 75)  
NATIONAL SCHOOL OF DRAMA 
  The Committee notes the role of the three Akademi s, viz . Sangeet Natak Akademi, Lalit 
Kala Akademi and Sahitya Akademi and National Schoo l of Drama in promotion of literary, 
visual and performing arts. However, the Committee is constrained to note that there has been 
lack of consistency in the matter of utilization of plan outlays in these Akademis during the year 
2009-10. An amount of Rs.13.10 crore was provided f or Sangeet Natak Akademi and was 
reduced to Rs.12.2 crore at RE stage. In the case of Lalit Kala Academy an amount of Rs.6 crore 
was reduced to Rs.1.5 crore at RE stage for which n o reasons have been given. An amount of 
Rs.10.5 crore allocated at BE 2009-10 was reduced t o Rs.6.30 crore at RE stage on account of 
slow pace of expenditure incurred in the first two quarters of the financial year. The Committee 
would like to know the actual reasons of non-utilization of funds by these Akademis and also the 
Committee recommends that the Ministry should have proactive approach while assessing the 
schemes to be carried out by the Societies during t he financial year, so that there is no last-
minute change which may result in non-utilization of plan outlays.   
 
  The Committee is of the view that Sangeet Natak A kademi has to do much more and reach 
the general public through mass media. Further, the re is need for innovative efforts so that the 
reach of Sangeet Natak Akademi is widened. The Committee hopes that Lalit Kala Akademi will 
be able to improve its infrastructural facilities before the Commonwealth Games. Further, more 
regional centres should be established with focus o n more art galleries. The Committee hopes 
that with the increased allocation, Sahitya Akademi  would be able to do its job effectively. 
However, there is need to set up more bookshops and  mobile vans so that the efforts of Sahitya 
Akademi reaches the masses. Mobile vans should visi t various schools, residential areas, office 
complex etc. to reach to the general public.    
INDIRA GANDHI NATIONAL CENTRE FOR THE ARTS (IGNCA) 
  The Committee notes the efforts made by IGNCA in promoting the activities under its 
control. The Committee recommends that there is nee d to set up more sub-centers in various 
parts of country.    (Para 84) 
NATIONAL GALLERY OF MODERN ART 
  The National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA) is a un ique institute of the country which 
represents the evolution and pictorial transformati on in the visual arts in India over the last 
century. However, the Committee is not happy over t he utilization of plan allocation by the 

NGMA. The amount of Rs.7 crore provided in BE 2009-10 was reduced significantly at RE stage 
to Rs.5.3 crore, on account of slow pace of expenditure incurred by NGMA. Committee finds the 
reason given for underutilization of funds ambiguous. The Committee notes that new building in 
New Delhi is under construction and hopes that it w ill be completed at the earliest. Further, the 
Committee is of the view that safety of the article s kept in the Museum is of paramount 
importance. The Committee recommends that necessary  fund may be provided for installing 
latest security gadgets to beef up the security of the Museum.    
THE ASIATIC SOCIETY, KOLKATA 
  The Asiatic Society undertakes activities connect ed with conservation of Art objects, 
sculptures and manuscripts, augmentation of researc h programmes, strengthening of 
publication activities, computerization of library activities etc. However, the Committee fails to 
understand as to why the work for improvement and u pgradation of the institution was not 
taken up as a result of which the amount of Rs.21 c rore allocated to the Asiatic Society was 
reduced to Rs.10.5 crore. The Committee hopes that they would be able to utilize the plan outlay 
optimally during the year 2010-11.   
 
NATIONAL ARCHIVES OF INDIA 
  The Committee notes that National Archives of Ind ia has highly competent professionals 
in the field of Archives record management, conserv ation, reprography and other related fields 
of Archival Science. The National Archives has grea t task before it of managing records. The 
Committee is also of the view that the National Arc hives should conduct regular courses for the 
employees of government offices and if possible dep ute staff from its organization to assist 
various offices to maintain record in a better way.  However, the Committee notes that the plan 
outlay of Rs.4.50 crore made in BE 2009-10 was redu ced to Rs.3.75 crore at RE stage. Again an 
amount of Rs.5.00 crore has been provided in BE 201 0-11. The Committee feels that there is 
some sort of inconsistency in the planning which has resulted in fluctuation of plan outlay at BE 
and RE stages which can affect the functioning and activities of the National Archives of India. (Para 95)  
MUSEUMS 
  The Committee notes that India has a number of Mu seums under its control, both 
Government and private. The allocations to the Muse ums is enough as is evident from the 
utilisation of allocation under Museum head. In 2009-10, allocation of Rs. 72.36 crore was made. 
The actual expenditure upto 31st March, 2010 is Rs.  71.50 crore. However, there is need for 
detailed pamphlets listing numerous exhibitions, IM AX and Space Shows, optional audio tours 
for an extra fee, special lectures and the Restaura nts which can create interest in the minds of 
people. (Para 104)  
  There is also need to review the timings of the M useum which should be extended in 
evening hours keeping in view that in most part of India, days are very hot. During School 
vacations, time should be extended so that children can visit it in the evenings with their parents. (Para 105)  
  Further, Security should be strengthened in all t he museums. The punishment for doing 
any damage to the museum should be explicitly displayed at all the prominent places. (Para 106)  
  More than that India’s brilliant IT and engineeri ng brains could be garnered to design 
new and exciting exhibits, marking the beginning of  transforming museums into “edutainment” 
destinations.    (Para 107)  

LIBRARIES 
  The Committee notes that the Central Secretariat Library with a huge depository of books 
and volumes are not accessible to the common public  due to security reasons. The Committee 
recommends that the Central Secretariat Library may  be moved out of the high security area 
and housed at a place where the researchers, studen ts, academician and general public can 
access it. The Ministry of Culture may also explore the possibility of constructing a new campus 
for the Central Secretariat Library. (Para 112)  
  The allocation to the Public Libraries was Rs. 40 .62 crore in the year 2009-10. The 
expenditure upto 31st March 2010 is Rs 40.13 crore.  The Committee is of the view that the 
Government should provide more funds to the Librari es through Ministry of Culture, if it can 
be utilised by them. However, there need to be some  improvement as such the catalogue of the 
libraries should be available on internet. The Computers and softwares installed in the libraries 
should be in working conditions all the time so tha t readers are not put to inconvenience. The 
concept of community libraries should be explored a nd they should be promoted with wide 
publicity.  (Para 120)  
  The Committee at the same time observed that wher ever large collections did exist, they 
were not fully used because of rigid rules. Library  users were not trusted and were asked 
security deposit, which every library users may not afford. (Para 121)  
PROJECT/SCHEMES FOR PROMOTION OF ART AND CULTURE FOR NORTH-EAST 
  The Committee is at a loss to note that there is no mechanism to reflect the expenditure 
incurred for North-East in the same financial year.  Thus the Committee finds the accounting 
procedure complex and it is not known how much expe nditure is incurred for North-East 
Region. Besides this how much the excess expenditur e includes the component of North-East 
Region is not clear. The Committee, therefore, reco mmends that necessary accounting system 
should be devised in consultation with C&AG and sim plified to give a true picture of the 
expenditure incurred in North-East Region.   
 
VACANT POSTS IN VARIOUS PREMIER INSTITUTIONS FUNCTI ONING UNDER THE 
MINISTRY OF CULTURE 
  The Committee notes that the cultural institution s in our country are in a state of crisis 
most of which have remained headless for many years . This Committee has been pointing out 
about this from time to time. The Committee notes t he efforts made by the Governments in 
appointing heads of various national institutions i n recent times. The Committee recommends 
that every effort should be made to ensure that all  the institutional heads are appointed at the 
earliest. The process further need to be streamlined and vacant post filled up on urgent basis. (Para 131)  
NATIONAL CULTURE POLICY 
  The Committee notes that the Government has neith er shown any long-term vision nor 
any substantial short-term action to arrest the dec ay that has set in. The Ministry of Culture 
must move its wheels to generate a vision for the f uture that includes a sensible policy towards 
their missions. The Committee recommends that the N ational Culture Policy may be brought 
out at the earliest.   (Para 134)  
ANTIQUITIES AND ART TREASURES ACT, 1972 

  The Committee is of the view that amendments in t he proposed Act are the need of 
hour and it should be done in a time bound manner. The apprehension of the public who 
are scared to show antiquities should be removed. There should be some mechanism of one 
time voluntary disclosure and registration of articles as there is a boom in art market and 
with the amendment in the Act, the trade can be done within, the country, lawfully 
benefitting the country. (Para 137)
