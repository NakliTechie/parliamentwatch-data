The Central Road Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2006 was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 18 th December, 2006. On
 26th December, 2006 Hon'ble Chairman Rajya Sabha, in consultation with the Hon'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha referred the
 Bill to the Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture for examination and Report.  Under the mandate, the
 Committee has examined the Bill and finalised the Report after hearing the views of the nodal Ministry, i.e. , Road
 Transport and Highways and the only stakeholder i.e., Ministry of Rural Development.  The Bill seeks to amend the
 Central Road Fund Act, 2000 which provides for levy and collection by way of cess, excise and customs duty on petrol
 and high-speed diesel oil for development and maintenance of National Highways, rural roads, State roads and
 improvement of safety at railway crossings.  Under Section 9 of the Act the Central Government has the power to
 administer the fund and allocate and disburse such sums as are considered necessary to the concerned departments
 responsible for the development and maintenance of rural roads.  Under section 10, the Central Government is
 responsible for allocation of fifty percent of the cess on high-speed diesel oil for the development and maintenance of
 National Highways, rural roads, State roads and construction of roads over or under railways.  The fund available under
 Section 10 is utilized for the provision of all-weather connectivity to rural habitations through centrally sponsored
 scheme “Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana”.  Under the Scheme, a target has been set to provide road connectivity to
 all rural habitations having a population of 1000 and above (500 and above in hilly and tribal areas) by 2009.  This will
 require an investment of about Rs.48,000 crores.  To bridge the funding gap, the Government intends to borrow
 Rs.16,500 crores over a period of four years. Since the Central Road Fund Act, 2000 does not have any provision for
 borrowing for rural roads and utilization of the proceeds of future cess for repayment of loans, the Central Road Fund
 (Amendment) Bill, 2006 seeks to amend the Parent Act to enable the Central Government to take such measures as may
 be necessary to raise funds for the development of rural roads, and it may use the share of the fund for the repayment of
 any loan taken for the purpose of development of rural roads in any State or Union territory.
2.         The Central Road Fund was in existence since 1929.  It was being governed by the Parliamentary Resolution of
 1977 according to which an amount not less that 3.5 paise per litre of petrol consumed was to be set apart out of the
 custom and excise duty levied on petrol for development of roads.  The amount was being distributed between the State
 and the Central Schemes at the ratio of 80:20 after defraying the cost of administration of the Fund.  The two Houses of
 the Parliament passed a revised Resolution in May 1988 according to which an amount not less than 5 per cent of the
 basic price of petrol and diesel was earmarked for the development of Road Sector.  However, this resolution could not
 be implemented due to reservations expressed by Ministry of Finance and difficult resources position.
3.         In recognition of the need for funds for the road infrastructure sector the Union Budget for 1988-89 provided for
 a levy of additional excise duty and additional customs duty of one Rupee per litre of petrol.  Subsequently, an
 additional duty of one Rupee per litre on imported and domestic high speed diesel was proposed in the Budget  for
 1999-2000.  The revenue from these two levies was to accrue to the dedicated Central Road Fund.  It was also decided
 that 50 per cent of the cess on diesel will be given to the Ministry of Rural Development for Rural Roads.  A legal
 instrument was necessary for administration of the Fund.  Accordingly, due to urgency of the matter the Central Road
 Fund ordinance was promulgated on 1.11.2000 for this purpose.  Subsequently the ordinance was replaced by the
 Central Road Fund Act, 2000.

 meeting held on 7th August, 2001.  The Cabinet Note, as approved, identified  the 50% share of the cess on high speed
 diesel as the readily available source of funding for the rural roads programme, and authorised the Department of Rural
 Development, in consulation with the Ministry of Finance, to generate additional financial resources, including
 borrowings from, inter alia, the World Bank and Asian Development Bank (ADB.)

 another Rs.24,000 crore.

17.       During the examination, the Committee further sought to know whether measures for raising funds would
 include borrowing from agencies other than Public Financial Institutions and even from public at large.  The Ministry

 Bharat Nirman.
18.       As regards, repayment of loan proposed to be raised for the development of rural roads, the Ministry  clarified
 that repayment shall be made out of the amount of cess allocated for rural roads under Sub-Clause (a) of Clause (viii) of
 Section - 10 of the Act.  However, the entire allocation shall not be pre-empted for repayment of loan and only a
 portion of this allocation will be set aside for repayment of loan.  A suitable ceiling for this purpose may be prescribed
 in the Rules.
19.       The Committee recommends that instead of incorporating a separate Clause - (ba) - under Section 9 (1) of the
 Parent Act, the purpose of the proposed amendment can be served by adding the term"and for the development of rural
 roads" at the end of the existing Clause 9(1)(b).
20.       As regards amendment of section 11 of the Parent Act is concerned, the Committee notes that it is being sought
 to make another enabling provision in the Parent Act so as to enable the Central Government to use the share of the
 fund for the repayment of any loan taken for development of rural roads.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 the ceiling on the quantum of loan to be taken and the manner of repayment should be clearly specified in the Bill
 itself.  The Committee feels that there is no such provision to put a check on the powers of borrowing of the
 Government and a situation should not arise when most of the proceeds of the fund are being utilized for repayment of
 loans only.
Overall Recommendation
21.       The Committee strongly feels that Central Road Fund Act was enacted only in the year 2000, wherein, no
 provision was made to enable the Government to garner funds for development rural roads.  Only after a short period it
 is felt by the Government that the legislation is not sufficient enough and leaves scope for further amendments.  The
 Committee observes that as per the Sub-Section (2) of Section 11 of the Central Road Fund Act, 2000, the Central
 Government has that power to retain the portion of the fund allocated until it is actually required for expenditure. The
 Sub Section (3) of Section 11 further states that if in the opinion of the Central Government, the Government of any
 State or the administration of any Union territory "failed to take such steps as the Central Government may recommend
 for the regulation and control of motor vehicles within the State or the Union Territory or delayed without reasonable
 cause the application of any portion of the fund allocated or re-allocated, as the case may be, for expenditure within the
 State or Union Territory, the Central Government may resume the whole or part of any sums which it may have at that
 time held for expenditure within that State or Union Territory".  Further, From a perusal of Sub-Section (4) and (5) of

lapsable nature in the hands of the Central Government. Whereas, in case of State Government/Union Territory the said
 fund becomes lapsable one as it allows the Central Government to re-allocate all sums resumed from the account of
 State Government/Union Territory in the ratio of main allocation for the financial year preceding the year in which re-
allocation is made, thereby, contradicting the spirit of the CRF Act, 2000.  The Committee strongly feels that generally
 poor and backward States are not being able to utilize the fund allocated to them and redistribution of the said fund
 amongst others would lead to vicious circle of under development in backward states and lop sided growth of the
 national economy.  Accordingly, the Committee observes that rules be framed in such a way that the share should be
 made available to those States as and when they comply with the conditions satisfactorily.
22.       The Committee further observes that Bill has been introduced by the Government to incorporate an enabling
 provision in the Central Road Fund Act, 2000 so that the Government can, if need be, borrow money for the Fund from
 the financial institutions etc. because there is no such provision in the Parent Act,  this is being done through a general
 power enabling the Central Government to take such measures as may be necessary to raise funds for the development
 of rural roads.  Borrowing is no doubt one way to raise funds but provision of a general power may be of far too
 sweeping in nature.  Therefore, the modes of borrowing should be specified in the Bill it self such as, borrowing from
 public financial institutions, private sector institutions, issuing public bonds, borrowing from international agencies etc.
 That will prevent the misuse of this provision at any given point of time.
23.       The Committee accordingly clears the Central Road Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2006 subject to the above
 recommendations made in the Report.  The Committee also recommends that the Title of the Bill should be changed
 from Central Road Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2006 to Central Road Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2007 the year has changed
 from 2006 to 2007 and in the enacting formula the words 'Fifty - Seventh' be substituted by 'Fifty - Eighth'.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
 
The Central Road Fund Act, 2000 provides for levy and collection by way of cess, excise and customs duty on
 petrol and high-speed diesel oil for development and maintenance of National Highways, rural roads, State roads and
 improvement of safety at railway crossings.  Under section 9, the Central Government has the power to administer the
 fund and allocate and disburse such sums as are considered necessary to the concerned departments responsible for the
 development and maintenance of rural roads.  Under section 10, the Central Government is responsible for allocation of
 fifty percent of the cess on high-speed diesel oil for the development and maintenance of National Highways, rural
 roads, State roads and construction of roads over or under railways.  The allocation under section 10 is utilized for
 provision of all-weather connectivity to rural habitations through centrally sponsored scheme “Pradhan Mantri Gram
 Sadak Yojana”.  Under this, a target has been set to provide road connectivity to all rural habitations having a
 population of 1000 and above (500 and above in hilly and tribal areas) by 2009.  This will require an investment of
 about Rs.48,000 crores.  To bridge the funding gap it has been decided to borrow Rs.16,500 crores over a period of four
 years.
 
2.         Since the Central Road Fund Act, 2000 does not have any provision for borrowing for rural roads and utilization
 of the proceeds of future cess for repayment of loans, the Central Road Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2006 seeks to amend
 the Act to enable the Central Government to take such measures as may be necessary to raise funds for the development
 of rural roads, and it may use the share of the fund for the repayment of any loan taken for the purpose of development
 of rural roads in any State or Union territory.
