REPORT
Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and
programmes for the development and promotion of tourism in the country. In the process, the
Ministry consults and collaborates with other stakeholders in the sector including various Central
Ministries/agencies, the State Governments/Union Territory Administrations and the representatives of
the private sector.
2. The administrative head of the Ministry is the Secretary (Tourism). The Secretary also acts as
the Director General (DG) Tourism. Directorate General of Tourism has a field formation of 20
offices within the country and 14 offices abroad and one sub-ordinate office/project i.e. Indian
Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM)/Gulmarg Winter Sports Project. The overseas offices
are primarily responsible for tourism promotion and marketing in their respective areas and the field
offices in India are responsible for providing information service to tourists and to monitor the
progress of field projects.
3. Tourism is promoted within India primarily by the Ministry of Tourism with the help of 5
Regional Offices and 15 Sub-Offices under them, with the involvement of the State Governments and
with the help of modern and conventional tools of promotion. India Tourism Development
Corporation (ITDC) is the only public sector undertaking under the Ministry of Tourism.
4. The Ministry also has the autonomous institutions viz., Indian Institutes of Tourism and Travel
Management (IITTM); National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology (NCHMCT);
and the 21 Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs).
5. The major Programmes/Schemes of the Ministry of Tourism are:–
(i) Product/Infrastructure Development for Destinations and Circuits;
(ii) Overseas Promotion and Publicity including Market Development Assistance;
(iii) Assistance to IHMs/FCIs/IITTM/NIWS (National Institute of Water Sports);
(iv) Domestic Promotion and Publicity including Hospitality;
(v) Capacity Building for Service Providers;
(vi) Incentives to Accommodation Infrastructure;
(vii) Computerization and Information Technology;
(viii) Assistance for Large Revenue Generating Projects;
(ix) Externally Aided Projects;
(x) Market Research including 20 Years Perspective Plans;
(xi) Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism Infrastructure Development;
(xii) Integrated Development of Theme-Based Tourist Circuits - SW ADESH DARSHAN; and
(xiii) National Mission on Pilgrimage Rejuvenation And Spiritual Augmentation Drive
(PRASAD).

9. The Committee notes that even though Planning Commission had made a very
ambitious plan for tourism promotion in the country during the 12th plan Period by making
an outlay of Rs. 15190 crore, the sanctions made during the first three years of the Twelfth
Plan period was Rs. 2930.00 crore only, which is just 19.28% of the total outlay. If the actual
spending is taken into consideration, it stands at Rs. 2566.48 crore which is 16.89% of the
12th Plan outlay. The Committee finds that expenditure and allocation have not been upto the
mark. It is a matter of concern as the targets also could not have been achieved due to less
spending. The Committee, therefore, is constrained to observe that such a pattern does not
augur well for Tourism, as it has highest potential for employment as well as revenue
generation for our economy.
10. The Government does not make adequate allocation for this Sector. The Committee is
afraid that if such a trend continues, the country may not be able to sustain the current
tourist inflow, not to speak of increasing it upto the desired level. It, therefore, may result
in the decline of not only the tourism industry as a whole but also so many other sectors
associated with it.

14. The Committee notes that total projects sanctioned during 2014-15 is only 50 which are
to be undertaken by 36 States/UTs; 18 States/UTs have 'Nil' projects sanctioned in their name.
Only Nagaland (10) has managed to get the number of projects in double digits and rest of
the others have very few projects sanctioned in 2014-15.
15. The Committee notes that PIDDC, which is the only flag-ship Centrally Sponsored
Scheme (CSS) for tourism infrastructure development, Human Resource Development (HRD),
Fairs and Festivals and Rural Tourism (RT), has not been allocated any fund this fiscal. The
Committee, in view of the importance of this scheme, recommends that more allocations be
made for these schemes at the RE stage. If the reasons for not allocating any funds for this
scheme is the devolution of funds to the States as per the 14th Finance Commission
recommendation, there was no clue as yet about when and how much and in what manner the
required funds will come from the States. Even Rs.20 crores given to Union Territories under
this scheme is not sufficient in view of vast potential these UTs have for tourism. The

5
Committee, therefore, calls upon Union and the State Governments to put their head together
at the earliest, to chalk out future plans to share funds so that on-going schemes do not
suffer.
16. The Committee is happy to observe that due to its repeated recommendations for their
tourism development, Jammu and Kashmir and North-East States got more projects
sanctioned for tourism promotion.
17. The Committee reiterates its recommendation given in its 195th Report that the
Ministry of Tourism should actively pursue each of the projects sanctioned to ensure that the
sanctioned amount is utilized in the prescribed manner for the approved project. The
Committee appreciates the limitation as most of Ministry's scheme are being implemented by
the States. The Committee, however, recommends that the Ministry should closely co-ordinate
with the State Governments to adhere to financial procedures/requirements to achieve the
desired targets.

21. The Ministry has informed that for 2015-16, an amount of Rs. 2897.15 crore was proposed.
However, an amount of Rs. 1483.20 crore only was allocated by Ministry of Finance.
22. The Committee also finds that some schemes such as UNDP-Endogenous Tourism
Projects, Creation of Land Bank for Hotels, Assistance to Large Revenue Generating projects
etc have 'Nil' allocation so far. The Committee recommends that if the above schemes are
redundant, they should be discontinued or these projects should be properly reviewed to make
it workable projects.
23. The Committee observes that allocations made for 'Assistance to Central Agencies for
Tourism Infrastructure Development' for 2015-16 is mere 14.25 crore even though the
previous years' allocation and spending was higher. The Committee recommends for allocation
of more funds under this scheme.
24. The Committee notes that for 'Incentives to accommodation infrastructure', only Rs. 10
lakh has been earmarked during 2015-16. The Committee is of the opinion that due to
shortage of hotel room and exorbitant room rent, many of the foreign tourists prefer to go
to neighbouring countries. The Committee, therefore, feels that accommodation
infrastructure should be given more incentives, thereby making available more budget hotels
to attract more and more tourists.
25. The Committee notes that during 2014-15, most of the allocation under PIDDC had gone
to the North Eastern States whereas most important tourism States like Kerala and Goa
showed nil allocations. It does not know the reason for this. The Committee recommends that
the States which are attracting largest number of tourists should be given special dispensation
for development of tourism and their allocation not to be withheld/reduced on account of
technicalities like, utilisation certificates, incomplete proposal etc.
26. The State Governments have been declined their share for infrastructure development
in Tourism Sector due to the introduction of PRASAD Scheme" and Swadesh Darshan
Scheme. The Committee reminds that the Government should provide adequate funding to
the State Governments, especially to Government of Kerala for developing tourism related
infrastructure.
27. The Committee finds that for 2015-16 only Rs.20 crore has been allocated under the UT head
for the PIDDC Scheme. As a result, Ministry will not be able to sustain its on-going schemes in the
State, let alone sanctioning new schemes to them.
28. The Committee observes that Union Territories have negligible project allocation and
very few projects have been sanctioned to them so far. The Committee is of the opinion that
Union Territories like Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Puducherry etc. have tremendous
tourism potential and also scope for growth in terms of tourist footfall. The 'Committee also
notes that only Nagaland could utilize the sanctioned amount during the period.

National Mission on Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Augmentation Drive (PRASAD)
34. In the Budget Speech 2014-15, the Government decided to launch a National Mission on
Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Augmentation Drive with an initial provision of Rs. 100 crore
at BE but at RE stage, it was reduced to Rs. 15.60 crore. This fiscal also, it has been allocated Rs.100
crores. The PRASAD will be joined with the HRIDAY Scheme implemented by Ministry of Urban
Development.
35. Under PRASAD, the following 12 religious sites have been identified for development viz.,
Dwarka, Amritsar, Ajmer, Mathura, Varanasi, Gaya, Puri, Amravati, Kanchipuram and Vellakini,
Kedarnath and Kamakhaya.
36. Mission Directorate for the Scheme has been set up under the Chairpersonship of Additional
Director General (Tourism). The first meeting of Mission Directorate has been held wherein three
projects were considered under the scheme such as Gaya in Bihar, Puri in Orissa and Mathura in Uttar
Pradesh.
37. The Ministry of Tourism has informed that for Vishnupad Temple, Gaya in Bihar a Central
Financial Assistance of Rs. 428.92 lakh has been sanctioned under PRASAD scheme and as 1st
installment of Rs. 85.78 lakhs (20%) has been released.
38. The Committee feels that this is a good initiative with a good start, as India has many
prominent religious places relating to Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism and Sufism

9
spread over different parts of the country. Pilgrimage is a motivation for travel, with major
tourist destinations having developed largely as a result of their connections to sacred places,
persons and events. To tap this potential, there is a need for holistic development of the
selected pilgrim destinations in co-operation with other stakeholders.
39. The Committee notes that so many important religious centers could not be included
under the PRASAD Scheme at the first stance. Certain famous pilgrim places like Madurai,
Sabarimala, Guruvayoor and Tirupati deserve to be mentioned in this regard. The Committee
recommends that such places should also be included in the second phase of the Scheme.
40. The Committee also finds out that allocations for these new schemes had to be reduced
at RE Stage which indicates slower procedure and execution. The Ministry should be careful
on these counts in future.

VISA-ON-ARRIVAL
88. The Government of India launched the Tourist Visa on Arrival (TVoA) enabled with Electronic
Travel Authorisation (ETA) Scheme on 27.11.2014. This facility is available to foreigners whose sole
objective of visiting India is recreation, sight- seeing, short duration medical treatment, casual
business visit, casual visit to meet friends or relatives etc. and not valid for any other purpose/
activities. This will allow entry into India within 30 days from the date of approval of ETA and will
be valid for 30 days stay in India from the date of arrival in India. The TV oA enabled with ETA cannot
be availed more than twice in a calendar year.
89. The Ministry has further stated that the TV oA enabled with ETA would enable the prospective
visitor to apply for an Indian Visa from his/her home country online without visiting the Indian
Mission and also pay the visa fee online. Once approved, the applicant will receive an email
authorizing him/her to travel to India and he/she can travel with a print out of this authorization. On
arrival, the visitor has to present the authorization to the immigration authorities who would then
stamp the entry into the country.
90. With effect from 15.1.2015, Guyana was added to this list thereby making it a total of 44
countries whose citizens are eligible for this facility. During the last three months, 87610 TV oAs have
been issued.
91. Subsequent to the stabilization of this Scheme and Budget announcement to extend this scheme
to 150 countries in stages, the Ministry of Tourism has submitted a list of 106 additional countries
to be considered for TYoA.

South East Asian Countries. Tourism sector in some of the key competing destinations in South Asian
countries attract much lesser taxes thereby making the products more competitive.

to give a further boost to the tourism sector in India.
 Availability of Cenvat Credit on service tax charged on services provided in a tour
package as well as on the overall tour package.
 Increase in Benefit under SFIS Scheme
 Reduction in V AT (Value Added Tax) on Airline Turbine Fuel.
 Reduction in monument entry fee to be charged to Inbound Tourists.
 Harmonization of state entry/Road Tax for commercial vehicles, so that travel is seamless
across India.
113. The Committee desires that the suggestions made by the Ministry should be adequately
looked into by the Ministry of Finance. The Committee observes that the recent increase in
service tax to 14% will adversely affect the hotel industry. Already, India has a higher hotel
tariff due to multiple tax regime of both States and Centre. The additional burden on hotel,
restaurant and transportation will discourage the middle class tourists, both domestic and
foreign, from visiting tourist places.
114. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism may actively take
up this matter with the Ministry of Finance so that some sort of relief may be made available
to the tourism sector in the coming years.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE —
AT A GLANCE
12th Five Year Plan Analysis
The Committee notes that even though Planning Commission had made a very
ambitious plan for tourism promotion in the country during the 12th plan Period by making
an outlay of Rs. 15190 crore, the sanctions made during the first three years of the Twelfth
Plan period was Rs 2930.00 crore only, which is just 19.28% of the total outlay. If the actual
spending is taken into consideration, it stands at Rs 2566.48 crore which is 16.89% of the 12th
Plan outlay. The Committee finds that expenditure and allocation have not been upto the
mark. It is a matter of concern as the targets also could not have been achieved due to less
spending. The Committee, therefore, is constrained to observe that such a pattern does not
augur well for Tourism, as it has highest potential for employment as well as revenue
generation for our economy. (Para: 9)
The Government does not make adequate allocation for this Sector. The Committee is
afraid that if such a trend continues, the country may not be able to sustain the current
tourist inflow, not to speak of increasing it upto the desired level. It, therefore, may result
in the decline of not only the tourism industry as a whole but also so many other sectors
associated with it. (Para: 10)
The Committee notes that total projects sanctioned during 2014-15 is only 55 which are
to be undertaken by 36 States/UTs; 19 States/UTs have 'Nil' projects sanctioned in their name.
Only Nagaland (11) and Sikkim (10) have managed to get the number of projects in double
digits and rest of the others have very few projects sanctioned in 2014-15. (Para: 14)
The Committee notes that PIDDC, which is the only flag-ship Centrally Sponsored
Scheme (CSS) for tourism infrastructure development, Human Resource Development (HRD),
Fairs and Festivals and Rural Tourism (RT), has not been allocated any fund this fiscal. The
Committee, in view of the importance of this scheme, recommends that more allocations be
made for these schemes at the RE stage. If the reasons for not allocating any funds for this
scheme is the devolution of funds to the States as per the 14th Finance Commission
recommendation, there was no clue as yet about when and how much and in what manner the
required funds will come from the States. Even Rs. 20 crores given to Union Territories under
this scheme is not sufficient in view of vast potential these States have for tourism. The
Committee, therefore, calls upon Union and the State Governments to put their head together
at the earliest, to chalk out future plans to share funds so that on-going schemes do not
suffer. (Para: 15)
The Committee is happy to observe that due to its repeated recommendations for their
tourism development, Jammu and Kashmir and North-East States got more projects
sanctioned for tourism promotion.
(Para: 16)
The Committee reiterates its recommendation given in its 195th Report that the
43

44
Ministry of Tourism should actively pursue each of the projects sanctioned to ensure that the
sanctioned amount is utilized in the prescribed manner for the approved project. The
Committee appreciates the limitation as most of Ministry's scheme are being implemented by
the States. The Committee, however, recommends that the Ministry should closely co-ordinate
with the State Governments to adhere to financial procedures/requirements to achieve the
desired targets. (Para: 17)
Analysis of Budgetary Allocations of 2013-14, 2014-15 and Annual Plan 2015-16
The Committee also finds that some schemes such as UNDP-Endogenous Tourism
Projects, Creation of Land Bank for Hotels, Assistance to Large Revenue Generating Projects
etc. have 'Nil' allocation so far. The Committee recommends that if the above schemes are
redundant, they should be discontinued or these projects should be properly reviewed to make
it workable projects. (Para: 22)
The Committee observes that allocations made for 'Assistance to Central Agencies for
Tourism Infrastructure Development' for 2015-16 is mere 14.25 crore even though the
previous years' allocation and spending was higher. The Committee recommends for allocation
of more funds under this scheme. (Para: 23)
The Committee notes that for 'Incentives to accommodation infrastructure', only Rs. 10
lakh has been earmarked during 2015-16. The Committee is of the opinion that due to
shortage of hotel room and exorbitant room rent, many of the foreign tourists prefer to go
to neighbouring countries. The Committee, therefore, feels that accommodation
infrastructure should be given more incentives, thereby making available more budget hotels
to attract more and more tourists. (Para: 24)
The Committee notes that during 2014-15, most of the allocation under PIDDC had gone
to the North Eastern States whereas most important tourism States like Kerala and Goa
showed nil allocations. It does not know the reason for this. The Committee recommends that
the States which are attracting largest number of tourists should be given special dispensation
for development of tourism and their allocation not to be withheld/reduced on account of
technicalities like, utilisation certificates, incomplete proposal etc. (Para: 25)
The State Governments have been declined their share for infrastructure development
in Tourism Sector due to the introduction of PRASAD Scheme and Swadesh Darshan Scheme.
The Committee reminds that the Government should provide adequate funding to the State
Governments, especially to Government of Kerala for developing tourism related
infrastructure. (Para: 26)
The Committee observes that Union Territories have negligible project allocation and
very few projects have been sanctioned to them so far. The Committee is of the opinion that
Union Territories like Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Puducherry etc. have tremendous
tourism potential and also scope for growth in terms of tourist footfall. The Committee also
notes that only Nagaland could utilize the sanctioned amount during the period. (Para: 28)

The Committee understands that Swadesh Darshan Scheme has been introduced after
reducing the allocations made to States for infrastructure development projects. With a view
to avoiding such projects facing resource crunch, the Swadesh Darshan Scheme needs to be

The Committee feels that this is a very good scheme and desires that the project

that efforts be made to see that all the States/Union Territories should be covered under this
scheme over the years. (Para: 33)
National Mission on Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Augmentation Drive (PRASAD)
The Committee feels that this is a good initiative with a good start, as India has many
prominent religious places relating to Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism and Sufism
spread over different parts of the country. Pilgrimage is a motivation for travel, with major
tourist destinations having developed largely as a result of their connections to sacred places,
persons and events. To tap this potential, there is a need for holistic development of the
selected pilgrim destinations in co-operation with other stakeholders. (Para: 38)
The Committee notes that so many important religious centers could not be included
under the PRASAD Scheme at the first stance. Certain famous pilgrim places like Madurai,
Sabarimala, Guruvayoor and Tirupati deserve to be mentioned in this regard. The Committee
recommends that such places should also be included in the second phase of the Scheme.
(Para: 39)
The Committee also finds out that allocations for these new schemes had to reduced at
RE stage which indicates slower procedure and execution. The Ministry should be careful on
these counts in future. (Para: 40)
Domestic Campaign
The Committee notes with concern that in 2014-15, BE allocation of Rs. 142 crore was
reduced to Rs 135.00 crore at RE stage; spending was only Rs 32.82 crore up to 31st
December, 2014. The Committee notes the under- utilisation and desires that the Ministry
should take adequate steps to spend the allocated money by removing difficulties in this
regard. (Para: 44)
The Committee notes that domestic campaign to promote tourism is mainly through
visual and print media. The Committee also notes that the spending pattern of allocated
money shows that the expenditure is booked during the last quarter of the financial year and
that they could spend only too little. The Committee feels that last minute spending unless
forced by genuine/unavoidable consideration, should not be resorted to as it reflects poorly on
our performance and foresight. (Para: 45)
The Committee recommends that the TV commercial campaigns and the print media
campaigns should be spread throughout the year. It should be consistent and regular to spread
the awareness about tourism. It should not be just fits and starts as happened during the year
2014-15. The Committee is of the view that administrative set up of the Ministry should have
advance and proper planning for the execution of the domestic campaign projects. (Para: 46)
The Committee also recommends that funds under the Domestic Campaign should
be shared among the State Governments also for the promotion of tourism in the
States. (Para: 47)

numbers of tourist spots/monuments of the country. The Committee recommends that the
Government should approach all the major corporates both in private and public sector to
utilize their CSR funds to adopt various tourist spots/monuments under the 'Clean India

Overseas Promotion and Publicity and Market Development Assistance
The Committee would like to know the details of the legal issues which led to reduced

USA, France and Australia. (Para: 62)
The Committee observes that there is a need to undertake a study on tourist arrivals
vis-a-vis spending abroad for publicity and market development. During 2014-15, there was a
drastic reduction in spending on overseas publicity and promotion but a slight increase in the
foreign tourist arrivals. This shows that there is a disconnect between the spending and tourist
arrivals. (Para: 63)
The Committee recommends that the overseas publicity may be reviewed in view of its
utility to attract tourists to visit India. A cost-benefit analysis in respect of our India Tourism
Offices abroad may be conducted and those offices which are unviable or becoming a burden

The Committee notes that the Incredible India website has been made available in 8

Visa-on-Arrival
The Committee notes the introduction of E-Visa facilities to be a step forward in
encouraging foreign tourists to visit India. The Committee has the view that the steps taken

reduce the bottlenecks further in accessing a Visa for a tourist to visit India. (Para: 93)
The Committee had sought clarification about Electronic Travel Authorization and Visa
on Arrival. There were reports in the media that many of the tourists are misguided by the
impression that they will get a visa on arrival. But in practice, every tourist has to submit
the on-line application for visa in advance. The Committee recommends that the Government
should take adequate steps to remove this misunderstanding and the tourists should be
advised that it is not a Visa on Arrival but an Electronic Travel Authorization. (Para: 94)
The Committee further recommends that Visa on Arrival in its true sense may be
implemented in more and more airports/ports wherever it is possible, which may further boost
the promotion of tourism. (Para: 95)
Hotel Infrastructure
The Committee is concerned to note that the Ministry is fully aware that one of the
bottlenecks in developing a hotel is the procurement of multiple clearance/approvals from
various Central and State Government agencies. The Committee understands that in order to
solve this issue, the Hospitality Development and Promotion Board (HDPB) was established.
The Committee was surprised to note that the HDPB is non- functional at present due to
shortage of manpower in the Ministry. The Committee strongly recommends that HDPB may
be made functional during the current financial year itself and the necessary budgetary and
manpower provisions may be made. The Ministry may furnish the action taken in this regard
in its ATR. (Para: 100)
The Committee recommends that the new initiatives taken by the Ministry in the hotel
sector should be started as soon as possible. (Para: 102)
The Committee observes that during 2013-14, Assam and Tripura could not find a place
in the Tourism Mart. Also, compared to other States, the activities in Meghalaya were
restricted to some fairs and festivals only and no infrastructure/Circuit oriented projects were
undertaken. The Committee also observes that the spending pattern has not shown an

49
improvement in the States, except that Meghalaya was s anctioned a minimum amount during
the period. However, Meghalaya has been left out from the list of allocation during 2015-16.
During 2014-15 also, the Committee could not find much hope in the utilization of allocated
funds among the North East States. Average spending could not go beyond the level of 20-25%
in some States and the actual figures in majority of North East States was even lower. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the projects started be promptly undertaken so that
lapsing of funds can be avoided. (Para: 107)
The Committee could not find any concrete action taken by the Ministry of Tourism on

Therefore, the Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in para 87 of its
195th Report and further recommends that development of Darjeeling should be taken up
alongwith the tourism development of North Eastern Region. (Para: 108)
Taxes in the Tourism Sector
The Committee desires that the suggestions made by the Ministry should be adequately
looked into by the Ministry of Finance. The Committee observes that the recent increase in
service tax to 14% will adversely affect the hotel industry. Already, India has a higher hotel
tariff due to multiple tax regime of both States and Centre. The additional burden on hotel,
restaurant and transportation will discourage the middle class tourists, both domestic and
foreign, from visiting tourist places. (Para: 113)
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism may actively take
up this matter with the Ministry of Finance so that some sort of relief may be made available
to the tourism sector in the coming years. (Para: 114)
National Permit System
The Committee notes the efforts by the Ministry of Tourism for stream - lining the
transportation sector . (Para: 118)
In this regard, the Committee does not feel that collecting the data of the vehicles
passing through various stretches is a cumbersome procedure in this era of technological
development. The Committee recommends that if the National Permit vehicles are provided
with a tracking system such as a GPRS linked device in the vehicle while issuing them
permits, at every entry point of the vehicle, its data can be collected on daily basis.
Accordingly, the tax component can be calculated State-wise and the same can be credited
automatically to each State's respective accounts on monthly basis. (Para: 119)
The Committee is aware of the nitty-gritty involved in the National Permit System for
tourist buses, but feels that the Governments can work out the appropriate procedures for
implementing the seamless travel, perhaps due to absence of required will. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Government should work out the systems and procedures for
implementing the National Permit System for seamless travel of the tourist buses with the
State Governments at the earliest. (Para: 120)
Connectivity
The Committee notes the various initiatives taken by the Ministry of Tourism for
improving connectivity to tourist destinations. The Committee recommends that dedicated

50
tourist trains which are both affordable and comfortable should be started on the lines of the
Mahaparinirvan Express, along with the development of theme based tourism circuits in the
country. The Committee recommends that this matter should be taken up with the Ministry
of Railways. (Para: 123)
Also, the Committee feels that now the air fares are on the higher side, which will
adversely affect the domestic tourist visits. The Committee recommends that Ministry of
Tourism may take up the issue with the Ministry of Civil Aviation and airlines operators etc.
so that a control over the exorbitant air fares in India can be exercised. (Para: 124)
Cruise Tourism
The Committee is happy to see that its repeated recommendations over the last many
years have made some impact in the cruise tourism sector, as six major ports have now got
the project sanctioned for receiving tourists from foreign countries. The Committee feels that
this will not only contribute to developing tourism in the country, but also help our major
ports to utilize their potential and thereby enhance their revenue generation and employment
opportunities. (Para: 129)
The Committee further observes that as per the data on financial support given during
the last couple of years on cruise tourism, Mumbai, New Mangalore and Tuticorin have not
yet got any sanctions. The Committee recommends that the projects on cruise terminal /
facilities should be provided to Ports on priority basis so that the project would not lag behind
for want of funds. (Para: 130)
Tourism in Lakshadweep Islands
The Committee recommends that Lakshadweep has immense scope for development of
tourism and that the Ministry should extend all support to the Union Territory for this. The
Committee also recommends that if there is any study report available on the development
of tourism in Lakshadweep, an action plan may be worked out and if not, necessary action
may be initiated for the same. (Para: 132)
The Committee notes that small States and Union territories may not have sufficient
funds for a sustained media campaign to attract tourists. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should give special emphasis in their media
campaign to the smaller States and Union territories. (Para: 133)
Buddhist Circuit
The Committee fails to understand why the idea of developing the Buddhist Circuits has
remained neglected all these years, despite its tremendous potential in our country.
Despite that, the country even today attracts about one lakh Buddhist tourists per year.
By developing the Circuit fully, the tourist footfalls is likely to increase to about 5 lakh
tourists and the foreign exchange earning of the country will also grow concomitantly.
(Para: 137)
The Committee is at loss to find that there is no concrete action plan so far; for
developing and integrating all important Buddhist centres in the country. Besides one or two
stray attempt, nothing could be done so far in this regard. A study done by the World Bank
years back, in consultation with stakeholders also has remained confined to Ministries shelf.

51
Even the important Union Ministries- Culture, Tourism, Civil Aviation, Roads and Highways
and the Governments of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar have not reached to any action plan for this
purpose. The Committee is of the opinion that funds may not be an issue for developing
Buddhist Centres, as these can be managed from various sources in view of the potential this
would have. Even neighbouring countries can fund the projects on mutually agreed lines. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry of Tourism must prepare an action plan
after wider consultation and start the process at the earliest, under intimation to this
Committee. (Para: 138)
The Committee notes that the Government is planning for large scale development in
Buddhist Circuits. However, the pace of work is very slow and lot needs to be done. The
Committee recommends that a stringent monitoring mechanism should be put in place to
evaluate the progress of work at each stage alongwith the Ministry of Culture and ASI. The
Committee hopes that such checks and balances will help in achieving the desired results
within the stipulated time period. (Para: 139)
The Committee also recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should take up the issue of
development of Kushinagar airport with Ministry of Civil Aviation as soon as possible.
(Para: 140)
Pollution and Environmental Issues
The Committee observes that tourism development should not adversely affect the nature and
natural growth and living of plants and animals. The beauty, serenity and natural
environment of the tourist spots must be maintained at any cost. The Committee recommends
that Ministry should promote sustainable tourism. The Committee also recommends that
steps should be taken to ensure that the pollution of Dal Lake of Kashmir, Vembanadu Lake
of Kerala etc. should be strictly monitored. (Para: 143)
Safety and Security of the Tourists
The Committee notes the various measures taken for ensuring safety and security of
the tourists in the country. The Committee recommends that:
(i) Along with the help line numbers published, the tourists should have a direct
access to police and such enforcement authorities.
(ii) All the major railway stations, bus stations also must carry tourist help desks
24X7.
(iii) All major tourist centres should display the guidelines and helpline numbers for
the use of tourists.
(iv) It should be made compulsory that on a twice a week basis, each foreign tourist
should report to a nearest information centre about their next place of visit and
the details of accommodation etc. (Para: 147)
ITDC
The Committee notes the progress made by ITDC in the recent years, but feels that the
standard and upkeep of accommodation and food in ITDC hotels leave much to be desired.
Budget hotels are giving serious challenge to its existing hotels. The Committee recommends
that ITDC may explore the possibilities of undertaking tour packages under the LTC package,
as done by the IRCTC. (Para: 151)

52
Transparency Initiatives
The Committee appreciates the initiative of the Ministry and hopes that this would
ensure greater transparency and quick processing of applications. (Para: 153)
The Committee appreciates the following initiatives taken by the Ministry, as stated in
its Annual Report (2014-15) :-
MEDICAL TOURISM
(i) The Ministry revised guidelines for the promotion of Medical Tourism as a Niche
Tourism Product on 21st August, 2014.
(ii) India excels among the several Asian destinations like Singapore, Malaysia and
Thailand that are offering Medical care facilities and promoting Medical Tourism
as there is no waiting time for availing the medical services.
(iii) 'Medical Visa' has been introduced, which can be given for specific purpose to
foreign tourists coming to India for medical treatment.
(iv) During the year 2014-15 (till December, 2014), the Ministry under Market
Development Assistance (MDA) scheme provided a financial assistance of Rs. 20,
25,000/- to the Medical Tourism Service Providers.
Wellness Tourism
(i) During the year 2014-15 (till December, 2014), the Ministry under MDA scheme
provided a financial assistance of Rs. 64,69,535/- to the Service Providers.
(ii) Yoga/Ayur veda/Wellness has been promoted in the past years in the print,
electronic, internet and outdoor medium under this Ministry's "Incredible India
Campaign". (Para: 154)
There are so many ideas/concepts in this vast and multifaceted country and a lot has
to do as mentioned above. The Committee hopes that the efforts being made by the Ministry
would prove to be fruitful in the coming years. (Para: 159)
OTHER OBSERV ATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism may develop a comprehensive
Mobile Application for the use of the tourists, and all the tourism service providers which will
enable them to access all the authenticated data about tourism in India. (Para: 160)
The Committee was informed that the Komagata Maru Memorial in Kolkata Port
remains neglected and has poor maintenance. In view of its great historical importance, the
Committee recommends that Komagata Maru Memorial in Kolkata Port may be developed
suitably to attract tourists by pooling efforts and funds from Ministries of Shipping, Culture
and Tourism in order to respect the sacrifices made by our people. (Para: 161)
The Committee notes that many of the tourists are availing services of roadside dhabas
and eateries, but they are functioning without much help and assistance from the Government
agencies. The Committee recommends that these need to be guided/helped in all possible
manner for making them tourist-friendly, cleaner and hygienic serving local cuisine in local
ambience. They should be incentivized to serve the tourists better. (Para: 162)

53
The Committee noted that the Sindhu Darshan programme is running without much
assistance from the Government. The programme needs to be publicized and tourists may be
provided with better facilities there. The Committee recommends that financial support
should be extended to Sindhu Darshan Programme. (Para: 163)
The Committee recommends that Gogamedi Festival in Rajasthan also may be promoted
under a suitable scheme by the Ministry of Tourism. (Para: 164)
The Committee finds that no transparent formula for pricing of air tickets is available
in the country at present as the air fares being charged by the airlines operating in the
country are on the higher side, which is a deterrent for tourism development in the country.
The Committee notes that there is no rationality or even fixed parameters to be followed for
fixing the airfares. The Committee, therefore, emphasizes the need for fixing a cap on the
lower and maximum air fare to be charged during the peak and non-peak seasons with a view
to bring more transparency in fixation of affordable airfare for the passengers. The
Committee, therefore, desires the Ministry to take up this issue with the Ministry of Civil
Aviation to have a regulatory mechanism for fixing the airfare at affordable level.   (Para: 165)
To conclude, the Committee has noted that its major scheme- the PIDDC is being
implemented by the States who have to follow procedures etc. Had the Ministry of Tourism
been a little proactive, it may have made a lot of improvement in implementing these
projects/programmes. The Committee could not find much problem in respect of availability
of funds, but a proper monitoring and close co-ordination among States for executing the
projects would have done wonders. It would have avoided lapsing of funds in most of the
projects. The Committee hopes that with the devolution of funds to the States and non-
allocation to its Centrally Sponsored Scheme- PIDDC, the excuse of the Ministry of Tourism
that States go slow will no more be there. It is hoped that such schemes will be executed by
the States with greater efficiency and pr omptness now. This will also leave enough time to the
Union Ministry for implementing Central Sector Schemes-older as well as new ones. The
Committee would like to extend its best wishes to the Ministry. The Committee desired that
the Ministry of Tourism should improve its performance in the coming years. (Para: 166)
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should desire strategic road maps for
each scheme/programme and monitor and evaluate them periodically. This would ensure that
bottlenecks are cleared up promptly. (Para: 167)
