INTRODUCTION 
Culture is the focal point around which the identity of a nation and its people gets 
established. Culture comprises the customary beliefs, social forms and material traits of a 
racial, religious or social group. Culture plays a pivotal role in establishing the uniqueness 
of a country especially in these times of globalization. Shared culture acts as a unifying 
factor among the citizens. A well -preserved, unique and vibrant culture of a nation is 
directly linked with its t ourism prospects and thus , frames a key component in the 
developmental agenda of any nation. 
2.  The primary mandate of Ministry of Culture is preservation and conservation of 
ancient cultural heritage and promotion of tangible and intangible art and culture.  Headed 
by Secretary, the administrative set up consists of 5 Bureaus and Divisions. The Ministry 
has two attached offices, six subordinate offices and thirty five autonomous organisations, 
which are fully funded by the Government which include seven Zonal  Cultural Centres. 
There are also four missions viz., the National Mission for Manuscripts, National Mission 
for Monument and Antiquities, National Mission on Libraries, and Gandhi Heritage Sites 
Mission.  
3.  This Ministry work s on the protection, developmen t and promotion of the country‟s 
tangible heritage, intangible heritage and knowledge heritage. In addition, the Ministry 
also harbours the responsibility of nurturing Gandhian heritage and commemoration of 
important historical events and centenaries of gr eat personalities. The Ministry manages 
all the Centrally protected monuments of national importance, through the Archaeological 
Survey of India. It supports museum movement in the country and majority of the 
museums are under its administrative control. T he Ministry also promotes regional 
museums through grant -in-aids. In the field of intangible heritage, the Ministry extends 
financial support to individuals, group of individuals and cultural organizations involved 
with the performing, visual and literary arts. Similarly, the Ministry through its 
organizations, is engaged in recognizing excellence in the field of art and culture by way 
of awards given by institutions like Sahitya Akademi and Sangeet Natak Akademi. The 
National School of Drama is involved in  promoting a vibrant theatre movement of 
contemporary relevance in the context of our traditions and cultural diversity. 
4.  The Ministry is the custodian of all the major libraries in the country. It frames 
policies and provides grants -in-aid for library dev elopment. Through National Archives 
of India, Ministry is responsible for maintenance of all archival records of the country. 
The Ministry protects and promotes Buddhist and Tibetan Culture through institutions 
located at Sarnath, Varanasi and Leh. Capacity building programmes in the field of Indian 
art and culture is promoted through institutions like the School of Archaeology, School of 
Archives, National Museum , Institute of History of Art, Conservation and Museology, 
Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Institute of  Asian Studies. The courses offered by National 
School of Drama and Kalakshetra Foundation are also some of the examples of capacity 
building programmes of the Ministry.

5.  On the international front, the Ministry is responsible for implementation of variou s 
UNESCO Conventions and for entering into Cultural Exchange Agreements with 
partnering countries.

8.  The Committee appreciates the fact  that the Ministry has been able to spend 
about 81% of the funds allocated to it under the Revenue Head till 31 st January, 
2020. However, the Committee is dismayed to note  that the expenditure incurred 
under the Capital Head is merely 6 3.5%. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry may strive to make optimal use of the funds available to it for the FY 2019 -
20 since it has already faced a severe cut at RE stage  and has only limited funds 
available for its schemes/activities for the year 2019-20. 
Sharp reduction at RE 2019-20 stage 
9.  Ministry of Culture has projected a demand of Rs. 4323.61 crore for 2019 -20. 
However, it has been allocated an amount of Rs. 3042.35 crore only. This comprises of 
Rs. 2953.21 crore under Revenue Head and Rs. 89.14 crore under Capital Head. This has 
been further reduced to Rs. 2547.00 crore at RE 2019-20 stage.  
10.  Actual expenditure of the Ministry for 2018 -19 has been Rs. 2602.19 crore, which 
was 92.93% of Rs. 2800.00 crore i.e., RE 2018-19. However, in 2019 -20 at RE stage the 
Ministry has been given reduced  RE of Rs. 2547.00 crore, as compared to allocation of 
Rs. 3042.35 crores for BE 2019 -20. No reasons as such have been attributed for this 
reduction by the Ministry of Finance. The major reduction in the RE was in the scheme 
allocation which got reduced fr om Rs.778.23 crore to Rs.560.00 crore. This reduction 
was probably due to less expenditure in the schemes and due to delayed release of grants 
to the Autonomous Bodies at the time of RE stage.  

4 
 
11.  The Committee sought to know the impact of the reduced RE 2019-20 allocation on 
the activities and schemes of the Ministry. The Ministry, in its written reply, has informed 
the Committee that t he reduction in the allocation affected various activities of the 
organizations of the Ministry which compelled it to re -workout the strategy to undertake 
the activities within the allocated financial resources. The funding of the schemes were 
reprioritized in view of the shortfall of the allocated outlay.  Similarly , Autonomous 
Bodies also reprioritize their activities for the rest of the financial year.  
12.  The Ministry, in its written reply, has informed the Committee that d ue to the paucity 
of funds, construction of Pt. Deendayal Institute of Archaeology at Greater Noida faced 
difficulty. Project was sanctioned in 2016 -17 at a cost of Rs. 289.37 crore. The project 
was to be completed by June 2019. However, due to paucity of funds, a sum of Rs. 85.0 
crore remained to be released to the executing agency namely NBCC during 2018 -19. No 
funds were allocated for the project during  2018-19 at RE stage. The project has so far 
not been completed due to non -payment of funds to NBCC. A sum of Rs. 45.0 crore still 
remains to be released to NBCC. Similarly , the payment to CPWD for construction work 
of Dandi Memorial has also been delayed.  
13.  The Committee observes that the Ministry has been allocated Rs. 2547.00 crore 
at RE 2019 -20 stage as against Rs.  3042.35 crore at B.E. 2019 -20 which is  a huge 
reduction of over 16%. The Committee observes that many of the projects/activities 
carried out by the Ministry of Culture, if postponed to a future date, will cause 
irreparable damage to the cultural heritge of our country. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry may ensure that the available funds are 
utilized in an efficient manner, without rushing expenditure at the end of a Financial 
Year. 
14.  The Committee notes that the work carried out by Archaeological Survey of 
India, being seasonal in nature, can take place only after the monsoon season. The 
Committee recommends that  the Ministry  of Culture  may communicate such 
reasons which cause low expenditure of funds available with the Ministry in the 
initial quarters to the Ministry of Finance with due justification for not reducing the 
allocations of the Ministry at the RE stage in the light of the low expenditure figures 
of the Ministry till that quarter. 
15.  The Committee may be apprised on how the Ministry plans to carry out the 
activities that were initially planned to take place , but later dropped due to 
reduction of fund allocation at RE 2019 -20 and whether any provision ha s been 
made for the same at BE 2020-21.  
16.  The Committee takes cognizance of the difficulties being faced in the 
construction of Pt. Deendayal Institute of Archa eology at Greater Noida. The 
Committee feels that there is  already a dearth of skilled manpower  in the 
Archaeology sector in India and timely completion of the Institute is directly linked 
to the availability of skilled personnel required for conservation and development of 
our ancient monu ments and tangible heritage. The Committee, therefore, 

5 
 
recommends that the payment due to be released to NBCC for construction work of 
the project may be released at the earliest. The Ministry may seek additional funds 
for the completion of the project at RE 2020-21 stage.

budget from the Government of India for various activities like conservation/preservation 
of protected monuments, excavations, exploration etc. ASI meets all expenditure from the 
budget allocation. Though the ASI generates the revenue collec ted through sale of entry 
fee at ticketed monuments/museums and other sources like filming permission, allotting

space for cultural events etc., the same is deposited to the Consolidated Fund of India and 
as such there is no provision to retain earned reve nue by ASI for any purposes. In 
addition, funding is provided by PSU/private organizations for project centric 
requirements through the National Culture Fund. ASI is also getting funds through work 
for conservation/ preservation and development of amenitie s at the monument through 
State Governments/ PSUs/organizations. 
37.  With regard to Adarsh monuments, the Ministry, in its written reply, has stated that 
various facilities like toilet, drinking water,pathways, ramps, wheel chair, cafeteria, 
souvenir shop, braille signages, Wi-fi, sound and light show, interpretation centre, etc are 
being provided at Adarsh monuments. Providing facilities at monuments wherever such 
facilities are not available and upgrading existing facilities is a regular phenomenon 
undertaken by ASI. In the year 2018 -19 and 2019 -20, though no new monuments have 
been included as Adarsh monuments , 52 monuments have been selected for upgradation 
and providing amenities. 
38.  The Committee is of the view that the preservation of our cultural herita ge – 
both tangible and intangible – should be given its due importance since the damage 
caused to the same, due to the lack of funds or otherwise, is often irreversible in 
nature. The Committee,therefore, observes that maintenance of monuments cannot 
be postponed to a later date due to non-availability of funds. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry may request the Government to allocate 
the revenue generated by ASI through sale of entry tickets and other sources, only 
for purpose of maintenance and preservation of the monuments. 
39.  The Committee is distressed to note that no new monument has been included in 
the Adarsh Monuments list in 2018-19 and 2019-20, though the Ministry has selected

decades to provide amenities at all the 3,691Centrally protected monuments.

NATIONAL CULTURE FUND  
131. NCF's primary mandate is to establish and nurture Public Private Partnerships (PPP) 
in the field of Cultural heritage.   NCF's role is to catalyze relationships between private, 
public, government, non -government agencies, private institutions and foundatio ns and 
mobilize resources for the restoration, conservation, protection and development of 
India's rich tangible and intangible cultural heritage. The National Culture Fund is 
managed by a Council and an Executive Committee . The Council has a maximum 
strength of twenty four members, with a maximum of nineteen eminent members 
representing the corporate and public sector, private foundations and nonprofit 
organizations. The Hon‟ble Minister, Culture, is the Chairperson of the Council. The 
Executive Committee is chaired by the Secretary, Ministry of Culture. 

32 
 
132.  NCF receives proposals from reputed agencies like the Archaeological Survey 
of India and State archaeological departments for projects related to monuments, 
monasteries and  other historically important buildings that are in urgent need of 
conservation, but due to lack of funding, their upkeep and maintenance becomes difficult. 
These projects are then taken forward for partnership with various Donors for partnership 
under their CSR activities. The projects received by NCF are evaluated and recommended 
by NCF Sub Committee for Project Selection. NCF presents these projects to the 
Executive Committee of NCF for final approval. Once a project is approved by the 
Executive Committe e, the NCF team enters into detailed negotiations with the 
implementing agency, such as the scope of work, time -frame, deliverables, schedule of 
payments and the duration of the project and A Memorandum of Understanding is signed 
by the donor organization, NCF and the proposer/implementing agency.  
133.  Each project is managed independently by a Project Implementation 
Committee (PIC) that has due representation from the donor/ contributor/ co -
promoter/implementing agencies. The Project Implementation Committee has 
representation from NCF and whenever required civic authorities and the Archaeological 
Survey of India so that proper assessment of work can be done and fund can be released 
accordingly.  A separate joint Bank Account is maintained for each project tha t is 
operated by the representative of NCF and the Donor/Funding Agencies. Detailed 
accounts of each project are incorporated in the NCF Accounts which are audited by the 
Comptroller and Auditor General of Indiaannually. 
134. The Committee notes that the NCF An nual Report has not been 
published/updated on the website since 2014 -15. Similarly, the last completed 
projects was updated on the NCF website in 2017. The Committee observes that the 
website of the NCF being its gateway to potential donors, both individuals as well as 
organizations and therefore, the website should be updated to showcase its activities 
to the visitors in a transparent manner. 
135. The Committee feels that since National Culture Fund considers private/public 
institutions and individuals as equal partners of the Government in the management 
of the cultural heritage of India, efforts should be made by the Ministry to maintain 
transparency in the works executed under the scheme to lend them a sense of equal 
ownership and proper utilization of funds to the private/public institutions and 
individuals who contribute to the NCF account. The Committee  further 
recommends that the Ministry may proactively keep the donor 
organization/individual updated in this regard.

prohibited and regulated areas of Centrally protected monuments for the purpose of construction,

facades, drainage system etc.  After approval of the heritage bye -laws by the Authority, the same
