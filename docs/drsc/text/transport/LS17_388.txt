Introduction 
1. I, the Chairman, Department -related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture, 
having been authorised by the Committee, have the honour  to present on its behalf this Report on the Demands 
for Grants (2026 -27) of the Ministry of Culture (Demand No. 18). The Committee was constituted in 
accordance with the Rules and Procedures governing Parliamentary Standing Committees and authorised by th e 
Hon’ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha, to examine the said Demands for Grants and to present its Report to the 
House. 
2. The Committee has examined the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in view of the central importance that 
the preservation, promotion, and dis semination of India’s tangible and intangible cultural heritage occupies in 
the nation’s civilizational identity and global standing. With a Budget Estimate of Rs. 3,416.63 crore for 2026 -
27 and a Ministry -projected requirement of Rs. 5,219.97 crore, the M inistry of Culture administers a wide -
ranging portfolio encompassing the Archaeological Survey of India, national museums, libraries, akademies, 
zonal cultural centres, Buddhist and Tibetan institutions, and flagship missions including the Gyan Bharatam 
Mission and the National Mission on Cultural Mapping. 
3. The Committee undertook a structured examination of the Demands in accordance with established 
parliamentary practice. The Ministry furnished its Detailed Demands for Grants (Demand No. 18, 2026 -27), and 
budgetary documentations comprising covering the Expenditure Statement, the Output Outcome Monitoring 
Framework (OOMF) for 2026 -27, head -wise financial summaries, and scheme -wise physical and financial 
targets. In addition, the Ministry submitted Writte n Replies to questions put by the Committee. The 
representatives of the Ministry were also heard in oral evidence on 24 February 2026. The Annual Report of the 
Ministry of Culture for the year 2025 -26 was also placed before the Committee and served as an i mportant 
corroborating source of performance information. 
4. The Committee’s examination encompassed five principal thematic areas: the Financial Architecture and 
Budgetary Discipline; Scheme -Wise Performance and Physical Outcomes covering the ASI, Centena ries and 
Anniversaries, Kala Sanskriti Vikas Yojana, Development of Museums, Development of Libraries, Gyan 
Bharatam Mission, Global Engagement, and the National Mission on Cultural Mapping; Historical Expenditure 
Trends and Savings Analysis; Cross -Cutting Governance and Equity; and Audit Compliance and 
Accountability.  
5. This Report synthesises evidence received from the Ministry, the detailed budgetary documentation, oral 
evidence tendered on 24 February 2026, and the Committee’s own analysis of multi -year financial trends. It 
places fiscal prudence, outcome accountability, and equitable regional development at the centre of its 
assessment. The Committee’s recommendations are framed in a spirit of constructive engagement and are 
intended to support the Mi nistry in strengthening the planning, execution, and monitoring of its programmes. 
The Committee expects that the Ministry will give careful and sympathetic consideration to these 
recommendations and that appropriate action taken notes will be communicated  to the Committee in a timely 
manner. 
6. The Committee places on record its sincere appreciation of the assistance rendered by the officers of the 
Rajya Sabha Secretariat in facilitating the examination of the Demands for Grants, and of the cooperation 
extended by the Ministry of Culture in providing detailed documentation, responses to the Committee’s 
questionnaire, and written submissions.

CHAPTER I: BUDGETARY ANALYSIS 
A. Overview of Demands for Grants (2026-27) 
1.1 The Ministry of Culture is the nodal agency for formulation of national policies and programmes for 
the preservation and promotion of India’s cultural heritage. The Ministry oversees the Archaeological 
Survey of India (ASI), National Archives of India, national museums, national academies (Sahitya 
Akademi, Sangeet Natak Akademi, Lalit Kala Akademi), the National School of Drama, Zonal Cultural 
Centres, and 34 Autonomous Bodies, among other institutions and organisations. 
1.2 The Ministry informed the Committee that for the financial year 2026 -27, it has been allocated Rs. 
3,416.63 crore (Demand No.  18) against its projected demand of Rs. 5,219.97 crore, a shortfall of Rs. 
1,803.34 crore (approximately 34.55 per cent). The approved allocation represents an increase of only Rs. 
55.67 crore (1.66 per cent) over the Budget Estimate of 2025 -26. The allocation comprises Revenue 
expenditure of Rs. 3,216.63 crore and Capital expenditure of Rs. 200 crore. 
1.3 The Committee notes that the Ministry’s budgetary structure is institution-centric, with approximately 
87 per cent of the allocation directed towards institutions and only 13 per cent available for central sector 
schemes. The ASI accounts for Rs. 1,235.78 crore (36.17 per cent of the total allocation). 
1.4 The Committee observes that the gap of Rs. 1,803.34 crore between the Ministry’s projected 
demand and the allocation constitutes a constraint on the Ministry’s capacity to fulfil its mandate. 
Given that the Ministry has reported near 100 per  cent expenditure rates and has received 
additional funds in Revised Estimates in the current year, the Committee is of the view that the 
budgetary allocation is not commensurate with the projected demand and the expenditure 
performance demonstrated by the Ministry. The Committee recommends that the Ministry engage 
with the Ministry of Finance to secure enhanced allocations, including through Supplementary 
Demands, and present a detailed justification note linking the additional demand to specific 
programme outcomes and deliverables. 
B. Budgetary Trends and Fund Utilisation 
1.5 The Committee notes that the Ministry has reported near 100 per cent expenditure rates in the current 
financial year and has received additional funds at the Revised Estimate stage. 
1.6 The Committee notes from the Detailed Demands for Grants that the Capital expenditure head has a 
Budget Estimate of Rs. 200 crore for 2026 -27. The actual Capital expenditure in 2025 -26 stood at Rs. 
66.84 crore (up to 19 January 2026), representing a ut ilisation rate of approximately 33.4 per cent. The 
Committee observes that this level of Capital expenditure is a matter of concern. 
1.7 The Committee recommends that the Ministry prepare a detailed Capital expenditure plan for 
2026-27 with quarterly milestones and furnish quarterly progress reports to the Committee. 

2 
 
C. Ticketing Revenue 
1.8 The Committee further notes that the Ministry stated that ticketing revenue from centrally protected 
monuments amounts to approximately Rs. 365 crore annually. This rev enue goes to the Government 
exchequer and is not available to the Ministry or the ASI as departmental revenue for reinvestment in 
conservation and maintenance activities. 
1.9 The Committee recommends that the Ministry, in consultation with the Ministry of Finance, 
earmark a portion of the ticketing revenue of Rs. 365 crore annually for reinvestment in the 
conservation, maintenance, and visitor infrastructure of centrally protected monuments. The 
Ministry may present a detailed proposal for such a ring -fenced revenue mechanism to the 
Committee within six months. 
D. Shortfall in Projected Demand 
1.10 The Committee repeatedly emphasised during the evidence that the budget is insufficient given the 
scope of the Ministry’s work. The Museums Development scheme and  the proposed new infrastructure 
scheme under the 16th Finance Commission cycle require substantially higher allocations. NITI Aayog 
has set annual targets of 8 new museums and 15 new public libraries. The Committee observed that these 
targets cannot be achieved with current funding levels. 
1.11 The Committee recommends that the Ministry prepare a demand note for the 16th Finance 
Commission cycle, clearly linking budgetary requirements to the NITI Aayog targets for museums 
and libraries (as noted in Para 1. 10), the proposed infrastructure scheme, and the institutional 
capacity requirements of autonomous bodies and subordinate organisations. The demand note 
should include a phased expenditure plan, institutional capacity assessments, and a strategy for 
leveraging additional resources including CSR funds, the National Culture Fund, and PPP models. 
E. Alternative Revenue and Funding Sources 
1.12 The Committee observed during the evidence that Rs. 35,000 crore in CSR funds are available 
nationally, while the Mini stry’s budget is approximately Rs. 3,416.63 crore. The Committee suggested 
that the Ministry should actively tap CSR funds for museums and libraries in each district. The 
Committee notes that under Schedule VII of the Companies Act, 2013, protection of national heritage, art 
and culture, including restoration of buildings and sites of historical importance, is already an eligible 
category for CSR expenditure. In FY 2022 -23, corporate spending on heritage, art and culture stood at 
approximately Rs. 441 crore  nationally, indicating significant headroom for enhanced engagement 
between the Ministry and the corporate sector. 
1.13 The Committee further suggested that cultural heritage should be included as an eligible category for 
Members of Parliament Local Area Development (MPLAD) fund expenditure. The Committee cited the 
example of earning Rs. 18 crore from a planetarium developed under the Smart City project. 
1.14 The Committee recommends that the Ministry develop a strategy for leveraging alternative 
funding s ources, including: (a) a dedicated CSR engagement plan for museums, libraries, and 
heritage conservation, with clear guidelines for corporate participation and model project 
frameworks to facilitate adoption by eligible companies; (b) a proposal to the Min istry of Finance 

3 
 
and Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation for inclusion of cultural heritage as an 
eligible expenditure category under the MPLAD scheme; and (c) expansion of the restructured 
National Culture Fund model to attract private don ations for conservation. The Ministry may 
furnish this strategy to the Committee within six months. 
F. Grant-in-Aid Salary Reduction 
1.15 The Committee notes that the GIA Salary head has been reduced from Rs. 429 crore (BE 2025-26) to 
Rs. 352 crore (BE 202 6-27), a reduction of Rs. 77 crore (17.95 per cent). All 34 Autonomous Bodies 
under the Ministry remain entirely dependent on Government grants-in-aid for salary expenditure. 
1.16 The Committee is concerned at the reduction of Rs. 77 crore in the GIA Salar y head for 
Autonomous Bodies at a time when these bodies already face significant vacancy levels. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish to the Committee: (a) a body -wise break-up of 
the GIA Salary allocation for 2026-27, with the rationale for the reduction; (b) the current vacancy 
position in each Autonomous Body; and (c) an assessment of the impact of the reduction on the 
functioning of these bodies. The Committee would like to be apprised of the action taken in this 
regard. 
G. Mandatory Sub-Allocations: SCSP, TSP, and NER 
1.17 The Committee notes that the mandatory sub -allocations for 2026-27 include Rs. 49.50 crore under 
the Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan (8.3 per cent of schemeable BE), Rs. 25.65 crore under the Tribal Sub-Plan 
(4.3 per cent), an d Rs. 59.64 crore for the North -East Region (10.0 per cent), totalling Rs. 134.79 crore 
(22.6 per cent of schemeable BE). 
1.18 The Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish a scheme -wise and State-wise break-up 
of expenditure incurred under the SCSP, TSP, and NER mandatory sub -allocations for 2024 -25 
and 2025 -26 (up to date), along with the physical outcomes achieved under each allocation. The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry ensure that these earmarked funds are utilised 
for the intended beneficiary categories and not diverted to general expenditure. The Ministry may 
furnish utilisation reports within three months.

CHAPTER II: SCHEME-WISE ANALYSIS 
A. Restructured Schemes for the 16th Finance Commission Cycle 
2.1 The Ministry informed the Committee that it is restructuring from 7 existing programmes to 6 new 
schemes for the 16th Finance Commission period (2026-31): 
(a) Centenaries and Anniversaries (with infrastructure component removed). 
(b) Kala Sanskriti Vikas Yojana (expanded scope). 
(c) Gyan Bharatam Mission (continuation with existing 5-year allocation). 
(d) Expanded Global Engagement Scheme, now incorporating Project Mausam (for multinational 
UNESCO nominations) and Project Bharat Bharat (focus on multinational heritage inscriptions). 
(e) New Infrastructure Scheme: Development and Enhancement of Sanskrit Sampada and Heritage, 
comprising seven elements: (i) 15 excavation and heritage site development; (ii) new science 
cities and centres; (iii) new public libraries; (iv) new museu ms and enhancement of existing 
facilities; (v) protection of architectural heritage (100 -200 year old facades); (vi) creative 
economy component for artisan and artist market opportunities; and (vii) YugeYugeen Bharat 
Museum. 
(f) YugeYugeen Bharat Museum as a standalone project. 
2.2 The Ministry informed the Committee that the proposed infrastructure scheme involves a proposed 
investment of Rs. 12,000 crore and above over five years. The Centre -State cost-sharing formula was not 
specified in the documents examin ed by the Committee. If Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) 
approvals are obtained, scheme funding would increase from the current Rs. 569 crore to approximately 
Rs. 2,000 crore. 
2.3 The Committee notes that the proposed infrastructure scheme uses a centra lly-sponsored format 
requiring State participation and ownership for sustainability. The Ministry stated that it has written to all 
State Governments seeking support. 
2.4 The Committee recommends that all EFC notes for new schemes under the 16th Finance 
Commission cycle be accompanied by: (a) detailed implementation roadmaps with year -wise 
milestones; (b) institutional capacity assessments, given the current staffing shortages across the 
Ministry and its subordinate organisations; (c) State -wise absorption plans; (d) a phased scaling 
mechanism linked to demonstrated expenditure performance; and (e) a clear framework for 
recurring operational costs, specifying the Centre -State cost -sharing formula and sustainability 
provisions beyond the scheme period. The Co mmittee further recommends that the Ministry 
present the draft EFC notes to the Committee before final submission. 
2.5 The Committee further recommends that the Ministry notify a performance -linked Centre -
State cost-sharing framework for the proposed herit age infrastructure scheme at the outset, with 
funding tranches to States tied to measurable outcomes such as conservation quality indices, visitor 
footfall growth, local artisan and craftsperson employment generation, and annual site 
maintenance compliance . The experience of outcome -based funding models in comparable 
centrally-sponsored schemes suggests that tying financial flows to demonstrated results promotes 

5 
 
both accountability and long -term sustainability. The Ministry may apprise the Committee of the 
framework so devised. 
B. Centenaries and Anniversaries 
2.6 The Ministry informed the Committee of the current commemoration programmes: (a) 150 years of 
Vande Mataram (continuing till November); (b) Lokmanya Ahilyabai (concluded); (c) Dr. Shyama Prasad 
Mukherjee 125th birthday; (d) Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji 350 years of martyrdom (ongoing); and (e) Acharya 
Vidyanand (Jain Muni, concluded). 
2.7 The Committee notes from the financial data that the Centenaries and Anniversaries scheme has 
exhibited pronounced inter-year volatility: Rs. 106.13 crore (actual 2024 -25), Rs. 35 crore (BE 2025 -26), 
and Rs. 98 crore (BE 2026 -27). The OOMF targets for 2026 -27 include 200 organisations, 230 events, 
and 5,00,000 footfall. 
2.8 The Committee recommends that the Ministry deve lop a standardised impact assessment 
framework for all national commemoration programmes, quantifying outcomes in terms of public 
participation levels, geographical spread of engagement, educational outreach conducted, digital 
and media reach, and measurab le citizen awareness generation. Each commemoration programme 
may be accompanied by a post -completion impact report to enable evidence -based evaluation of 
programme effectiveness and to inform the planning of future com memorations. The Committee 
expects to be apprised of the framework so developed. 
C. Kala Sanskriti Vikas Yojana (KSVY) 
2.9 The Committee notes that the KSVY’s BE for 2025 -26 was enhanced from Rs. 198.50 crore to Rs. 
375 crore at the RE stage to address pending liabilities. The BE for 2026-27 has been set at Rs. 150 crore. 
The Committee observes that the RE enhancement of Rs. 176.50 crore to address pending liabilities 
indicates a structural problem in liability management under the scheme. 
2.10 The Ministry informed the Committee of a new init iative at the Lalit Kala Akademi, where the 64th 
National Exhibition of Art received 5,900 entries, from which the jury selected 283 for exhibition. Under 
a new policy, all exhibited artworks were priced with the artists’ consent and included in the catalogue for 
purchase. The Ministry stated that 64 pieces were sold, with 90 per cent of proceeds going to the artists 
and 10 per cent to the artists’ welfare fund. Over Rs. 1.5 crore was raised for artists. 
2.11 The Committee notes with appreciation the market -access model adopted by the Lalit Kala 
Akademi and recommends that this model be replicated across all national academies and Zonal 
Cultural Centres. The Committee recommends that the Ministry formulate guidelines for 
integrating commercial viability and market access into exhibition and performance programmes, 
while ensuring that the primary mandate of the academies is not diluted. The Committee further 
recommends that the Ministry examine the feasibility of dedicating a small percentage of proceeds 
from all Akademi-facilitated art exhibitions and cultural sales to a Heritage Conservation Corpus, 
thereby creating a sustainable link between contemporary artistic activity and the preservation of 
India’s cultural patrimony. The Committee further recommends th at the Ministry furnish a note 
on the pending liabilities under the KSVY, the measures taken to prevent their recurrence, and the  

6 
 
adequacy of the Rs. 150 crore allocation for 2026 -27. The Committee would like to be apprised of 
the action taken in this regard. 
D. Gyan Bharatam Mission 
2.12 The Ministry informed the Committee that the Gyan Bharatam Mission continues with its existing 5-
year allocation of Rs. 60 crore (BE 2026-27). The Committee notes from the financial data that the actual 
expenditure in 2025-26 stood at Rs. 5.53 crore (up to 19 January 2026), representing a utilisation rate of 
approximately 9.2 per cent. In 2024 -25, actual expenditure was Rs. 2.63 crore. The OOMF targets for 
2026-27 include 25 lakh manuscripts surveyed, 15 crore pages digitised, and 5,000 persons trained. 
2.13 The Committee is concerned at the persistent near -zero expenditure under the Gyan 
Bharatam Mission over successive years against a BE of Rs. 60 crore. The Committee recommends 
that the Ministry furnish: (a) a detailed explanation for the underutilisation of funds in 2024-25 and 
2025-26; (b) the institutional readiness assessment for absorbing the Rs. 60 crore allocation in 2026-
27; and (c) a quarterly expenditure plan with milestones linked to the OOMF targets. The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry consider adopting a milestone -linked fund 
release mechanism for the Mission, whereby subsequent quarterly tranches are released upon 
demonstrated achievement of the preceding quarter’s physical and financial targets , as reported 
through the Public Financial Management System (PFMS). This approach would incentivise timely 
execution and strengthen expenditure accountability. The Committee would like to be apprised of 
the action taken in this regard. 
E. Global Engagement 
2.14 The Committee notes that the Global Engagement scheme recorded actual expenditure of Rs. 1.18 
crore against a BE of Rs. 4.65 crore in 2025 -26 (up to 19 January 2026), a utilisation rate of 
approximately 25.4 per cent. The BE for 2026 -27 has been set  at Rs. 30 crore, an increase of more than 
six-fold. 
2.15 The Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish a detailed justification for the six -fold 
increase in the Global Engagement allocation from Rs. 4.65 crore to Rs. 30 crore, along with a 
scheme-wise activity plan and the absorptive capacity assessment. Given the utilisation rate of 25.4 
per cent in 2025 -26, the Committee expects the Ministry to demonstrate that institutional 
arrangements are in place to utilise the enhanced allocation productively. The Committee further 
emphasises that any substantial enhancement in allocation ought to be preceded by evidence of 
improved delivery capacity, and recommends that future funding under this scheme be linked to 
measurable international outcomes, including t he number of bilateral cultural agreements 
operationalised, joint exhibitions and exchange programmes conducted, and multinational 
nomination dossiers advanced. The Ministry may furnish this information within 90 days. 
F. Development of Libraries 
2.16 The Committee notes that the Development of Libraries scheme recorded utilisation of 
approximately 18 per cent in 2023 -24 (Rs. 4.69 crore against Rs. 26.02 crore). In 2025 -26, actual 
expenditure stood at Rs. 22.75 crore against a BE of Rs. 53.19 crore (up to 1 9 January 2026). The BE for 
2026-27 is Rs. 50 crore.

2.17 The Committee is concerned at the chronic underutilisation under the Development of 
Libraries scheme. The Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish: (a) an explanation for the 
18 per cent utili sation in 2023 -24; (b) the specific constraints that have impeded expenditure; and 
(c) a corrective action plan for achieving full utilisation in 2026 -27. The Committee further 
recommends that the Ministry align its expenditure plan with the NITI Aayog tar get of 15 new

G. National Mission on Cultural Mapping 
2.18 The Committee notes that the National Mission on Cultural Mapping recorded near-zero expenditure 
of Rs. 0.02 crore against a Revised Estimate of Rs. 1.50 crore in 2025 -26. The BE for 2026 -27 has been 
set at Rs. 58.39 crore. 
2.19 The Committee is concerned  at the near -zero expenditure under the National Mission on 
Cultural Mapping in 2025 -26 and the proposed increase to Rs. 58.39 crore in 2026 -27. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish: (a) reasons for the expenditure failure in 2025 -
26; (b) the  status of the scheme’s institutional infrastructure and implementation machinery; and 
(c) a detailed quarter -wise expenditure plan for 2026 -27 with measurable milestones. The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry constitute a dedicated Mission Im plementation 
Unit for the National Mission on Cultural Mapping, staffed with adequate technical, data 
management, and field coordination personnel, to provide focused institutional capacity for the 
Mission’s execution. The experience of dedicated implement ation structures in comparable 
national-level digital missions may serve as useful organisational models. The Committee further 
recommends that the Ministry undertake a mid -year review to assess the progress of 
implementation and expenditure under the sche me and take necessary corrective measures, if 
required.

CHAPTER III: ARCHAEOLOGICAL HERITAGE AND MONUMENT 
CONSERVATION 
A. Archaeological Survey of India: Overview 
3.1 The Committee notes that the ASI maintains 3,685 centrally protected monuments through 38 Circles. 
The ASI has been allocated Rs. 1,235.78 crore for 2026 -27, constituting 36.17 per cent of the Ministry’s 
total allocation. During 2025 -26, ‘Maratha Military  Landscapes of India’ was inscribed as a World 
Heritage Site, and 14 sites were added to India’s UNESCO Tentative List, bringing the total to 69. India 
now ranks 6th globally for World Heritage Sites. 
3.2 The Committee notes from the financial data that AS I conservation expenditure has maintained 
utilisation rates above 99 per cent over the period 2021 -22 to 2024 -25. In 2025 -26, the utilisation rate 
stood at 67.17 per cent (Rs. 232.52 crore against Rs. 346.15 crore, up to 21 November 2025). 
3.3 The Committee notes the sustained high utilisation rates in ASI conservation expenditure over 
2021-22 to 2024-25 and recommends that the Ministry ensure that the utilisation rate for 2025 -26 is 
brought to the level of previous years by the close of the financial year.  The Ministry may furnish 
the final utilisation figures for 2025-26 within 90 days of the close of the financial year. 
3.4 The Committee further notes that India’s growing portfolio of World Heritage Sites, now 
ranking sixth globally with 44 inscribed properties, carries with it a corresponding obligation under 
the UNESCO Operational Guidelines to maintain standards of conservation, interpretation, and 
visitor management commensurate with the Outstanding Universal Value of these sites. With the 
inscription of the Maratha Military Landscapes in 2025, India’s international visibility in the 
heritage domain has been further enhanced. The Committee recommends that the Ministry, in 
coordination with the ASI, undertake a comprehensive review of maintenance standards and visitor 
facilities at all World Heritage Sites and prepare a time -bound upgradation plan to ensure that the 
condition, interpretation infrastructure, and management of these sites are aligned with their 
global recognition. The Committee expects to be apprised of the action taken in this regard. 
B. Sites and Excavations 
3.5 The Ministry informed the Committee that the development of 15 significant archaeological sites has 
been announced, including Rakhigarhi, Dholavira, Hastinapura, Adichanallur, and Shivsagar, with visitor 
infrastructure and interpretation centres. 
3.6 The Ministry stated that the excavation process is archaeologist-driven. Under the new initiative, sites 
will be developed for visitor access, a departure from the traditional practice of closing excavated sites. 
3.7 The Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish a detailed project -wise status report for 
all 15 archaeological site development projects, including physical and financial progress, timelines 
for completion, and provisions for ongoing conservation and visitor management, within 90 days. 
C. Underwater Archaeology 
3.8 The Ministry stated that underwater archaeology offers significant scope for expansion. The Ministry 
informed the Committee that it has planned 8 -10 expedition s in the coming financial year through 

9 
 
collaborations with the National Institute of Oceanography, the Indian Navy, the Indian Coast Guard, and 
international groups, including a Danish collaboration for a 17th century shipwreck off the Tamil Nadu 
coast. The Ministry stated that the division is being strengthened. 
3.9 The Committee recommends that the Ministry present an action plan for underwater 
archaeology to the Committee within 90 days, including: (a) the proposed expeditions with 
timelines and budgetar y provisions; (b) the institutional strengthening plan for the underwater 
archaeology division; (c) international collaboration agreements; and (d) a strategy for display of 
findings. The Committee further recommends that all findings from underwater archa eological 
expeditions be systematically documented and made accessible through a dedicated digital 
repository, thereby preserving and disseminating the knowledge generated from these explorations 
for research and public awareness. The Committee also recomm ends that the Ministry explore 
structured collaboration with the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways for logistical and 
maritime support in underwater heritage survey and recovery operations. 
D. Monument Protection and Maintenance 
3.10 The Committee raised concerns during the evidence about ASI -protected sites lacking protectors. 
The Committee noted that monuments are being used as relieving spots due to the absence of staff 
presence. The staffing implications are discussed in detail in Chapter VII. 
3.11 The Ministry informed the Committee that it denotified 18 sites during the current year after careful 
survey, while notifying 15 -16 new sites. The Ministry stated that the denotification  process is conducted 
carefully to prevent manipulation by real estate developers. 
3.12 The Committee raised the specific issue of British -era horse monuments in various parts of the 
country where the ASI’s restrictions prevent repair of a school located w ithin 100 metres of the protected 
site. 
3.13 The Ministry informed the Committee that the AMASR Act amendment has been prepared for 
Parliament. The Ministry stated that the new rules will make the 100 -metre and 200 -metre buffer zone 
limits flexible. 
3.14 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry ensure expeditious passage of the AMASR 
Act amendment, with particular attention to the flexible buffer zone provisions; (b) the ASI 
formulate clear Standard Operating Procedures for denotification decisions, with a defined 
timeline not exceeding 90 days from receipt of application to final decision; (c) the Ministry 
establish a time -bound mechanism for responding to denotification requests, particularly where 
essential public infrastructure such as schools and  hospitals is affected; and (d) the staffing 
measures recommended in Chapter VII be implemented on priority for ticketed monuments. The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry actively explore models of structured community 
participation in the cons ervation and upkeep of protected monuments, particularly in areas where 
ASI staffing levels remain below sanctioned strength. Drawing upon the experience of the ‘Adopt a 
Heritage’ initiative and community -based conservation models, the Ministry may develop  
guidelines for engaging local communities, heritage volunteers, and self-help groups in the custodial 
care and routine maintenance of monuments, with appropriate training and supervision 
frameworks. The Ministry may report on each of these measures within 90 days.

the souvenir shop programme, including revenues generated, artisan participation, and a timeline 
for reaching the target of 200 ticketed monuments. 
F. National Culture Fund Restructuring

Donors can select an empanelled archi tect, choose a monument, and execute conservation under ASI 
supervision. 
3.18 The Committee recommends that the Ministry set measurable annual targets for the 
restructured National Culture Fund, including: (a) the number of monuments covered; (b) the total 
investment mobilised from corporate and private donors; and (c) a quality assurance framework to 
ensure conservation standards are maintained. The Ministry may report progress annually. 
G. Red Fort Complex 
3.19 The Ministry informed the Committee of the c uration of the Red Fort complex with new museums 
and galleries. The Ministry stated that all barracks are now curated and the complex functions as a 
cultural hub. (A detailed list of galleries is at Annexure-I.) 
3.20 The Committee notes with appreciation the curation of the Red Fort complex and recommends 
that the Ministry document this model and examine the feasibility of replicating it at other major 
heritage sites.

CHAPTER IV: MUSEUMS AND LIBRARIES 
A. Museum Development 
4.1 The Ministry informed the Committee during the evidence that the Museums Development scheme 
had a BE of Rs. 210 crore  which was revised downward to Rs. 37 crore. The Committee notes that the 
OOMF and Detailed Demands for Grants indicate a BE of Rs. 150 crore for 2026-27 for the Development 
of Museums head, with OOMF targets of 8 museum projects and 3 cultural complexes. The Committee 
notes a discrepancy between the figures stated during oral evidence and those in the budgetary 
documentation and suggests the Ministry to clarify the same. 
4.2 The Committee repeatedly emphasised the need for museums and libraries in each district nationwide. 
The Committee noted the ability to donate 30,000 to 40,000 square feet of land for an art, craft, and 
culture centre. 
4.3 The Ministry informed the Committee that science museums and planetariums are largely self -
sufficient in generating revenue, but pure cultural museums require Government grants for sustenance. 
4.4 The Committee is concerned at the inadequacy of the Museums Development allocation against 
the NITI Aayog target of 8 new museums annually, as noted in Para 1.10. The Committe e 
recommends that the Ministry: (a) clarify the discrepancy between the Rs. 210 crore and Rs. 37 
crore figures stated during oral evidence and the Rs. 150 crore figure in the Detailed Demands for 
Grants; (b) secure restoration of adequate allocation throug h a Supplementary Demand if 
warranted; and (c) prepare a district -wise museum development plan, prioritising districts that 
currently lack any museum facility. 
B. YugeYugeen Bharat Museum 
4.5 The Ministry informed the Committee of the proposal to convert t he North and South Blocks into the 
YugeYugeen Bharat Museum. 
4.6 The Ministry stated that the Light and Lotus exhibition at Qila Rai Pithora, showcasing the recovered 
Pipurva relics, serves as a precursor for the quality standards to be maintained at the Y ugeYugeen Bharat 
Museum. 
4.7 The Committee recommends that the Ministry furnish a detailed project plan for the 
YugeYugeen Bharat Museum, including timelines, budgetary requirements, institutional 
arrangements for curatorial and administrative management, and the strategy for sourcing 
collections. 
C. Libraries and Archives 
4.8 The Ministry informed the Committee of a major National Archives digitisation project targeting 30 
crore pages. The Ministry stated that 11 crore pages (approximately one -third) have been digitised and 
uploaded on the Abhilek portal. The Ministry stated that it plans to complete the remaining two -thirds in 
the next year. 

13 
 
4.9 The Ministry informed the Committee that it is taking the digitisation initiative to States through a 
new project with funding support. 
4.10 During the evidence, the Committee raised concerns regarding the Khuda Bakhsh Oriental Library, 
Patna, where the Director’s post has been vacant since August 2023 despite the submission of panels for 
appointment. 
4.11 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry set a defined timeline for the completion of 
the National Archives digitisation project and furnish quarterly progress reports to the Committee; 
(b) the Ministry develop Standard Operating Procedures for State archive  coordination, including 
quality standards for digitisation and conservation, to be issued as formal guidelines to all State 
Governments; (c) the Ministry fill the vacancy of Director, Khuda Bakhsh Oriental Library, within 
90 days and furnish a report on t he reasons for the delay; and (d) the underutilisation of the 
Development of Libraries scheme, as noted in Para 2.17, be addressed with urgency. The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry develop a strategy for the meaningful utilisation 
of digitised archival content, ensuring that the digitisation effort translates into enhanced access for 
researchers, students, and the general public. The Ministry may explore integration of digitised 
collections with educational platforms and the National Digital Library ecosystem, in coordination 
with the Ministry of Education, to maximise the scholarly and public value of the National 
Archives’ digital holdings.

CHAPTER V:  
REPATRIATION OF ANTIQUITIES AND INTERNATIONAL CULTURAL 
ENGAGEMENT 
A. Repatriation of Antiquities 
5.1 The Ministry informed the Committee of the recovery of the Piprahwa relics from Hong Kong after 
127 years. The Ministry stated that the relics, originally excavated in 1898, were brought to India on 30 
July, 2025 and the exhibition was inaugurated on 3 January, 2026. 
5.2 The Ministry further informed the Committee during the oral evidence that the United States has 
repatriated many objects in recent years, and the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, and Germany are 
expected to repatriate 8-10 more objects in the next two months. 
5.3 The Committee recommends that the Ministry: (a) prepare and publish a repatriation 
roadmap, including a prioritised international inventory of Indian antiquities in foreign collections, 
legal strategies, and bilateral engagement plans; (b) develop a self -surrender portal for voluntary 
return of heritage items; and (c) furnish to the Committee a status report on all ongoing 
repatriation cases within 90 days. The Committee notes with appreciation the rec overy of the 
Piprahwa relics. 
B. UNESCO Inscriptions and Nominations 
5.4 The Ministry informed the Committee of the following UNESCO inscriptions achieved in 2024: (a) 
Diwali inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage representative list; (b) 12 forts listed as 
World Heritage Sites under “17th to 19th century forts of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj”; (c) Lucknow 
listed under the Creative Cities Network; and (d) the Natya Shastra and Bhagavad Gita manuscripts 
inscribed in the Memory of the World register. 
5.5 The Ministry informed the Committee that nominations in progress include: (a) Chhath Mahaparv as a 
multinational nomination; (b) shadow puppetry; and (c) Valmiki Ramayana manuscript. 
5.6 The Committee recommends that the Ministry prepare a five -year UNESCO nomination 
strategy, including: (a) a pipeline of at least 10 potential nominations; (b) a structured engagement 
plan with partner countries for multinational nominations; and (c) dedicated staff and budgetary 
provision for preparing nomination d ossiers. The Ministry may present this strategy within one 
year. 
C. International Conservation and Cultural Diplomacy 
5.7 The Ministry informed the Committee that the ASI undertakes international conservation work with 
Ministry of External Affairs funding in Cambodia, Myanmar, Maldives, and Mongolia. 
5.8 The Ministry informed the Committee of India’s participation in the Venice Biennale after years of 
absence, under a public-private partnership, with five emerging artists representing regional diversity. 

15 
 
5.9 The Committee recommends that the Ministry in coordination with the Ministry of External 
Affairs develop a structured international cultural diplomacy framework, formalising partnerships 
with major international museums and institutions. 
D. Buddhist Heritage Initiatives 
5.10 The Ministry informed the Committee that the Government has been taking Lord Buddha’s relics to 
other countries. The Ministry stated that recent tours include Vietnam, Russia (Kalmykia province), 
Bhutan, and Sri Lanka. 
5.11 The Committee recommends that the Ministry prepare a Buddhist Heritage Action Plan, 
integrating relic tours, monastery preservation, and Buddhist circuit development (in coordination 
with the Ministry of Tourism), with measurable targets.

CHAPTER VI:  
DIGITAL INITIATIVES AND AI INTEGRATION 
A. AI-Enabled Unified Search Tool 
6.1 The Ministry informed the Committee that it has approximately 60 portals with no unified search 
capability. The Ministry stated that it is engaging an agency to build an AI tool connecting all portals for 
searchability, expected to launch in the first quarter of 2026-27. 
6.2 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry ensure the AI -enabled unified search tool is 
launched within the first quarter of 2026 -27 as committed and furnish a progress report; (b) the 
tool incorporate multilingual capability in a t least 10 Indian languages and English; and (c) the 
Ministry integrate the AI tool with social media platforms for wider public engagement. 
B. Mera Gaon Mera Dharohar (National Cultural Mapping) 
6.3 The Ministry informed the Committee that the MGMD portal has received inputs from villages across 
the country, with 50-60 per cent response from some States and 4 lakh and above inputs being updated. 
6.4 The Committee noted that data is not up to date and many villages only show a name without details. 
The Committee observed that mapping 6 lakh villages requires nodal districts and States for coordination. 
6.5 The Ministry informed the Committee that the portal will be opened for crowdsourcing. 
6.6 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry establish a nodal officer or agency at the 
district level for MGMD data collection and quality assurance; (b) a minimum data standard be 
defined for each village entry; (c) the crowdsourcing module include a verification mechanism; and 
(d) the Ministry set annual targets for MGMD coverage and quality, with a progress report 
furnished every six months. The Committee further recommends that both the AI -enabled unified 
search tool and the MGMD cultural mappin g portal incorporate user feedback mechanisms and a 
structured data correction feature, enabling citizens, local researchers, and cultural practitioners to 
flag errors, suggest corrections, and contribute verified local knowledge. Such participatory quality 
assurance would enhance the reliability and comprehensiveness of the cultural databases. The 
Committee further notes the near -zero expenditure under the National Mission on Cultural 
Mapping in 2025 -26 as discussed in Para 2.19 and expects the Ministry to  ensure that the 
institutional machinery for this Mission is fully operational in 2026-27.

CHAPTER VII:  
MANPOWER AND STAFFING 
7.1 The Committee notes with concern that all departments within the Ministry of Culture are 
experiencing severe staffing sho rtages. The Committee noted during the evidence that ASI sites 
designated as “protected areas” have no protectors present. Inadequate staffing directly impacts 
conservation, maintenance, visitor experience, and revenue collection. 
7.2 The Committee notes f rom the CAG Report No.  3 of 2024 that the Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Manav 
Sangrahalaya (IGRMS), Bhopal, has a vacancy level of 30.51 per cent and that only 26 per cent of its 
objects are displayed. The Committee views this as indicative of a wider institutio nal capacity deficit 
across the Ministry’s organisations. 
7.3 During the evidence, the Committee raised the concern that they had been unable to get an 
appointment with the Ministry since April. The Ministry apologised during the evidence and committed 
to ensuring it does not recur. 
7.4 The Committee recommends that the Ministry: (a) prepare a time -bound recruitment plan for 
all vacant posts across the ASI, national academies, Zonal Cultural Centres, museums, and all 
subordinate organisations; (b) ensure th at every centrally protected monument has minimum 
designated staff for protection, maintenance, and visitor management; (c) institute a standard 
protocol for ensuring accessibility of senior Ministry officials to Members of Parliament; and (d) 
furnish a specific action plan for addressing the 30.51 per cent vacancy at the IGRMS, Bhopal, and 
for increasing the proportion of objects on display from the current 26 per cent.

CHAPTER VIII:  
NORTH-EASTERN REGION 
 
8.1 The Committee emphasised during the evidence that the North East Zonal Cultural Centre (NEZCC) 
should undertake tangible documentation work, rather than confining itself to event participation. The 
Committee noted that the current programme showed 6 of 9 activities as merely “par ticipating” in 
festivals elsewhere. 
8.2 The Committee notes that the North -Eastern region has oral traditions rather than manuscripts, 
requiring specialised documentation methods. 
8.3 The Ministry informed the Committee that it would provide technical supp ort for developing unique 
scripts for 26 tribes in Arunachal Pradesh, rather than using Roman letters. The Santhal community’s 
Olchiki script was cited as a model. 
8.4 The Committee notes that not a single NEZCC direction meeting has been held recently and  that 
State-level meetings have not been held for two years. 
8.5 The Committee recommends that: (a) the NEZCC be directed to shift its focus from event 
participation to substantive documentation of oral traditions, folk arts, and tribal heritage, with 
measurable annual documentation targets; (b) the Ministry pursue the development of unique 
scripts for Arunachal Pradesh tribes as a priority initiative, with a status report within one year; (c) 
the Ministry coordinate with North -Eastern State Governments to resolve the recurring cost 
impasse for non -functional cultural centres; (d) NEZCC direction meetings be resumed 
immediately, with at least two meetings per year; and (e) the Ministry develop a dedicated 
Northeast cultural heritage documentation programme w ithin the new infrastructure scheme 
under the 16th Finance Commission cycle.

CHAPTER IX:  
INSTITUTIONAL AND GOVERNANCE ISSUES 
 
A. State Coordination 
9.1 The Committee noted that State archaeological sites are often poorly maintained, with contractor -
driven repairs that alter the original character of structures. The Committee called for SOPs for 
intervention. The need for a formal Centre -State coordination mechanism for archives and heritage was 
also emphasised. The Committee noted that Zonal Cultural Centres typically cover 8 States but 
coordination is weak. 
9.2 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry develop and issue Standard Operating 
Procedures for conservation intervention at State -level archaeological sites; (b) a formal 
mechanism for Centre-State coordination on archives and heritage preservation be established; and 
(c) the functioning and coordination of Zonal Cultural Centres be reviewed, with findings 
presented to the Committee within 90 days. 
B. Heritage Preservation Beyond ASI Jurisdiction 
9.3 The Committee raised the issue of British -era heritage sites requiring preservation. Specific examples 
cited included: (a) 74 district Collectorat e buildings being demolished; (b) the DG post building in 
Darbhanga (150 years old); and (c) the Patna clock tower. The Committee also noted the need for 
preservation of churches and chapels in Goa and nationwide. 
9.4 The Committee raised concerns about un manned forts, including Sasaram Fort (Sher Shah Suri) in 
Bihar. 
9.5 The Committee further suggested coordination with Railways, Shipping, and Aviation ministries for 
heritage preservation of rail engines, ships, and aircraft. The Committee also raised the need for penal 
provisions for defacing cultural items. 
9.6 The Committee recommends that: (a) the Ministry prepare an inventory of non -ASI heritage 
structures at risk, in coordination with State Governments; (b) the protection of architectural 
heritage com ponent under the proposed new infrastructure scheme specifically address 100 -200 
year-old facades and structures; (c) the Ministry coordinate with the Ministries of Defence, 
Railways, Ports, Shipping and Waterways, and Civil Aviation for preservation of he ritage assets; 
and (d) the need for penal provisions for defacing or destroying cultural property be examined and 
a legal note submitted to the Committee within one year. 
C. Audit Compliance 
9.7 The Committee notes that as of February 2026, two CAG audit p aragraphs remain outstanding: (a) 
Report No.  10 of 2022, concerning ASI monument preservation, with a revised Action Taken Note in 
process; and (b) Report No.  3 of 2024, concerning IGRMS Bhopal (30.51 per cent vacancy; 26 per cent 
objects displayed), with a final Action Taken Note in process. 

20 
 
9.8 The Committee views the non -finalisation of Action Taken Notes on outstanding CAG audit 
paragraphs as a serious matter. The Committee recommends that the Ministry finalise and submit 
the Action Taken Notes on both outstanding CAG audit paragraphs within 90 days and furnish 
copies to the Committee. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry institute a standing 
mechanism for time-bound disposal of CAG audit paragraphs. 
D. Branding and Public Engagement 
9.9 The Committee suggested developing “Incredible Culture” as a keyword branding initiative and that 
all Ministry presentations should be drafted as educational courses for curricula. The Committee also 
noted the formation of 60 international Parliamentary frie ndship groups and recommended formal 
Ministry engagement. 
9.10 The Committee recommends that the Ministry: (a) develop a cultural branding strategy, 
including the feasibility of an “Incredible Culture” initiative, and present it to the Committee 
within 90 days; (b) coordinate with the Ministry of Education for integration of cultural heritage 
content into school curricula; and (c) engage with the parliamentary friendship groups to provide 
structured cultural content for international engagement. 
E. Members’ Accessibility to Sites and Information 
9.11 The Committee raised the issue of Vedic heritage centres in villages named Riga, Athari, Yajur, and 
Samar in Bihar. 
9.12 The Committee also highlighted the existing state of infrastructural facilities, its poor conditions and 
expensive costumes at the National School of Drama. 
9.13 In view of the observations regarding the villages of Riga, Athari, Yajur and Samar in Bihar,  
which are believed to have possible associations with the Vedic tradition, the Committee 
recommends that the Ministry examine the matter and explore the feasibility of undertaking 
appropriate studies, in consultation with the Archaeological Survey of Indi a and other concerned 
institutions, to ascertain the historical and cultural significance of these sites. The Committee also 
recommends that the Ministry take appropriate steps to address issues relating to infrastructural 
facilities and the cost of costumes at the National School of Drama. The Committee would like to be 
apprised of the action taken in this regard.
