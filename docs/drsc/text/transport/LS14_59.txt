R E P O R T
 
INTRODUCTION
 
Demand No. 76 pertains to the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.  The Ministry of Surface Transport
 has been bifurcated into two Ministries i.e. Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and Ministry of Shipping.  The
 Department of Road Transport and Highways, which was part of the erstwhile Ministry of Surface Transport has been
 notified as a separate Ministry of Road Transport and Highways w.e.f. 7
th November 2000.
 
2.       The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is responsible for development, formulation and implementation
 of policies relating to Road Transport, National Highways and Transport Research.  Its main function relates to road
 development and maintenance including Border Roads, Planning, Co-ordination and Road Transport.
 
3.       The Ministry is administratively divided into two wings namely Road Transport Wing and Highways Wing.
 
4.       Road Transport Wing is concerned with the formulation of broad policies relating to Road Transport, Road
 Safety, Motor Vehicles Act, legislation relating to Road Transport Corporations, besides making arrangements for

5.       Highways Wing is mainly concerned with the matters relating to:-

REVIEW OF NINTH FIVE-YEAR PLAN
 
6.         For the Ninth Five-Year Plan, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways had proposed an outlay of Rs.
 23000 crores as worked out by the working group on roads.  However, the Planning Commission had approved an
 outlay of Rs. 8862.20 crores.  During 1999-2000 and 2000-2001, substantial additional funds were made available at
 R.E. stage and as a result of which the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways was allocated a plan outlay of 19700.82
 crores.  However, the Ministry could utilise only Rs.13929.11 crores only.  Resulting in a shortfall in utilisation of
 funds to the extent of Rs. 5771.71 crores.  Major reasons for the under-utilisation was that expenditure had been low in
 the beginning of 9
th Five Year Plan as only the feasibility studies were being done for NHDP and pace of expenditure
 increased after the works were awarded.  In respect of Special Repair Programme, shortfall was due to the fact that the
 funds were provided at the fag end during 1999-2000 and therefore these could not be utilised fully.
 
7.         For E & I works out of CRF, works costing about Rs. 231 crore were approved in principle but detailed estimate
 for all works not submitted by States/UTs.
 
8.         The Committee regrets to note that on one hand the Ministry had proposed an outlay of Rs. 23000 crores for the
 9
th Five Year Plan where as on other hand they could utilise only 13929.11 crores out of the approved plan outlay of
 Rs. 19700.82 crores during the 9 th Plan period leaving a shortfall of Rupees 5771.71 crores.  It appears that while
 proposing its plan outlay, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways had not been realistic in its approach. The
 utilisation of remaining approved outlay could have improved the road scenario in the country.  In future, the Ministry
 should have realistic planning so that the approved outlay can be utilised fully.

NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
 
16.       National Highways being a Central Subject, the entire expenditure on its development and maintenance is met
 by the Government of India.  In addition, financial assistance is provided in the form of grants-in-aid for development
 of certain selected State roads/bridges.  Funds are also provided to State Govts. and Border Road Development Board
 for some Strategic Roads and certain selected roads in far flung inaccessible areas.
 
17.       The length of National Highways for which the Government of India is constitutionally responsible stood at
 57,737 km at the end of March 2001.  During the current year, a further length of 373 km. has been added to the NH
 Network and at present it stands at 58,112 kms.
 
18.       National Highways Authority of India is looking after 14000 kms of National Highways, the Border Roads
 Organisation has 4000 kms of National Highways and about 41000 kms of National Highways are being looked after
 by the Ministry directly.
 
19.       National Highways Development Project (NHDP) comprises 4/6 laning of Golden Quadrilateral connecting
 Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata and North-South and East-West corridors from Srinagar to Kanyakumari and
 Spur from Salem to Cochin with a total length of about 13151 Kms. The target date for completion of works for North-
South and East-West corridors is December, 2007 and for Golden Quadrilateral it is December, 2003 and it envisages
 total investment of Rs. 57000.00 crores.  Out of total 13151 km for NHDP, 1779 kms have already been 4-laned and
 4602 km is under implementation.
 
20.       Keeping in view the quantum of investment involved in the NHDP, the Committee desired to know whether the
 Government have any  standardise cost per km or whether the cost varied from  place to place.  In his reply, the
 Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways submitted as under:
 
“…… we prepare estimation on the basis of the schedule of roads. It comes to Rs. 4 to 4.5 crores/km on the
 average.... the standard cost per kilometer would be very difficult to arrive at. It depends on the soil condition
 and so many other factors. All things vary from place to place. When we say Rs. 4 to 4.5 crores, it is the average
 of all the areas....”
 
21.       The Ministry further informed the Committee that the designs of roads and bridges are based on the standards
 and specifications prescribed by Indian Roads Congress and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.
 Construction of roads includes embankment, pavement, culverts and bridges, ROB/RUBs etc. Also, the height of
 embankment, pavement thickness, design of culverts and bridges in one stretch of road are generally not identical with
 those in other stretch of the road.
 
22.       The cost of construction of roads is based on various parameters like specifications of construction, cost of
 construction materials, type of soil, type of terrain, cost of labour and cost of equipment. Though specifications of
 construction may be uniform, other factors vary and are different in different regions. Even in a region the factors such
 as terrain, soil characteristics, source of construction materials etc may vary significantly resulting in variation in cost
 of construction.  The estimate for construction cost of a particular stretch of road in a region is estimated based on
 construction specifications and standard schedule of rates applicable in that area. The rates for various items of work in
 the schedule of rate are the analysed rates considering the requirements of labour, material and their leads and
 equipment and their standard output. Thus, the schedule of rates gives standard rate per unit of quantity in a particular
 region. It is evident from above that the cost of construction of one km of road will depend on design features and the
 region of its location. It is therefore, difficult to standardize the cost of construction of roads in different regions
 because of variable parameters as noted above. An average cost of construction of roads may however be assessed for
 planning purposes.

PROBLEM OF OVERLOADING
 
32.       Taking up discussion on Road Transport Sector, the Committee drew  the attention of the Ministry of Road
 Transport and Highways to the expanded chassis and size of truck as result of which there are instances of overloading
 upto 100 per cent.  The Committee desired to know the precaution being taken and the provisions of the law in this
 regard.
 
33.       In their reply, the Ministry of Road  Transport and Highways informed the Committee that under section 113 of
 Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 prohibits plying of over loaded vehicles. Section 114 of the Act empowers officers of the
 Transport Department or officers authorised by the State Government to direct any vehicles, if suspected of overloaded,
 to the nearest way bridge enroute or nearest destination. If weighment establishes overloading, excess load has to be
 off-loaded. Section 194 lays down penalty for overloading, in addition of off- loading of the excess load. The power
 and responsibility to enforce these provisions rests with the States. These instructions have been reiterated to the States
 on number of occasions. The Ministry of Finance has also been approached to provide suitable fiscal incentive by way
 of lower excise duty on multi-axle trucks to encourage increasing use of these road friendly vehicles and to reduce
 instance of overloading.
 
34.       For design of roads, the overloading of vehicles is measures in terms of Vehicle Damage Factor (VDF). The
 VDF is a multiplier to convert the number of commercial vehicles of different axle loads and axle configuration to the
 number of standard axle repetitions. It is defined as equivalent number of standard axles per commercial vehicles. The
 VDF is very high on our roads.
 
35.       Overloading of vehicles has been contributing to the premature failure of several road stretches. Studies have
 revealed that with 10% over loading over the permissible axle load limit, the pavement life gets reduced by about 35%
 and with 30% over loading pavement life gets reduced by about 65%. To illustrate this, if strengthening of pavement
 for a 200 km long section, to meet the needs of 10.2 tonnes axle load, requires an investment of Rs. 100 crore for a
 design life of 10 years and with 30% over-load (5tonnes more pay load), it would last for only 3.5 years, implying
 further investments of the order of Rs.100 crore at accelerated intervals. In spite of provisions of designs, premature

 failure of pavement cannot be ruled out because of continued overloading of vehicles, which goes unchecked, both in
 weight as well as repetitions.
 
36.       The Committee feels that overloading of trucks is a serious problem, which requires closer attention by Ministry
 of Road Transport and Highways alongwith State Governments. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry
 of Road Transport and Highways should strongly take up this serious issue and a meeting at the minister level with all
 State Governments should be called at the earliest to devise means for curbing this problem. The Committee also
 recommends that the Ministry of Finance should provide fiscal incentives by way of lower excise duty on multi axle
 trucks to encourage increasing use of these road friendly vehicles and to reduce instance of over loading.
 
37.       The Committee also recommends that Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should have co-ordination with
 the Ministry of Industry and other concerned agencies involved in manufacturing of heavy Motor vehicles so that there
 can be a check on manufacturing of expanded chassis.  The Committee is of the view that this step can check
 overloading to a great extent.
 
38.       The Committee is also of the view that there is lack of Public awareness in respect of this problem. The Ministry
 of Road Transport and Highways has failed on the front of publicity efforts, which were to be taken in this regard. The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry Road Transport and Highways should come up with publicity
 efforts, at large scale, addressing to the truck drivers and transport agencies engaged in transportation business
 including general public.
COMPUTERISATION IN ROAD TRANSPORT SECTOR
39.       With a view to induct information technology in road transport sector, back-end and front end computerization is
 being introduced. Back-end computerization envisages incorporation of details of existing driving licenses &
 registration certificates on common software at RTO level and then linking it up at State level. Front-end operation
 involves issue of smart card-based driving licenses and Registration Certificates of motor vehicles, based on common
 ISO 7816 specifications. This would ensure that documents issued by one state could be read in other States as well.
 
40.       The Committee enquired about the present status of computerization and any time frame for it. In their reply, the
 Ministry stated that Standardized software for both front-end and back-end computerization has been made available to
 all the States/UTs. As per information available, back-end computerization has been operationalised in about more than
 fifty RTOs in the country. For front-end computerization, Government of Gujarat has gone ahead in respect of both
 driving license and registration certificates. The back-end computerization envisages incorporation of various details in
 respect of driving license, registration certificates and permits for commercial vehicles, at the RTO level in a standard
 computerized format. The front-end operation envisages issue of smart card based driving license and registration
 certificates at the RTO level.
 
41.       The data available from some of the RTOs where back-end computerization has been done establishes that
 substantial increase in revenue has accrued to the States. The State/UTs have been urged to expedite the
 computerization process, however no realistic time frame for completion of the activities on part of Government of
 States/UTs can be indicated at this juncture.
 
42.       The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways in respect of
 computerization in Road Transport Sector. The Committee feels that computerization is very essential in the field of
 Road Transport Sector. The Committee is of the view that without any time limit/frame for any scheme, the Ministry of
 Road Transport and Highways will not achieve its set targets.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry
 of Road Transport and Highways should frame the time limit for computerization in Road Transport Sector and
 completes it as early as possible.

REVIEW OF NINTH FIVE-YEAR PLAN
 
The Committee regrets to note that on one hand the Ministry had proposed an outlay of Rs. 23000 crores for the 9th Five
 Year Plan where as on other hand they could utilise only 13929.11 crores out of the approved plan outlay of Rs.
 19700.82 crores during the 9 th Plan period leaving a shortfall of Rupees 5771.71 crores.  It appears that while
 proposing its plan outlay the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways had not been realistic in its approach. The
 utilisation of remaining approved outlay could have improved the road scenario in the country.  In future, the Ministry
 should have realistic planning so that the approved outlay can utilise fully.

ANALYSIS OF DEMANDS FOR GRANTS
 
The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has proposed to surrender an
 amount of Rs. 38.00  crores during the financial year of 2001-02.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of
 Road Transport and Highways has main responsibility to have proper monitoring and utilisation of the funds allocated
 to the States.  The Committee therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should
 impress upon the State Government to ensure that the funds allocated for the development and maintenance of National
 Highways are utilised properly and utilisation certificate should be sent to the Central Government at the earliest so as
 to enable the Government to release the next instalment of funds.
(Para 15)
 
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
 
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is implementing the ambitious National
 Highways Development Project (NHDP) which consists of Golden Quadrilateral, North-South and East West corridors
 involving an investment of Rs. 57,000 crores approximately.  However, the Committee is surprised that the Ministry
 has not got done any standardisation of the cost per kilometer for the construction of National Highways under National
 Highways Development Project.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and
 Highways should get done the standardisation of cost of construction of National Highways region wise. It will help the
 utilisation of allocated amount in a proper way.
 (Para 23)
 
CENTRAL ROAD FUND
 
The Committee is of the view that in the past, there were scarcity of funds for the development of roads and National
 Highways in the Country. Now with the constitution of dedicated Central Road Fund (CRF), the financial crunch of
 funds of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways and State Governments have been reduced to a large extent for
 the development & maintenance of all categories of roads. The Committee regrets to note that the utilisation by the
 States is not commensurate with allocation of funds.  The Committee is of the view that non-utilisation and diversion of
 funds, if any, will defeat the very purpose of the constitution of Central Road Fund.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should take up the matter of proper utilisation of funds
 from Central Road Fund at every possible stage/level with State Governments. For this purpose, a Monitoring Cell may
 also be set up to oversee the progress of projects of National Highways in different states. The State Governments
 should be informed about the lack of progress in the National Highways Projects. This should be a continuous process
 through out the year for overseeing the proper utilisation of Central Road Fund in different states.
