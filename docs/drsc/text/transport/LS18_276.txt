REPORT 
The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation of national policies 
and programmes for the development and regulation of the Civil Aviation sector in the 
country. It is responsible for the administration of the Aircraft Act, 1934, Aircraft Rules, 
1937 and various other legislations pertaining to the aviation sector in the country. 
2. In addition to primary functions of framing of policies, the Ministry provides 
guidance to the organizations in the implementation of policy guidelines, monitors and 
evaluates their activities and also provides their interface with Parliament. It also supervises 
implementation by the organizations of special programmes of the Government, particularly 
those intended for the weaker sections of society. 
 3. The Ministry of Civil Aviation  has the following organizations under its 
administrative control:- 
(i) Attached Offices /Organizations 
 Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) 
 Bureau of Civil Aviation Authority (BCAS) 
 Commission of Railway Safety (CRS) 
 Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) 
(ii) Autonomous Bodies 
 Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi (IGRUA) 
 Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) 
 Rajiv Gandhi National Aviation University (RGNAU) 
(iii) Public Sector Undertakings 
 Airports Authority of India (AAI) 
 Air India Limited (AIL) 
 Pawan Hans Limited (PHL) 
4. The Department -related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism 
and Culture considered Demand No.8 containing the details of budget allocations of the 
Ministry of Civil Aviation for the financial year 2020 -21 at it s meeting held on the 20 th 
February, 2020. During the meeting, the Committee heard the views of the Secretary and 
other senior officers of the Ministry as well as the Heads of the organizations under its 
purview, mainly on budgetary aspects  and incidental aspects relating thereto. 
5. The Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation, while making a presentation before the 
Committee, on Demands for Grants (2020 -21) of the Ministry, mentioned that actual 
investment for this sector comes from airports and airlines. H e added that about 1441.71 lakh 
passengers were carried by domestic airlines during January to December, 2019 as against 
1389.76 lakh during the corresponding period in 2018, indicating an annual growth of 3.74%.

13.2. In its written reply, the Ministry has informed that the Ministry of Finance has not 
provided any reason for less allocation of budget against the projections given by the 
Ministry of Civil Aviation. Due to cut in projected budget for the year 2020 -21, some of the 
activities under Revenue Section  of the Ministry will be affected. In order to meet the 
requirement for the on -going projects of the Ministry, proposals will be taken up in th e first 
Supplementary and RE 2020 -21. In response to a query as to how the Ministry proposes to 
utilize the proposed budgetary allocation, the Secretary, while deposing before the 
Committee, stated that out of Rs. 3480.42 crores (Schemes), Rs. 810.23 crore s will be spent 
for purchase of two VVIP aircrafts, Rs. 2205 crores has been given to Air India for service of 
debts and the rest will be utilized for other activities. 
13.3. The Committee notes that compared to BE 2019 -20 of Rs. 4500 crores and Rs. 
3700 crores at RE 2019-20, Rs. 3797.71 crores has been allocated to the Ministry of Civil 
Aviation for 2020-21.  Elaborating the reasons for the reduction of Rs. 800 crores at RE 
stage, the Secretary clarified that the delivery of two Special Extra Section Fligh t 
(SESF) VVIP aircrafts which were bought through Air India have been rescheduled 
from January, 2020 to June, 2020 and  hence this reduction. The Committee notes that 
the Capital side allocation had remained the same, whereas, on the revenue side , more 
than 50% of allocation is meant for servicing the debt of Air India and Purchase of 2 
VVIP Planes and  the remaining allocation is available for other activities of the 
Ministry.  Keeping in view the security threat prevailing in the aviation sector all over 
the world, the Committee is of the considered view that the inadequacy of budgetary 
allocation would hamper vital activities, such a s installation of body scanners and  
provision of Capital for Rohini Heliport . The Committee feels that it is high time for 
installation of body scanners at all the airports in the country and therefore, fund 
allocation to the Ministry be substantially increased at the R.E. stage for the purpose.

14.1. The savings of Rs.1056.86 crore in Revenue section includes the surrendered amount

of Finance in  RE 2019 -20 is Rs.3700.00 crore against BE 2019 -20  of Rs.4500.00 crore. As 
such, Rs.800.00 crore will be surrendered in the month of February/March-2020. 
14.2. Considering the vital role played by the aviation sector in the growth and 
development of the nation and the growth registered by the aviation industry in the 
recent years, the Committee f ails to understand why the Ministry requires more funds 
when even the allocated fund is not being spent fully both at the B.E. and R.E. stage of 
2019-20, and there is a saving of Rs. 76.80 Crores in the Revenue Section.  The 
Committee desires to know the r easons for such a saving whereas crucial schemes like 
RCS are not being reimbursed fully to AAI.  The Committee, therefore, opines that 
efforts should be made to fully utilize the funds allocated before seeking funds. 
Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA) 
15. The Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation, while deposing before the Committee, 
stated thus: 
".....I would like to say that there is a small challenge before us which I would 
like to bring to the notice of the Committee.  This Carbon Offsetting and 
Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA) has said that airlines 
pollute.  They burn fuel, so they pollute.  So, this is said that whatever we 
pollute in the year 2020 will become our benchmark, and beyond that, if we 
pollute, we will have to buy carbon credits.  Now, this has serious implications 
for our aviation industry in the sense that we are still a growing industry.  The

Shortage of pilots 
26. The following steps have been taken by DGCA to enhance the availability of pilots:- 
(i)  There is the shortage of type r ated commanders in the country due to growth in 
aviation industry and induction of new type of aircraft in the fleet by the airline. Though 
sufficient numbers of Indian Co -pilot are available and employed by the airlines, they do not 
possess the level of s kill and experience the commander s require on the latest fleet of 
aircrafts. To cover the shortage of type rated pilots, validation of foreign pilots is done as per 
Rule 45 of Aircraft Rules, 1937. As per their policy , the expat pilots are phased out by th e 
airlines once the Co-pilots inducted by them become eligible for commander after undergoing 
requisite training for the same . The present policy of hiring foreign pilots by Airlines has 
been extended up to 31.12.2020 by the Central Government. 
(ii)  The c ondition of having flying experience in preceding Five years for issue of 
Commercial Pilot Licence has been done away with by amending Section J of Schedule II of 
The Aircraft Rules, 1937 (containing the requirements for the issue of Commercial Pilot 
Licence) so that more number of aspiring pilots after completing the requisite number of 
flying hrs and undergoing recency and skill test, may now apply for the issue of CPL. 
(iii)   In order to have more number of experienced instructors and examiners with the  
Flying Training Organization (FTO) an amendment in Section Q & R of Schedule II of 
Aircraft Rule 1937 {containing the requirements for the issue of Assistant Flight Instructor 
(AFIR) and Flight Instructor Rating (FIR) respectively} has been carried out. B y easing out 
amendment in Section Q&R, more CPL holder can apply for AFIR & FIR and the shortage of 
Instructors / examiners in FTO will be addressed. Subsequently, after acquiring more 
experience, these Instructors / examiners can apply for FTO. 
(iv)  DGCA has also made provision for conversion of AFIR/FIR Ratings obtained on the 
foreign licence to ensure the availability of more Instructors. 
(v)  DGCA has also amended the flying experience requirements for issue of Airlines 
Transport Pilots Licence (Aeroplane/Helicopter) by amending the Section N &M of Schedule 

18 
 
II of Aircraft Rules, 1937 containing the requirements for the issue of ATPL. By amendments 
in Section M & N more Co -pilots may get ATPL and may become eligible for Pilot -in 
command and more number of defence pilot will become eligible to get ATPL. 
26.1. The Committee notes that  it is a matter of grave concern that the country needs 
about 1000 pilots a year and that around 200 to 300 pilots only are trained, which leads 
to  shortage of type rated commanders in the country. The Committee strongly feels 
that with the huge manpower potential and skilled workforce available in the country, it 
is just a matter of time and effort that these resources are tapped into.

Regional Connectivity Scheme (RCS) 
 The Committee notes that RCS has given the Indian aviation industry a huge 
fillip. This Scheme has made air travel more affordable and also the connectivity to  
remote and hilly regions of the country has improved.  The Committee notes that 
availability of funds and high cost of operations are the major constraints under the 
Scheme.  The Committee finds that out of 688 valid routes awarded, only 256 rout es 

32 
 
have commenced operations, which constitute only 37% of the awarded routes.  Despite 
the fact that the Ministry frequently conducts bidding for RCS routes and awards

response from the stakeholders including the passengers and significant change noticed 
in the economic activity of the connected routes with current level of 37% routes, there 
is a huge potential in terms of connectivity and the resultant economic prosperity  under 
the Scheme.  The Committee, therefore, desires that concerted efforts should be made to 
operationalize all the awarded routes at the earliest. The Committee also desires  that 
periodic evaluation of the Scheme be conducted to address the constraints of the issues 
in implementation.

 The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Ministry for seamless

Ministry of Tourism, for early release of  100% VGF for the above mentioned routes.
