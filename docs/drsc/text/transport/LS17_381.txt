AIR INDIA-RELATED EXPENDITURE 
Recommendation/Observation  
1. The Committee notes that a disproportionately large share of the budget, amounting 
to ₹945.51 crore, has been allocated to the Air India Asset Holding Ltd. (SPV), in addition 
to another ₹80 crore for tax matters and ₹125 crore for medical benefits for retired 
employees of erstwhile Air India. While the Committee recognises the necessity of 
addressing legacy liabilities, it expresses deep concern over the continued fiscal burden 
placed on public funds. The Committee therefore recommends, Ministry to establish a five-
year phased reduction plan for financial obligations to AIAHL, with clear annual targets 
for decreased funding. This plan should include monetization of remaining non-core assets 
held by AIAHL including stakes in Hotel Corporation of India, artwork, and real estate 
properties to offset ongoing liabilities. Secondly, implement a cost-sharing mechanism for 
medical benefits to retired Air India employees, exploring options such as a one-time 
settlement or transition to a contributory healthcare scheme that gradually reduces 
government financial exposure while ensuring continued care for beneficiaries. Thirdly, 
create a dedicated oversight committee to monitor and regularly report on the utilization of 
funds allocated to AIAHL, ensuring transparency in expenditure and accelerating the 
resolution of outstanding liabilities to minimize long-term financial impact on the aviation 
budget. 
(Para 12)   
Reply of the Ministry   
1.1 These funds are required for servicing the interest on two Half-Yearly 7.39% p.a. GOI – 
Assured and Guaranteed 10-year NCDs issued by AIAHL: series-2 for ₹7000 crores and series-3 
for ₹7985 crores, payable in October 2025 (for September dues) and April 2026 (for March 
dues). The Government of India (GOI), having assured and guaranteed these NCDs to refinance 
Air India's debt post-disinvestment, is obligated to provide funding through Demand for Grants. 
This funding will ensure the servicing of principal repayments and interest payments on the 
NCDs, subject to the availability of monetized/realized earnings from parked funds with AIAHL. 
The total principal repayment obligation for these NCDs amounts to ₹14,985 crores, which must 

3 
 
be fulfilled by October 2029. The total principal amount of ₹14,985 crore will be paid by 2026, 
within the five-year phased plan as recommended by the Committee.  
1.2 Additionally, the allocation advised for AIAHL for FY 2025-26 also includes 
indemnification of taxation liabilities arising from cases predating Air India's disinvestment in 
January 2022, as outlined in the tripartite agreement (SPA).  
1.3 Upon disinvestment, 68 identified immovable properties were book-transferred to 
AIAHL without title transfer. Out of these, AIAHL has sold/returned 25 properties. Certain 
transferred properties have issues such as unavailable ownership documents or being under 
litigation, with Air India remaining the title owner. AIAHL is actively coordinating with Air 
India and relevant authorities to monetize or surrender these properties.  
1.4  The GOI is currently evaluating the future of AIAHL's subsidiary, Hotel Corporation of 
India. Additionally, as per GOI directives, the artworks and artifacts of Air India are being 
transferred directly to the National Gallery of Modern Art, New Delhi (NGMA) under the 
Ministry of Culture.  
Employees of Air India Limited were provided medical facilities as per the Air India 
medical scheme. Following the disinvestment, the government decided that medical benefits 
would be made available to retired and retiring eligible beneficiaries of Air India and its 
subsidiaries by the government. This scheme mandates that new retirees who join after January 
27, 2022, can access subsequent benefits aligned with the CGHS medical scheme, administered 
by AIAHL under GOI provisions.  
AIAHL regularly submits detailed reports on its budgetary allocations and fund 
utilization to the IFD Division of MoCA. The utilization of GOI grants funds is audited by the 
Internal Auditor, statutory Auditor, and the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) 
periodically, ensuring compliance and accountability.  
Furthermore, the monetization of assets is carried out under the approval and guidance of 
an oversight committee consisting of ex-CVC Shri. Pratyush Sinha and ex-CAG Shri. Vijender 
Nath Kaul. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2. The Committee further recommends that the existing Tariff Monitoring Unit 
(TMU) within DGCA be revamped with AI-driven tools for real-time data analysis and 
anomaly detection.  

4 
 
(Para 12.3)   
 
Reply of the Ministry   
 
2.1 Airfares are not subject to regulation by the Government and airlines have the flexibility 
to determine their airfares based on their operational needs, while adhering to Rule 135 of the 
Aircraft Rules, 1937.  
Globally, most countries have deregulated their aviation sector, i.e., removing government 
imposed entry and price restrictions on airlines. Deregulation has led to increased competition 
between airline carriers, leading to decrease in airfare. As a result of deregulation, entry into the 
airlines industry for a potential new airline has become easier, resulting in many new airlines 
entering the market, thus increasing competition. The direct impact of deregulation is that a 
passenger in a lower income group can afford to travel by air.  
Given the complex dynamics of the Indian aviation industry, Government is playing the role of a 
facilitator by way of creating enabling environment to support the growth of the sector. 
However, while the government generally refrains from regulating airfares to maintain market 
competitiveness, however, it remains vigilant, and the Government intervenes to shift capacity 
from one sector to another to prevent exorbitant pricing to ensure passenger comfort and welfare.  
Further, Government also encourages Airlines to induct new aircrafts and increase their fleet so 
as to improve connectivity and as a result reduction in airfares. Airlines have placed orders of 
more than 1500 aircrafts which will further improve connectivity and reduce airfare.  
With the enhancement of capacity by induction of more aircraft fleet, modernization of airports 
and development of new airports, domestic passenger traffic has increased to 153,674,310 in 
2023-24 as compared to 136,028,656 in the year 2022-23. In the current financial year 2024-25, 
the domestic passenger traffic has surpassed the volume during the corresponding period in the 
year 2023-24, reflecting an impressive growth rate.  
Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has setup a Tariff Monitoring Unit (TMU) that 
monitors airfares on select domestic sectors on random basis by using airlines' websites on 
monthly basis to ensure that the airlines do not charge airfares outside the range declared by 
them.These selected sectors represent almost 20% of the passenger traffic in 2024.  
The analysis of TMU reveals that the airfare per kilometer offered by Indian carrier is low in 
India when compared globally. Furthermore, TMU is being strengthened and concerted efforts 
are being made towards the digitisation of TMU.  
 
2.1 Regular consultations are held with airlines who are also being sensitized to exercise 
moderation in pricing and to keep passengers' interest in mind. The airlines have committed to 
ensure that airfares do not surge during events such as natural disasters, calamities, Mahakhumb, 
National emergent situations, etc. 
To address concerns over high airfares to Prayagraj during Maha Kumbh 2025, the Ministry held 
a meeting with airline operators on 29.01.2025. Key issues discussed included air connectivity 

5 
 
and fare rationalization. Following the Ministry’s intervention, airlines increased weekly flights 
from 51 to 533, connecting Prayagraj with 18 cities and accommodating around 70,000 
passengers weekly. As a result, airfares dropped by nearly 50%. 
In the wake of the tragic terror attack in Kashmir, Ministry moved swiftly to ensure the safety 
and well-being of affected tourists and victims. As part of immediate relief measures, four 
special flights from Srinagar – two to Delhi and two to Mumbai, were arranged, with additional 
flights kept on standby to cater to further evacuation needs. 
DGCA issued an advisory to increase the number of flights in response to heightened demand 
and also to ensure uninterrupted connectivity from Srinagar to various destinations across India, 
facilitating evacuation of stranded tourists. Meetings with all airline operators was held and a 
strong advisory was issued against surge pricing. Airlines were directed to maintain regular fare 
levels, ensuring that no passenger is burdened during this sensitive time. 
Additionally, all airlines were asked to extend full cooperation for the transportation of deceased 
individuals to their respective home states, working in sync with state governments and local 
authorities. 
The airlines were sensitised to consider waiving cancellation and rescheduling fees and provide 
all necessary assistance to tourists facing unexpected circumstances and challenges during this 
difficult time. 
DGCA also issued an advisory to airlines on passenger handling measures in view of Airspace 
Restrictions Resulting in Extended Flight Durations and Technical Stops. 
2.3 To ensure prompt and effective resolution of grievances from passengers and other 
stakeholders, Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA) launched Air Sewa portal on November, 2016. 
It provides a common platform for air travelers to submit grievances related to air travel and seek 
information about flight status. The platform is accessible through both a web portal and a 
mobile app available on Android and iOS platform. 
Airsewa aims to offer a convenient and hassle-free air travel experience by addressing traveler 
concerns and providing real time updates on flight status, schedules and airport information.   
The system offers interactive grievance management, enabling two-way communication between 
stakeholders and complainants. It includes enhanced flight information and real-time tracking 
capabilities. A mobile app is available for nodal officers, allowing them to log in and receive 
notifications. The system can integrate with airport AODB systems to fetch live flight status 
data. Additionally, grievances can be escalated if resolutions are unsatisfactory or delayed, and 
they can be transferred to other relevant nodal authorities when needed.

Recommendation/Observation  
3. The Committee observes and appreciates the fact that the Directorate General of 
Civil Aviation (₹300 crore) and the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (₹80 crore) have been 
accorded significant funding. However, the Commission of Railway Safety (₹22 crore) has 
received markedly lower funding, despite its critical role in ensuring passenger and freight 
safety in railway operations. The Committee advises a reassessment of this allocation, with 
a view to strengthening railway safety mechanisms. Simultaneously, it urges regular 
performance evaluations of DGCA and BCAS funding to ensure that these resources 
translate into tangible improvements in aviation safety, regulatory efficiency, and security 
preparedness. The Committee also recommends that Ministry should establish an 
integrated safety oversight mechanism that promotes collaboration between transport 
safety regulators including DGCA, BCAS, and the Commission of Railway Safety, utilizing 
shared resources where appropriate and developing common standards for multi-modal 
transport safety. Further, Committee recommends for development of a sustainable 
funding mechanism for aviation oversight functions at both national and regional levels, 
potentially including air safety charges, to ensure regulatory bodies can fulfill their 
mandates effectively without complete dependence on general budgetary allocations. 
(Para 13)   
Reply of the Ministry   
3.1 The budgetary allocation is based on requirement of funds sought by the concerned 
organisations of MoCA and availability made at the BE stage. However, at RE stage, the 
requirement of funds is re-assessed, which depends on the actual utilisation and further 
requirements and requisite changes are made in the allocation by way of re-appropriation and by 
providing additional allocation. Further, it is to state that the budgetary allocation for 
Commission of Railway Safety was increased from Rs 20.00 crore for BE 2024-25 to Rs 22.00 
crore at RE stage. 
 The role of Commission is primarily inspectorial and investigative in nature, focusing on 
providing statutory safety clearances for Railway projects and conducting inquiries into serious 
Railway accidents. In meeting this prescribed role, the Ministry of Civil Aviation continues to 
remain committed in supporting Commission of Railway Safety in terms of capacity building, 
modernization, and strengthening of institutional capabilities for any additional requirements. 
 The Ministry also concurs with the Committee's broader recommendation for fostering 
integrated safety oversight across transport sectors. Necessary steps are being explored for inter-

7 
 
agency collaboration, including sharing of best practices, harmonization of safety standards, and 
coordinated training programs. 
 The Committee’s observations have been noted. DGCA agrees with the suggestion that 
regular performance reviews are important to ensure that funding results in real improvements in 
aviation safety and security. As regards creation of an integrated safety oversight system 
involving DGCA, BCAS and the Commission of Railway Safety, same shall be valuable in terms 
of better coordination, resource sharing, and common safety standards. The recommendations of 
the committee have been noted for compliance. 
 
 
REGIONAL CONNECTIVITY SCHEME AND INFRASTRUCTURE EXPANSION 
Recommendation/Observation  
4. The allocation of ₹540 crore to the Regional Connectivity Scheme, with ₹240 crore 
earmarked for the North-Eastern Region, demonstrates the government's commitment to 
regional connectivity. However, the Committee notes that historical data shows significant 
variation in route sustainability and regional imbalances in performance. To enhance the 
effectiveness of this allocation, the Committee recommends: 
Institution of a tiered funding approach that prioritizes routes with proven 
passenger load factors exceeding 70% while providing targeted support for 
underperforming routes with high development potential, particularly in the Eastern, 
Northeastern, and Central regions where recent data shows promising growth trajectories. 
 
(Para 14 & 14.1)   
Reply of the Ministry:-  
4.1 The recommendation has been duly noted. In line with this, routes operating in 
challenging regions— namely the North-eastern states, Himalayan belt, and Island territories—
are already being extended enhanced financial support due to their higher operational costs and 
strategic importance. The Ministry will evaluate a tiered funding approach in Modified UDAN.

Recommendation/Observation  
5. The Committee has reviewed the expenditure pattern over the last three FYs and 
notes that the actual expenditure up to January 2025 indicates significant underutilisation 
of funds, particularly for DGCA (₹15.14 crore spent out of ₹22.64 crore), BCAS (₹4.42 
crore out of ₹8 crore), and the Ministry (Secretariat + AAIB), which spent only ₹1.78 crore 
out of ₹13.70 crore. While provisional savings have been cited as the reason, this pattern do 
raises concerns about inefficiencies in fund deployment and potential delays in planned 
projects. The Committee therefore urges the Ministry to exercise greater fiscal 
accountability and timely fund utilization to ensure that allocated resources are effectively 
employed within the financial year. It may establish robust fund absorption mechanisms 
and quarterly review systems to track project progress and financial performance so that 
risk of unspent allocations may be minimized.  
(Para 16)   
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
5.1 Regarding expenditure pattern, it is to state that the expenditure of DGCA under Capital 
Section up to 31.03.2025 was Rs 21.54 crore against the RE allocation of Rs 22.64 crore, for 
BCAS the actual expenditure up to 31.03.2025 was Rs 6.55 crore against the RE allocation of Rs 
8.00 crore and for Ministry (Secretariat + AAIB), the actual expenditure up to 31.03.2025 was 
Rs 11.76 crore against the RE allocation of Rs 13.70 crore. The recommendation of the 
Committee to exercise greater fiscal accountability and timely fund utilisation to ensure that 
allocated resources are effectively employed within the financial year has been noted for 
compliance. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
6. The Committee notes that Rohini Heliport Limited, which received a nominal 
allocation of ₹0.26 crore in 2024-25, does not feature in the 2025-26 budget. Similarly, 
several aviation infrastructure projects lack consistent funding streams which do raise

hinder aviation infrastructure expansion and regional connectivity, and private sector 
confidence in Government-led aviation projects. The Committee therefore urges the 
Ministry to chart a dedicated and sustained funding approach for aviation infrastructure 
projects which can ensure that capital outlays are not arbitrarily introduced and

Reply of the Ministry  
6.1 Rohini Heliport Limited (RHL) received a nominal allocation of ₹26 lakh in 2024–25 to

required for payment of fees and stamp duty to the Registrar of Companies (RoC) as part of the

Recommendation/Observation  
7. The Committee also observes a sharp reduction in total capital outlay from ₹755.18 
crore in 2023-24 to ₹70 crore in 2025-26. While fiscal prudence is necessary, the Committee 
feels such a steep decline may impact the aviation sector’s capacity for growth, 
modernisation, and global competitiveness. The Committee is also concerned that 
allocations are increasingly concentrated in select areas, while other critical aviation 
functions receive marginal funding. The Committee strongly recommends a re-evaluation 
of capital budget reductions and adoption of a nuanced/balanced distribution of capital 
outlay across core aviation functions so that necessary investments in aviation safety, 
regulatory strengthening, and infrastructure development are not compromised.  
(Para 18)   
Reply of the Ministry     
7.1 Reasons for sharp reduction in capital outlay from ₹755.18 crore in 2023-24 to ₹70.00 
crore in 2025-26, are given as under:- 
(a) The one time equity infusion of Rs 600.00 crore in Alliance Air in the FY 2023-24, which 
was not repeated in the FY 2024-25. 
(b) Total expenditure of Rs 126.18 crore was done by DGCA, BCAS and AAIB for balance 
payment against the cost of building for Common office complex (Udaan Bhawan) to AAI, 
which was not repeated in the FY 2024-25, hence the capital outlay was dropped. 
The recommendations of the Committee have been noted for compliance. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
8. The Committee examines the response of the Ministry on the status of the working 
and implementation of the Regional Connectivity Scheme-Ude Desh ka Aam Naagrik. The 

10 
 
Committee observes that the Ministry has reported a total expenditure of ₹4,309.34 crore 
towards the development of aerodromes under both Phase 1 and Phase 2, with 88 
aerodromes developed and operationalised to date. While acknowledging the progress 
made, the Committee recommends implementing a multi-dimensional Return on 
Investment (ROI) assessment framework that measures outcomes beyond mere 
infrastructure development. The Ministry should establish a comprehensive digital 
monitoring system capturing granular data on economic multipliers, including tourism

derived from passenger volumes, operational consistency, and regional economic 
indicators. Additionally, the Ministry should develop a specialized Asset Utilization

aerodromes and implements corrective measures, potentially including alternative aviation 
uses for facilities with persistently low passenger traffic.

Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
8.1 The recommendations of the committee have been noted for compliance. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
9. The Committee appreciates that 619 routes have been operationalised, including 90 
routes in the North-Eastern region, and that 88 RCS airports are now connected, with 12 of 
these located in the North-East. To address regional imbalances, the Committee 
recommends developing a Geographic Information System (GIS)-based "Connectivity Gap 
Index" that quantifies accessibility deficits using multiple parameters including travel time 
to nearest operational airport, population density, economic activity centers, and existing 
transportation alternatives. This index should prioritize future route development with 
mandatory allocation of at least 30% of new routes to regions scoring above a defined 
threshold. The Committee further recommends implementing a "Hub-and-Spoke+" model 
specifically tailored for remote regions, wherein strategically located airports serve as 
regional hubs connected to smaller airfields through fixed-wing aircraft, while final 
connectivity to truly remote locations is provided through helicopter services. The Ministry 
should also introduce "Seasonal Route Flexibility" provisions allowing operators to 
temporarily redirect capacity to meet seasonal demands (such as festival periods, harvest 
seasons, or tourism peaks) while maintaining essential connectivity year-round.  
(Para 27)

Recommendation/Observation  
10. The Committee notes with appreciation that 149 lakh passengers have availed RCS 
flights, alongside the operation of 2,96,000 flights under the scheme. To enhance long-term 
financial sustainability, the Committee recommends implementing a graduated "Viability 
Transition Program" wherein routes beginning with full VGF support would transition 
through defined sustainability milestones—including load factor thresholds, operational 
reliability metrics, and ancillary revenue generation—with proportional reduction in 
subsidies linked to each milestone achievement. The Ministry should develop specialized 
"Multi-Use Aerodrome" guidelines enabling operators to generate supplementary revenue 
through co-located facilities such as cargo operations, aircraft maintenance services, 
aviation training centers, and non-aeronautical commercial activities appropriate to local 
market conditions. Additionally, the Committee recommends establishing an "Aviation 
Enterprise Zone" framework offering tax incentives and streamlined regulatory processes 
for businesses establishing operations within defined proximity to RCS airports, creating 
economic ecosystems that generate passenger traffic organically rather than through fare 
subsidies.  
(Para 28)   
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
10.1 The recommendations of the committee have been noted for compliance. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
11. The Committee notes the ongoing 5.5 round of bidding for helicopter and seaplane 
routes and recognizes their potential for enhancing connectivity in challenging terrain. To 
accelerate development in this sector, the Committee recommends implementing a 
specialized "Waterway Aerodrome Development Program" that identifies and categorizes 
potential water landing sites based on multiple factors including water body stability, 
surrounding infrastructure, tourism potential, and connectivity needs. This program 
should include standardized floating infrastructure designs that can be rapidly deployed 

12 
 
with minimal environmental impact. For helicopter operations, the Committee 
recommends developing modular, prefabricated heliport infrastructure kits specifically 
designed for rapid deployment in remote and challenging terrains, complete with 
standardized navigation aids and essential ground support equipment. The Ministry should 
also establish a "Mixed-Mode Operations Framework" enabling seamless passenger 
transitions between conventional aircraft, helicopters, and seaplanes with harmonized 
scheduling, ticketing, and baggage handling protocols, creating truly integrated multi-
modal connectivity networks rather than isolated transport solutions. 
(Para 30)   
Reply of the Ministry  
11.1 The recommendations of the committee have been noted for compliance. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
12. The Committee acknowledges the Ministry’s assertion that the Project Evaluation 
Committee conducts regular reviews to monitor progress under the Regional Connectivity 
Scheme (RCS). However, it does note that delays persist due to regulatory approvals, land 
constraints, difficult terrain, prolonged State Government clearances, and defence-related 
permissions. In the view of the Committee, the Ministry may explore the establishment of a 
dedicated task force to streamline approvals, enhance inter-agency coordination, and

9.1 Regulatory Framework for Aircraft Seating and Cabin Fabrics in India  
  
In India, all aircraft seats and cabin interior fabrics (including carpets, rugs, and other textile 
materials) used by airlines must comply with pre-defined standards. These include:  
1. Flammability Standards  
 All cabin materials must meet fire resistance requirements in line with TSO (Technical 
Standard Order) criteria.  
 Tests include vertical Bunsen burner, oil burner, and heat release/smoke density 
evaluations to ensure safety during fire incidents.  
 
2. Toxicity & Smoke Emission  
 Materials are tested for:  
 Smoke density  
 Toxic gas emissions (e.g., carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide)  
 Compliance with internationally recognized protocols like Boeing BSS 7239 or Airbus 
ABD 0031 is standard practice.  
 
3. Durability & Wear Resistance  
 Materials must pass: 
 Abrasion resistance tests (e.g., Martindale)  
 Colorfastness to light and rubbing  
 Tear strength  
 These ensure longevity, passenger comfort, and aesthetic integrity.  

30 
 
9.2 Role of TSO/ITSO and DGCA Oversight  
 These standards are enforced through the Technical Standard Order (TSO), which defines 
minimum performance requirements for specific materials, parts, processes, and 
appliances used in civil aircraft.  
 The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), India, accepts these standards as part 
of the Type Certification process for aircraft operating in Indian airspace.  
 DGCA also approves Indian Technical Standard Orders (ITSOs) for seats designed and 
manufactured domestically.  
 
9.3 Operational Compliance and Maintenance  
 Aircraft seats and interior elements are maintained to minimum airworthiness standards 
throughout their operational life.  
 Seat manufacturers may issue mid-life Service Bulletins (SBs) to improve or rectify 
existing components.  
 Airline personnel, along with DGCA inspectors, conduct regular inspections to ensure 
ongoing compliance. Any component found below standard is declared unserviceable.  
 
9.4 Interior Design and Maintenance Protocols  
 
 Aircraft interiors are designed by manufacturers following stringent requirements for:  
 Safety 
 Security  
 Ergonomics  
 Emergency responsiveness 
 
 Airlines must maintain interiors in accordance with the manufacturer's certified design 
and standards accepted by DGCA.  
 Any cabin defects encountered during operations are recorded in the Cabin Defect 
Logbook (CDL) by the cabin crew.  
 These defects are addressed by qualified engineers post-flight.  
 Seats or cabin components found non-compliant are:  
 Clearly marked as unserviceable  
 Not assigned to passengers  
 Note: DGCA may constitute a committee comprising of officers from different 
Directorates for reviewing and revising the Aviation Interior Quality system and standardisation 
of cabin interiors, including ergonomic, recyclability requirements, VOC emissions limits, and 
lifecycle assessment obligation.

AIR INDIA-RELATED EXPENDITURE 
Recommendation/Observation  
1. The Committee, while examining the Demand for Grants of the Ministry of Civil Aviation 
for 2025-26, recommends the establishment of a stringent airfare pricing regulation mechanism 
under the framework of an AI-powered predictive monitoring system, tentatively named 
"AirPrice Guardian." This system should aim to address consumer grievances related to 
unpredictable and exorbitant airfare surges, particularly during peak travel periods and 
emergencies, while ensuring transparency and fairness in pricing practices. The Committee 
notes that the current regulatory framework under DGCA lacks the capacity to proactively 
monitor and regulate airfare trends, necessitating a transformative approach leveraging 
advanced technology. 
(Para 12.1)   
Reply of the Ministry     
1.1 Airfares are not subject to government regulation, and airlines have the flexibility to 
determine their fares based on operational needs, adhering to Rule 135 of the Aircraft Rules, 
1937.  
Globally, most countries have deregulated their aviation sector, i.e., removing government 
imposed entry and price restrictions on airlines. Deregulation has led to increased competition 
between airline carriers, leading to decrease in airfare. As a result of deregulation, entry into the 
airlines industry for a potential new airline has become easier, resulting in many new airlines 
entering the market, thus increasing competition. The direct impact of deregulation is that a 
passenger in a lower income group can afford to travel by air.  
Given the complex dynamics of the Indian aviation industry, Government is playing the role of a 
facilitator by way of creating enabling environment to support the growth of the sector. 
However, while the government generally refrains from regulating airfares to maintain market 
competitiveness, however, it remains vigilant, and the Government intervenes to shift capacity 
from one sector to another to prevent exorbitant pricing to ensure passenger comfort and welfare. 
Further, Government also encourages Airlines to induct new aircrafts and increase their fleet so 
as to improve connectivity and as a result reduction in airfares. Airlines have placed orders of 
more than 1500 aircrafts which will further improve connectivity and reduce airfare. 

49 
 
With the enhancement of capacity by induction of more aircraft fleet, modernization of airports 
and development of new airports, domestic passenger traffic has increased to 153,674,310 in 
2023-24 as compared to 136,028,656 in the year 2022-23. In the current financial year 2024-25, 
the domestic passenger traffic has surpassed the volume during the corresponding period in the 
year 2023-24, reflecting an impressive growth rate. 
Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has setup a Tariff Monitoring Unit (TMU) that 
monitors airfares on select domestic sectors on random basis by using airlines' websites on 
monthly basis to ensure that the airlines do not charge airfares outside the range declared by 
them. These selected sectors represent almost 20% of the passenger traffic in 2024. 
The analysis of TMU reveals that the airfare per kilometre offered by Indian carrier is low in 
India when compared globally. Furthermore, TMU is being strengthened and concerted efforts 
are being made towards the digitisation of TMU. 
1.2  Regular consultations are held with airlines who are also being sensitized to exercise 
moderation in pricing and to keep passengers' interest in mind. The airlines have committed to 
ensure that airfares do not surge during events such as natural disasters, calamities, Mahakhumb, 
National emergent situations, etc. 
To address concerns over high airfares to Prayagraj during Maha Kumbh 2025, the Ministry held 
a meeting with airline operators on 29.01.2025. Key issues discussed included air connectivity 
and fare rationalization. Following the Ministry’s intervention, airlines increased weekly flights 
from 51 to 533, connecting Prayagraj with 18 cities and accommodating around 70,000 
passengers weekly. As a result, airfares dropped by nearly 50%. 
In the wake of the tragic terror attack in Kashmir, Ministry moved swiftly to ensure the safety 
and well-being of affected tourists and victims. As part of immediate relief measures, four 
special flights from Srinagar – two to Delhi and two to Mumbai, were arranged, with additional 
flights kept on standby to cater to further evacuation needs. 
DGCA issued an advisory to increase the number of flights in response to heightened demand 
and also to ensure uninterrupted connectivity from Srinagar to various destinations across India, 
facilitating evacuation of stranded tourists. Meetings with all airline operators was held and a 
strong advisory was issued against surge pricing. Airlines were directed to maintain regular fare 
levels, ensuring that no passenger is burdened during this sensitive time. 
Additionally, all airlines were asked to extend full cooperation for the transportation of deceased 
individuals to their respective home states, working in sync with state governments and local 
authorities. 
The airlines were sensitised to consider waiving cancellation and rescheduling fees and provide 
all necessary assistance to tourists facing unexpected circumstances and challenges during this 
difficult time. 

50 
 
DGCA also issued an advisory to airlines on passenger handling measures in view of Airspace 
Restrictions Resulting in Extended Flight Durations and Technical Stops. 
1.3  To ensure prompt and effective resolution of grievances from passengers and other 
stakeholders, Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA) launched Air Sewa portal on November, 2016. 
It provides a common platform for air travellers to submit grievances related to air travel and 
seek information about flight status. The platform is accessible through both a web portal and a 
mobile app available on Android and iOS platform. 
Airsewa aims to offer a convenient and hassle-free air travel experience by addressing traveller 
concerns and providing real time updates on flight status, schedules and airport information. 
The system offers interactive grievance management, enabling two-way communication between 
stakeholders and complainants. It includes enhanced flight information and real-time tracking 
capabilities. A mobile app is available for nodal officers, allowing them to log in and receive 
notifications. The system can integrate with airport AODB systems to fetch live flight status 
data. Additionally, grievances can be escalated if resolutions are unsatisfactory or delayed, and 
they can be transferred to other relevant nodal authorities when needed. 
 
Further Recommendations/Observations 
1.4 The Committee appreciates the acknowledgement of the need to strengthen the 
TMU and efforts towards its digitization. However, the proposed "AirPrice Guardian" 
system goes beyond mere monitoring. It envisions an AI-powered predictive mechanism. 
To truly address consumer grievances, the Committee recommends that the DGCA, 
through "AirPrice Guardian," be empowered with quasi-judicial authority. This authority 
should enable the DGCA to: 
 Impose temporary price caps or penalties on airlines found to be engaging in 
exploitative pricing practices, especially when the AI system flags significant 
deviations from predicted fair price bands. 
 Establish dynamic fare bands for each route, updated periodically (e.g., quarterly) 
through the predictive models of "AirPrice Guardian." These fare bands would 
serve as a transparent benchmark for consumers and a clear guideline for airlines, 
allowing for market-driven pricing within reasonable parameters. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2. The proposed “AirPrice Guardian” system will utilize machine learning algorithms and 
predictive analytics to forecast airfare trends based on historical data, demand patterns, fuel 

51 
 
costs, seasonal fluctuations, and external factors such as festivals or emergencies. By 
establishing dynamic fare bands for each route, updated quarterly through predictive models, 
this system will empower regulators to monitor deviations in real-time and take 10 preemptive 
action against excessive pricing. The Committee recommends that DGCA be empowered with 
quasi-judicial authority to impose temporary price caps or penalties on airlines engaging in 
exploitative practices. Additionally, a Pricing Transparency Index should be developed to rate 
airlines on their adherence to fair pricing norms, with this information made publicly available 
through a consumer-facing portal. 
(Para 12.2)

The Committee appreciates that RCS-UDAN is dynamic, but the "modular approval 
approach" aims to provide structural flexibility beyond just recalibration. The Committee 
recommends: 
 Principal Scheme Approval with Component Flexibility: The core framework and 
long-term vision of the UDAN scheme should receive a principal, overarching 
approval. However, specific elements, such as the inclusion of new 
unserved/underserved destinations, changes in VGF parameters for specific regions, 
or modifications to route types (e.g., seaplanes, heliports), should be adaptable 
through a streamlined modification protocol. This would prevent the need for re-
approving the entire scheme for minor adjustments, which can indeed lead to 
perceived "paralysis" at the operational level. 
 Pre-approved Frameworks for Hilly, Aspirational, and North-Eastern Regions: 
Given the specific focus on these regions, the Ministry should develop pre-approved 
frameworks or templates for route identification, infrastructure development, and 
VGF allocation for these areas. This would expedite approvals for proposals falling 
within these defined parameters. 
(Para 3.2) 
 
The Committee strongly urges the Ministry to reassess the budgetary allocation for aero 
sports and significantly enhance it, moving beyond a "token amount" to a sum 
commensurate with the aspirations of the National Aero Sports Guidelines and the 
potential of India's aero sports sector. This enhanced budget should not be merely for 
event-specific grants but for: 
 Long-term ecosystem development: Funding for the establishment and upgrading of 
aero sports infrastructure, procurement of modern equipment for various aero 
sports disciplines (Ballooning, Aeromodelling, Gliding, Microlights, etc.), and the 
development of dedicated aero sports facilities across states. 
 Support for Flying Training Organizations (FTOs): Financial assistance to FTOs 
affiliated with ACI to upgrade their fleets, enhance training infrastructure, and 
provide affordable training, thereby increasing the pool of skilled pilots and aero 
sports enthusiasts.
