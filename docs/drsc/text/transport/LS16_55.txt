R E P O R T
 INTRODUCTION
 
The Department of Tourism, in the Ministry of Tourism and Culture, is the nodal agency for the formulation of
 national policies and programmes and for the coordination of activities of various Central Government Agencies, State
 Governments/Union Territories and the Private Sector for the development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The
 role of the Department of Tourism has been to coordinate and supplement the efforts of the State/U.T. Governments in
 development of tourism through Central financial assistance, to catalyze private investment, strengthen promotional and
 marketing efforts and to provide trained manpower resources. The main activities of the Department are:-
 
(i)
                   Policy formulation and planning
(ii)                  Co-ordination
(iii)                Regulation
(iv)                Infrastructure and Product development
(v)                  Human Resource Development
(vi)                Publicity, Marketing and Facilitation
(vii)               Research, Analysis, Monitoring and Evaluation
(viii)              International Co-operation and External Assistance
(ix)                Legislation and Parliament work
(x)                  Management of Public Sector Undertakings and Autonomous bodies
 
2.       The administrative head of the Department is the Secretary (Tourism).  The Department is assisted by an attached
 office headed by the Director General.  The office of the Director General of Tourism provides executive directions for the
 implementation of various policies and programmes.  Directorate General of Tourism has a field formation of 21 offices
 within the country and 18 offices abroad.  The overseas offices are primarily responsible for tourism promotion and
 marketing in their respective areas and the field offices in India are responsible for providing information service to tourists
 and to monitor the progress of field projects.
 
3.       The Department of Tourism has the following public sector undertaking/autonomous institutions under its
 administrative control:-
 
a)            India Tourism Development Corporation Limited (ITDC);
b)           National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology and the Institutes under its control (NCHMCT)
c)            Indian Institute of Tourism & Travel Management (IITTM);
d)           Indian Institute of Skiing & Mountaineering (IISM);
e)            National Institute of Water Sports (NIWS).

INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF TOURIST CIRCUITS
 
15.     Rs.41.50 crores have been allocated for integrated development of tourism circuits.  The objective is to identify six
 tourism circuits on an annual basis (one each in North, South, East, West, Central and North East Zones) and develop them
 to international standards.  The aim is to provide all infrastructure facilities required by the tourists within these circuits and
 convergence of resources and expertise through co-ordinated action with States/Union Territories and private sector.  In the
 Annual Plan 2002-2003, the Department of Tourism has
proposed to develop the following travel circuits:-
 
(i)
           Buddhist Tourist Circuits - Bodhgaya-Rajgir-Nalanda-Varanasi
(ii)      Buddhist Monastic Circuit - Kullu-Manali-Lahul-Spiti-Leh
(iii)     Western Heritage Circuit - Gujarat-Ahmedabad-Rajkot-Dwarka-Porbundar-Somnath-Girnar-Palitana-Ahmedabad.
(iv)
        South Heritage Circuits - Chennai-Mahaballipuram-Kanchipuram-Thiruvallamalai-Ginjee-Pondicherry
(v)       Kerala Circuit - Palakkad-Kochi-Kottayam-(Kumarakom)-Thiruvananthapuram
(vi)        North Eastern Circuit - Guwahati-Kaziranaga-Shillong-Tawang
 
16.     The final decision regarding these circuits will be taken up in consultation with the State Governments.  Amount
 proposed will be utilized for the comprehensive development of the tourist infrastructure in these circuits.  The activities
 would include preparation of master plans in association with the State Government, improvement in accessibility,
 improvement in environment including solid waste and sewerage management around the destination, illumination of the
 destination, lighting of the approach etc.
         
17.     The Committee appreciates the proposal of the Department of Tourism for developing six tourist circuits in the country
 on an annual basis and to provide the necessary infrastructural facilities within these circuits in coordination with the
 States/private sector.  However, the Committee is apprehensive that these projects would not meet the same fate as those of
 the Mega Tourism Projects, which were started during the 8
th plan period, but hardly any appreciable progress had been
 made in any of the projects under the scheme.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Tourism
 should make concrete efforts in coordination with all the agencies involved so that early decisions are taken in the matter and
 the circuits identified are developed in a time bound schedule.  The Committee also recommends that the Department of
 Tourism should explore the possibility of including the new-found discovery i.e. Dholavira, the sea beaches and the
 adventure tourism spots in Kutch District of Gujarat in the Western Heritage Circuit.

24.     The Committee observes that the Corporation achieved a profit (after tax) of Rs.43.30 crores at the beginning of the
 Ninth Plan i.e. 1997-98.  Since 1999-2000, the Corporation has incurred increasing losses. The turnover during 2000-2001
 increased by 5.9% to Rs.305.60 crores compared to Rs. 288.50 crores in 1999-2000. There was operating loss of Rs.22.81

 Rs.24.04 crores in 1999-2000. The performance of ITDC during 2001-2002 is likely to be the worst so far, with the
 Corporation having recorded a loss of Rs.42.62 crores upto September 2001.
 
25.     The Committee was informed during the oral evidence that out of the 26 hotel properties, 9 have been disposed off,
 some other hotels were in the process of disinvestment and four hotels namely Ashok Hotel, Lalit Mahal Hotel, Samrat Hotel
 and Janpath Hotel have been withheld from the process of disinvestment.
 
26.     The Committee enquired from the representatives of the Department of Tourism that since "Hotel", the core activity of
 ITDC, were under process of disinvestment, what efforts had been made to tap/generate new sources of income for ITDC to
 operate as a self-sustained and profitable organisation.  The Committee was informed that the disinvestment of ITDC hotels
 had necessitated restructuring of the Corporation, whose original mandate is and has been "Development of Tourism". 
 Focussing on this original mandate and also realising the fact that ITDC can only survive if it is a self-sustained and
 profitable organisation, ITDC has initiated a number of steps to rejuvenate itself some of which are as under:-
 
(a)
          Reform/restructuring exercise is presently being carried out in the existing business units and leftover hotel
 properties.  The emphasis is on improving efficiency, and inculcating a corporate work culture environment.
 
(b)
         The various business units of ITDC have now been restructured into independent business divisions which shall aim
 to work as profitable commercial entities.
 
(c)
          A process of reforms is also being carried out in the various divisions of ITDC, which are aimed at hastening the
 decision making process and simplifying activities.  These reforms will help ITDC in effectively competing with
 the private sector.
 
(d)
         ITDC is entering into Memorandum of Understanding with various State Governments with a view to providing
 them services in the field of development of projects/tourist sites, production of collaterals, mounting of sound
 and light shows, marketing of accommodation units, providing training etc.
 
(e)          ITDC has also entered into Memorandum of Understanding with NIIT, Central Cottage Industries Emporium and

28.     The Committee observes that the Performance Budget 2002-2003 of the Department of Tourism still mentions "to
 construct, manage and market hotels, Beach Resorts, Traveller's lodge/restaurants" as one of the main objects of the
 corporation.    The performance of ITDC in the earlier years had been quite successful and the Corporation had succeeded in
 achieving its objectives by promoting the largest hotel chain in the country and providing all touristic services under a single
 window.  The Committee understands that with the decision of the Government to withdraw from the hotel activity,
 changing tourism scenario and financial crunch, ITDC is passing through a difficult time.  The Committee also takes note of
 the steps initiated/avenues being explored by ITDC to rejuvenate itself.  The Committee recommends that keeping in view
 the emerging business scenario, ITDC should redefine its objectives and diversify into new business areas that are
 commercially viable and in line with ITDC's expertise in rendering customised and effective consultancy and implementation
 solutions to the tourism industry.  The Committee hopes that the steps taken by ITDC and a careful planning for the future
 would certainly help the ITDC in its retransformation as a self-sustained and profitable organisation.
 
29.     During the course of the evidence, the Secretary (Tourism), while apprising the Committee of the disinvestment
 process, stated as under:
 
"………Out of 26, 9 hotels have been disposed of.  Some other hotels are in the process of disinvestment.  Four
 hotels have been withheld from the disinvestment process.  They are Ashok Hotel, Lalit Mahal Hotel, Samrat
 Hotel and Janpath Hotel.  The Ashok Hotel had no bidders.  It is a very large hotel.  I think the climate is not quite
 good for such a large investment from industry.  Therefore, it has been decided to retain it for the time being.
 ……… Samrat Hotel, Delhi, has been retained for security purposes."
 
The Committee enquired from the Secretary that on the one hand it was being stated that disinvestment process is very
 satisfactory and on the other hand the Secretary stated that the atmosphere was not conducive for disinvestment of Hotel
 Ashok at this moment.  In response, the Secretary (T) clarified as under:-
 
"…….. However, on the point of disinvestment, I would just like to clarify that it is the Department of Disinvestment,
 that is totally looking at the subject." 
 
30.     The Committee is at a loss to understand the contradictory approach adopted by the Government in the disinvestment
 process.   The Committee feels that if security is the reason for not disinvestment of Samrat Hotel, same parameter should be
 followed for the disinvestment of other hotels also.  Also, while the Government considered the atmosphere as un-conducive
 for the disinvestment of one particular hotel, how could the atmosphere be considered as conducive for disinvestment of
 other hotels?  In such a scenario, the disinvestment of the hotels of ITDC may not fetch the actual commercial value of these
 properties.  The Committee recommends that instead of disinvestment, the Government should explore the possibility of
 giving these hotels on a long-term lease.  In this way the Government will not be losing the asset value of the hotel properties
 and the ownership rights of these hotels will also remain with the Government.

RURAL TOURISM
 
51.     One of the activities to get the focus of the Department of Tourism under 'Major Destination Development including
 Infrastructure' scheme is Rural Tourism.  The Annual Plan 2002-2003 of the Department of Tourism states that priority
 would be given to promote village tourism as the primary tourism product to spread tourism and its socio-economic benefits
 to rural and its new geographic regions.  Key geographic regions would be identified for development and promotion of rural
 tourism.  The implementation would be done through a conversance Committee headed by the District Collector.  A sum of
 Rs.20 lakhs would be made available for each project and activities like improving the environment, hygiene, infrastructure
 etc. would be eligible for assistance.
 
52.     The Committee enquired from the representatives of the Department of Tourism how they proposed to develop Rural
 Tourism and what projects had been conceived in this regard?  The Committee also desired to know the steps taken to
 provide road connectivity in the areas identified for development of tourism.

 concept.  She informed that the Department was going to take up five pilot projects of Rural Tourism.  She clarified that the
 Department was yet to define their own model of Rural Tourism.  They would aim not only to attract Indian and foreign
 tourists but also take into account the marketing aspect as well as the environment aspect.  As regards road connectivity, it
 was informed that they had been in constant touch with the roadways department.
 
54.     The Committee appreciates the idea of the Department of Tourism in developing Rural Tourism. The Committee feels
 that the concept of Rural Tourism would also help in generation of employment at the rural level, dispensing with the need of
 migration to distant places for earning of livelihood by the people living in the rural areas.  However, the Committee feels
 that the infrastructural facilities like road connectivity, wayside amenities etc. are essential in the development of such kind
 of tourism.  While taking steps for development of Rural Tourism, the Department of Tourism should simultaneously take
 steps for developing the infrastructural facilities in these areas.

COORDINATION BETWEEN DEPARTMENT OF TOURISM AND MINISTRY OF RAILWAYS
 
59.     As recommended by the Committee in para 46 above, the domestic tourism has to act as a catalyst for growth and
 development of tourism in the country.  For this it is necessary that besides coordination with various other governmental
 agencies, the Department of Tourism should also have close interaction with the Ministry of Railways for providing
 basic/minimum facilities to the domestic tourists.  It is important to remember that the domestic tourists particularly from the
 middle class do not travel by air.  This class of tourists mostly travels by rail.  Besides promoting domestic tourism, the
 Department should also promote and encourage pilgrimage tourism which has tremendous potential.  However, in this area
 the Department of Tourism have not devoted the kind of attention which it ought to have done, particularly interacting with
 the Ministry of Railways.  There are number of places in the country where basic facilities are lacking not only of
 accommodation but also other minimum facilities like porters.  For example, Kanniyakumari is one of the most important
 tourist destinations in the country.  In fact, the Department of Tourism has identified this destination as one of the
 destinations for development.  To reach Kanniyakumari there are only two modes, namely, the rail and the road transport. 
 However, the facilities at Kanniyakumari railway station are rather rudimentary and there are no porters.
 
60.     The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should take up with the Ministry of Railways for
 providing some alternative method of carrying baggage.  One of the solutions could be by providing trolleys of the kind that
 are available at the airports.  These trolleys could be made available to the tourists against payment as in any case the tourists
 will have to pay the porterage charges to the porters. Similarly, Rameshwaram is also a place of tourist attraction.  The
 Department of Tourism, in collaboration with the Railways, should develop and provide residential accommodation for the
 tourists at reasonable rates, which at present is totally lacking, in fact, non-existent.  Further, the Department of Tourism
 should identify the areas of collaboration with the Railways and submit a detailed note to the Committee in the matter.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
REVIEW OF THE NINTH PLAN (1997-2002) AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2002-2003
 
The Committee is happy to note that not only the plan allocation of Rs.150.00 crores of the Department of Tourism was not
 reduced at R.E. stage during 2001-2002 but the outlay for the year 2002-2003 has also been hiked by 50% from Rs.150.00
 crores to Rs.225.00 crores.  The Committee hopes that the increase in plan outlay would have a positive effect on the
 schemes/projects of the Department. However, The Committee is of the opinion that the financial requirements of the
 Department of Tourism are much more and hopes that the Plan outlays of the Department would be increased even more in
 the coming years.  The Committee also appreciates that during the years 1997-98 to 2000-2001, the Department of Tourism
 has been able to achieve almost 100% utilisation of its revised plan allocations i.e. an amount of Rs.442.78 crores out of the
 revised allocation of Rs.445.00 crores.
(Para 8)
 
The Committee notes that for the financial year 2001-2002, the expenditure upto January 2002 has been only 33.92% i.e.
 Rs.50.89 crores out of the total plan allocation of Rs.150 crores.  However, the Department of Tourism have assured that the
 total allocation would be spent by the end of the financial year i.e. 31
st March 2002.  The Committee is at a loss to
 understand as to how the Department would be able to spend the entire plan allocation in the remaining 2 months of the
 financial year when it could spend only 33.92% of the allocation in the initial 10 months of the year.  The Committee
 recommends that the Department should prioritise the various projects at the beginning of the financial year and ensure early
 utilisation of the funds so as to avoid the under-utilisation of the Plan allocation.
(Para 10)
 
INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF TOURIST CIRCUITS
 
The Committee appreciates the proposal of the Department of Tourism for developing six tourist circuits in the country on an
 annual basis and to provide the necessary infrastructural facilities within these circuits in coordination with the States/private
 sector.  However, the Committee is apprehensive that these projects would not meet the same fate as those of the Mega
 Tourism Projects, which were started during the 8th plan period, but hardly any appreciable progress had been made in any of
 the projects under the scheme.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Tourism should make
 concrete efforts in coordination with all the agencies involved so that early decisions are taken in the matter and the circuits
 identified are developed in a time bound schedule.  The Committee also recommends that the Department of Tourism should
 explore the possibility of including the new-found discovery i.e. Dholavira, the sea beaches and the adventure tourism spots
 in Kutch District of Gujarat in the Western Heritage Circuit.
