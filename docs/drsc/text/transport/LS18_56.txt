4.         The above figures show that the Department of Culture has not utilised its allocated outlay fully during the 9 th
 five-year plan period.   The organisation wise break up of the outlay and the actual expenditure during the 9 th Plan
 period is at Annexure-I.
5.         During the course of examination budgetary details of the Department of Culture it was observed that during
 2001-02 at the RE stage the Department had proposed an R.E. of Rs.203.54 crores for plan activities. The Ministry of
 Finance instead of increasing the allocation at R.E. stage the allocation was reduced to Rs.175.00 crores from the
 originally allocated plan outlay of Rs.190.45 crores. In this connection the Committee had in its     49 th Report
 recommended for increase in plan outlay at RE stage during 2001-02.
6.         One of the reasons cited by the Department of Culture for reduced allocation of the fund was that the Ministry of
 Finance took into consideration the quantum of expenditure incurred till the month of September during that financial
 year and reduced the plan out lay accordingly at R.E. stage.  Giving the reason for less utilisation of fund till the month
 of September the Department of Culture informed the Committee that the Department administers 16 schemes
 providing financial assistance to institutions/NGOs’/individuals.  Selection of the beneficiaries under them are done on
 the recommendations of the Expert Committee constituted by the Department.  The schemes are advertised in print-
media for wide publicity and applications are invited.  The applications are received till the end of July.  After their
 receipt, they are scrutinised and short-listed before being placed before the respective Expert Committees.  Meetings of
 these Committees take place in October-November.  Obviously bulk of the expenditure under the schemes can be
 incurred only in the later half of the year. 
7.         Archaeological Survey of India spends considerable amount of funds annually on conservation and preservation
 of their monuments as also on new excavations.  ASI’s working-season starts after monsoon.  Its bulk of the
 expenditure thus gets booked only during the period October-March.
8.         Institutions/organisations assisted by the Department for conducting exhibitions, seminar, lectures, work-shops
 etc. too start their activities after September.
9.         The Department has some major libraries under its charge.  They have programmes of purchase of books on
 which they spend fairly good amount of funds.  Each one of the libraries have their individual purchase committees.

 By the time the procedural formalities are accomplished and purchasing of books begins on the recommendations of
 these committees, half of the financial year gets over.
10.       It is, therefore, pleaded that no cut should be applied to the plan of the Department on the basis of the
 expenditure incurred upto the end of September.
11.       The Committee notes with concern that during the 9 th plan, an allocation of  Rs.920.41 crores was approved for
 the Department and the Department could utilise only Rs.711.42 crores.  On the one hand the Department is asking for
 more money and on the other hand they are not able to utilise the amount made available to them fully.  The Committee
 recommends that the Department should review its functioning so as to ensure that the plan outlay is utilised in higher
 proportion till the month of September so that the Department can approach the Ministry of Finance strongly for higher
 allocation of plan outlay at R.E. stage.
12.       The Committee also does not approve the arbitrary approach of the Ministry of Finance for arriving at the
 amount at R.E. stage for any Ministry/Department on the basis of expenditure incurred till the month of September in
 any financial year.  The Committee is of the view that the functioning of any Ministry/Department/Agencies should be
 judged on the basis of their financial performance during the last year.  The Ministry of Finance should also take into
 consideration the future requirement of Ministry/Department/Agencies while deciding the plan outlay at R.E. stage.
13.       The Ministry of Finance should also appreciate the fact that the activities of the Department of Culture have
 large social impact albeit its plan allocation is just 0.16% of the total plan-outlay of the Government.  Any cut in its
 allocation would have serious repercussions, which will be far disproportionate to the actual reduction in the budget.
 The Committee therefore recommends that the Ministry of Finance should be more realistic while considering the
 proposals of the Department of Culture and accommodate the financial requirement of plan outlay to the possible
 extent.

22.       Festivals of India was introduced with the objective of promoting cultural links with foreign countries by
 holding festivals of India in those countries and organizing reciprocal festivals of those countries in India.  This also
 helps in projecting India’s cultural image abroad and also enhances the tourism potential of various destinations in
 India.  During the course of examination it is observed that during the year 2001-02 an amount of Rs.3.00 crores was
 allocated at B.E. stage which was reduced to Rs.0.45 crores at R.E. stage and for the year 2002-03 an amount of
 Rs.0.31 crores has been allocated. The Committee desired to know the reasons for reduced allocations.           
23.       The Department of Culture in their reply informed that a budget provisions of Rs.3 crores was allocated for the
 Festival at BE 2001-02 with the anticipation that remaining 25% of the approved estimate submitted by the
 implementing agencies will have to be released during that year i.e. after completion of the Festival.  However, when
 the implementing agencies rendered the audited accounts for the events organized by them, it was seen that substantial
 savings accrued on various items viz. transportation, accommodation, hiring of equipments, etc. as a result of bringing
 down all the rates initially offered by the concerned agencies after negotiations.  Secondly, substantial savings could be
 achieved due to sponsorship of some events.  All the implementing agencies had rendered accounts except Indian
 Council for Cultural Relations, Sangeet Natak Akademy and Department of Science & Technology.  The additional
 requirement over and above the released amount had been met by releasing funds out of this year’s budget, which
 amount to 0.45 crore only.  Therefore, the allocation of Rs.3 crores at BE stage was reduced to Rs.0.45 crores at RE
 stage.  It has been ascertained that the accounts would be rendered by ICCR, SNA and Department of Science &
 Technology shortly and the expenditure incurred by these agencies would not exceed the approved budget for the
 events organized by them.
24.       As regards the allocation of Rs.0.31 crores in the BE 2002-03 it is stated that no proposal for holding a festival
 during this year has been finalized as yet.  The allocation made this year is to meet the office expenditure, publications,
 and expenditure on travels which are necessary for ongoing negotiations.  In case any proposal for holding Festivals in
 India or abroad is received, additional allocation will be sought at RE stage.
25.       The Committee fails to understand the attitude of the Government with regard to Festivals of India.  The Festival
 of India was introduced by the Department with the objective of promoting cultural links with foreign countries on
 reciprocal basis and to have more and more foreign tourists in India.  The Committee notes that Festivals of India are
 not being organised regularly and last year a provision of Rs.3.00 crores was reduced to Rs.0.45 crores and this year
 also only Rs.0.31 crore has been approved.  It appears that Festival of India has lost its utility so far as the financial
 outlays for this activity are concerned. The Committee recommends that the Government should chalk out a strategy to

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
45.       The Archaeological Survey of India was established in 1861.  It is a pioneer organization in field of archaeology-
oriented activities.  The main tasks of ASI include (i) Maintenance, conservation and preservation of centrally protected
 monuments/sites; (ii) conducting archaeological explorations and excavations; (iii) Chemical preservation of
 monuments and all antiquarian remains; (iv) Architectural Survey of monuments; (v) bringing out archaeological
 publications; (vi) carrying out archaeological expeditions outside India (both excavations and conservation); (vii)
 underwater archaeology; (viii) environmental development of monuments through horticultural works; (ix)
 administration of the Institute of Archaeology; (x) Implementation of the Ancient Monuments and  Archaeological
 Sites and Remains Act, (AMASR) in 1958; (xi)  Implementation of Antiquities and Art Treasure (AAT) Act, 1972;
 (xii)  development of epigraphical researches; (xiii) setting up and reorganization of site museums; (xiv) study and
 documentation of colonial architecture; (xv) reviving popular interest and awareness of monuments; (xvi) cultural
 exchange programmes and (xvii) maintenance of archaeological libraries.

SECURITY AT THE TAJ MAHAL, AGRA
61.       The Taj Mahal at Agra is one of the Seven Wonders of the World.  Therefore, there is hardly any need to
 emphasize the importance of maintaining the highest security of the Taj and its surrounding areas.   It has been reported
 that necessary measures have been taken to maintain security of the Taj and to protect it from the possible terrorists
 attacks.  Visitors to the Taj are reportedly frisked by the security personnel.  No type of firearm including match boxes
 are allowed inside the Taj.  Smoking inside the Taj is reportedly prohibited.
 
62.       The Committee, however, has learnt with concern from one of the electronic media channels telecast on April
 15, 2002 that the security of the Taj, particularly in the present day security scenario, is rather lax and hence totally
 inadequate.  A recent fire incident inside the Taj which is reportedly to have been caused by throwing of a cigarette butt
 took almost one hour to brought that small fire in the bush under control.  There were no arrangements for water to
 extinguish fire.  Water had to be taken out from the canal through buckets and that probably explains the delay in
 controlling fire.
 
63.       It was shown that security personnel deployed were not alert and seemed to take their job rather casually?  The
 metal detector through which the visitors are required to pass through is not always operational as there are frequent
 power cuts in Agra.
 
64.       The Committee, therefore, would like the Department of Culture to take up the matter with the State
 Government for ensuring proper protection of the Taj Mahal.  Arrangements should also be made for providing round
 the clock fire fighting services and captive power supply to ensure uninterrupted operations of the metal detector.  For
 security of the Taj complex and its environment, possibilities of raising a separate force dedicated exclusively for its
 protection may also be explored.
 
EXCAVATIONS
65.       Excavation is one of the major activities of the ASI.  The Committee was informed that a number of sites and
 ancient remains, dating from pre-historic to medieval times have been disconcerted in different parts of the country
 under various schemes, including problem oriented investigation. The Committee wanted to know whether there was
 any Expert Committee to advise on excavations to be taken up and whether any consultations are held before taking up
 any site for excavation and development.
66.       The Department of Culture informed that the Standing Committee of the Central Advisory Board of
 Archaeology consisting of five professional archaeologists scrutinize and selects proposals for excavations and
 explorations to be conducted by the Archaeological survey of India and other organizations for each field season.
67.       Further the Committee desired to know the details of backlog in respect of excavation reports and steps taken to
 publish these Reports.
68.       The Department of Culture informed the Committee  that  major/important excavations conducted by the retired
 or serving archaeologists of the Archaeological Survey of India had been identified for detailed reports and the
 archaeologists had been requested  to complete their respective reports in the minimum possible time.  Logistic and
 technical support will be extended to them, if needed.  The Department also furnished a list giving the details of
 excavations in respect of which the final reports are yet to be published.
69.       Status of excavation Report of ASI revealed that 177 cases of excavation work which were taken up by the ASI
 personnel who have since retired without giving the final report.  Another 80 cases of excavation work, which were
 undertaken by the ASI archaeologist who are  still in service and yet to furnish the final reports.  Some of the reports
 are pending since as far back as the year 1947.
70.       The Committee is aghast to find that final reports on excavation works have been pending for more than fifty
 years. Some of the excavation work were taken up during 1947.  The Committee is at a loss to understand as to how the
 Department will get the final reports on such excavation work.  The Committee feels the Department was having a
 lackadaisical approach in the matter.  In absence of these reports, the Department cannot justify the expenditure
 incurred in those excavations.  The Committee recommends that the Department of Culture should identify the cases
 where the final excavation report can be procured and take steps to get them published at the earliest.

GENERAL OBSERVATION
87.       In its last year’s Report on Demands for Grants of the Department of Culture, the Committee had commented
 upon the format of performance budget of the Department of Culture and had recommended that information regarding
 targets proposed to be achieved in respect of any scheme/project should be clearly specified in performance budget. 
 However, the Committee finds that performance budget for the year 2002-2003 is more or less similar to that of the last
 year.  The performance budget does not give any specific information of the ongoing activities in the various
 organizations of the Department of Culture.  For example the performance budget states (at page 53)  “Work on
 Science City at Jalandhar is in progress”.  From perusal of this statement one cannot judge what was the target, what
 progress has been made and what is the current status of the projects.  In absence of such details, the Committee finds it
 difficult to assess the performance of any organization under the Department of Culture.   The Committee, therefore,
 reiterates its earlier recommendation that the performance budget should contain the physical targets proposed vis-à-vis
 the achievements in respect of any projects/schemes of the various organizations.  The physical/financial achievements
 should be clearly indicated together with the reasons for shortfall, if any, in respect of each organization under the
 Department of Culture so that the Committee can make proper assessment of the performance of the various
 organizations.

APPRAISAL OF NINTH FIVE YEAR PLAN

 consideration the future requirement of Ministry/Department/Agencies while deciding the plan outlay at R.E. stage.

 impact albeit its plan allocation is just 0.16% of the total plan-outlay of the Government.  Any cut in its allocation
 would have serious repercussions which will be far disproportionate to the actual reduction in the budget. The
 Committee therefore recommends that the Ministry of Finance should be more realistic while considering the proposals

FESTIVALS OF INDIA
The Committee fails to understand the attitude of the Government with regard to Festivals of India.  The Festival of
 India was introduced by the Department with the objective of promoting cultural links with foreign countries on
 reciprocal basis and to have more and more foreign tourists in India.  The Committee notes that Festivals of India are
 not being organised regularly and last year a provision of Rs.3.00 crores was reduced to Rs.0.45 crores and this year
 also only Rs.0.31 crore has been approved.  It appears that Festival of India has lost its utility so far as the financial
 outlays for this activity are concerned. The Committee recommends that the Government should chalk out a strategy to
 make it a regular phenomenon so that the objective of promotion of cultural links with foreign countries should be
 achieved which in turn will also attract the foreign tourists to India.

 science cities, the private sector may not have active role in such activities. Therefore, it becomes obligatory for the
 Central Government to promote and popularize the science in the country.  The Committee notes that there is already
 active participation of the State Governments in setting of Science City Projects.  The Central Government should
 supplement the efforts and ensure that there should be no fund constraints for Science City Projects.  The Committee
 recommends that Science City Project at Jalandhar should be completed at the earliest so that there is no cost escalation
 burden on the State of Punjab.
(Para 82)
LIBRARIES
The Committee notes with concern that the libraries (National Library, Kolkata, Central Reference Library, Kolkata and
 Central Secretariat Library, New Delhi) under the Department have not utilised their sanctioned allocation during the
 9
th plan period.  Most of the libraries under the Department are not upto the standard.  Even though sufficient funds are
 available the libraries are not utilising the funds allocated to them.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
 Department should take due care in this regard and also ensure that the allocated funds are utilised fully. The
 Committee feels that the function of the libraries is not merely maintaining the books but also the acquisition of books. 
 If the libraries do not acquire new books, the very purpose of the setting up of the libraries will get defeated.  The
 Committee also recommends that the Department of Culture should prepare its future plans year-wise for acquisition of
 books in the next three years and apprise the Committee.
(Para 86)
GENERAL OBSERVATION
In its last year’s Report on Demands for Grants of the Department of Culture, the Committee had commented upon the
 format of performance budget of the Department of Culture and had recommended that information regarding targets
 proposed to be achieved in respect of any scheme/project should be clearly specified in performance budget.  However,
 the Committee finds that performance budget for the year 2002-2003 is more or less similar to that of the last year.  The
 performance budget does not give any specific information of the ongoing activities in the various organizations of the
 Department of Culture.  For example the performance budget states (at page 53)  “Work on Science City at Jalandhar is
 in progress”.  From perusal of this statement one cannot judge what was the target, what progress has been made and
 what is the current status of the projects.  In absence of such details, the Committee finds it difficult to assess the
 performance of any organization under the Department of Culture.   The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier
 recommendation that the performance budget should contain the physical targets proposed vis-à-vis the achievements
 in respect of any projects/schemes of the various organizations.  The physical/financial achievements should be clearly
 indicated together with the reasons for shortfall, if any, in respect of each organization under the Department of Culture
 so that the Committee can make proper assessment of the performance of the various organizations.
