4.      On scrutiny of the said Action Taken Replies, the Committee has made observations on each item of Action
 taken/proposed to be taken by the Department. The recommendations of the Committee contained in the One Hundred
 and Second Report, action taken thereon by the Department, and recommendations/observations of the Committee on

(Recommendation at Para 23)
5.      The Committee finds that despite its earlier recommendations on the aspect of funds utilization and the
 need for constant monitoring of projects, the Department of Road Transport and Highways has done precious
 little to improve the situation. This is evident from the reply of the Department with regard to non-utilisation of
 funds that the allocation for road sector is based on anticipated implementation of projects every year. The
 trend from the year 2002-03 onwards would reveal that the actual utilization has been more or less steady and
 consistent. The slippages occur when major projects do not get implemented for the years they are projected for
 implementation. For instance, allocations for programmes of NHDP Phase-III and SARDP-NE did not
 materialize fully because the approvals were not available in the time-frame projected. The Committee desires to
 know as to why approvals were not available in time and whether any responsibility has been fixed in the
 matter. The Committee observes that the budgetary allocation of the Department of Road Transport and
 Highways was substantially increased from 2004-05 (revised estimates) of Rs. 8566.00 crore  to Rs. 13293.75
 crore during 2005-06 and for 2006-07 (budget estimates) the allocation has again been increased to Rs 15307.31
 crore. The Committee, therefore, re-emphasises that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should
 devise strategies, in such a way so that they are able to fully utilize the funds placed at their disposal. They
 should also evolve some mechanism to identify bottlenecks causing delays and work towards resolving all such
 hindrances in coordination with all the concerned agencies. This would also help them to avoid time overrun and
 consequential cost overrun of the projects to be executed by them.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
6.      The reason for non-utilisation  of funds cannot be solely attributed to the delayed implementation of projects. It is
 also due to the fact, particularly, in case of NHDP Phase-III that the projects awarded have actually not yet been started
 owing to the reason that the stipulated time of 180 days given to the concessionaires for achieving financial close is yet
 to be over. The disbursement of grants will start only after the appointed date. Besides, in most of the projects, the
 bidders have offered negative grant against the grant anticipated by NHAI to be paid to the concessionaire. This has
 resulted in low level of expenditure which gives impression of less utilization of the allocated funds.
7.      Slow progress in some of the projects has also resulted into the less utilization of funds. The reasons for the slow
 progress include tardy performance of the contractors, delayed acquisition of land, delay in shifting of utilities, delay in
 forest/environment clearances etc. However, as suggested by the Committee, the bottlenecks causing delays have
 been/are being identified on project to project basis and remedial/corrective actions are also being taken wherever

8.      Union Minister of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways visited the North Eastern region in August, 2004 and
 held discussions with the Chief Ministers, Ministers, Members of Parliament of Assam at Guwahati and with the Chief
 Minister of Mizoram and the PWD Ministers of North Eastern States at Shillong and with the offices of North Eastern

 Investment Board (PIB) in its meetings held on 4th and 17th February, 2005 and approved on 21st February, 2005. It

 (CoS). The proposal was subsequently considered by the CoS on 5th April, 2005, 15th July, 2005 and 3rd August,
 2005. The phase-A of ‘Special Accelerated Road Development Programme’ in North Eastern region (SARDP-NE)
 comprising improvement to 1310 km. length of National Highways, State Roads and GS Roads at an estimated cost of
 Rs. 4618 crore was approved on 22.9.2005. Subsequent to the approval by CCEA, implementing agencies
 (NHAI/BRO/State PWDs) were directed to prepare Detailed Project Reports (DPRs). The DPRs were in progress by
 the implementing agencies tilll the end of 31st March, 2006; therefore, funds earmarked for SARDP-NE during 2005-
06 could not be utilized.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
9.      The Committee takes a serious view of the non-utilization of funds earmarked for SARDP-NE during 2005-
06. The National Highways serve not only as life line but also are strategically important taken into account the
 location of North East States. Therefore, the Committee again emphasise that the Government must take
 measures to ensure that funds earmarked for road sector are utilized optimally. The Committee also
 recommends that Department of Road Transport and Highways should take step so that the implementing
 agencies submit DPRs in time.
 
(Recommendation at Para 24)
10.    The Committee notes that in the Mid-Term Appraisal of the Tenth Five Year Plan, it is recommended that
 the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways should prepare a detailed programme for the next two
 years keeping in view the level of budgetary support available and the need for leveraging to the maximum
 extent by adopting a proper mix of Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) and Build Operate and
 Transfer (BOT) projects. In view of the repeated under-utilization of budget allocation year after year, the
 Committee feels that the Department should not feel aggrieved by the fact that the budget provision for 2006-07
 has been enhanced by only 10 per cent from the budget provision for the year 2005-06 despite enhancement of
 11.2 per cent in the cost of construction during 2005 following increase in price indices of the basic ingredients of
 the road sector-steel, cement and bitumen. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways should devise effective institutional mechanisms to constantly monitor the progress of
 the projects under implementation and to ensure optimum utilization of funds. This mechanism should also
 involve means to fix responsibility at Project Head Level for the delay in completion of projects thereby acting as
 a deterrent for cost and time over-runs. The Committee recommends that the Department should also explore
 the possibility of opening of more budget / accounting offices keeping in view the increasing number of projects
 under implementation.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
11.    As per the recommendations in the Mid-Term Appraisal of the Tenth Five Year Plan, a comprehensive month-
wise and project-wise monitorable  target has been fixed, both for the physical and financial progress. The actual
 achievement against these targets is constantly being monitored and the reasons for slippages are critically analyzed to
 avoid their recurrence and to improve the progress of work. Dy. Chairman, Planning Commission also hold meetings
 on quarterly basis to review the progress against the monitorable  targets. As suggested by the Committee, the projects
 are being awarded by adopting a mix of EPC and BOT projects for NHDP Phase-II and BOT projects for NHDP Phase-
Ill onwards as per the policy decision taken by the Government that all future projects would be awarded only on BOT
 basis (either based on the toll or annuity or a suitable toll / annuity hybrid), with EPC award being made in specified
 exceptional cases only.
12.    To ensure optimum utilization of funds, effective institutional mechanisms are already in place in NHAI both at

14.    The recommendation of the Committee to explore the possibility for opening more budget/accounting sections

(Recommendation at Para 42)
15.    The Committee is constrained to note that the Golden Quadrilateral Project which was initially scheduled
 to be completed by the end of 2003 will now stretch upto 2007-08 and only 557 km. of North South - East West
 (NSEW) Corridor Project of 7,300 km. has been completed against the deadline for completion of entire project
 by December, 2007. The Committee recommends that action for revised estimates awarding the Ganjam
 -Icchapuram section of National Highway 5 of 50.8 km. length in Orissa, which is a part of the Golden
 Quadrilateral project are yet to be awarded, should be completed at the earliest and it should be ensured that
 the actual construction work is completed within the stipulated time-frame. The Committee takes exception to
 the fact that in NS-EW Corridor project, the target for award of contracts of 5,722 km. by December, 2005 in a
 phased manner could not be achieved by the Department and the target date has been shifted to March, 2006. In
 this context the Committee is surprised to note that the BE allocation for NHAI has been scaled down
 considerably during 2004-05 and 2005-06 in the respective revised estimates. The reasons for slowing down of
 the pace of construction put forward by the NHAI is unacceptable to the Committee in view of the fact that
 although Golden Quadrilateral Project is in the final stage of completion, but considerable work is still pending
 in NS-EW Corridor Project. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should play a pro-
active role in ensuring strict compliance of the targets fixed for completion of awarded projects and also the
 schedules for award of contracts in the pending projects. The Committee is of the view that proper verification
 of bank guarantees submitted by contractors should be undertaken at the initial stage itself to avoid termination
 of the contracts at a later stage. The Committee notes that increasing number of contracts are under
 implementation and being awarded. Therefore, it is recommended that the Department should be vigilant in
 respect of each contract and should strictly  enforce the compensation conditions in the contracts in respect of
 non-performing contractors. The Committee, further, recommends that the Department should not wait for the
 targeted date of completion to initiate action but should fix short-term targets for the contractors so that the
 projects can be monitored constantly and immediate and early remedial action may be taken in case of
 anticipated delays. The Committee also feels that the Department should draw-up a database of the performance
 of the contractors involved in NHDP and encourage outstanding contractors, who strictly comply with the
 construction standards and quality control and also complete the projects well within the targetted date, to
 undertake more and more projects as incentives.
 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
16.    The recommendations of the Committee are noted for further compliance. In regard to completion of Ganjam -
 Ichaapuram section of NH-5, the work was rescinded due to non-performance of the contractor. New tenders were
 called based on the court decision and work awarded to a new contractor but the expelled contractor again filed a writ
 in the District Court. NHAI filed a relief petition in Supreme Court. The Hon’ble Supreme Court heard the case on
 3.7.2006 and vacated the stay granted to the expelled contractor relating to award of balance of work on Ganjam  -
 Ichaapuram section of NH-5 by District Court. Agreement with new contractor has been signed on 5.7.2006 with
 stipulated date of completion of work as 12.11.2008.
17.    It is a fact that BE allocation for NHAI during the year 2004-05 and 2005-06 has been scaled down. The reduction
 is mainly in respect of external assistance against Externally Aided Projects and also in respect of IEBRs which
 constitute the borrowings against 54 EC bonds and the overdraft from commercial banks. The reason for reduction in
 external assistance is that the works which were anticipated for award during 2004-05 were delayed due to several
 reasons, prominent amongst them were reinvitation of bids due to very high quotes by the bidders and also the delay in
 getting approval/clearance from the multi-lateral agencies. The reduction in IEBRs during 2004-05 was mainly due to
 the reason that the available funds from surplus of previous years were sufficient to meet the projects related
 expenditure. Hence, it was not thought prudent to borrow funds from the market. During 2005-06, a conscious decision
 was taken that instead of keeping surplus funds in the form of liquidity with NHAI, the funds may be arranged under
 overdraft facility from banks to bridge the mismatch between resources and expenditure. The provision under BE was

19.    Regarding non-performing contractors, a Committee of Chief General Managers has been constituted to look into

20.    Regarding encouraging of outstanding contractors, the contract agreements contain provisions of incentives for

(Recommendation at Para 48)
21.    The Committee is surprised to note that no new works under the scheme of Inter- State Connectivity and
 Economic Importance has been sanctioned so far during the year 2006-07. The Committee recommends that the
 Department should expedite the on-going projects and should devise clear guidelines on sanctioning such
 projects so that new places of economic and tourist importance can be provided connectivity thereby, giving a
 boost to the economic growth and also to the efforts for realizing the vast tourism potential of our country.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
22.    The present guidelines for approval of road/bridge works under the Centrally sponsored scheme of Inter State
 Connectivity and Economic Importance (ISC and EI) broadly cover following categories of works:—
              (i)    Inter-State roads/bridges necessary for ensuring through communication;
              (ii)   Roads/bridges connecting National Highways;
              (iii)   Roads/bridges required for opening up new areas for economic growth where railway facilities cannot be
 provided in near future; and
              (iv)  Roads/bridges which would contribute materially to rapid development, e.g. in hilly areas and areas having
 rich mineral resources for exploitation.
23.    CRF Rules are under formulation. Inclusion of provisions covering road/bridge projects connecting places of
 tourist importance under the schemes of EI and ISC is under consideration. The State Governments have already been
 requested to expedite the on-going projects.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
24.    The Committee observes that Department of Road Transport and Highways has prepared guidelines for
 approval of road/bridge works under the centrally sponsored scheme of Inter State Connectivity and Economic
 Importance (ISC&EI). However, the Committee is also surprised to notice that the Department has not
 explained the reasons for not sanctioning any new work under this scheme during the year 2006-07 despite being
 pointed out by the Committee. The Committee accordingly recommends that Central Road Fund Rules should
 be formulated soon and revision of provisions for EI and ISC scheme should be done expeditiously.
(Recommendation at Para 51)

 finalise its report by the end of March, 2006. The Committee expects that after finalisation of the report further

(Recommendation at Para 57)
31.    The Committee feels that the instances of collapse of newly built or under construction National Highways
 is a cause of concern and it also leads to the delay in the timely completion of project, which in turn disturb the
 budgetary estimates and planning. The Committee recommends that the recommendations contained in the
 reports of the Inquiry Committees should be utilized for avoiding faulty constructions in other projects. Besides,
 exemplary action should be taken against the contractor, concessionaire and supervision consultants. The
 Committee also finds confusion with regard to the norms/guidelines for designing of roads in the country. The
 Committee also takes exception to the fact that despite having built thousands of kilometers of highways, no
 norms for designs of roads and bridges have been framed and international norms are being followed. The
 Committee feels that there exists a need for indignisation of norms regarding designing of roads and bridges
 taking into account the local site conditions. The Committee also expects that the designs of roads and bridges
 executed by NHAI and other agencies should be strictly according to the local site conditions.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
32.    The remedial measures and restoration work as per recommendations of the Enquiry Committee is being taken up
 by National Highway Authority of India (NHAI). The recommendations of the Committee will be taken into account
 for future projects in order to avoid any such occurrence.
33.    The Ministry issues guidelines specifying standards and specifications for conceptualization, planning, design and
 implementation of road projects pertaining to National Highways and other Centrally sponsored schemes and updates
 the same from time to time based upon the findings of specific research schemes, studies conducted, code of
 practices/guidelines issued by Indian Roads Congress and Bureau of Indian Standards, experience gained, feedback
 received etc. However, in the absence of any specific norms in these guidelines in respect of particular instances, the
 international norms are generally followed.
34.    The projects of the NHAI are designed by the specialized consultants following the norms/guidelines of Ministry

(Recommendation at Para 59)
35.    The Committee feels that the encroachment of land under National Highways is a serious issue as it
 hampers the smooth development of highways and is also an irritant in the timely completion of various
 projects. The Committee recommends that suitable action should be taken to prevent encroachment in the first
 place. The Committee is surprised to note that there was considerable delay in enforcing the Control of National
 Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002. Further, the Presiding Officers for only two of eight National Highway
 Tribunals have been appointed so far. The Committee notes that the National Highway Administrations have
 been empowered to remove encroachments on National Highways under its jurisdiction and the power of appeal
 vests with National Highway Tribunal, which is the Appellate Authority on all such matters. The Committee is
 of the view that the number of National Highway Tribunals (NHT) is disproportionate to the number of
 National Highway Administrations set up so far. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the number of
 National Highway Tribunals (NHT) should be increased and preferably one in each State should be opened. The
 Committee also recommends that the existing vacancies of Presiding Officers should be filled up at the earliest.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
36.    The Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002 has come into effect from 27.1.2005. In order to
 implement the various provisions of the Act, 192 National Highway Administrations and 8 National Highway Tribunals
 have been set up. As per the Act, Highway Administrations should be in possession of the land registers showing the
 details of the land under their jurisdiction within the Right of Way of the National Highway. The work relating to the
 preparation of land registers has been very slow due to non-availability of proper records. As a result, the action on the
 part of the Highway Administrations to remove encroachments from the National Highway land has been slow. Till
 now, cases have come up only in the Chandigarh Branch of the Tribunal. The slow progress in the implementation of
 the provisions of the Act is due to the problems involved in the preparation of the land registers and not due to
 inadequate number of National Highway Tribunals. Efforts are on to speed up the process of preparation of land
 registers. The proposals approved by the Government in connection with the enactment of the Control of National
 Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002 have provisions for the setting up of 233 National Highway Administrations
 and 28 National Highway Tribunals. Therefore, as .and when the work load increases, the number of National Highway
