6.  The Planning Commission has projected that the exports and imports will grow at the rates of 20  
per cent and 23 per cent respectively during the 11 th plan period. By 2011-12 it is projected that India’s merchandise trade will 
reach 650 billion US Dollars as against 312.86 billion US Dollars during 2007-08. 
7.  The Committee notes that the shipping sector plays an important role in India’s economy. Almost 90% of  the 
country’s trade by volume is conducted via  sea and the country boasts of having the largest m erchant shipping fleet 
among the developing nations. The Indian shipping i ndustry not only transports national and internatio nal cargos, but 
also provides various other facilities such as ship building, ship-repairing, lighthouse facilities, fr eight forwarding, etc. 
However, certain core problems such as Port congest ion and lack of depth in channels are plaguing the shipping 
industry.  
8.  The Committee notes that the working group constitu ted for Eleventh Plan had recommended an outlay of 
Rs.7689 crore as GBS for Shipping and IWT Sector, b ut the Planning Commission restricted it to Rs.1301  crore only. 
The Committee, in view of the increasing share of S hipping Sector in Indian trade, feels that the curr ent level of 
allocations is highly inadequate and it will curtai l the growth of shipping and IWT. The Committee, th erefore, 
recommends that the Government of India should increase the allocation for both shipping as well as port sectors for the 
remaining Plan period. The Ministry of Shipping, th erefore, should also come forward with proposals fo r development 
and the Planning Commission should allocate the funds for the purpose.  
9.  The Committee emphasises the need for strengthening the shipping sector to take up with the demands granted by 
the globalisation and liberalisation. The Indian sh ipping industry must acquire new heights in terms o f demand and 
infrastructural development.

15.  The Committee notes that one of the reasons for the  under utilization of the allocation during 2009-10 is a general 
cut in expenditure introduced as a result of fiscal restrictions. The Committee strongly disapproves the across the board 
imposition of fiscal restrictions without taking in to account the relative merit and importance of the  schemes/projects 
for our economy. The Committee recommends that Mini stry of Shipping may take up the matter with Planni ng 
Commission for a review and not apply such general restrictions on Shipping and Port Sectors that play  major role in 
Indian trade.  
16.  The Committee notes the that Ministry of Shipping could not utilize even the reduced allocation made at RE stage. 
Out of Rs.1566.33 crore, only an amount of Rs.1110 crore is likely to be utilized during 2009-10. The Committee finds no 
justifiable reason for this. In such a situation, t he Ministry of Shipping may not justify any demand for higher 
allocation. The Committee recommends the Ministry s hould be proactive and take steps in advance to ens ure that 
allocated amount is utilized evenly and optimally.  
17.  The Committee expresses dismay over non-utilisation  of funds under Ship Building Subsidy Scheme till d ate, 
though the Scheme was discontinued in August, 2007.  The reason given to the Committee was that it bein g a ‘well 
defined’ Scheme with ‘fairly rigid and detailed requirement’, none of the applications pertaining to pre-2007 cases were 
found complete, where ‘in principle’ approval was g iven. It appears to the Committee that the Scheme h ad not been 
structured properly and was full of inherent compli cations. It sounds very strange that the requiremen t under the 
scheme were so unrealistic that these could not be met so far since 2007. The Committee recommends the  Government 
to revisit this scheme by taking a practical and ho listic view. And if it wishes to utilize the amount  kept for the Subsidy 
Scheme, the applicants be helped to meet the requirement to avail the benefit of the Scheme.

PORTS-MAJOR AND MINOR  
23.  It was brought to the notice of the Committee that in 1999 Major Ports accounted for almost 95% o f the country’s total 
cargo handling while the share of minor ports stood at only 5%. However, in 2009, share of the major ports plummeted to 65%, 
whereas that of minor ports increased to 35%. Minor  ports offer much better facilities as compared to the major ones. Cargo 
clearance and other formalities are undertaken in a hassle-free manner, which lure shipping firms to avail their services. 
24.  Another advantage enjoyed by minor ports is th at since they are listed under the concurrent list of the Indian Constitution, 
most of the decisions regarding their developmental activities are undertaken by the respective State Governments. In fact many 
States have undertaken several measures to expand t heir port capacity. Minor port projects worth Rs 5, 163 crore to be 
undertaken under the National Maritime Development Programme (NMDP) are expected to further bolster bu siness of minor 
ports in the country. 
25.  On a query the Secretary informed the Committe e that “the cargo handling at Colombo is cheaper as  compared to Indian 
Ports. Indian ports charges are notified by Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP), an independent re gulatory authority, on 
cost plus basis. The cost of the port operations is  higher because of multiple handling of cargo, poor  infrastructure and high 
levels of manual handling of work. 
26.  The Ministry further informed that Port of Sin gapore Authority is the biggest Port in Singapore, handling primarily 
containers. They handled 2,99,20,000 TEUs in the year 2008. Indian Ports, except JNPT, are handling multiple cargo liquid and 
solid bulk as well as containers. So, the traffic handled by them cannot be compared. However, the containers handled by JNPT 
are 39,53,000 TEUs in 2008-09. Chennai had handled 11,44,000 TEU and Visakhapatnam handled 88,000 TEUs  in the year 
2008-09. All the Indian Ports together handled only 65,88,000 TEUs. . 
27.  The Committee is of the view that the ports play a major role in the economic development of the count ry. The 
Committee, however, notes that the. major ports of the country are already facing stiff competition fr om the ports of 
neighbouring countries particularly from Sri Lanka and Singapore. Now, development of private and mino r ports are 
taking away the business of Major Ports. This according to the Committee is clear reflection on the manner in which the 

Government are handling the affairs of the Major Po rts, wherein the crore of rupees of tax payers have  been invested. 
The Committee recommends that the Government should ensure level playing field for the Major Ports so that they can 
perform and compete with private and minor ports. I n this connection the Committee would like to reite rate its earlier 
recommendations contained in its 134th report which is reproduced as below :– 
“The Committee notes that the Department of Shippin g is in the process of formulating a Maritime Polic y for the 
maritime sector which seeks to ensure a coordinated  development of Ports, Shipping, Inland Water Trans port, 
Ship building and Ship repairing with the objective  to upgrade and modernize the port infrastructure i n India to 
benchmark its performance against global standards.  The Committee also gathered from the replies that the 
regulatory authority will be set up at an appropria te time. The Committee observes that since much of the 
activities in the port sector are taking place with  private participation, there is need to establish regulatory 
authority well in time and that too before coming a cross any problem for better control, coordination and 
monitoring for achieving excellence in the port and  shipping sector besides addressing serious securit y 
implications. The Committee, therefore, recommends,  the Department to take steps for setting up of a r egulatory 
authority on the pattern of Telecom Regulatory Auth ority of India (TRAI) and Insurance Regulatory and 
Development Authority (IRDA) for creating a level p laying field and foster healthy competition and to encourage 
investments for the shipping and port sector beside s monitoring performance standards and adjudication  of 
disputes and disposal of appeal thereof.”  
28.  The Committee appreciates the performance of minor ports, whose cargo handling has grown up from mere 5 per 
cent in 1999 to 35 per cent of total cargo handling  in 2009. This only that implies that Government’s policy has not 
allowed major ports to grow and therefore, is losin g business. The Government themselves admitted that  minor ports 
offer better facilities, cargo clearance and other formalities in a hassle free manner.  
29.  The Committee is of the view that this is high time  when the ports within the country both major and p rivate 
should be developed in an integrated manner so that  they can unitedly compete with the ports of neight bouring 
countries.  
30.  It was brought to the notice of the Committee that some major countries give exclusive coastal Shippin g rights to 
their vessels. The Committee, therefore, recommends that while considering the demand of the foreign v essels for cargo 
shipping the interests of the Indian vessels and Indian Shipping Industry should be fully protected. 
International Container Trans-shipment Terminal (lCTT) 
31.  The Ministry of Shipping informed that the Gov ernment of India had approved the Capital Dredging for deepening and 
widening of the approach channel and berth basin of  International Container Trans-shipment Terminal (I CTT), at an estimated 
cost of Rs.381.25 crore, with a provision of fundin g through Grants-in-aid to the extent of Rs.297.42 crore and loan assistance 
of Rs.83.83 crore for the capital investment. The following are the advantages of the project: 
(i) A substantial portion of country’s container tr affic can be handled through this trans-shipment te rminal, which is 
presently trans-shipped through Colombo, Salalah, Dubai and Singapore. 
(iii) Saving to the tune of Rs.6000 to Rs.16,000 pe r container to the Indian Exporters. The anticipated traffic that would 
have the benefit on the fully developed stage is to  the tune of 1 million containers reckoning the min imum saving 
of Rs.6000 per container the annual anticipated economic saving is to the tune of Rs.600 crore. As this saving is in 
the freight cost and container handling cost in the  third country foreign port, this saving can also t o be reckoned as 
saving in foreign exchange. 
(iii) With the reduced transportation cost, India’s  produce will become more competitive in the intern ational market 
which consequently increases the foreign trade volume. 
(iv) Economic utilization of the cargo carrying cap acity of Indian Railways and Konkan Railways and Na tional 
Highways since a substantial volume of containers will be transported between hinterland and Port by land mode. 
(v) Additional employment opportunities to the tune  of 25,000 to 50,000.  
(vi)  Development of Port based industries. 

32.  As per the contract conditions of the capital dredging project, during the progress of work, inte rim payments are to be

completion of capital dredging work. The capital dr edging work is scheduled for completion by May 2010 . Navigational aids

the import of crude oil to Indian Refineries, in mo vement/storage of the indigenously produced Bombay High crude and in 
cross trades. The Company’s 10 Product Tankers are engaged in coastal movement of petroleum products a s well as in cross 
trades. SCI’s 5 specialised vessels are engaged in transportation for import/coastal movement of LPG, import of Phosphoric 
acid as also in movement of Ammonia. 
38.  The company operates four India-centric contai ner services, in consortia with internationally reputed Shipping Lines., one 
each serving the U.K.-Continent and India Middle Ea st trades and two serving the Far East trade. A Joi nt service with M/s 
Rickmers Linie, Germany for shipment of break-bulk/ general cargo from European ports to India. Joint F eeder Services-The

39.  An outlay of Rs. 2,408 crore for SCI has been approved for meeting its payment obligations toward s vessels already on 
order and investments in Joint Ventures. 
40.  The Committee was informed that the year 2009- 10 being the mid-year of the Eleventh Five Year Pla n, the performance 
of the SCI was reviewed. While reviewing the progress made during the Five Year Plan period, SCI considered the achievement 
made so far, the requirement of SCI in the next few  years, the prevailing down turn in the shipping ma rkets, the financing 

difficulties faced by shipping companies for acquis ition of ships and also the opportunities available  to acquire 
secondhand/resale vessels at lower values. 
41.  Based on the review, SCI had substituted some of the projects of BE 2009-10 with secondhand/resale acquisition projects 
at RE stage and some projects have been deferred. This has resulted in reduction in the projected outlay at RE stage. 
42.  With regard to the increase in the outlay requ irement of BE 2010-11 as compared to BE & RE 2009-1 0, SCI has 
envisaged acquisition of 62 vessels during the Elev enth Plan period. Out of these 62 vessels, during t he first 3 years (till date) 
SCI has been able to order/acquire 25 vessels and balance 37 vessels are planned during the remaining 2 years. 
43.  The Committee was informed that the shipping i ndustry has been going through turmoil due to the o ngoing recession in 
the global economy. This has also resulted in decline in vessel prices from late 2008 onwards in the n ew as well as secondhand 
markets. Due to this market turbulence and in antic ipation of better prices, SCI had taken a decision to wait till the new ship

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS –– AT A GLANCE  
ELEVENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN (2007-2012) OF THE MINISTRY OF SHIPPING 
  The Committee notes that the shipping sector play s an important role in India’s economy. Almost 90% of the 
country’s trade by volume is conducted via  sea and the country boasts of having the largest m erchant shipping fleet 
among the developing nations. The Indian shipping i ndustry not only transports national and internatio nal cargos, but 
also provides various other facilities such as ship building, ship-repairing, lighthouse facilities, fr eight forwarding, etc. 
However, certain core problems such as Port congest ion and lack of depth in channels are plaguing the shipping 
industry.     (Para 7)  
  The Committee notes that the working group consti tuted for Eleventh Plan had recommended an outlay o f 
Rs.7689 crore as GBS for Shipping and IWT Sector, b ut the Planning Commission restricted it to Rs.1301  crore only. 
The Committee, in view of the increasing share of S hipping Sector in Indian trade, feels that the curr ent level of 
allocations is highly inadequate and it will curtai l the growth of shipping and IWT. The Committee, th erefore, 
recommends that the Government of India should increase the allocation for both shipping as well as port sectors for the 
remaining Plan period. The Ministry of Shipping, th erefore, should also come forward with proposals fo r development 
and the Planning Commission should allocate the funds for the purpose.  (Para 8)  
  The Committee emphasises the need for strengtheni ng the shipping sector to take up with the demands granted by 
the globalisation and liberalisation. The Indian sh ipping industry must acquire new heights in terms o f demand and 
infrastructural development. (Para 9)  
ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2010-11 
  The Committee notes that one of the reasons for t he under utilization of the allocation during 2009-10 is a general 
cut in expenditure introduced as a result of fiscal restrictions. The Committee strongly disapproves the across the board 
imposition of fiscal restrictions without taking in to account the relative merit and importance of the  schemes/projects 
for our economy. The Committee recommends that Mini stry of Shipping may take up the matter with Planni ng 
Commission for a review and not apply such general restrictions on Shipping & Port Sectors that play m ajor role in 
Indian trade.    (Para 15)  
  The Committee notes the that Ministry of Shipping  could not utilize even the reduced allocation made at RE stage. 
Out of Rs.1566.33 crore, only an amount of Rs. 110 crore is likely to be utilized during 2009-10. The Committee finds no 
justifiable reason for this. In such a situation, t he Ministry of Shipping may not justify any demand for higher 
allocation. The Committee recommends the Ministry s hould be proactive and take steps in advance to ens ure that 
allocated amount is utilized evenly and optimally.           (Para 16)  
  The Committee expresses dismay over non-utilisati on of funds under Ship Building Subsidy Scheme till  date, 
though the Scheme was discontinued in August, 2007.  The reason given to the Committee was that it bein g a ‘well 
defined’ Scheme with ‘fairly rigid and detailed requirement’, none of the applications pertaining to pre-2007 cases were 
found complete, where ‘in principle’ approval was g iven. It appears to the Committee that the Scheme h ad not been 
structured properly and was full of inherent compli cations. It sounds very strange that the requiremen t under the 
scheme were so unrealistic that these could not be met so far since 2007. The Committee recommends the  Government 
to revisit this scheme by taking a practical and ho listic view. And if it wishes to utilize the amount  kept for the Subsidy 
Scheme, the applicants be helped to meet the requirement to avail the benefit of the Scheme.        (Para 17)  
PORT SECTOR 
  The Committee finds that the total allocation for  ports sector during 2009-10 under both plan and no n-plan was 
Rs.700.03 crore. Out of this amount Rs. 279.11 cror e only the Ministry was able to utilize up to Febru ary, 2010 which is 
close to 40 % of the total allocation. This slow pa ce of programme implementation is a matter of serio us concern. 
Already our port carriage is negligible compared to  the global capacity. Time bound completion of proj ects not only 
controls cost escalations but also the revenue earn ings directly and indirectly as a result of increas ed imports and 

exports. The Committee therefore recommends that th e Ministry should have a clear picture of the progr ammes to be 
implemented during the financial year with the allocated grants. (Para 22)  
PORTS-MAJOR AND MINOR 
  The Committee is of the view that the ports play a major role in the economic development of the cou ntry. The 
Committee, however, notes that the major ports of t he country are already facing stiff competition fro m the ports of 
neighbouring countries particularly from Sri Lanka and Singapore.Now, development of private and minor  ports are 
taking away the business of Major Ports. This according to the Committee is clear reflection on the manner in which the 
Government are handling the affairs of the Major Po rts, wherein the crore of rupees of tax payers have  been invested. 
The Committee recommends that the Government should ensure level playing field for the Major Ports so that they can 
perform and compete with private and minor ports. I n this connection the Committee would like to reite rate its earlier 
recommendations contained in its 134th report which is reproduced as below : 
“The Committee notes that the Department of Shippin g is in the process of formulating a Maritime Polic y for the 
maritime sector which seeks to ensure a coordinated  development of Ports, Shipping, Inland Water Trans port, 
Ship building and Ship repairing with the objective  to upgrade and modernize the port infrastructure i n India to 
benchmark its performance against global standards.  The Committee also gathered from the replies that the 
regulatory authority will be set up at an appropria te time. The Committee observes that since much of the 
activities in the port sector are taking place with  private participation, there is need to establish regulatory 
authority well in time and that too before coming a cross any problem for better control, coordination and 
monitoring for achieving excellence in the port and  shipping sector besides addressing serious securit y 
implications. The Committee, therefore, recommends,  the Department to take steps for setting up of a r egulatory 
authority, on the pattern of Telecom Regulatory Aut hority of India (TRAI) and Insurance Regulatory and  
Development Authority (IRDA) for creating a level p laying field and foster healthy competition and to encourage 
investments for the shipping and port sector beside s monitoring performance standards and adjudication  of 
disputes and disposal of appeal thereof.” (Para 27)  
  The Committee appreciates the performance of mino r ports, whose cargo handling has grown up from mere 5 per 
cent in 1999 to 35 per cent of total cargo handling  in 2009. This only that implies that Government’s policy has not 
allowed major ports to grow and therefore, is losing business.The Government themselves admitted that minor ports 
offer better facilities, cargo clearance and other formalities in a hassle free manner.       (Para 28)  
  The Committee is of the view that this is high ti me when the ports within the country both major & private should 
be developed in an integrated manner so that they can unitedly compete with the ports of neightbouring countries. 
         (Para 29)  
  It was brought to the notice of the Committee tha t some major countries give exclusive coastal Shipp ing rights to 
their vessels. The Committee, therefore, recommends that while considering the demand of the foreign v essels for cargo 
shipping the interests of the Indian vessels and Indian Shipping Industry should be fully protected.                (Para 30)
