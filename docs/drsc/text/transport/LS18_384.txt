CHAPTER-I 
  
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY 
THE MINISTRY 
  
DEMANDS FOR GRANTS (2025-26) 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
1. The Committee acknowledges the Ministry's commitment to infrastructure 
development but emphasizes the necessity for enhanced financial oversight, timely 
project execution, and rigorous quality control standards. It recommends establishing a 
comprehensive and independent monitoring framework, supported by advanced project 
management technologies and regular audits, to proactively address delays in land 
acquisition, regulatory approvals, and contractor inefficiencies. Additionally, the 
Committee advises careful and strategic management of asset monetization initiatives to 
safeguard long-term revenue sustainability without compromising future income 
streams.  
 (Para 7) 
Reply of the Ministry 
1.1 MoRTH has institutionalized independent online monitoring framework for addressing 
the delay in Land Acquisition, Environment & Forest Clearance and GAD for ROBs/RUBs 
approval.  
1.2 The complete Land acquisition process has been made online through Bhoomi Rashi 
Portal by leveraging the technology.3,200+ projects have been facilitated, covering over 
145,000 hectares of land and issuing 13,500+ gazette notifications. This has enabled direct 
transfer of Rs. 19,500 Cr to landowners, reducing leakages and also eliminating the need for 
Rs. 27,000 Cr. of float funds in CALA accounts. Overall, LA process has been shortened by 
~3 months, thus significantly improving efficiency and transparency. 
1.3 MoEF&CC has developed the Parivesh Portal 2.0 and customized for MoRT&H 
providing a single-window system for monitoring and managing environmental, forest, 
wildlife, and CRZ clearances. This enabled tracking the online status of clearances which 
helps in early Forest and Wildlife clearances. With real-time dashboards, color-coded alerts 
for delays, and a three-tier oversight structure, it ensures faster, transparent and accountable 
clearance processes while reducing bottlenecks. Analysis of delay shows that 75% of the 
delay is at State level and 25% is at central level. 
1.4 Similarly, MoRTH and the Ministry of Railways jointly launched RoB/RUB Portal, in 
July 2025, to centralize approvals for Road Over Bridges and Road Under Bridges. The 
portal sets fixed timelines for approvals, reducing the time for GADs from 70 to 50 days, 

- 3 - 
 
structural drawings to 11 days (plus 7 after compliance), and launching schemes and 
QAP/WPSS approvals within 30 days. With role-based access, dashboards, and auto-
notifications, the portal ensures predictable, time-bound approvals and closer coordination 
between highway and railway agencies. 
1.5 Data Lake MIS is MoRT&H’s in-house project and contract management system that 
enables continuous project monitoring, payment tracking and dispute resolution. Provides 
real-time project monitoring through GIS-enabled dashboards and customized reports for 
senior management. Accountability is ensured at all levels, from concessionaires and 
consultants to PIUs, regional offices, and headquarters, with automated alerts highlighting 
pending actions. Includes a dispute resolution module to ensure timely redressal of contractor 
and stakeholder issues.  
1.6 Together, these platforms form a cohesive digital stack that supports MoRTH’s vision 
for efficient National Highway development. By digitizing key processes across the lifecycle 
from land acquisition and clearances to implementation and monitoring, MoRTH has 
significantly improved project delivery and stakeholder coordination. 
1.7 Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs authorized MoRTH / NHAI to monetize 
public funded NH projects which are operational and are generating toll revenues for at least 
two years (which was later reduced to one year) after the Commercial Operations Date 
(COD) subject to approval of the Competent Authority in MoRTH/NHAI. Cabinet also 
approved the proposal to authorize MoRTH / NHAI to set up Infrastructure Investment Trust 
(InvIT) and monetize completed and operational NH projects. NHAI is also authorized to 
raise long term finance from banks by securitizing user fee receipts from toll plazas as an 
alternate mode of asset monetization.  
1.8 Accordingly, MoRTH through NHAI monetizes assets through three modes – Toll 
Operate & Transfer (ToT), InvIT, and Securitization [Project based Financing through 
Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)]. Under ToT and InvIT, operational assets with stable cash 
flow are offered to investors, freeing them from pre-construction and construction risks. The 
concessionaires are exposed to revenue risk and operations and maintenance obligations. 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
2. The relatively small reduction in maintenance funding (Rs.4,595 crore allocated 
against Rs.4,695 crore projected) may seem insignificant but requires deeper analysis. 
Research indicates that when agencies attend to routine maintenance (filling potholes, 
maintaining pavement edges) but neglect periodic maintenance and rehabilitation, they 
may save money in the short term but eventually face expensive reconstruction costs. 
(Para 16)

Recommendation/Observation 
3. The Ministry should transform its approach to maintenance by implementing a 
comprehensive 10-step maintenance planning process that includes facility audits, task 
definition, prioritization, scheduling, resource allocation, implementation, safety 
protocols, performance metrics, emergency preparedness, and documentation. This 
structured approach would ensure that maintenance budgets, though slightly reduced, 
deliver optimal outcomes through strategic targeting and efficient execution. 
(Para 16.1) 
Recommendation/Observation 
4. For effective implementation, the Ministry should develop a digitized asset 
management system that tracks the condition of all national highways and predicts 
maintenance needs based on traffic patterns, climate conditions, and construction 
materials. This system should prioritize maintenance based on safety concerns, 
economic importance, and cost-effectiveness rather than simply responding to visible 
deterioration. Furthermore, maintenance contracts should incorporate performance-
based incentives that reward contractors for maintaining high-quality road conditions 
rather than merely completing specified activities. 
(Para 16.2) 
Reply of the Ministry (2 to 4, above) 
4.1 Maintenance expenditure in the ongoing works which are ongoing and during Defect 
Libility Period / Concession period of Projects under EPC/HAM/BOT/TOT/INvIT are taken 
up the respective Contractor and Concessionaire. Such expenditure are not included in the 
MoRTH expenditure statement as these expenditure are not being done through Government 
Budget 
 
4.2 For all remaining sections of NHs stretches, where DLP has ended or is not under any 
concession period of BOT/HAM/TOT/InvIT project, Government has taken a policy decision 
to undertake maintenance works through Performance Based Maintenance Contract (PBMC) 
or Short Term Maintenance Contract (STMC). While STMC works are generally undertaken 
for a contract period of 1-2 year, PBMC works are undertaken for a contract period of about 
5-7 years. The maintenance work on PBMC mode is in line with the recommendation of the 
committee which ensures minimum performance standards in maintenance with time bound 
defect rectification. Any delay in account of defect rectification attracts automatic penal 
provisions.

Recommendation/Observation 
5. For effective implementation, the Ministry should develop a digital dashboard 
accessible to all stakeholders that provides real-time updates on project status, fund 
utilization, and achievement of milestones. Each project should have clearly defined 
quarterly targets with automatic escalation procedures if deviations exceed 
predetermined thresholds. This approach ensures early identification of implementation 
challenges and enables prompt corrective action before projects face serious delays. 
(Para 17.1) 
 
Reply of the Ministry 
5.1 MoRTH has developed online project monitoring portals (Data lake used by NHAI 
and Project Monitoring & Information System (PMIS) used by all other implementing 
agencies) in which access to dashboard is provided to all implementing agencies for getting 
real-time updates on achievements vis-à-vis targets, project progress, fund utilization,

to have a single portal accessible to all implementing agencies. Data Lake portal enables 
access to all stakeholders that provides real-time updates on project status, fund utilization, 
and achievement of milestones. Each project should have clearly defined quarterly targets 
with automatic escalation procedures if deviations exceed predetermined thresholds. This 
ensures early identification of implementation challenges and enables prompt corrective 
action before projects face serious delays.

Recommendation/Observation 
6. The monitoring system should also incorporate emergency preparedness 
elements to address unexpected challenges such as natural disasters, material shortages, 
or contractor defaults. By establishing clear protocols for these contingencies, the 
Ministry can minimize disruption and maintain project momentum even when facing 
unforeseen obstacles. Additionally, comprehensive documentation and training 
procedures should be implemented to ensure institutional memory and consistent 
application of best practices across all projects regardless of individual staffing changes. 
(Para 17.2) 
Reply of the Ministry 
6.1 The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRT&H) has recently notified 
Disaster Management Plan (DMP). The DMP provides comprehensive framework for 
managing disasters with a focus on maintaining and restoring traffic connectivity on roads 
and highways. The institutional framework of MoRT&H shall be laid down under two sub-
plans, one for all disaster events (eg. Landslide, Flood, Earthquake etc.) other than “Road 
Accident” (Volume-I: Overview and Disaster Management Sub-Plan for Highways) and 
"Road Accidents" as Nodal Ministry (Volume-II: Disaster Management Sub-Plan for Road 
Accidents). 
6.2 The document provides a comprehensive and integrated approach of managing 
disasters and reducing risk of disaster, with a clear-cut hierarchy and well assigned roles & 
responsibilities of officers of various agencies of MoRT&H. A Disaster Management 
Division (DM-Div.) headed by Chief Engineer/Joint Secretary level officer has been 
envisaged under this DMP, which shall act as Nodal Division of MoRT&H to coordinate 
with Ministries/Departments of the Central Government, State Government/ District 
Administration as well as executing agencies of MoRT&H. Further, a Standing Committee 
comprising of senior officials of the Ministry has been envisaged and has been assigned with 
the Role to approve policy / guidelines. 
6.3 Further, financial delegation have been assigned at various levels for sanctioning & 
execution of immediate restoration work in the wake of a Disaster event with bare minimum 
time ensuring uninterrupted traffic movement. 
6.4 Also all officers have been sensitized for effectively using the National Database 
for Emergency Management (NDEM) for study of flood pattern, hydrological study and 
Disaster Prone Zone to minimize the adverse impact of eco sensitive areas.

Recommendation/Observation 
7. Furthermore, the Ministry should commission independent third-party audits of 
selected projects to validate internal monitoring data and identify opportunities for 
systemic improvement. These audits should evaluate not just compliance with financial 
and technical specifications but also assess whether projects are delivering anticipated 
economic and social benefits. This external perspective would strengthen accountability 
while providing valuable insights for future planning and implementation. 
 
(Para 17.3) 
Reply of the Ministry 
7.1 As the NH network expanded in the last decade from ~91,000 km to 1.46 Lakh km, 
management and monitoring of NH quality is critical. To ensure standardized monitoring, 
equipment based condition monitoring system to monitor roughness, cracking, rutting, and 
potholes was designed by MoRT&H through Network Survey Vehicle (NSV). NSVs are 
mobile data collection and processing centres with sensors like laser profile meter, laser crack 
measurement system, GPS, 360 video imagery etc. To enable continuous pavement quality 
monitoring, MoRT&H has instituted periodic surveys (once in 6 months) of the NH network 
through Network Survey Vehicle.  A Centralized GIS platform developed for objective 
assessment of pavement condition based on inputs from NSV within 48 hrs. Process planned 
for report generation and responsibility fixing matrix within 10 days. For ensuring third party 
audit, Ministry has started the independent Network Survey Vehicle (NSV) survey for 
ensuring the pavement quality. This independent NSV survey is done apart from the regular 
NSV surveys through Supervision Consultant / Authority Engineer / Independent Engineer 
on the recently completed stretches which are within the defect liability period and also in 
PBMC contracts.  
7.2 MoRT&H has initiated NSV survey of the network, prioritizing the 38,000 km under 
O&M stage with NHAI currently - Phase 1 covering 21,000 km (about 1 Lakh lane km )has 
been completed for 1st round and initiated for 2nd round. Phase 2 covering remaining 17,000 
km of NHAI has been initiated in Oct’25.Government is planning to implement this survey 
PAN INDIA across all agencies.  
7.3 Additionally, Ministry also appoints Third Party Quality Auditors for systematic, 
independent and documented process of investigating the Quality Assurance/ Quality Control 
system of National Highway Projects as per the provisions of IRC: SP: 112-2017. Retired 
Officers from Central / State Government are empanelled as Team Leaders by NHAI and 
Special Project Monitors (SPMs) by NHIDCL for Quality Inspections of NHAI & NHIDCL 

- 10 - 
 
projects respectively. Above empanelled Team Leaders in NHAI / Special Project Monitors 
(SPMs) are also engaged by MoRTH as Independent Quality Auditors (IQAs) for conducting 
Quality Inspection on NH works executed through State / UT PWDs / RCDs/ MoRTH PIUs / 
BRO. In addition to quality of works, scope of Third Party Quality Audits covers safety 
adherence, occupational health and environmental compliance of the projects thus ensuring 
economic and social well-being of local workforce and nearby population. 
Recommendations/Observations 
8. The Committee is equally concerned by the lack of an effective emergency 
response system. While noting the pilot implementation of the cashless treatment 
scheme in six states, the Committee finds the pace of expansion inadequate. The 
Committee therefore directed the Ministry to ensure its nationwide rollout as a matter 
of priority, integrating it with a trauma care network along major highways and 
establishing clear coordination protocols between ambulance services, hospitals, and 
enforcement agencies. 
(Para 25) 
Recommendation/Observation 
9. Drawing from established emergency response frameworks, the Ministry should 
implement a four-phase emergency response strategy: detection, notification, response, 
and treatment. This requires developing an observability platform that integrates 
traffic monitoring systems, emergency call centres, ambulance services, and hospital 
trauma units into a unified response network. The cashless treatment scheme should be 
expanded nationwide within 12 months, accompanied by establishment of fully 
equipped trauma care centres at 100-kilometer intervals along all national highways. 
(Para 25.1) 
Reply of the Ministry (8 & 9, above) 
9.1 MoRTH has notified the cashless treatment of road accident victims scheme, 2025 on 
5th May, 2025 in consultation with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) to 
provide cashless treatment to victims of road accidents in the country. The detailed guidelines 
including process flow, stakeholder wise standard operating procedures and clearly 
delineated roles and responsibilities have also been notified on 4th June, 2025. 
9.2 Further, to motivate the citizens for helping victims of road crashes without fear, the 
Government has notified Rule 168 and Rule 169 under the Central Motor Vehicle Rules, 
1989 to protect the person, who in good faith, provide assistance to the victims of road 
crashes.  

- 11 - 
 
 9.3 The Good Samaritan Law allows a person, without expectation of payment or reward 
and without any duty of care or special relationship, voluntarily come forward to administer 
immediate assistance or emergency care to a person injured in an accident, or crash, or 
emergency medical condition. Good Samaritan Law protects Good Samaritans from 
harassment on the actions being taken by them to save the life of the road accident victims. 
9.4 MoRTH also administering a scheme for grant of Award to the Good Samaritan, who 
has saved life of a victim of an accident involving a motor vehicle by administering 
immediate assistance and rushing the victim to Hospital/Trauma Care Centre within the 
Golden Hour of the accident to provide medical treatment. The scheme provides each Good 
Samaritan with cash award of Rs. 25000/- and a certificate of appreciation per incident, 
subject to certain conditions including maximum 5 awards to an individual in a year.  
9.5 The response time for emergency services at accident sites depends on various factors 
including the traffic, weather, road condition, time, surroundings of the area etc. The 
Government has taken various initiatives for the emergency services at accident sites which 
inter-alia include: 
(i) Integrated Emergency Response Support System (ERSS) is designed with a single

vulnerabilities has been undertaken. The Ministry should implement a comprehensive 
drainage vulnerability assessment framework that systematically evaluates existing 
highway networks for drainage adequacy. This assessment should classify road segments 
according to risk levels based on historical precipitation data, topographical analysis, soil 
characteristics, traffic volume, and documented water damage. For high-risk areas, the 
Ministry should mandate the implementation of advanced drainage solutions including 
subsurface drainage systems to prevent moisture accumulation in pavement layers. All 
new project designs should incorporate hydrological modelling to determine optimal 
drainage configurations, with particular attention to cross-slope design, adequate 
shoulder drainage, and strategic placement of culverts and side drains. Additionally, the 
Ministry should establish permanent monitoring stations at critical drainage points 
along major highways to provide real-time data on water levels and flow rates, enabling 
predictive maintenance during monsoon seasons before significant damage occurs. 
(Para 40) 
Reply of the Ministry: 
19.1 The DPR consultants undertake the survey of proposed road projects and make a 
comprehensive assessment of drainage vulnerabilities and provide for due drainage 
arrangement, accordingly, in the DPR, after taking into account the soil characteristics, 
historical precipitation data, and topographical analysis of the area. For all new projects, DPR 
consultants are incorporating the hydrological modeling to determine optimal drainage 
configurations, cross slope design, etc. based on the hydrological data received from 
concerned Irrigation Department / Agency during the preparation of DPR. Provision of lined 

- 41 - 
 
drains is generally made in built-up sections and unlined drains in rural sections. Also, during 
construction stage, proper drainage out-falls is tonsured to avoid stagnation of water on the 
NH to avoid pre-mature deterioration of road surface. 
19.2 Further, The Concessionaires / Contractors carry out repairs over the pavement 
rutting, surface undulations and inadequate drainage before monsoon. 
19.3 IE/AE conducts regular inspection of site for O&M obligations including drainage 
vulnerability and submits inspection report for necessary compliance to be done by 
Concessionaires/ Contractors including drainage adequacy.  
19.4 Pre-Monsoon & Post-Monsoon Inspections are carried out by the Maintenance / 
Construction Agencies as per the provision of Contract Agreement. 
19.5 Before the onset of Monsoon, special emphasis is made as part of maintenance 
requirement. Additionally, proactive measures are put in place to address contingencies in 
flood-prone and landslide-prone areas, ensuring the safety and traffic-worthiness of the 
highways during adverse weather conditions.  
19.6 During the monsoon especially at High-Risk areas, the following action plan is 
formulated:- 
(i). Regular Inspections of the vulnerable spots for preventive action and installation of 
warning signs. 
(ii). Keeping in stock/ on-offer excavators / backhoe loaders / dozers, saw cutters for tree 
cutting, sand bags, traffic cones & Signages, etc., in required quantities at required locations. 
(iii). Alerting and instructing the concerned contractors for the works like bridge closing, 
tree removal, landslide clearance, diversion of roads, etc.

across all states and there was a delay of 3 to 4years. Now Electronic-Detailed Accident

in 36 states and union territories across the country. The project enables real-time collection 

- 46 - 
 
of accident data through an integrated digital platform for all stakeholder departments. It is

(Electronic Detailed Accident report). Its complete integration with VAHAN, SARATHI, PM

measures are ensured quickly.
