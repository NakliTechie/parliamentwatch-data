I. BRIEF BACKGROUND 
 
1. Tourism plays a key role in socio-economic progress through employment generation, 
creation of infrastructure and revenue earnings. Not only does the tourism sector spearhead 
growth, it also improves the quality of people‟s lives with its capacity to create large scale 
employment of diverse kind.  
 
2. Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency to formulate national policies and 
programmes for the development and promotion of tourism. In the process, the Ministry 
consults and collaborates with other stakeholders in the sector including various Central 
Ministries/ Agencies, State Governments/ Union Territory Administrations and the 
representatives of the private sector.  The Committee has been informed that Facilitation as 
well as strengthening of tourism in India is the main objective of the Ministry. Augmenting 
tourism infrastructure, easing of visa regime, assurance of quality standards in services of 
tourism service providers, projection of the country as a 365 days‟ tourist destination , 
promotion of sustainable tourism etc. are some of the policy areas which need to be 
constantly worked upon to increase and facilitate tourism in our country. 
 
3. Secretary (Tourism) is the executive chief of the Ministry. The Directorate General of 
Tourism has 20 Domestic field offices and an Indian Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering 
within the country and 08 overseas offices. The overseas offices promote I ndian tourism in 
the markets abroad. 
 
4. The domestic field offices also play a vital role for promotion of tourism sector in the 
country. They are also involved in monitoring the progress of implementation of projects 
sanctioned by the Ministry to the State Governments/Union Territories. 
 
5. India Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) is the only public sector undertaking 
under the charge of the Ministry of Tourism.

9. As per the information furnished in the Annual Report (2021-22) of the Ministry, the 
activities of this Ministry revolves around promoting interna l tourism, i.e. both inbound and

objectives of the Ministry pertain to projecting the country as a 365 days‟ tourist destination, 
promoting tourism in a sustainable manner by active participation of all segments of the 
society, assuring quality standards amongst tourism service providers, etc. Apart from these, 
the focus is also o n integrated development of tourism infrastructure and facilities through 
effective partnership with various stakeholders.

19. The Committee notes that although there has been marginal increase in the 
budgetary outlay for the Ministry from Rs. 2026.77 crore for FY 2021 -22 to Rs. 2400.00 
crore for FY 2022 -23, it is still lesser than the BE 2020 -21 allocation of Rs. 2499.83 
crore. Though the BE 2022 -23 allocation is 18 -41% higher than the BE 2021 -22 outlay, 
there is a substantial shortfall of Rs. 537.92 crore vis -à-vis the projected demand of Rs. 
2937.92 crore by the Ministry for FY 2022 -23 and that too at a time when this sector is 
reeling under losses in the wake of COVID -19 pandemic. The Committee notes that this 
budgetary outlay for 2022 -23 has been reduced to Rs 416 crore  as compared to the BE 
2021-22 of Rs. 668.72 crore under the head- Promotion and Publicity . The Committee 
finds this reduction alarming because the tourism and hospitality sector has been hit the 
hardest by COVID-19 pandemic and with massive job and revenue losses, it is essential 
that the Government promotes India on all possible platforms, both national and 
international, as a safe and must -visit destination and for this, instilling confidence  
among touriststhrough use of technology with the help of skilled manpower, investment 
in skilling, reskilling and upskilling is an imperative need. The Committee, therefore , 
apprehends that the marginal increase of Rs. 333.77 crore in the budgetary allocation 
for 2022 -23 is inadequate as it will be consumed by inflation. The Committee is 
simultaneously appreciative of the fiscal constraints faced by the Ministry of Finance 
due to deceleration in the growth rate in the Indian economy on account of global and 
domestic slowdown in the aftermath of COVID -19 pandemic. The Committee observes 
that with the accelerated pace of vaccine roll -out and easing of restrictions, the Indian 
economy has started to rebound and the additional resources would become available as 
a result of revenue buoyancy in FY 2022 -23. Given t he fact that the activities of the 
Ministry harness the direct and multiplier effect on economic development and 
employment generation in the country and integrated development of infrastructure 

 
10 
 
and facilities through effective partnerships and convergence  with stakeholders can 
propel India to a higher growth trajectory, the Committee recommends that depending 
on better financial performance and greater absorption capacity of the Ministry, 
quantum of funding for the Ministry of Tourism be enhanced at the RE  2022-23 stage. 
Simultaneously, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should initiate and 
implement better fiscal discipline and ensure that the scarce financial resources do not 
remain parked idelling. 
 
20. From a perusal of Table No. 2, the Committee is constrained to note that there is 
a substantial underutilization of the budgeted funds during the FY 2021 -22. Against the 
RE 2021-22 allocation of Rs. 969.80 crore, only 47.70% amounting to Rs. 462.55 crore 
could be spent till the 31 st January, 2022 . It m eans that the remaining expenditure 
would have to be squeezed in the last two months of FY 2021 -22. Since as per norms 
stipulated by the Ministry of Finance, there is a ceiling of 25% on expenditure in the 
last quarter, occurrence of huge savings is a cert ainty. It may be pertinent to mention 
here that a zero based budgeting exercise which requires that budget request be 
evaluated thoroughly, starting from zero base, is in practice and the financial outlays 
are earmarked based on factors like past trend of expenditure, stages of 
formulation/implementation of various projects, the pre -projects approvals etc. 
Therefore, savings of Rs. 1056.97 crore vis -à-vis the BE 2021 -22 allocation not only 
betrays fiscal indiscipline by way of underutilization of the earmar ked funds which will 
have an adverse bearing on infrastructure development oriented activities of the 
Ministry, but is also indicative of shortcomings in budgetary planning and monitoring 
of the budgeted funds. The Committee observes that a resource constrained country like 
India cannot afford to keep a vast chunk of its resources locked up and surrendered 
towards the end of the financial year. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
realistic projection of fund requirements should be given utmost priorit y at the highest 
level of the Ministry so that the Budget Estimates are formulated with application of 
proper financial yardsticks and large variations between the Budget Estimates, Revised 
Estimates and Actual Expenditure needs to be  avoided. The Ministry  must also pay 
attention to streamlining its monitoring mechanism and ensure optimal and more 
efficient deployment and utilization of its financial resources for smooth execution of its 
programmes within the approved cost and designated timeline. 

 
11 
 
21. The Committee also takes a serious note of keeping the unspent provision of Rs. 
1056.97 crore till the close of financial year 2021 -22 despite reduction in the budgetary 
outlay at the RE 2021 -22 stage, in disregard of the provisions of General Financial 
Rules requiring the unspent provisions in a grant to be surrendered to the Ministry of 
Finance as soon as these are foreseen, without waiting for the last day of the financial 
year. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should exercise greater 
fiscal prudence, and ensure that unspent budgetary provisions are surrendered timely 
in future. 
 
22. Scrutiny of Annexure I  reveals to the Committee that a number of Utilization 
Certificates are pending under the Swadesh Darshan Scheme, Assistance to Central 
Agencies, Assistance to IHMs/FCIs/NCHMCT/IITTM and PRASAD and the 
cumulative amou nt involved therein is to the tu ne of Rs. 184.55 crore. Since 
conditionality regarding submission of Utilization Certificates is a bar for release of 
funds, the pendency of Utilizati on Certificates and the amount involved therein have 
implications for further release of funds and impinges on the effective implementation 
of tourism infrastructure projects formulated by the Ministry. Though the Ministry ha s 
taken some measures to liquidate pending UCs, given the fact that pending UCs pertain 
to projects sanctioned as far back as 2010 -11, it is obvious that a lot still remains to be 
done. The Committee, therefore, desires to be informed of the detailed action plan on 
bringing down the pen dency and the success achieved so far so that a fair assessment 
can be made about the efficacy of the plan.

30. The Committee notes that as per the World Travel and Tourism Council 
(WTTC), prior to the COVID -19 pandemic, Travel and Tourism accounted for 10.4% 
of global GDP. In many parts of the world, tourism is a major engine of growth. The 
Committee in its 275 th Report presented to the Parliament on the 3 rd March 2020 had 
noted that the contribution of tourism to nationa l GDP was over 10% for most 
countries where tourism happens to be the main driver of national economy. However, 
in India which has unique endowments of biodiversity, forests, rivers, its rich culture 
and heritage and many World Heritage sites, the tourism sector has not proved to be 
the proverbial engine of growth for our economy. This is evident from the fact that 
India's total share in global international arrivals remains a modest 1.57% against the 
target of 3 per cent set by the NITI Aayog to be achieve d by 2022. Similarly, the 
contribution (direct and indirect) of tourism in the GDP and the total jobs in the 
country are estimated to be 5.16 per cent and 15.34 per cent respectively during 2019 -
20. The Committee is of the firm view that if India has to mo ve towards becoming USD 
5 trillion economy and a global economic powerhouse, as envisioned by our Hon'ble PM 
Shri Narendra Modi, then the contribution of tourism to India's GDP has to be raised 
from the current 5.16% to 10% in the next five years. The Comm ittee, while welcoming 
the various measures taken by the Ministry to double the contribution of tourism to 
India's GDP, feels that the vast untapped potential of India as a tourism destination 
cannot be realized unless tourism is included in the Concurrent  List of the Constitution 
of India. Taking note of the fact that Travel and Tourism does not figure in the Centre, 
State or Concurrent Lists and since in a federal polity like ours, the responsibility for 
policy making and implementation of tourism project s are fragmented across different 

 
17 
 
levels - Central and State Governments and coordination between them is often lacking 
due to absence of clearly defined mandate, the Committee in its 288 th Report had 
recommended inclusion of tourism in the Concurrent List . Since tourism is yet to be 
included in the Concurrent List, the Committee reiterates its recommendation for 
inclusion of tourism in the Concurrent List of the Constitution of India. The Committee 
is convinced that this will go a long way in channelising the development of tourism and 
positioning and maintaining tourism development as a national priority.  
 
31. The Committee observes that Emirate of Dubai does not have the abundant 
wealth of natural oil reserves and nor does it have a pre -existing plethora of natural, 
historical and internationally significant tourist sites. Despite that, it has emerged as a 
multi-billion dollar economy and amongst the world's most visited tourist destinations, 
all based on the Government's tourism centric approach towards nati onal policy 
making. The Committee wishes that India should also endeavour to imbibe creating 
potential for tourism in the very core of its policy making. Although tourism is an 
important activity in India, its share in global market is extremely small and the 
potential of tourism sector in India is yet to be unleashed. The actual implementation of 
tourist projects takes place at State/UT level but in most of the States/UTs , the outlay 
for Department of Tourism is meagre. The Committee, therefore, recommends  that in 
order to give policy push to the tourism industry, a National Tourism Counc il chaired 
by the Prime Minister  and composed of Chief Ministers or Tourism Ministers of all 
States should be established. This National Council should be an empowered legi slative 
body on the lines of GST Council. This will enable fast -track Centre-State level tourism 
decision making, leveraging and utilising full synergies of India's tourism potential 
across all our States. 
 
32. The Committee observes that despite the fact that India offers a diverse portfolio 
for travel and tourism, the share of India's International Tourist Arrivals (ITAs) in the 
ITAs of the world was merely 1.57 per cent in 2020. There is increased competition 
among various countries to attract foreign visitors. There is, therefore, an imperative 
need for India to prepare suitable market strategy to attract foreign visitors. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry in collaboration with a 
professional body should carry out market research of foreign tourist source markets to 

 
18 
 
identify the expectations, requirements, psychographics, demographics and many other 
aspects of a foreign tourist willing to travel to India, thereby enabling to create a

Ministry of Tourism with respect to inbound tourism in India and provide a blueprint 
on the various policy objectives and infrastructure development needed to strengthen 
inbound tourism in the country.

35. In reply to a question t he Ministry has informed the Committee that to assess the 
impact of Covid-19 pandemic on the tourism sector, the Ministry of Tourism, Government of 
India got conducted study on “India and the Corona Virus Pandemic: Economic Losses for 
Households Engaged in Tourism and Policies for Recovery”. Th is study captures the 
economic losses considering the changes experienced during the entire period of this 
pandemic situation with the help of data from different sources that reflects changes over the 
said period. The study also probed into the likely rec overy phases of the tourism sector post 
pandemic to understand how the sector would regain its pre- pandemic position.  
 
Objectives of the study 
36. This study aims to assess the estimated loss to the economy and to the income of 
households because of Covid-19 pandemic. The key objectives are: 
(i) Quantify the sector -wise (or industry -wise) direct and indirect impact of tourism 
activities on overall economy as a whole and especially on household income, which 
may be further disaggregated into different types of hou seholds like formal and 
informal. 
(ii) Quantify the loss in income, sector -wise and in the overall economy and of the 
household sector; and also the loss in jobs, due to the impact of Coronavirus 
pandemic on the tourism sector.  
(iii)Estimate the impact of gradual o pening up of domestic tourism activities and the 
sectors related to tourism.  
(iv) Estimate the likely impact of opening of international tourism, in a phased manner.  


 
21 
 
(v) Propose policy measures appropriate to provide relief to the tourism sector, in 
general, and households involved in tourism related activities, in particular, based 
on feedback received from various stakeholders.  
 
Key Findings 
Tourism Economy in a normal situation 
37. The Tourism Satellite Account (TSA) is an important tool to estimate the 
contributions of Tourism to the economy. According to the second and third TSAs, tourism 
sector contributed as much as 6.8 percent and 5.2 percent directly and indirectly to the 
country‟s total GDP. The direct shares for these years were 3.7 percent and 2. 7 percent 
respectively. 
 
38. For the present study, we have also derived the income multiplier to estimate the 
impact of increase in tourism demand on overall household income of the economy through 
the preparation of Social Accounting Matrix (SAM). This is be cause SAM constitutes not 
only the production account but, in addition, also incorporates the household account. Hence, 
it presents the transactions between production sectors and the households drawing income 
from these sectors. The SAM based multiplier a nalysis reveals that the income multiplier of 
the tourism sector is 1.6332. Hence if the tourism demand or tourism expenditure increases 
by, say, Rs. 1 crore, then owing to the direct and indirect linkages among the sectors of the 
economy, the overall hous ehold income of the economy is expected to increase by Rs. 1.63 
crore.  
 
Tourism Economy during Covid-19 pandemic   
39. The impact of pandemic on tourism, through the estimation of TDGVA, is captured 
for all of these three quarters and across the following asp ects so that the impact of pandemic 
due to different types of shocks is separately captured. 
 
40. Impact due to supply side shock:  This relates to the overall economic slowdown 
during 2020-21 and assumes that tourism was rather resilient and continued as before. Hence, 
this scenario takes into account only the supply side shock. Therefore, for all the quarters of 
2020-21, Tourism Industry Ratios (TIRs) are assumed to remain the same as those for pre -
pandemic years. The findings are: 

 
22 
 
 While overall economy fell b y 20.1 percent in Q1; 5.1 percent in Q2 and grew at a 
positive rate of 4.3 percent in Q3, all in nominal terms, tourism economy or TDGVA 
saw a much greater fall of 42.8 percent in Q1; 15.5 percent in Q2 and continued to 
suffer from the negative growth even in Q3, with a fall of 1.1 percent. 
 
41. Impact due to demand side shock : This refers to the impact of pandemic on tourism 
economy given both supply side shock coming from the economic slowdown and demand 
side shock resulting from the limited tourism activity in the country during the three quarters 
of study. The limited tourism activity translates to huge reduction in tourism expenditure 
incurred by all types of tourists – inbound, domestic and outbound. Hence these estimates 
reflect the most likely loss in TDGVA. The findings are: 
 It is estimated that TDGVA plummeted by as much as 93.3 percent in Q1 of 2020 -21 
over its level in the same quarter of previous year. It picked up slightly to post a fall 
of 79.5 percent in Q2 and that of 64.3 percent in Q3, but continued to be at extremely 
low level in negative territory while overall economy posted a positive growth of 4.3 
percent in Q3. 
 
42. Impact due to inbound demand side shock:  This refers to the independent impact of 
fall in inbound tourism expenditure, assumi ng that all other forms of tourism remained the 
same during the study reference period. The TDGVA is estimated to have decreased by 61.7 
percent in Q2 of 2020 -21 over the same quarter of 2019 -20. Going forward, this is estimated 
to have fallen by 38.0 percent in Q2 and 25.7 percent in Q3. 
 
43. Impact due to domestic demand side shock : This refers to the independent impact of 
fall in domestic tourism expenditure, assuming that all other forms of tourism remained the 
same. Notably, the negative impact of fall in domestic tourism activity is much more 
significant than that of inbound tourism activity. The fall in all the three quarters is estimated 
to be 20 to 30 percentage points higher than that in the case of inbound tourism. 
 
44. Estimated loss in jobs during Covid 19 pandemic:  A significant number of jobs were 
lost in the tourism sector once the lockdown was implemented. However, recovery was fast 
once the unlock phases started and the economy was opened up. The findings are: 
 

 
23 
 
45. As many as 14.5 million jobs, out of a n estimated 34.8 million jobs in the pre -
pandemic period of 2019, are expected to have lost as a repercussion of the pandemic and the 
resultant lockdown in the first quarter of 2020-21. This is equivalent to a fall of 41.7 percent. 
 
46. In terms of absolute nu mber, the loss of jobs is more pronounced in the case of 
salary/wage earners, 7.8 million of whom were left without job during Q1. However, in terms 
of percent fall, those with the status of self -employed were hit harder as their number fell by 
42.8 percent, as compared with employees, whose number shrunk by 40.7 percent.  
 
47. As the economy, in general, and tourism activity, in particular, started picking up in 
the subsequent quarters, many jobs are estimated to have gradually returned in these quarters. 
Till the third quarter, however, the number of tourism jobs is still estim ated to run a deficit of 
1.8 million.   
 
Estimated loss in household income during Covid19 pandemic 
48. The impact on income is estimated to be much more severe as compared with the 
impact on loss in jobs. This might owe to the cut in salaries, explaining huge  loss in wage 
income and much higher loss in non -wage income among business owners who not just paid 
salaries but were also required to incur other committed expenditures like payment towards 
rent, bills for utility services and so on.   
 
49. While wage income  fell by 41.6 percent in Q1, non -wage income, that is, income of 
the self -employed or business owners nosedived by 124.4 percent. This shows not many 
businesses not just witnessed a severe fall in income but also posted a deficit in Q1. 
 
50. Going forward, wit h the pickup in activities, the income levels also showed an 
improvement but continued to post a negative growth of 3.4 percent in the case of wage 
income and 21.2 percent in the case of non-wage income, both in Q3. 
 
51. On the other hand, in sharp contrast wi th the tourism household income, total 
household income saw a steeper drop in income level in the case of wage income or salary 
income of the employees. This is because while tourism industries are mostly service 
industries, overall economy also includes a  large proportion of primary sector and 

 
24 
 
manufacturing industries, both of which are highly labour intensive.  
 
Tourism Economy going forward – Future Outlook 
52. With the gradual opening of international borders, regain in confidence to travel, 
successful vacc ination drive and lessons learnt from previous waves of infections, tourism 
activity is expected to move towards normalcy soon. In order to put forward our outlook, we 
have built following three scenarios for all forms of tourism, that is, inbound, domesti c, 
outbound and also outbound tourists diverted to domestic trips: 
a. Optimistic  
b. Most likely 
c. Pessimistic 
 
53. These scenarios are based on the assumptions using our judgement of the likely 
situation going forward. The key findings are: 
 
54. It is expected that tourism economy, in terms of tourism expenditure, will regain its 
pre-pandemic level by 2024 -25, primarily driven by domestic tourism. With respect to 
inbound tourism, the revival is expected to be slower and it is likely to reach the pre -
pandemic level not sooner than 2026-27, even in optimistic scenario. 
 
55. On the other hand, even in pessimistic scenario, domestic tourism (complemented by 
the outbound-turned domestic tourism) is likely to reach the pre-pandemic level much sooner, 
by 2024-25.  
 
Recommendations for the revival of Tourism in India 
The proposed recommendations are as follows: 
56. Promotion of Domestic Tourism: while an average domestic trip costs the least, only 
Rs. 5429, as compared with the cost of an inbound trip (Rs. 2.10 lakh) and an outbound trip 
(Rs. 14,286 as pre-trip and about Rs. 60,000 on-trip), but given its huge volume in number of 
trips, the total tourism expenditure incurred by domestic tourists is over 70 percent of total 
internal tourism expenditure. Hence, whatever tour ism contributes to the economy is mainly 
on account of domestic tourism. 
 

 
25 
 
57. Diverting outbound tourists to domestic trips: by creating an enticing bucket for 
domestic destinations; developing new approach through working holidays; and promoting 
regional or local destinations.  
 
58. Target incentives to industries contributing most to tourism economy: The data on 
tourism expenditure reveal that of all the tourism characteristic products and services, food 
providing services, road transport services and healthcare providing services account for 
close to 80 percent of the total tourism expenditure. Hence, the government policies need to 
be prioritised and targeted towards these important industries. 
 
59. Other broad recommendations include: 
a. Re-establish trust in travel and tourism 
b. Adherence to safety and mandatory health and hygiene protocols to the highest 
standards possible 
c. Protocols should be mandatory for tourists also. 
d. High-value, low-volume (HVLV) approach (as followed in Bhutan) 
e. Government responsiveness towards preparing Tourism Recovery Plan 
f. Provide tax benefits, subsidies and incentives to industry players so that they can 
survive and overcome this pandemic shock 
g. Integrate Public Health with Tourism  
h. Issuance of vaccine certificates 
i. Create healthy environment which is a natural barrier to future pandemics 
j. Technology innovation, like digital marketing, virtual tours etc. 
k. Use of digital services to improve the customer safety experience and adapt quickly if 
the health context in local area changes. 
l. Targeted support for vulnerable communities 
m. Support businesses in diversifying revenue sources 
n. Identify and target diverse customer base  
o. Maintain customer and employee confidence 
 
60. It is imperative that the policy measures need to include all above key areas which are

69. The Committee in its 288th Report had recommended that to provide relief to the 
tourism and hospitality sector so as to enhance export competitiveness of Indian 
tourism, the payments received by all the tourism and  hospitality entities in convertible 
foreign exchange be considered as "deemed export" and exempted from GST and the 
concept of zero rating be applied to tourism foreign exchange earnings. In this 
connection, the Ministry in its action Taken Notes had stat ed that "…this is a policy 
decision to be taken by GST Council, a reference from Ministry of Tourism is being 
made to GST Council in this regard…" The Committee reiterates its recommendation 
that tourism services provided to foreign tourists in India on receipt of foreign exchange 
should be treated as "deemed export" and such transactions should be zero -rated for 
GST without stopping the flow of input credits. 
 
70. The Committee notes that Finance Act, 2020 which was announced just before 
the start of COVID -19 crisis, inter alia, came up with new insertion of Tax Collected at 
Source (TCS) provisions under Section 206C  (1G)(a) of Income Tax Act, 1961  
pertaining to TCS @ 5% on foreign remittance through Liberalized Remittance  
Scheme (LRS) and under Section 206 C (1G)(b) pertaining to TCS@5% on selling of 
overseas tour packages by tour operators. The Indian Tour Operators face challenges to 
match the prices offered for outbound tours in comparison to foreign tour service 

 
36 
 
providers due to additional burden of taxes which include GST and newly inserted TCS 
provisions under Section 206C of the Income Tax Act, 1961 because overseas agents and 
Online Travel Agents (OTAs) do not charge the same since their base is out of India. 
The Committee is of the view that the above TCS provisions will not only increase the 
cost of packages sold by Indian tour operators, they will also shift all sales of outbound 
tourism to overseas suppliers, thereby denying the Government of income tax and GST 
revenue. These newly inserted TCS provi sions, therefore, are detrimental to tourism 
business in India. The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to allow 
domestic tour operators a level playing field in comparison with foreign tour operators 
in the market of outbound tourism, the above mentioned TCS provisions be rolled back. 
The Committee is of the view that once TCS on outbound tourism is withdrawn, it will 
enable the Indian Travel Agents and Tour Operators to stage a recovery post the 
pandemic. 
 
71. The Committee in its 288 th Report had observed that under the Service Exports 
from India Scheme (SEIS) the Government  offered domestic exports duty credit scripts 
in the range of 5% to 7% of the net foreign exchange earned depending upon the nature 
of services and recommended that at a time when the tourism sector is faced with the 
crisis of the century, the notified services be incentivized at an enhanced rate of 10% for 
both tour operators and hotels in order to provide them with cash flow support. The 
Committee notes that the much awaited extension and the details on the Services 
Exports from India Scheme for FY 2019 -20 have been notified by the Directorate 
General of Foreign Trade  vide notification No. 29/2015 -20 dated September 23, 2021. 
The Committee is, however, disappointed to note that SEIS benefits for the tourism 
sector has been granted  at 5 per cent on net fore ign exchange earnings as opposed to 
earlier 7 per cent. SEIS rates for hotels have been reduced to 3 per cent from earlier 
specified rate of 5 per cent. Entitlement under SEIS for services exports has also been 
capped at Rs. 5 crore per exporter. The Commi ttee observes that the reduction in 
percentage of SEIS benefits comes at a time when the tourism and hospitality industry 
is struggling to recover their losses or even to stay afloat in many cases. The Committee 
would like to point out that the travel and hospitality businesses are expected to be very 
subdued due to the decline in business scenario post COVID -19 pandemic and there is 
an imperative need to infuse some liquidity into this cash -strapped sector. The 

 
37 
 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Min istry of Tourism should take the SEIS 
rate reduction issue with the Ministry of Commerce and impress upon them that the 
dependency of the hospitality industry is high on import of goods to maintain global 
standards which warrants SEIS benefits to be lucrat ive to reduce the import cost and, 
therefore, the scrips generated by the travel and hospitality sector should be allowed at 
the rate  of 10% vis -à-vis the existing rates and the SEIS benefits should also be 
extended for further period of two-three years for enabling cost savings. 
 
72. The Committee also observes that SEIS is the only incentive for service exporters 
and, therefore, imposing a limit on the total entitlement under the SEIS at Rs 5 crore 
per exporter will impact top revenue producers adversely. Lar ger organisations fetch 
volume based on higher reliability and are necessary to increase the footfall in India. 
Capping the SEIS benefit at Rs. 5 crore will force companies to split up entities to 
continue to avail full benefits which will not be helpful. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should take up with the Ministry of 
Commerce this issue so that the capping of Rs 5 crore for SEIS benefit is removed.

III) CONTRIBUTION OF TOURISM TO GDP 
 
The Committee notes that as per the World Travel and Tourism Council 
(WTTC), prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, Travel and Tourism accounted for 10.4% of 
global GDP. In many parts of the world, tourism is a major engine of growth. The 
Committee in its 275th R eport presented to the Parliament on the 3rd March 2020 had 
noted that the contribution of tourism to national GDP was over 10% for most countries 
where tourism happens to be the main driver of national economy. However, in India 
which has unique endowment s of biodiversity, forests, rivers, its rich culture and 
heritage and many World Heritage sites, the tourism sector has not proved to be the 
proverbial engine of growth for our economy. This is evident from the fact that India's 
total share in global inter national arrivals remains a modest 1.57% against the target of 
3 per cent set by the NITI Aayog to be achieved by 2022. Similarly, the contribution 

 
67 
 
(direct and indirect) of tourism in the GDP and the total jobs in the country are 
estimated to be 5.16 per c ent and 15.34 per cent respectively during 2019 -20. The 
Committee is of the firm view that if India has to move towards becoming USD 5 trillion 
economy and a global economic powerhouse, as envisioned by our Hon'ble PM Shri 
Narendra Modi, then the contribut ion of tourism to India's GDP has to be raised from 
the current 5.16% to 10% in the next five years. The Committee, while welcoming the 
various measures taken by the Ministry to double the contribution of tourism to India's 
GDP, feels that the vast untappe d potential of India as a tourism destination cannot be 
realized unless tourism is included in the Concurrent List of the Constitution of India. 
Taking note of the fact that Travel and Tourism does not figure in the Centre, State or 
Concurrent Lists and si nce in a federal polity like ours, the responsibility for policy 
making and implementation of tourism projects are fragmented across different levels - 
Central and State Governments and coordination between them is often lacking due to 
absence of clearly d efined mandate, the Committee in its 288th Report had 
recommended inclusion of tourism in the Concurrent List. Since tourism is yet to be 
included in the Concurrent List, the Committee reiterates its recommendation for 
inclusion of tourism in the Concurren t List of the Constitution of India. The Committee 
is convinced that this will go a long way in channelising the development of tourism and 
positioning and maintaining tourism development as a national priority.  
(Para 30) 
The Committee observes that Emira te of Dubai does not have the abundant 
wealth of natural oil reserves and nor does it have a pre -existing plethora of natural, 
historical and internationally significant tourist sites. Despite that, it has emerged as a 
multi-billion dollar economy and amon gst the world's most visited tourist destinations, 
all based on the Government's tourism centric approach towards national policy 
making. The Committee wishes that India should also endeavour to imbibe creating 
potential for tourism in the very core of its  policy making. Although tourism is an 
important activity in India, its share in global market is extremely small and the 
potential of tourism sector in India is yet to be unleashed. The actual implementation of 
tourist projects takes place at State/UT level but in most of the States/UTs, the outlay for 
Department of Tourism is meagre. The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order 
to give policy push to the tourism industry, a National Tourism Council chaired by the 
Prime Minister and composed of Chief  Ministers or Tourism Ministers of all States 

 
68 
 
should be established. This National Council should be an empowered legislative body on 
the lines of GST Council. This will enable fast -track Centre-State level tourism decision 
making, leveraging and utilising  full synergies of India's tourism potential across all our 
States. 
(Para 31) 
The Committee observes that despite the fact that India offers a diverse portfolio 
for travel and tourism, the share of India's International Tourist Arrivals (ITAs) in the 
ITAs of the world was merely 1.57 per cent in 2020. There is increased competition 
among various countries to attract foreign visitors. There is, therefore, an imperative 
need for India to prepare suitable market strategy to attract foreign visitors. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry in collaboration with a professional 
body should carry out market research of foreign tourist source markets to identify the 
expectations, requirements, psychographics, demographics and many other aspects of a 
foreign tourist willing to travel to India, thereby enabling to create a strategic plan to 
revive inbound tourism. The research should be conducted with an objective to provide 
an authentic feedback, roadmap and recommendations to the Ministry of Tourism wi th 
respect to inbound tourism in India and provide a blueprint on the various policy 
objectives and infrastructure development needed to strengthen inbound tourism in the 
country.

Jammu and Kashmir; Eco Circuit in Jharkhand, rural circuits in Kerala. Spiritual 
Circuits in Maharashtra, Eco Circuit in Punjab and North -East Circuit in Tripura. The 
Committee observes that the creation of tourism infrastructure is the very foundation on 
which Indian tourism is to be built and a prerequisite towards improving the country's 
tourism productive capacity and attaining economic growth. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Ministry should analyse the reasons behind the underperformance 
of the above infrastructure projects and take remedial measures accordingly. The 
Committee desires to be apprised of the measures taken to speed up execution of the 
above projects. 
(Para 78) 
The Committee observes that Spiritual Tourism holds a special place in India and 
also appeals to international tourists as it presents an immersive spiritual experience. 
The presence of several sacred destinations of various faiths in the c ountry makes these 
destinations among the most visited sites globally. Since infrastructure sector is a key 
driver for spiritual tourism, the Committee recommends to streamline the development 
of the entire religious tourism circuit in each region/area as it will increase the carrying 
capacity of these destinations which is required to meet the large influx of tourists at 
these destinations. 
(Para 79)

VIII. CRUISE TOURISM 
 
The Committee observes that despite being endowed with 7500 km coastline and 
a vast network of lakes and rivers, and a steady in crease in the number of ships and 
cruise passengers calling on Indian ports over the past few years, cruise tourism in India 
is still at a nascent stage. Though various initiatives have been taken by the Ministries of 
Tourism and Ports, Shipping and Waterw ays to develop India as destination as well as 
source market for cruise tourism and increase the number of cruise shipping calls and 
passenger arrivals, India's enormous cruise potential still remains untapped. While 
appreciating the initiatives taken by t he Ministries of Tourism and Posts, Shipping and 

 
77 
 
Waterways to develop India as an attractive cruise tourism destination, the Committee 
recommends that the following measures may also be taken: 
i. Training institutions for various services in cruise tourism be developed. 
ii. The taxation structure in this segment may be revamped to bring down cost for 
passengers, making cruise shipping affordable for middle class population that 
constitute the major portion of Indian demographics. This will go a long way in 
boosting participation of the middle class in this segment of tourism. 
iii. Synergy between the various stakeholders of this industry like cruise companies; 
tour operators, and immigration and customs departments may be utilised to 
optimise the gains for all.  
iv. An effective promotional campaign be developed to put Indian Cruise industry on 
the Global map. 
(Para 98) 
IX. DOMESTIC PROMOTION AND PUBLICITY (DPPH) SCHEMEINCLUDING 
MARKET DEVELOPMENT ASSISTANCE 
The Committee observes that in most countries with a large popu lation, 
domestic tourism is the foundation of a vibrant tourism industry. India also has a 
strong domestic tourism base which is evident from the fact that Domestic tourism visits 
were 2321.98 million during 2019. However, owing to the prolonged COVID -19 
pandemic, the erstwhile booming tourism and hospitality sector has taken a severe 
beating due to which the number of domestic tourists slumped to 610.22 million during 
2020 registering a negative growth of 73.3%. The trajectory of international tourism 
remains uncertain because new COVID -19 variants are emerging in some parts of the 
world due to which new restrictions are being introduced. With the rapid pace of the 
nation-wide COVID vaccination, the revival of the Indian economy has gained 
momentum and Indi an tourist destinations have risen to prominence as a result of 
strong pent -up demand and the relaxation of domestic restrictions. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the publicity campaigns of the Ministry should be focused 
on following safety proto cols and helping restore confidence among tourists and 
promoting domestic tourism.  
(Para 106) 

 
78 
 
The Committee also recommends that niche tourism avenues such as medical 
tourism, Buddhist tourism, sports tourism, ayurveda tourism, yoga tourism, spiritual 
tourism, adventure tourism, culinary tourism, cruise tourism, etc. should be promoted 
aggressively through the media campaigns. 
(Para 107) 
The Committee observes that high net worth Indian nationals opt for foreign 
tourism instead of domestic tourism. One of the reasons behind this is the exhaustion of 
the timeworn tourist destinations and absence of new avenues. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that unexplored and unexploited tourist destinations should be 
identified, developed and marketed to revive t he curiosity of high-end domestic tourists 
and the publicity campaign should aim at persuading high end domestic tourists to 
substitute domestic tourism for foreign tourism. 
(Para 108) 
The Committee recommends that on the lines of World Tourism Rankings an d 
"Swachh Survekshan", a competitive Tourism Ranking System should be introduced 
amongst Indian cities which would provide the impetus for them to create, maintain 
and promote more and more tourist spots within their cities. This ranking system may 
be updated every year and the cities with podium finishes in varied categories may be 
incentivised. 
(Para 109) 
A very rapidly emerging trend in global tourism scenario, especially amongst the 
youth is that of Night Tourism. Though India has opened some of its mon uments for 
public to visit in night, it is still in a very nascent stage. Sufficient information is also not 
available regarding the participating sites, their timings and other such details. The 
Committee, therefore, desires that the Ministry should focus on night tourism and make 
more sites available for visit during night, allow extended hours, provide exhaustive 
information on websites and social media pages and aggressively market the concept. 
This will open a new dimension of looking at our existing t ourism heritage and invite 
repeat visitors. 
(Para 110)

X HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT 
 
The Committee notes with concerns that in order to generate sufficient 
manpower in order to meet the needs of the tourism and hospitality industry, the 
Ministry extends assistance to State Governments and Union Territories for 
establishment of Institutes of Hotel Managements and Food Craft Institutes. As of now 
47 Institutes of Hotel Management and 14 Food Craft Institutes have been set up with 
the support of the Ministry and one Central IHM at Jagdishpur, Uttar Pradesh is under 
construction. Given the fact that India faces intense shortage of trained manpower at 
various levels of the travel and hospitality sector, the Committee is of the view that the 
existing institution al capacities would not be able to meet the requirement of trained 
manpower in the tourism and hospitality sector. As this sector has great potential for 
creating enormous number of jobs, the Committee recommends that the gap at the skill 
level should be b ridged and new IHMs and FCIs in the States/UTs that have not been 
covered as yet, should be established to enlarge the quality and quantity output of 
human resources required by the hospitality sector. 
(Para 115) 
The Committee also desires to be apprised o f the physical and financial progress 
in respect of the 3 Food Craft Institutes which have been sanctioned to be set up in the 
State of Madhya Pradesh. 
(Para 116) 
The Committee notes that tourism is a labour -intensive industry and a multi -
sector activity c haracterized by multiple services provided by a range of suppliers and, 
therefore, this sector has huge potential for generation of jobs and absorption of a 
substantially higher workforce. The Committee notes from the Market Pulse data 
supplied by the Mini stry that the hospitality and allied sectors face huge deficit in 
trained manpower. As against annual requirement of 902890 for 2022, the total supply 
including attrition is only 84128 and the demand -supply gap is to the extent of 818762. 
Tourism sector is likely to witness a boom post pandemic because there is lot of pent -up 
demand for travel. This boom may bring an exponential demand for the workforce at 
managerial, supervisor and skilled levels in hospitality and tourism sector. This leads the 
Committee to the irresistible conclusion that the existing institutional capacities would 

 
80 
 
be unable to fulfil the estimated additional requirement of manpower in the hospitality 
and allied sectors. The Committee is of the view that since training is a key interventi on 
and issues of human resource development and capacity building are extremely 
important, the realization of the country's huge, barely tapped tourism potential cannot 
be realized without addressing the challenges of capacity constraints. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should adopt a proactive interventionist 
approach to give a major boost to the various initiatives relating to human resource 
development for the hospitality and tourism sector through convergence with the private 
sector.  
(Para 120) 
The Committee is of the view that there is an imperative need for the hotel 
management institutes to be updated and integrated with the hotel industry. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the complete curriculum of the hotel 
management institutes be revised. The curriculum also needs to be updated regularly to 
reflect the constantly changing dynamics of the industry in the post COVID era. 
(Para 121) 
The Committee in its 288th Report had observed that till the year 2015 -16, the 
Hunar Se Rozgar Tak Programme was geared only towards the creation of employable 
skills. However, with the adoption of Common Norms on Skill Development notified by 
the Ministry of Skill development and Entrepreneurship, it has been made mandatory 
on the part of the Training Provider Institutes to arrange placement/self-employment for 
at least 70% of the certified trainees within three months of completion of the course." 
The Committee would like to be updated whether the implementing agencies have 
adjusted to thi s provision. The Committee also desires to be apprised of the Training 
Service Provider Institutes who have lagged behind the targets set in this regard. The 
Committee also recommends that the Ministry should closely put in place an intensive 
monitoring me chanism for fulfilment of the norms stipulated by the Ministry of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship. 
(Para 122) 
The Committee notes that a number of programmes like Hunar Se Rozgar Tak, 
Skill Testing and Certification, Entrepreneurship Programmes, Tou rism Adventure 
Courses, etc., have been launched under the Scheme of Capacity Building for Service

XI. ASSISTANCE TO CENTRAL AGENCIES FOR TOURISM INFRASTRUCTURE 
DEVELOPMENT 
The Committee observes that the creation of tourism infrastructure by way of 
roads, cruise terminals, railway connectivity, and medical facilities has its multiplier 
effect in terms of overall economic growth and employment generation and acts as a 
catalyst to economic development of the country. Under the Scheme, "Assistance to 
Central Agencies for Tourism Infrastructure Development", a total of Rs. 62.85 crores 
has been released to various Central Agencies for creation of tourism related 
infrastructure. T o make an assessment of the success of this Scheme, the Committee 
desires to be furnished with the total number of projects sanctioned and taken up for 
implementation, along with the released amount vis -à-vis the sanctioned budget during 
the last 5 years, year-wise.  
(Para 125) 
The Committee observes that easy access to tourism destinations and facilities for 
easy movement within the destinations are prerequisites for the development of tourism. 
However, road connectivity has been one of the impediments for the growth of tourism 
in India. It is in this context that the list of 50 Tourist Destinations submitted by the 
Ministry of Tourism to the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways assume 
significance. The Committee notes that out of the 50 destinations selected for road 
connectivity, 23 destinations fall under the purview of Ministry of Road Transport and 
Highways/NHAI where the work is going on. However, no forward movement seems to 
have been made as regards road connectivity to the remaining 27 tourist sites which are 
under the  purview of the State Governments. Though the Ministry of Tourism have 

 
82 
 
taken up the matter with the concerned State Governments, it has not had the desired 
result. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should once 
again take up this matter at the highest level with the concerned State Governments and 
impress upon them that better connectivity to the tourist sites will unlock more tourism 
opportunities for the State and the economic benefits will flow into their economy in the 
shape of increased State revenues and employment as road connectivity is one of the 
fundamental components for successfully developing tourism market. The Committee 
desires to be apprised of the efforts made in this regard and the outcome thereof. 
(Para 127) 
The Committee notes that Ministry of Tourism has shared with the Ministry of 
Road Transport and Highways a new list of 114 destinations for improving connectivity 
to these destinations. Good road, railway or air connectivity to tourist destinations is of 
paramount importance to attract domestic as well as foreign tourists because when these 
destinations are well connected, entrepreneurs in hospitality sector, tour and travel 
operators become confident of making investments and promoting these destinations. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that a dedicated mechanism be put in place for 
planning, coordination and completion of the road connectivity projects to the 114 
tourist destinations. 
(Para 128) 
The Committee observes that last mile connectivity to se veral tourist 
destinations is a deterrent to promotion of tourism. Since last mile connectivity is a key 
driver for promotion of tourism, the Committee recommends that while preparing 
Master Plan for connectivity to tourist destinations, last mile connecti vity should 
mandatorily be included in the concept and the Detailed Project Report. 
(Para 129) 
There is lack of good wayside amenities including toilets at several tourist 
destinations which causes inconvenience to tourists and discourages them from visiti ng 
such places. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should focus more 
on the improvement of wayside amenities at tourist destinations to facilitate travelers. 
(Para 130) 
The Committee notes that with a view to providing enhanced amenitie s and 

 
83 
 
facilities for tourists at railway stations, 22 projects have been sanctioned by the 
Ministry of Tourism at a cost of Rs. 108.54 crores. Though more than ten years have 
elapsed since their sanction in FY 2011 -12, their full operationalization is stil l a far cry. 
The Committee is unhappy at the continuing delay in full operationalization of these 
projects and constrained to observe that the desired priority has not been accorded to 
the upgradation of facilities and amenities at the 22 railway stations selected for the 
purpose. The Committee, therefore, recommends with all the power at its command that 
concrete steps be taken to translate into reality the vision of developing top -class 
facilities at the 22 railway stations within a designated timeline. T he Committee desires 
to be kept apprised of the progress made in this regard. 
(Para 133) 
 
XII SAFE AND HONOURABLE TOURISM 
The Committee notes that 14 States have deployed tourist police in one form or 
the other. Keeping in mind the importance of safety and  security of tourists, the 
Committee in its 275th and 288th Reports presented to the Parliament on the 3rd March 
2020 and 9th March 2021 respectively, hadrecommended that the Ministry of Tourism 
should take up the matter with the remaining States. The Comm ittee is, therefore, 
dismayed to note that no further headway has been made by the remaining States 
towards creation and deployment of tourism police at major tourist destinations. Safety 
and security of the tourists are a determining factor for choosing a  destination and also 
vital for providing quality services in tourism and, therefore, the destinations must have 
an image of utmost tourist safety and security. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its 
earlier recommendation that Tourist police should be s et up in all important tourist 
destinations of each State/UT and should provide travelers security through a spirit of 
courtesy and friendliness. 
(Para 139) 
The Committee is aware that the tourist police system prevails in other countries 
also. The Committee would like the Ministry of Tourism to document such best practices 
from these countries and help adopt such practices in our country. The Committee 
desires to be kept apprised in this regard.  
(Para 140)

MISCELLANEOUS 
Adventure tourism in India has a huge potential for growth as India offers the 
keen adventurers a fascinating range of thrills and activities to choose from. The 
Ministry of Tourism has issued guidelines for approval of Adventure Tourism Operators 
which is  a voluntary scheme. The Committee notes that Adventure Tourism is largely 
unorganized and unregulated. The Committee, therefore, feels that there is an urgent 
need to regularize the Adventure Tour Operators Market and promote registration of 
companies indulging in these activities on a centralized platform to increase credibility 
and enforce safety regulations.
