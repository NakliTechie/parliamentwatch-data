REPORT
 
 The Civil Aviation Sector provides services, which can be categorised as operational, infrastructural and
 regulatory-cum-developmental. The task of framing of national policies and programmes for development
 and regulation of Civil Aviation Sector in the country has been entrusted to the Ministry of Civil Aviation.
 The Ministry of Civil Aviation is also responsible for planning or executing the schemes for qualitative
 growth and expansion of civil air transport. The Ministry oversees the establishment of new aerodromes,
 maintenance and upgradation of the existing aerodromes, regulation of carriage of traffic by air and
 ensuring civil aviation security. Railway Safety, including enquiries into serious railway accidents, also
 constitutes one of the allied functions of the Ministry.
2. The civil aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organisations under the Ministry of
 Civil Aviation i.e. Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation Security. The
 functions pertaining to Railway Safety are entrusted to a Commission. The Ministry of Civil Aviation has
 the following Public Sector Undertakings/Companies/Autonomous Bodies under its administrative
 control:-
i. Air India Limited and its wholly owned subsidiaries viz. Hotel Corporation of India Limited and Air
 India Charters Limited.
ii. Indian Airlines Limited and its wholly owned subsidiary viz. Airlines Allied Services Limited.
iii. Airports Authority of India.
iv. Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited.
v. Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Academy.

BUREAU OF CIVIL AVIATION SECURITY
8. Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS) is responsible for laying down the standards of pre-
embarkation security and anti-sabotage measures in respect of civil flights in India. Actual enforcement on
 the ground is entrusted to the respective State/UT Police and Central Industrial Security Force (CISF).
 The Bureau keeps a constant vigil and monitors the enforcement of the security measures.
9. The approved plan outlay of BCAS for the year 2000-2001 is Rs.5.72 crores which includes Rs.90.00
 lakhs for setting up of Civil Aviation Security Academy for imparting training to the security personnel
 deployed at the various airports. The project is in the formulation stage and a token provision has been
 made for this scheme. The Committee was informed that since the land for the proposed Academy could
 not be identified, the work could not commence and the provision made for this scheme had to be
 dropped. The Committee feels that security, an important element of civil aviation operations, is
 being ignored by the Government, as is evident from the way the project of setting up of Civil
 Aviation Security Academy is being handled. The Committee is disappointed to note that amounts
 are being earmarked for setting up the Civil Aviation Security Academy since 1998-99, however, till
 now even the land where the Academy is proposed to be set up has not been identified. During 1998-
99 and 1999-2000, amounts to the tune of Rs.10.00 lakhs and Rs.50.00 lakhs respectively, allocated
 for the purpose, lapsed. Now for the year 2000-2001, Rs.90.00 lakhs have been earmarked. The
 Committee is of the view keeping Rs.90.00 lakhs as token provision is on the high side. The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that necessary formalities may be completed expeditiously so

ANTI HIJACKING MEASURES
10. The Bureau of Civil Aviation Security has a Contingency Plan to deal with emergencies arising out of
 hijacking and other threats to civil aviation. Among other things, it provides for the establishment of a
 Control Room at each airport. The Control Room comes into action immediately on the occurrence of any
 emergency, like unlawful seizure of aircraft etc. Keeping in view the operational requirements, the
 Contingency Plan is being suitably amended and is under review.
11. The Committee was apprised that subsequent to the incident of hijacking of Indian Airlines flight IC-
814 at Kathmandu, the following special steps have been taken to prevent hijacking and other terrorist
 activities:
(i) Deployment of Central Industrial Security Force (CISF) personnel in place of State Police
 for security duties at the domestic operational airports in a phased manner. The CISF has
 already taken over the security duties at some of the airports.
(ii) Frisking of passengers and hand baggage at the time of entry into sterile area has been
 tightened. Ladder point secondary frisking has been introduced.
(iii) Strict control on access to the airports is being ensured by comprehensive review of Photo
 Identity Cards to restrict the number of pass-holders and banning of entry of the visitors upto
 31.3.2000.
(iv) Deployment of Sky Marshals on flights at random basis as an added safety precaution.
(v) Replacement of old X-ray machines and installation of new colour X-ray machines
 wherever necessary so as to ensure that at least two X-ray machines are available at every
 point.
(vi) Modernisation and upgradation of security related technological set up of the airports is
 being done in a phased manner.
12. The Committee expresses its concern on the hijacking of the flight IC-814 at Kathmandu. The
 Committee also notes the security measures taken for further beefing up security at airports and
 safety of air passengers after the hijacking incident. The Committee feels that the contingency plan
 devised by BCAS has not proved effective while handling the plane-hijacking incident. The
 Committee is of the view that any contingency plan should ensure avoiding delay in decision-
making process and recommends that the latest technological equipments available in the field of
 airport security may be procured at the earliest to prevent recurrence of any such incident. The
 Committee feels that the lesson drawn from the handling of the hijacked flight No.IC-814 should
 help the concerned Departments in drawing future plans to handle such situations. While
 strengthening the airport security, due care should be taken that the passengers are not
 unnecessarily harassed.

16. The Committee finds that the profits of the AAI have remained stagnant at Rs.208.40 crores
 during 1998-99 and 1999-2000 and that only a marginal increase of Rs.5.57 crores has been
 projected for the year 2000-2001 in comparison to the profit of previous year. The Committee fails
 to understand how the AAI would continue to make profits without augmenting their earnings. The
 Committee recommends that the Government should take steps to ensure maximum utilisation of
 available capacity and efficient management of airports infrastructure. The Committee also
 recommends that a rationalised system of airport charges be introduced for optimum utilisation of
 the airports by using peak and off-peak time charges and promoting airports in far-flung regions by
 having varying airport charges from airport to airport depending upon the facilities available at the
 airport.
17.
 On being asked about the reasons for under-utilisation of funds during 1999-2000, the Airports
 Authority of India submitted that the some of the reasons therefor were as under:-
i. Due to dispute of patent rights, there was delay in placing orders for aerobridges for Chennai and
 Calcutta airports resulting in shortfall of Rs.5.88 crores.

 contracts subsequently were rescinded and the balance works were awarded as separate packages
 resulting in the overall delay and under utilisation of funds amounting to Rs.18.00 crores
 approximately.
iii. The land acquired from State Government/Defence for airports upgradation at Leh, Jammu, Jaipur,
 Silchar, Manglore etc. was not cleared for execution of the work which has resulted in the delay of
 ongoing works and in some cases the work could not be started. This resulted in under-utilisation of
 funds to the tune of Rs.5.0 crores.

 of finalisation of their projects so that the necessary formalities are completed in advance and work
 is carried out without any obstacle and the funds are fully utilised. They should also enforce a
 monitoring system at their headquarters to look after timely utilisation of funds and progress of
 various projects undertaken by it. Efforts should also be made to remove the bottlenecks identified
 as causing delays in projects.

 declared operational in November, 2000. Airports Authority of India has plans to install Category II ILS
 at Lucknow, Jammu and Amritsar Airports by 2001.
20. The Committee was also informed that the task of restructuring of airports under AAI, as and when
 found suitable, will be undertaken through long-term lease route subject to:-
i. The ownership will remain with Airports Authority of India.
ii. Lease rental will accrue to Airports Authority of India enabling it to subsidise the development and
 upgradation of rest of the airports without depending upon budgetary support; and
iii. Restructuring will be achieved in a shorter-time frame.
21. The Committee notes that the proposal for restructuring of airports has been under
 consideration of the Government for a long time. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 Government should take a decision at the earliest whereby the benefits arising out of restructuring
 should not only be confined to the airports in major cities but also to the medium and small airports
 in remote areas, which would go a long way in giving a much needed fillip to the development of
 airports in the country.

26. Air India is taking action to rationalise its present fleet by studying the possibility of phasing out some
 of the oldest aircraft types, like the B747-200/300 and A300s, dry leasing aircraft types already in the fleet
 (such as the B747-400 and A310-300) and thereby reducing the number of aircraft types in its fleet to
 three types and possibly even to two types only.
27. Air India is also striving to increase its market share through code shares and alliances. These alliances
 and tie-ups have enabled Air India to expand its network and market presence without undertaking any
 resource commitments or risks of operations and to earn profits, which are a net contribution to Air
 India’s bottom line.
28. Air India has presently seven route-specific code share arrangements, viz with air France, Swissair,
 Asiana, Bellview, Kuwait Airways, SAS and Austrian, through which Air India collectively offers 781
 seats per week in each directions (equivalent to almost 4 A310 services per week) and is estimated to
 carry 34000 passengers during 1999-2000. Also a code-share arrangement has been concluded with
 Virgin Atlantic Airlines of UK recently for services between Delhi and London. As some of these code-
share partners increase their services to/from India, the total number of seats that Air India will operate
 through the code shares by Winter-2000-2001 will increase to around 1118 seats per week in each
 direction including the proposed implementation of the AIVB code-share arrangement.
29. The Committee is happy to note that the plan outlay of the Air India for the year 2000-2001 has
 been increased by more than 55% in comparison to that of 1999-2000. The Committee is also happy
 to note that losses have also been going down which are likely to be to the tune of Rs.92.13 crores for
 the year 2000-2001 as compared to the estimated losses of Rs.146.18 crores for the year 1999-2000.
30. The Committee notes the steps being taken by Air India not only to minimise its losses but also to
 make it a profitable organisation e.g. standardisation of fleet, joining global alliances through code-
share. The Committee hopes that fleet with less types of aircrafts will reduce the demand for
 investment and spares, maintenance facilities and simulators and will also result in better utilisation
 of aircrafts, infrastructural facilities and technical manpower of pilots and maintenance engineers.
 The Committee recommends that Air India should decide as to what kind of fleet they should go in
 for so that necessary steps may be taken immediately to rationalise their fleet without waiting for
 the final decision for restructuring of the Air India.
31. The Committee considers the code-shares and alliances as effective tools to supplement the
 physical operations of Air India and appreciates Air India’s effort in developing a secondary
 network through airline alliances in the form of code-share and recommends that Air India should
 continuously evaluate the possibility of increasing the scope of such code-share arrangements
 wherever the market potential exist.

32. While inviting the attention of the Ministry of Civil Aviation to the incident of off-loading of 150
 passengers that took place in the last week of February 2000, the Committee desired to know the norms of
 over-booking of seating capacity. In their reply the Ministry of Civil Aviation informed that as an industry
 practice, flights are overbooked so as to optimise seat utilisation. Based on the history of loads carried,
 routes, seasonality, etc. during the peak periods, flights are generally over-booked upto 5% of the total
 seat capacity and during off peak period, this percentage is increased upto 25%. On 18th February 2000,
 flight AI-129 on the sector Mumbai/Delhi/London/ Chicago was cancelled due to non-availability of the
 operating crew. Owing to this, the booked load of five first class/19 executive class/445 economic class
 passengers had to be transferred on to subsequent flights. This also had a snow balling effect on all
 subsequent flights for a fortnight, which resulted in off-loadings. To cater to the stranded passengers, Air
 India operated two extra section flights on 2
nd & 8th March 2000 to London with a B747-400 aircraft.
33. The Committee is of the view that such incidents are certainly bound to undermine the image of
 India amongst the NRIs and foreigners, which may in turn affect the foreign tourist arrivals in the
 country. The Committee recommends that adequate steps may be taken to ensure that such
 incidents do not occur in future.
HAJ FLIGHTS
34. The Committee drew the attention of the Ministry to the delay in arrival of Haj flight on 22.3.2000.
 Also the baggage of about 100 Haj pilgrims was not brought in the flight concerned. The time of the
 arrival of the flight was revised four times but no staff of Air India was present to update or give any
 information to the waiting visitors/relatives at the Haj Terminal during the night. This resulted in
 inconvenience to the Haj pilgrims as well as the visitors/relatives waiting at the airport.
35. Every year a large number of flights operate for Haj pilgrims. The Committee takes a serious
 note and fails to understand the reasons for mis-management in handling Haj flights. The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that instead of the prevalent adhocism, the Government should
 devise a well-planned permanent mechanism for management of Haj flights so as to avoid the
 recurrence of such incidents in future. The Committee feels that Air India officials should provide
 information regarding exact arrival time of flights to the waiting visitors/relatives and the names of
 the passengers whose luggage could not be accommodated. The arrangements at the airports should
 also be reviewed for smooth arrival of Haj flights.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
The Committee is of the view that Civil Aviation sector is closely linked to the tourism and trade sectors.
 Therefor, it is important that airport infrastructure and air service should not be allowed to suffer for want
 of budgetary support since degradation of Civil Aviation sector would also affect the trade and tourism.
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry of Civil Aviation should make a realistic assessment
 of requirements of various organisations and if necessary, increase the budgetary support to maintain the
 existing Civil Aviation infrastructure properly till a final decision is taken about restructuring thereof.
 (Para 6)

The Committee feels that security, an important element of civil aviation operations, is being ignored by
 the Government, as is evident from the way the project of setting up of Civil Aviation Security Academy
 is being handled. The Committee is disappointed to note that amounts are being earmarked for setting up
 the Civil Aviation Security Academy since 1998-99, however, till now even the land where the Academy
 is proposed to be set up has not been identified. During 1998-99 and 1999-2000, amounts to the tune of

Anti Hijacking Measures
The Committee expresses its concern on the hijacking of the flight IC-814 at Kathmandu. The Committee
 also notes the security measures taken for further beefing up security at airports and safety of air
 passengers after the hijacking incident. The Committee feels that the contingency plan devised by BCAS
 has not proved effective while handling the plane-hijacking incident. The Committee is of the view that
 any contingency plan should ensure avoiding delay in decision-making process and recommends that the
 latest technological equipments available in the field of airport security may be procured at the earliest to
 prevent recurrence of any such incident. The Committee feels that the lesson drawn from the handling of
 the hijacked flight No.IC-814 should help the concerned Departments in drawing future plans to handle
 such situations. While strengthening the airport security, due care should be taken that the passengers are
 not unnecessarily harassed.
(Para 12)
Airports Authority of India
The Committee finds that the profits of the AAI have remained stagnant at Rs.208.40 crores during 1998-
99 and 1999-2000 and that only a marginal increase of Rs.5.57 crores has been projected for the year
 2000-2001 in comparison to the profit of previous year. The Committee fails to understand how the AAI
 would continue to make profits without augmenting their earnings. The Committee recommends that the
 Government should take steps to ensure maximum utilisation of available capacity and efficient
 management of airports infrastructure. The Committee also recommends that a rationalised system of
 airport charges be introduced for optimum utilisation of the airports by using peak and off-peak time
 charges and promoting airports in far-flung regions by having varying airport charges from airport to
 airport depending upon the facilities available at the airport.
The Committee is concerned to note the slow pace of developmental work at the airports & the under-
utilisation of funds by the Airports Authority of India during the last few years. The Committee observes
 that the reasons furnished by Airports Authority of India show a lackadaisical attitude on its part. The
 Committee feels that routine matters like contractual and construction problems should be taken care of at
 the planning stage itself so that funds allocated therefor do not lapse. The Committee recommends that
 Airports Authority of India should make efforts to overcome delays and evolve methods for time-bound
 completion of schemes. The Committee also recommends that Airports Authority of India should involve
 all the concerned agencies at the time of finalisation of their projects so that the necessary formalities are
 completed in advance and work is carried out without any obstacle and the funds are fully utilised. They
 should also enforce a monitoring system at their headquarters to look after timely utilisation of funds and
 progress of various projects undertaken by it. Efforts should also be made to remove the bottlenecks
 identified as causing delays in projects.

Air India Limited
The Committee is happy to note that the plan outlay of the Air India for the year 2000-2001 has been
 increased by more than 55% in comparison to that of 1999-2000. The Committee is also happy to note
 that losses have also been going down which are likely to be to the tune of Rs.92.13 crores for the year
 2000-2001 as compared to the estimated losses of Rs.146.18 crores for the year 1999-2000.
The Committee notes the steps being taken by Air India not only to minimise its losses but also to make it
 a profitable organisation e.g. standardisation of fleet, joining global alliances through code-share. The
 Committee hopes that fleet with less types of aircrafts will reduce the demand for investment and spares,
 maintenance facilities and simulators and will also result in better utilisation of aircrafts, infrastructural
 facilities and technical manpower of pilots and maintenance engineers. The Committee recommends that
 Air India should decide as to what kind of fleet they should go in for so that necessary steps may be taken
 immediately to rationalise their fleet without waiting for the final decision for restructuring of the Air
 India.
The Committee considers the code-shares and alliances as effective tools to supplement the physical
 operations of Air India and appreciates Air India’s effort in developing a secondary network through
 airline alliances in the form of code-share and recommends that Air India should continuously evaluate
 the possibility of increasing the scope of such code-share arrangements wherever the market potential
 exist.

 amongst the NRIs and foreigners, which may in turn affect the foreign tourist arrivals in the country. The
 Committee recommends that adequate steps may be taken to ensure that such incidents do not occur in
 future.

Haj Flights
Every year a large number of flights operate for Haj pilgrims. The Committee takes a serious note and fails
 to understand the reasons for mis-management in handling Haj flights. The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that instead of the prevalent adhocism, the Government should devise a well-planned
 permanent mechanism for management of Haj flights so as to avoid the recurrence of such incidents in
 future. The Committee feels that Air India officials should provide information regarding exact arrival
 time of flights to the waiting visitors/relatives and the names of the passengers whose luggage could not
 be accommodated. The arrangements at the airports should also be reviewed for smooth arrival of Haj
 flights.

Indian Airlines Limited
The Committee notes that the Plan Outlay of the Indian Airlines has been only marginally increased for the
 year 2000-2001 as compared to 1999-2000. The net profit of the Company is expected to go up to
 Rs.39.25 crores for the year 1999-2000 as against Rs.14.17 crores during the previous year. The
 Committee appreciates that the Company has been able to increase its profit inspite of general recession in
 the economy and sluggish market conditions witnessed during the year.
The Committee is aware of the proposal of restructuring of the Indian Airlines whereby the equity share
 holding of the Government in the Company will be brought down to 49%. However, before the final
 decision in the matter is taken, the Committee would like the Government to take into consideration
 certain facts. The Committee is of the view that Indian Airlines has to play a role distinct from that of
 privately owned commercial airlines. In the Indian Airlines, a considerable amount of public investment
 had been made in the past and therefore, it is required to meet certain social and strategic requirements
 which may not be commercially so viable but are essential in the national interest. The company has
 developed expertise not only in the provision of air services but also in the field of engineering services
 and development of highly skilled personnel. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the potential of
 Indian Airlines not only to provide air services but also to provide training and engineering services to
 other users and airlines, both domestic and regional, should be exploited. The Committee also
 recommends that the Government should initiate measures to make the Indian Airlines a viable and a
 vibrant enterprise and it should be given autonomy in their day-to-day working, without the Government
 relinquishing its responsibility, and evolve fleet planning strategy taking into account the route structure
 of the Indian Airlines so as to ensure optimum utilisation of resources, particularly aircrafts.
(Paras 41 and 42)
Coordination Between National Carriers
The Committee observes that both the national carriers, Air India and Indian Airlines, instead of
 competing with the private airlines, are competing with each other and this has resulted in an unproductive
 and unhealthy competition between the two airlines, which has affected the efficiency and profitability of
 both the airlines. The Committee feels that there has not been an effective implementation of the measures
 planned to achieve greater coordination between the two airlines. The Committee, therefore, recommends
 that coordinated steps should be taken for optimum utilisation of the existing resources of both the
 national carriers with a view to improve operational and financial efficiency and feels that it would also
 result in better utilisation of aircrafts, infrastructural facilities and manpower.
