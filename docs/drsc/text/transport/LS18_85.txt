 culture is the most crucial role-player which gives meaning to our present existence. The role of Culture lies not only in the context of promotion of economic growth but also in relation to
 other objectives of creating an environment that encourages self-expression and exploration on the part of individuals and communities besides support to arts and artists and encouraging

2.         The Ministry of Culture, which was set up in 1985 through the 174 th Amendment of the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 was part of the Ministry of
 Human Resource Development; later it was transferred to the newly created Ministry of Tourism and Culture. In May 2004 it has been declared as an independent Ministry and is now
 Ministry of Culture. The Ministry is responsible for the promotion, preservation and conservation of the tangible and intangible cultural heritage of the country. It also promotes mutual
 understanding, goodwill and foster closer relations with foreign countries. Preservation of ancient and historic monuments and records, exploration and excavation of archaeological sites
 and remains, maintenance and expansion of libraries and museums of national importance and observance of centenaries and anniversaries of eminent personalities are among its other
 responsibilities. The institutions of the Ministry, which are engaged in promotion, and dissemination of art and culture broadly are the Zonal  Cultural Centers, Center for Cultural Resources
 and Training, and through the Schemes operated Departmentally. The public private participation in the Ministry is done under the aegis of the National Culture Fund.
3.         The programmes undertaken by the Ministry of Culture can broadly be classified as, Activities of North East Region, Archaeology, Museums, Anthropology and Ethnology,
 Archives and Archival Libraries, Tibetan/Buddhist Studies, Libraries, Akademies and National School of Drama, Promotion and Dissemination of Culture, Memorials, Centenaries and
 Anniversaries, Cultural Relations and Festival of India, IGNCA and Secretariat.
4.         Plan programmes of the Ministry of Culture relating to promotion, preservation and conservation of the cultural heritage of the country is implemented through a network of 2
 attached offices, 6 subordinate offices and 26 Autonomous organisations under its control and through 16 schemes directly operated by the Ministry for promotion and dissemination of art
 and culture.  Its activities and programmes have been organized under eleven broad heads. They are Promotion & Dissemination, Archaeology, Museums, Archives, Anthropology,
 Performing Arts, Libraries, Buddhist and Tibetan Institutes, Activities for North Eastern Region and Other Expenditure.

8.         Demand No.20 containing the details of budget allocation of the Ministry of Culture for the financial year 2005-06 was considered by the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
 Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture at its meeting held on the 29
th March 2005.  The Secretary Ministry of Culture during the course of oral evidence held on 29 th March 2005,
 deposed before the Committee :
“This year has been a difficult year for them as the Ministry had major financial constraints.  The budget was passed late and they had accordingly a shorter time to spend the
 money allocated to them.  There was also a cap by the Ministry of Finance.   Accordingly the Ministry of Culture had to spend 65% of the money by December and thereafter
 there was a slash of about Rs.65 crore at the revised budget stage.  After intervention of the Ministry they got restoration of the cut to the extent of Rs.20 crore.  But this has
 certainly put a strain on the institutions/field offices functioning under the Ministry.”
 
9.         The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Culture had proposed an outlay of Rs.551.12 crore and also got an approved outlay of the same amount with a capital component
 of Rs.61.80 crore.   The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Culture will be able to utilize the funds for implementation of new Schemes/Projects optimally. 
10.       The Committee, however, is not convinced about the reasons cited by the Ministry that budget was passed late and accordingly, there was shorter time available to spend the money
 allocated to them and observes that if the annual plan starts after the beginning of the financial year itself, then it considerably reduces the capacity of the Ministry to plan out expenditure.  
 Therefore, the planning for financial progress has to start even before as the broad heads are already known to the Ministry under the contours of the five-year plan.  The Committee also
 fails to understand as to how this intra-year financial development should deter the Ministry in planning it in such a manner that none of the financial allocations made can be successfully
 used by the end of the year.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the Finance Ministry recalling the money which has been already allocated is a serious problem and the
 Ministry should take adequate care and evolve a sound method of financial planning so that such kind of circumstance may never occur in future.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
 14.      The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) was established in 1861.  It is an attached office of the Ministry of Culture.  Since its establishment, 144 years ago, the ASI has grown into
 a large organization having its network throughout the country.  At present, the ASI has declared 3656 monuments, which are of national importance in the country, including 17 properties
 inscribed on the World Heritage List by UNESCO.  The major activities of the ASI are maintenance, conservation and preservation of centrally protected monuments/sites and remains.  It
 conducts archeological exploration and excavation regularly.  It also keeps developing epigraphical research, setting up and reorganization of site museums and training in archaeology. The
 Archaeological Survey of India has prepared perspective plans for the 10
th Five Year Plan period which envisaged an integrated approach covering conservation, preservation, landscaping,
 organizing/ re-organising of site museums and problem oriented excavations and exploration.   With a holistic approach, the Archaeological Survey of India is devoted towards creation of
 cultural hubs encompassing facilities for domestic and foreign tourists.  The proposed cultural hubs are, i.e. Mahabalipuram, Ajanta & Ellora , Hampi, Kurukshetra, Chittorgarh,
 Kumbhalgarh, Sibsagar monuments and   Fatehpur Sikri.
15.       Plan allocation to the Archaeological Survey of India has increased by Rs.10 crore in the BE 2005-06 in comparison to BE 2004-05 of Rs.70.00 crores which was reduced to
 Rs.58.00 crores in the RE stage.  The Committee desired to know the reasons for increasing the fund under this head.  The Ministry has replied that the original budget allocation under Plan
 during the year 2004-05 was Rs.70.00 Crores.  Subsequently Ministry of Culture has reduced the Plan budget allocation to Archaeological Survey of India for 2004-05 to Rs.48.00 Crores. 
 Anticipating that as this drastic cut would affect the conservation and other archaeological works of the ASI, ASI requested to reconsider the reduction in budget allocation to ASI.  Taking
 into consideration the above, the Ministry of Culture partially restored the budget allocation (Plan), under R.E. for 2004-05 to Rs.58.00 Crores.  Ministry of Culture has conveyed that
 Rs.80.00 Crores has been allocated to ASI for the financial year 2005-06.  The allocation has been increased with a view to give special focus on management plan of the World Heritage
 Sites and special focus on strengthening of selected site museums.
16.       The Committee desired to know the details of projects works to be undertaken by ASI during the year 2005-06.  To this the Ministry replied that all appurtenant lands adjoining the
 protected monuments, which are also a part of the protected area, are being surveyed and fenced to develop as greens. Linkages are being developed between the Ministry of Culture and the
 Department of Tourism in order to ensure that growth in a particular cluster of monuments is integrated.Greater  thrust is being given to collaborations with the Corporate Sector such as the
 Taj Group of Hotels World Monuments Fund etc. for formulation of Master Plans and for implementation of integrated development of some important monuments.  ASI has taken-up a
 five years Project of comprehensive Conservation and Environmental Development of certain monuments such as Ajanta , Ellora, Pitalkhora and Aurangabad Caves, Bibi-ka-Maqbara ,
 Daulatabad Fort under Ajanta Ellora Conservation and Tourism Project – Phase-II with loan from Japan Bank of International Cooperation (JBIC), Japan. The six Excavation Branches will
 undertake large-scale excavation and simultaneous conservation, while some of the Circles will undertake the small scale excavation and exploration for the year 2004-05. In total the ASI
 has to undertake excavation and simultaneous conservation work at 16 sites, most of which will continue next year also (2005-06).  It is proposed that the ASI will take up the Underwater
 Archaeology, the excavation at Dwaraka (Gujarat ) besides ongoing projects.  Additional funds under Plan in 2005-06 have been allocated to Archaeological Survey of India for
 augmentation on the ongoing schemes, for establishment of 25 new site Museums, digital documentation of objects of site Museums and also for construction of  ongoing building projects
 of ASI including the proposed building for Institute of Archeology  at Greater Noida.

18.       The Committee notes that the Plan Allocation for ASI has been increased by Rs.10.00 crore in BE 2005-06.  The Committee hopes that this increased allocation would be utilized on

(i)         Non-Writing/Publication of Excavation Reports
19.       In the absence of timely publication of reports, achievements of ASI remains largely unattended and unnoticed by the public. Therefore the Committee enquired about the
 outstanding delay in publication/non-writing of Excavation Report. In their replied the Ministry of Culture informed the Committee that a Steering Committee was formed by the DG, ASI
 to look into the question of pending excavation reports.  The Steering Committee has identified 56 pending excavation reports out of which 17 are pertaining to large-scale excavations and
 39 to small-scale excavations.  The Committee laid down a time frame of 6 months for submission of reports of small-scale excavations and 15 months for large-scale excavations. 
 However, 32 reports are to be written by the retired officers and most of them have expressed their inability to write the reports or asked for several facilities for completing them.  Hence, it
 has been decided to assign this task to the serving officers who were earlier associated with the excavations.  A time frame of 24 months is considered to be reasonable for completion of the
 excavation reports. The Ministry also informed that the Archeological Survey of India has communicated to all the excavators both in service and the retired, requesting them to complete
 them pending excavation report within stipulated time-frame.  The Ministry further informed that some of the excavators have positively responded to this.
20.       The Committee in its 80
th Report on the Demands for Grants (2004-05) had recommended that the Ministry should not only take initiative in publishing reports in time but also to
 digitize the reports as a result of which those can be preserved for longer period.  The Ministry of Culture in the Action Taken Replies to this recommendation had observed that
 "Digitization of various archeological reports may be taken up after observing the required codal formalities".  The Committee again further enquired that as to how long the Ministry would
 take to implement this and fulfill the required codal formalities.  At this stage, the Ministry informed that ASI has not yet taken up any programme of digitization of archaeological reports. 
21.       The Committee is of the opinion that if the excavation reports are not published in time then all the tax payers money spent on them goes waste.  The Committee is utterly dissatisfied
 that despite repeated recommendations made earlier by this Committee, the Ministry is still not in a position to indicate specific time frame by when this job will be accomplished.  The
 Committee observes that the labour and the money gone into excavations undertaken by the Archaeological Survey of India for the knowledge to crystallize for generations and the efforts
 to make excavations more understandable becomes fruitful only when the findings are documented.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that all excavation reports should be published
 within a specific time frame in order to check this massive national loss. 
22.       The Committee feels that the Ministry of Culture has taken the recommendation of the Standing Committee contained in its 80
th Report on the Demands for Grants (2004-2005)
 regarding digitization of Archaeological Reports in a casual manner and till now has not been able to firm up its view in the matter.   Therefore, the Ministry may consider the
 recommendation of the Committee and take up the programme of digitization of  Archeological Reports. 
 
(ii)        Merger of National Mission on Antiquities and National Mission on Built Heritage:
 
23.       The Ministry of Culture had launched two Missions namely, National Mission on Antiquities and National Mission on Built Heritage, with following major objectives:-
·        To create widespread awareness about the antiquities and built heritage and the laws governing it.
·        Creation of a credible National Data Bank of Built Heritage and Antiquities.
·        Selected monuments from different parts of the country will be taken up for structural conservation.
·        Out reach programmes will be organized to create public awareness of our rich cultural heritage and the ways by which it can be sustained.  Besides attempt will be made to create
 synergy amongst various institutions and organisations working towards a similar goal.
·        Mission would help in capacity building in heritage care in State Archaeology, Museums and NGOs.
·        Mission would help the concerned authorities in the formulation of national, state and local policy in safeguarding and management of heritage.
 
24.       The Ministry has further informed the Committee vide their Action Taken Replies to para 70 of the 80
th Report on the Demands for Grants (2004-05) that both these missions are
 proposed to be merged together.  Subsequently, the Ministry was asked to justify the rationale behind launching these missions separately and merging it together afterwards.  The
 explanation put forth by the Ministry was as follows:-
“Initially it was proposed to set up two separate Missions – National Mission on Built Heritage, Monuments and Sites and National Mission on Antiquities.  The estimated cost for
 the two Missions when the proposals were initially formulated was Rs.250 crore and Rs.150 crore respectively.  Based on the comments received from the Planning Commission,
 Department of Expenditure, IFD etc., the matter was reconsidered in consultation with the Archaeological Survey of India.  It was suggested that since the nature of work for the two

25.       The Director-General, Archeological Survey of India during the course of oral evidence submitted before the Committee that :
 
"… there is a shortage of staff in the ASI mostly on the conservation side.  We do not have conservation professionals in various disciplines.  So far as the mission is

26.       In view of the explanation given by the Ministry the Committee concludes that the whole process indicates confusion in the decision making process and complete lack of
 farsightedness on the part of the Ministry in planning its programmes and projects which has not only affected the rich cultural heritage of the country but also led to valuable wastage of
 time and resources. 
27.       The Committee fails to understand that why so much additional work was given to the ASI when ASI was not able to successfully complete the work already assigned.  The
 Committee is also not convinced about the objectives of these Mission modes.  On the one hand the Ministry has stated that they are not recruiting more staff.  On the other hand the
 Committee has been informed that for the Mission purpose the Ministry will be contractualising jobs on short term basis.  In view of this the Committee directs that the Ministry should
 adopt a clear cut guideline in this regard and the ASI should be provided with appropriate wherewithal so that laudable goals of these Missions do not remain unachieved.
 
(iii)       Problem of Encroachment and Security of Monuments/Sites under the Protection of ASI:
 
28.       The Committee was quite concerned about the illegal encroachment of monuments/sites under the protection of ASI and desired to know from the Ministry as to what was being done
 to check the encroachment around the monuments.  The Ministry of Culture replied that as and when an encroachment is noticed in the protected area of a monument, the Superintending
 Archaeologist issues a notice to the encroacher as per the provisions of the Ancient  Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958.  A complaint is also lodged with the local
 police.  When no response is received from the encroacher, the DG, ASI issues a notice ordering the illegal occupant to vacate the occupied site and remove the encroachment.  If no action
 is taken by the encroacher, the DG, ASI issues an order under Section 19 (2) of the Ancient  Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958 and Rule 38 (2) of the Ancient
 Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Rules, 1959 to the Collector and the concerned district authorities for demolition and removal of the encroachment.  The Archaeological
 Survey of India also initiates legal action in the courts against the encroachers.  Though the Superintending Archaeologists have been vested with the power of Estate Officer and authorized
 to issue eviction notices under the Public Premises Eviction of Unauthorized Occupants (Act), 1971, the successful removal of encroachment depends upon  entirely on the cooperation of
 the police and district authorities, which is seldom forthcoming.   In the Delhi Circle specifically, there are encroachments on 14 centrally protected monuments and action is under way for
 their removal.  In the case of several encroachments, the matter is sub-judice in the courts of law.  In so far as the Siri Fort Wall is concerned, the protected  stretch of the fortification wall is
 free from encroachment, but a portion of the Siri Fort Wall, which is not under the protection of the Archaeological Survey of India and falling within Shahpur Jat Village, is encroached
 upon by the villagers.   There are two cases in the Court in respect of Siri Fort Wall.
29.       The Director-General, ASI during the course of oral evidence also submitted that  :
"Encroachment in and around ASI monuments is one of the problems ASI is facing.  3,658 monuments are protected.  If you put a reasonable number of people to protect it, it
 would require at least 7-8 thousand people.  If you take monuments like the Taj or Red Fort, each would take at least 200 people.  In fact, it is consuming one-third of the
 staff to protect just these two.  There is no physical protection at all and the encroachment is going on for several years.  We have powers to issue notice for the people to
 vacate.  But we don't have the power to compel them to vacate. This State Government police support hardly ever comes."

 Fort in Delhi and the Taj Mahal in Agra.  For 459 CISF personnel employed in these two sensitive monuments, the financial implication per year is Rs.9.25 crores.  Besides, 100 State
 Police personnel are being paid for deployment at some monuments and museums.  The total payment involved to the State Police personnel is Rs.1.5 crores annually.  However a large
 number of protected monuments all over the country are not having any security.  There is no precise estimate of the funds required for providing security guards to all these monuments. 
 However on purely tentative estimates, it is estimated that an amount of Rs.100 crores may be required annually for proper physical security to all the protected monuments by way of

ZONAL CULTURAL CENTRES
75.       The Zonal Cultural Centres (ZCC) were created to promote cultural awareness and to identify, nurture and promote vanishing folk art traditions in the rural and semi urban areas. 
 The seven Zonal Cultural Centres, encompassing all the states and union territories of the country, were set up to extend local creative support to folk and traditional artisans of India.  Each
 Centre functions as an autonomous body, with the Governor of the State, where it is located serving as the ex officio  Chairman.  In Budget Estimate 2005-06 an amount of Rs.30 crore has
 been allocated to the Zonal Cultural Centres as against of Rs.19 crore allocated in RE 2004-05. The main source of income for meeting administrative expenditure of the ZCCs is the
 interest accrued on the  fixed deposits of the corpus fund.  The falling interest rate has resulted in the reduction of income from the corpus fund while expenditure on establishment costs
 have increased due to inflation, increase in artists remuneration, increase in salary etc.   To tide over the financial crises an allocation of Rs.35 crores was provided in 10
th Five Year Plan i.e.
 Rs.5 crores per ZCC for corpus fund.
76.       The corpus of the ZCCs were enhanced by Rs.5 crores each to boost their financial strength of which Rs.2 crores has already been released to each of ZCCs.   In the forthcoming
 financial year an additional corpus of Rs.3 crores will be sanctioned. In  view of the special status of NE States and the problem faced by NEZCC the total additional corpus of Rs.5 crores
 has already been released to them.  Further, an amount of Rs.15 lakhs for Shilpgram for each of the ZCCs will be sanctioned in the forthcoming financial year for their Shilpgram activities. 
77.       The Committee notes that Zonal Cultural Centres are playing important roles to create cultural awareness among the public specially in rural and semi urban areas.  The efforts of the
 Ministry to strengthen the Zonal Cultural Centres by enhancing the allocation is praise worthy.   The Committee directs the Ministry to ensure that the additional amount of Rs.5.00 crore
 released to North East Zonal Cultural Centres should be fully utilized by them.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS 
 AT A GLANCE
 
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Culture had proposed an outlay of Rs.551.12 crore and also got an approved outlay of the same amount with a capital component
 of Rs.61.80 crore.   The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Culture will be able to utilize the funds for implementation of new Schemes/Projects optimally. 
            The Committee, however, is not convinced about the reasons cited by the Ministry that budget was passed late and accordingly, there was shorter time available to spend the money
 allocated to them and observes that if the annual plan starts after the beginning of the financial year itself, then it considerably reduces the capacity of the Ministry to plan out expenditure.  
 Therefore, the planning for financial progress has to start even before as the broad heads are already known to the Ministry under the contours of the five-year plan.  The Committee also
 fails to understand as to how this intra-year financial development should deter the Ministry in planning it in such a manner that none of the financial allocations made can be successfully
 used by the end of the year.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the Finance Ministry recalling the money which has been already allocated is a serious problem and the
 Ministry should take adequate care and evolve a sound method of financial planning so that such kind of circumstance may never occur in future.
            The Committee notes that the Ministry of Culture this time has given more emphasis to various reputed institutes of art and culture like, Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies,
 Gandhi Smriti and Darshan Samiti, Buddhist and Tibetan Institutes, Multi Purpose Cultural Complexes, Ram Krishna Mission Institute of Culture, Kolkata, etc.  The Committee hopes that
 the construction of proposed new Multi Purpose Cultural Complexes in Manipur, Nagaland, Assam, Rajasthan, Chattisgarh  and Andhra Pradesh would be completed in time.  The
 Committee further directs that the additional amount of Rs.46 crore which has been for the first time provided for Centenaries and Anniversaries Celebration will be spent fairly and in an
 unbiased manner. The Committee also recommends that ongoing projects on publication of volumes of works of Lok Nayak Jaiprakash Narayan be completed in time and funds for that
 purpose will be provided as assured by the Ministry.    Moreover, Birth Anniversary Celebrations of Lok Nayak Jaiprakash Narayan and Choudhary Charan Singh should be continued
 every year.  The Committee strongly recommends that the entire provisions made by the Ministry of Culture be utilised during the year 2005-06.

MERGER OF NATIONAL MISSION ON ANTIQUITIES AND NATIONAL MISSION ON BUILT HERITAGE
 
In view of the explanation given by the Ministry the Committee concludes that the whole process indicates confusion in the decision making process and complete lack of

(Paras 26 & 27)
 
PROBLEM OF ENCROACHMENT AND SECURITY OF MONUMENTS/SITES UNDER THE PROTECTION OF ASI
 
            The Committee expresses its anxiety over the incidents of encroachment upon the land of Centrally Protected Monuments .  The Committee is more concerned about the inability of
 Archeological Survey of India to get back the land from encroachers.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that a complete assessment should be made regarding encroachment
 of monuments/sites under the protection of Archaeological Survey of India all over the country.  Prompt action ought be taken against the encroachers with the help of State
 Governments/Local Administrations and the matter should be pursued at the highest level.  The Committee also recommends that adequate budgetary provisions should be made for security
 arrangements and protection of monuments, as this is the most crucial aspect of preserving our cultural heritage and wherever possible modern gadgets like, electronic surveillance cameras
 should be installed in the monuments.  The Committee feels that security is a sovereign function.  Hence, it is the responsibility of Central Government to get the monuments and other
 important assets protected.  In view of this, the Committee is of the opinion that the Union Home Ministry should pay the bills towards the expenses incurred for paying the Central Security
 Agencies under this head.  Accordingly, the Ministry of Culture should pursue the matter at the highest level. 
(Para 31)
DISPLAY OF ANTIQUITIES AT SITES MUSEUMS
            The Committee observes that shortage of trained manpower is the reason behind non-displaying of objects found during excavations at various site museums. Therefore, the
 Committee recommends that the ASI should have trained personnel in this field and adequate financial allocation should also be made for the purpose.
(Para 33)
 
NATIONAL MESUEM AND MESUEM SECURITY
The Committee expresses its concern towards the prevailing scenario of museum security all over the country especially, the National  Museum.  It is a matter of urgent need to have
 full proof security to guard the invaluable antiquities preserved at museums.  The Committee, therefore, recommends to acquire modern security gadgets to strengthen the museum security
 besides training of security personnel to operate on these security gadgets, since it is felt that modernization and security are closely inter-related.  The Committee further directs the
 Ministry to provide museums with adequate funds to strengthen their security and to appoint a consultant to solicit global expertise as this is a very specialized subject.

 recommends that it should be filled up by a regular Director immediately.
