INTRODUCTION
            Over the past two decades, tourism has been identified as one of the world's largest industries with a growth rate
 of 5 per cent, per annum. The year 2003 saw tourism in our country emerging as a major sector of our economy,
 although it constitutes only a fraction of its vast potential.  A survey conducted by Conde Nast UK, the leading
 international travel magazine, ranked India amongst the top ten destinations of the world.  A poll conducted by Lonely
 Planet has ranked India as one of the top five destinations in the world. This Committee i.e. the Department-related
 Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture discussed the kind of development that is taking

FORMATION AND OBJECTIVES OF ITDC
3.         The ITDC was established on the 1
st October, 1966 under the Company's Act, 1956 with the amalgamation of
 three undertakings namely Hotel Corporation of India Ltd., India Tourism Transport Undertaking Ltd. and India
 Tourism Corporation Ltd. into a unified Corporation.  The Corporation was incorporated with the sole objective of
 developing and expanding tourism infrastructure in the country thereby promoting India as a tourist destination.
 Subsequently, two Government companies namely the Ashok Hotels Ltd. and Janpath Hotel Ltd. were merged with
 ITDC on 20th March, 1970.  The mission of ITDC is to provide leadership and play catalytic role in the development of
 tourism infrastructure in the country and to achieve excellence in its strategic business units through professionalism,
 efficiency, value for money and customer focussed service.  Broadly, the main objectives of the Corporation are:
construction, management and marketing of hotels  and catering units.
providing tourist transport facilities and money changing facilities.
providing duty free shopping facilities to the tourists.
providing entertainment facilities to the tourists.
providing consultancy-cum-management services and training for tourism related projects.
production, distribution and sale of tourist publicity material.
promoting tourism through food festival, fairs and joint ventures in India and abroad.
ROLE OF ITDC
4.         The Committee was informed that ITDC is disseminating the benefits of its skills and experience for
 development of tourism in the country.  It is offering consultancy and management services both to private and public
 sectors and also undertaking the joint venture hotel projects in collaboration with the State Governments/State
 Corporations.  In addition, ITDC is popularizing Indian Cuisines abroad by participating in food-cum-cultural festivals. 
 Further, Corporation has diversified its activities by providing Management and Consultancy Services in collaboration
 with the Government both at Central and State levels, relating to tourism and engineering projects.  The Corporation
 has been playing a crucial and strategic role in ensuring proper dispersal of socio-economic benefits of tourism,
 promoting national integration and international understanding, helping removal of regional imbalances, generating
 employment opportunities, augmenting foreign exchange earnings and also acting as a catalyst in the development of
 tourism.  ITDC, thus, represents a major public sector effort towards the development of tourism within the country and
 abroad.      
5.         During the course of oral evidence, the Secretary, Department of Tourism has deposed before the Committee
 that:
"Sir, we entirely agree with the observation of the Committee that there has been a complete change, as a matter of fact,
 in the context in which the ITDC is now operating.  At this moment, everybody knows and recognizes that the economy
 has changed.  The economy has become very, very complex and has much more developed than it was in the early 50's
 or 60's.  The hospitality industry itself has grown beyond recognition. Remarkable growth has taken place in this sector
 and it is also poised for further growth in the years to come. At the same time, the private sector has come on its own. 
 Tourism industry in the private sector has established itself and, as a matter of fact, some of them have set world-class
 benchmarks for others in the world to emulate.  Besides, earlier, even the Department of Tourism itself was not very
 active.  Now the Department of Tourism has taken on itself the role of promoting tourism. The Department of Tourism
 is meant not only for the ITDC, but also for the entire tourism sector, which also includes the private sector in a major
 way, so, that is why, now it has been a Government effort, but driven by the private sector in a major way.  That is how
 the change is taking place."

ORGANIZATIONAL SET-UP
7.         The present organizational set up at the corporate level comprises of C&MD, Functional Director and heads of
 business groups viz. Ashok Groups of Hotels & Catering.  Ashok International Trade, Ashok Travel & Tours, Ashok
 Reservation & Marketing Services, Ashok Creatives, Ashok Institute of Hospitality & Tourism Management and
 Engineering Consultancy and Business Development.  Besides, there are some support/service departments namely
 Human Resource Management, Finance & Accounts, Public Relations, Secretarial and Vigilance & Security.
BOARD OF DIRECTORS
8.         To a query regarding the criteria for selection of non-official Government Directors on the Board of ITDC and
 their contribution in the functioning of ITDC, the Department of Tourism has submitted that the posts of part-time non-
official Directors on the Board of ITDC are lying vacant at the moment as the Government is in the process of filling up
 these posts.  The ITDC Board of Directors, presently consists of five Directors.  In addition, there is one functional
 Director and three Government Directors. 
9.         ITDC, being a Public Sector Enterprise, the appointment of Directors – both executive and non-executive is
 made by the Government of India.  Presently, the Board of Directors comprises of CMD.  Director (Finance) and three
 part-time official Directors representing Government of India namely Shri Parvez Dewan, CMD, Shri P. P. Singh,
 Director (F), Shri P. K. Mishra AS&FA(Tourism), Shri Atul Chaturvedi, Director (T) and Shri Manoj Sethi, Finance
 Controller(Tourism).  The following criteria is adopted for selection and appointment of such Directors:
(a) Qualification Minimum qualification for part-time non-official Directors would be
 graduate degree from a recognized university.
(b) Experience Not less than ten years at the level of Joint Secretary and above in the
 government; CMD/MD in Corporate Sector/PSU; Professor level in
 academic institution or eminent professional of repute.
(c) Age The age band should be between 45-65 years (minimum/maximum limit).
 
10.       As per guidelines issued by Department of Public Enterprises, the Board of Directors of Public Sector
 Enterprises consist of Functional Directors, Government Directors and Non-official Directors.  The Functional
 Directors are full time operational Directors and are appointed through selection by Public Sector Enterprises Selection
 Board.  The Government Directors are appointed by administrative Ministries and are the officers dealing with
 concerned enterprises.  The Non-official Directors are also nominated by the administrative Ministries and are to be
 drawn from public-men, technocrats, management experts and consultants and professional managers in industry and
 trade with proven ability and managerial expertise with the objective of making the Board more professional.
11.       Regarding monthly remuneration the Department informed that no pay structure is in vogue in respect of the
 non-official Directors.   They are paid for attending the meetings @Rs.1000/- per meeting plus TA/DA as per
 Department of Public Enterprises Guidelines.  During the year 2004-05, six meetings of the Board of Directors were
 held to transact the business of the company.
12.       The Committee notes with concern that the re-constitution of the ITDC's Board of Directors with appointment of
 non-official part time Directors remains under consideration of the Department.  The Committee feels that presence
 of    non-official Directors on the Board will make the Board more professional.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the posts of part time non-official Directors on the Board of ITDC should be filled up by the
 Government from the concerned areas without fail.

 development of tourism infrastructure spreading all over the country. The Committee also notes that ITDC has set-up a
 Tourism Task Force for promotion of camping and adventure tourism in the remote areas in collaboration with the local
 Governments/authorities.  The Committee finds that consequent upon the implementation of disinvestment policy, the
 presence of the Corporation in the country has shrunk and ITDC stopped  setting up of new hotels/resorts in the
 country.  The Committee feels that this has not only confined the activities of ITDC to metropolitan cities but also
 diminished the geographic spread of ITDC.  The Committee, however,  recommends that the geographic spread of
 ITDC should not be confined only to metro cities of the nation but it should further be expanded to rural areas and
 hinterlands to provide required logistics to foreign and domestic tourists and the Department of Tourism should provide
 all support/help to ITDC in this regard.  The Committee further recommends that Department of Tourism should
 consider the detailed project reports prepared by ITDC under Rural Tourism Development Scheme and release funds
 for execution of works.  The Committee also recommends that ITDC should monitor the execution under the Rural
 Tourism Development Scheme.   This will create infrastructure in the villages, which in turn will play catalytic role for
 attracting private investment there.

REASONS FOR LOW PERFORMANCE AND MEASURES TAKEN FOR IMPROVEMENT IN THE
 FUNCTIONING
23.       The Committee desired to know the reasons for low performance by the ITDC in earlier years and whether any
 measures have been put -in place for improvement of performance and future plans to meet such eventualities, the
 Department of Tourism in their written replies informed the Committee that Tourism scenario in the country was
 adversely affected by following national and international events/factors which resulted into low performance of ITDC
 :
Lower turnout of international business travellers due to following international and domestic reasons:
Terrorists attack in USA on 11
th Sept., 2001.
Conflict in Afghanistan.
Terrorists attack on Indian Parliament on 13
th Dec., 2001
Gujarat Riots
Tension on Indo-Pak Borders and advisories issued by a number of foreign countries against visiting India.
Media publicity on  ITDC disinvestment adversely affected business inflow into hotels from prospective clients and
 potential markets.
Lack of refurbishing/upgradation of hotel properties because of the process of disinvestment.
Uncertainty and psycho-fear created in employees due to disinvestment in ITDC.
Substantial increase in the license fee and royalty in respect of duty free shops payable to AAI.
Increase in availability of hotel rooms in approved sector resulting in cut-throat competition and price war.
24.       As regards measures taken for improvement in performance, the Committee was informed that ITDC
 management has been very conscious and concerned about the low performance in its profitability.  The following
 measures have been taken by the ITDC management to improve financial performance of the Corporation:
Aggressive marketing efforts including promotional schemes, special packages etc.
Consolidation in the existing facilities/services of the ITDC and sub-contracting some services.
To have strict control over wastage and pilferage.
Recovery of outstanding dues form public/private parties.
After the disinvestment of 18 ITDC hotels, ITDC has diversified and entered into new business activities such as Money
 Changing and Western Union Money Transfers, Consultancy and execution of tourism & engineering, projects, training
 consultancy in hospitality sector.  Even Management, Mounting of SEL Shows.
Leasing out of some business areas in few ITDC Hotels to private parties for generating fixed income.
Rationalisation of manpower through VRS and redeployment of manpower.
Reduction in wage bill by roll back of retirement age from 60 to 58 years.
Control of cost by exercising economy in expenditure.
Opening up of new Duty Free Shops at Hyderabad, Bangalore, Calicut and Ahmedabad.
25.       During the course of oral evidence, the Secretary, Department of Tourism deposed before the Committee that: 
"that the financial problems of the ITDC arose during the short period of 3-4 years, when it went into a tailspin.  Before
 that, ITDC was earning profits, and after that also, ITDC has started marching ahead and making profits, and that too,
 at a very high rate. It was a temporary phase and now we have recovered our ground.  From the last year onwards, the
 growth has also been every high.  Operational profits and net profits have also shown an increase and we are very
 hopeful that we will be able to achieve our targets by 2010.  Our projections are based on reality.  It is not something
 very, very imaginative, and it is possible to achieve them.  And I have also given reasons that losses were only limited
 only to a particular period say only 3-4 years.  The entire strategy is laid before you division-wise as to what exactly we
 propose to do.  Hotel division is also earning profit; it is not in losses.   Last year onwards, hotel division has also been
 started earning profits.  I agree some joint ventures are not doing very well."
26.       The Department further informed that to counter the adverse affects of extraneous factors, ITDC is looking for
 the domestic tourism market particularly religious tourism which is far more stable and lucrative and ITDC shall focus
 its activities in large part to this sector in the foreseeable future and introduced packages for Sindhu Darshan, Visit to
 Tirupathi, Dussehra Package as an effort towards promotion of domestic tourism.

 India as a tourist ­­­­­­­­­­­destination of the globe by providing all tourism related services under a single roof.  The

 and act as a catalyst for development of Tourism across the country.                                   (Para 6)

 Trivandrum, Goa, Calicut, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad and Bangalore airports.  The Committee recommends the
 Department to make all out efforts in securing the contract for operating Duty Free Shops in these airports.
            The Committee notes with dismay that the issue of rent/royalty between AAI and ITDC has not yet been settled
 and the case is under mediation.  The Committee is of the view that ITDC though a commercial entity is exposed to
 business risk but at the same time, the incidence of high rate of rent have adversely affected the product pricing,
 profitability and competitive edge of the AITD.  The Committee opined that inspite of the contract, the dispute be
 resolved on the basis of equity between AAI and ITDC and some concessions may also be offered.  The Committee
 recommends the Department to pursue the matter with the Ministry of Civil Aviation and Ministry of Finance for early
 settlement of issue of royalty.                 (Para 50, 52 & 54)
ASHOK TRAVELS & TOURS
            The Committee notes with dismay that Ashok Travel and Tours Division of ITDC is continuously incurring
 losses because of the highly competitive market conditions.  The Committee is of the view that the deficit can be
 balanced by the ITDC from its corporate earnings.  The Committee recommends the Department to suitably restructure
 the Division to function as a major tour operator in the country and take advantage of market conditions and prevailing
 boom in the tourism sector.                                                (Para 58)
ASHOK RESERVATIONS AND MARKETING SERVICES
            The Committee notes that Ashok reservations and marketing services division of ITDC has been appointed as
 event manager by various Ministries/Organisations for handling conferences, events and exhibitions. The Committee
 recommends the Department to resort to aggressive marketing plans so that ITDC becomes the leading event manager
 of the country.                                      (Para 61)

 State.                    (Para 77)

 run by the ITDC.  The Committee also notes that this service station is running into losses.  The Committee
 recommends the Department to restructure the service station suitably besides, implementing sales-promotion schemes
 for recovering from the burgeoning losses in the future.                       (Para 79)
DISINVESTMENT OF ITDC HOTELS
            The Committee is at pain to note that the role of ITDC was limited only upto providing
 information/data/material to the Advisor, Ministries of Disinvestment  and Tourism and to implement decision of the
 Government for disinvestment of hotels.  The apex corporation in the field of tourism was not even given a reasonable
 opportunity for making presentation and defending its case before the Disinvestment Commission or pleading its case.
 The Committee strongly denounces the move of the Government  for not giving any opportunity to the ITDC for being
 heard before disinvestment of its properties although it was performing well.  Further, by putting few other ITDC hotels
 in the disinvestments list for some years and subsequently removing those therefrom, also dampened the spirit of the
 employees working in these hotels and of the Corporation.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that a thorough
 probe in the matter of disinvestment of ITDC Hotels  be undertaken by the Government to know the entire sequence  of
 disinvestment of ITDC's properties and reasons for doing so by some independent agency. The probe should also
 include as to what was the end use of the money collected through disinvestments of ITDC properties.             
            The Committee notes that disinvestment and its impact on the ITDC has made the organisation crippled.  The
 Committee finds that during 1999-2003, ITDC made a total loss of Rs. 159.52 crore and before and after that period,
 ITDC has generally been performing well.  The Committee observes that during the process of disinvestment, ITDC
 also faced certain difficulties because of some external factors and suffered losses during four consecutive years leading
