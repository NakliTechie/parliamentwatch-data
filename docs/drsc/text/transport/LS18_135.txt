REPORT
The Department of Road Transport and Highways under the Ministry of Shipping, Road
Transport and Highways has been entrusted primarily with the functions of construction and
maintenance of National Highways (NH), administration of Motor V ehicle Act and Central Motor
Vehicles Rules, formulation of broad policies relating to road transport, environmental issues,
relating to vehicular movements automotive norms, etc. besides making arrangements for
movements of vehicular traffic with neighbouring countries.
2. India has a vast road network of 3.32 million km. of which the National Highways and
the State Highways together account for 1,95,000 kms. The length of National Highways
network is only 66754 kms. (at present) constituting about 2 per cent of the total road network
in the country and carrying about 40 per cent of the total traffic across the length and breadth
of the country. The number of vehicles has been growing at a rapid pace of 12 per cent per
annum and consequently traffic on the roads is growing at 7-10 per cent per annum. The share
of roads in total traffic has been growing from 12 per cent of freight traffic and 31.6 per cent
of the passenger traffic in 1950-51 to a projected 65 per cent of freight traffic and 87 per cent
of passenger traffic.
3. To cope with this increase, appropriate expansion and improvement of the road network
is necessary. All this requires a massive infusion of funds. Historically, investments in roads,
especially highways, were mainly made by the Government because of the large volume of
resources required, long gestation period of projects, uncertain returns and various associated
externalities. But in recent times, the private sector has been actively involved in the development
and maintenance of the National Highways. The Ministry has formulated policy guidelines to
encourage private sector participation in the highway sector, besides providing several incentives
such as tax exemptions and duty free import of road building equipment and machinery. Model
Concession Agreements have also been finalized for major road projects.
4. The Road Transport Wing of the Department is concerned with the formulation of broad
policies related to road transport besides making provisions for the movement of vehicular traffic
with the neighbouring countries. The following Acts/Rules which embody the policy relating to
motor vehicles and State Transport Corporations are being administered in the Road Transport
Wing of the Department:—
• The Motor V ehicles Act, 1988
• The Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989
• The Road Transport Corporations Act, 1950
• The Carriers Act, 1865
5. The development and maintenance of National Highways are being done on agency basis.
The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), State Public Works Departments (PWDs) and
the Border Roads Organisation are the main agencies. Out of the total National Highways length
of 66754 km, about 19706 km. have been entrusted to National Highways Authority of India
(NHAI) for improvement under National Highway Development Project (NHDP), and some other
non NHDP scheme. About 46050 km. length of National Highways is at present entrusted to
State PWDs and Border Road Organisation (BRO) as agencies of the Ministry and about 998 km.
length are yet to be entrusted.

major bottlenecks in achieving the target.

Department in executing the bypass projects, the Secretary responded that:
“...Our achievement has been poor, but the main reason has been the problem related to
land acquisition. These bypasses variably need fresh alignment and new land acquisition.
That is one reason. The second, of course is inadequacy of funds. ...Our policy is, first,

15. The Committee finds that despite its earlier recommendations on the aspect of
funds utilization and the need for constant monitoring of projects, the Department of
Road Transport and Highways has done pr ecious little to improve the situation. This is
evident from the reply of the Department with regard to non-utilisation of funds that the
allocation for road sector is based on anticipated implementation of projects every year.
The Committee is of the opinion that the road sector plays a vital role in providing good
infrastructure in the country. The Committee feels that submission of routine replies for
underutilization of funds and poor performance in achieving the physical targets is not
going to yield any result. Instead of that the Department may list out the bottlenecks and
draw a strategy to overcome such bottlenecks. The Committee is of the opinion that
active involvement of project executing agencies such as PWD, NHAI and State
Governments would only help the Department in utilizing its fund to the maximum
extent. The Committee, therefore, recommends that proper co-ordination with all these
organizations should be maintained in order to achieve physical targets satisfactorily.

26. The Committee notes that the growth of Motor Vehicles in India is unprecedented.
The process of liberalization and globalization augmented the number of vehicles to
beyond the imagination of our planners of road sector development. The infrastructure
available is not at all commensurate with the growth of vehicles. The Committee also
notes that the allocation to the Department is almost static in real terms. There is an
increase of 20 per cent in Annual Plan outlay and 7.6 per cent in budgetary support. The
above increase in Annual Plan outlay and budgetary support may not be adequate even
to meet the increase in cost. Thus, it poses a great mismatch between the availability of
the road infrastructure and vehicular population.
27. Inadequate development of road sector will result in congested roads, traffic jams,
recurring accidents and chaos in the road sector. Moreover, the development of road
infrastructure is immensely important to sustain the growth of the economy. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should allot funds to the
Department of Road Transport and Highways so as to ensure the development of road
infrastructure in proportion to the growth of vehicles. The Committee further
recommends that demands of the Department may be acceded to at the RE stage.

30. The National Highways Development Project (NHDP) — the largest Highway Project ever
undertaken by the country, which is being implemented by the NHAI, consists of the following
components:
NHDP PHASE-I
30.1 The Committee was informed that NHDP phase-I envisages upgradation of 7498 km.
mainly consisting the stretches on Golden Quadrilateral connecting the four metro cities of
Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkatta, other road projects on North-South and East-West corridor
and projects of road connectivity to major ports. This was approved in December, 2000 with

8
an estimated cost of Rs.30,300 crore. As on 31.1.2008, 5640 km. length has already been four
laned under Golden Quadrilateral and work is continuing on the remaining length of 206 km.
under Golden Quadrilateral. Out of remaining 1652 km. under NHDP Phase I comprising of
NSEW corridors, Port connectivity and other projects, upgradation works have been completed
in a length of 1370 kms. and works are under implementation in remaining 282 km. length.
NHDP PHASE-II
30.2 NHDP Phase-II is the work of upgradation of 6644 km. mainly consisting of stretches
on North-South and East-West Corridors connecting Srinagar in the north to Kanyakumari in the
south, including spur from Salem to Kochi, and Silchar in the east to Porbandar in the west and
some other road projects approved in Dec., 2003 with an estimated cost of Rs.34,339 crore. As
on 31.1.2008, four/six laning of 778 km. has already been completed under Phase-II and ‘work’
on 5807 km. is under implementation. 840 kms. is remaining to be awarded.
NHDP PHASE-III
30.3 Under NHDP-III, the Government has approved 4-laning of 12109 kms. of National
Highways on BOT basis under NHDP Phase-III at an approved cost of Rs.80626 crore in phases
during March, 2005, October, 2006 and April, 2007. As on 31.1.2008, upgradation has been
completed in 304 kms and works are under implementation in 1757 kms. length under 31 nos.
of contracts.
NHDP PHASE-IV
30.4 The Phase-IV involves upgradation of about 20000 kms. of National Highways to 2 lane
standards with paved shoulders. However, the programme is yet to be approved by the
Government.
NHDP PHASE-V
30.5 The Government has approved six laning of 6500 km. of existing 4-lane National
Highways Sections on Design-Build-Finance-Operate (DBFO) basis in October, 2006, with an
estimated cost of Rs.41,210 crore. As on 31.1.2008, 2 contracts covering a length of 148 km.
are under implementation in NHDP Phase-V .
NHDP PHASE-VI
30.6 The NHDP Phase-VI consists of the work of construction of 1000 km. of Expressways
on Design-Build-Finance-Operate (DBFO) basis with an estimated cost of Rs.16,680 crore has
been approved by the Government in November, 2006.
NHDP PHASE-VII
30.7 The Government has approved construction of ring roads, bypasses, grade separators,
flyovers, elevated roads and tunnels at a cost of Rs.16,680 crore under the NHDP Phase-VII.
Of the total cost, Rs.6,302 crore will be provided by the government while Rs.l0,378 crore will
come from the private sector. The larger chunk of the total investments about Rs.10,500 crore
will be spent on construction of 700 km. of ring roads and bypasses. The remaining amount
of Rs.6,180 crore will be spent on stand alone grade separated intersections, road over
bridges, elevated roads, tunnels, underpasses and service roads. The works of stand alone ring
roads and bypasses are likely to be awarded by March, 2011 and is likely to be completed by
December, 2014.

ROADS OF INTER-STATE CONNECT IVITY AND ECONOMIC IMPORTANCE
34. The Committee was informed that the Government of India, enacted the Central Road
Fund Act 2000 on 27th December, 2000, by levying cess on diesel and petrol, and decided that
10 per cent of the share of CRF for State roads be earmarked for development of roads under
Ministry’s Scheme for improvement of State roads of Inter-State connectivity and Economic
Importance. With the revamped Central Road Fund coming into force, it has been decided that
the road/bridge projects of Inter-State Connectivity will be fully funded and projects of Economic
Importance will be funded to the extent of 50 per cent by the Government of India. Broadly,
the following categories of road/bridge projects are eligible for grant of assistance under the
scheme:
(i) Inter-State roads/bridges necessary for ensuring through communication.
(ii) Roads/bridges connecting National Highways.
(iii) Roads/bridges required for opening up new areas for economic growth where
railway facilities cannot be provided in the near future.
(iv) Roads/bridges which would contribute materially to the rapid development, for
instance, in hilly areas and minerally rich areas.
35. The year-wise details of projects approved ‘in principle’ for improvement of State roads
under these schemes is given below:

83. The Department informed the Committee that under Section 59 of the M.V . Act, 1998
Central Government has powers to fix the age of motor vehicles of different categories.
However, as yet this Section has not been invoked. We have only fixed the ‘age’ of certain
categories of vehicles for the purpose of tourist permit and national permit, under rule 82 and
88 of CMVR, 1989. This Ministry’s stand has been that a well maintained older vehicle can be
less polluting than an ill maintained newer vehicle. A vehicle can ply on the road as long as it
meets the requirements of the Motor Vehicles Act and Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 with
regard to safety, emission and fitness norms. Fixation of age of vehicle would also be not
advisable keeping in view the socio-economic conditions of the country.

91. When the Committee expressed its disappointment over the delay in the establishment of
National Road Safety and Traffic Board, the Secretary responded:
“.....What they have recommended is based on experience in other countries where road
safety is really given a very high priority. What they have said is really practical and
perhaps will be an effective way of handling the issue. Normally, what happens is these
Boards are set up, but there is no source of funding. They are suggesting one percent
Cess to be earmarked for road safety. For that they are suggesting amendment to the
Central Road Fund also. That is perhaps the right way. In there functions and duties all
this included”.
92. The Committee feels that the time has come for the creation of the much talked
about National Road Safety Board as the number of road accidents on national highways
is increasing in an alarming manner. The Committee recommends that the Department
should pursue the matter vigorously and take necessary actions to establish this Board at
the earliest.
TOLL TAX ON BRIDGES AND NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
93. The issue of collection of toll tax on bridges and national highways was raised by the
Committee during the course of evidence by the Secretary, Department of Road Transport and
Highways. The Committee in its 67th and 118th reports discussed the subject in detail and
recommended that once the cost of construction of a roadlbridge plus interest is recovered, the
collection of toll should be stopped. The Committee also felt that there exists a need for the
rationalisation of the toll tax being charged on the National Highways. To a specific

21
recommendation of the Committee in its 118th Report for creation of one time revolving fund,
the Department replied that present levy of cess is tied up for the implementation of NHDP and
Prime Minister’s Gram Sadak Yojana and the improvement of sanctioned works of State Road.
Therefore, it may not be possible to create a revolving fund until and unless the amount of cess
is increased. However, this is being taken up with Ministry of Finance.
94. The Committee once again recommends that once the capital and interest is
recovered, the users’ fee must be discontinued. The Committee is of the opinion that
there is a need for establishment of a regulator to determine the reasonable return to
the investor on road projects which is being considered as a yardstick for fixation of toll.
The Committee appreciates that in view of the resource crunch being faced by the
Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and there is a genuine need for generation of
additional revenue by the Ministry. Whenever any National Highway is constructed, it
leads to huge value addition to the land adjoining the National Highway and these benefits
of addition in value have so far  been enjoyed by private agencies only. The Government
should also get advantage of the appreciation of land value because it is the taxpayer’s
money that has led to the value addition of that property. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Government may explore the possibility of imposing some cess/tax
on land adjoining National Highways which is being utilized for commercial purpose on
annual basis. This could serve as a permanent source of income for the Government and
the revenue so generated could be ploughed back for the construction and maintenance
of National Highways in the country. Provision of ducting at the time of construction of
National Highways for laying telephone and electric cables also leads to value addition and
can be explored for generating more funds for the road sector. These measures may go
a long way to meet the resource gap for maintenance and construction of National
Highways in the country.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
ANNUAL PLAN 2007-08
The Committee observed that out of the RE (2007-08) Plan allocation
of Rs.14066.00 crore, an amount of Rs.11887.44 was spent (provisional) up to
12th March, 2008 which stands at 84.5 per cent of RE. The overall expenditure profile of
the Department is also not showing any encouraging trends. Out of Rs.16481.85 crore
(RE) for the year 2007-08, a sum of Rs.13338.03 crore was expended (provisional) till the
12th March, 2008 which stands at 81.0 per cent of the RE. This is very much in contrast
to the quantum of expenditure incurred in the year 2006-07 where it stood at a good
97.9 per cent of RE allocated for that year. (Para 11)
The Committee finds that despite its earlier recommendations on the aspect of
funds utilization and the need for constant monitoring of projects, the Department of
Road Transport and Highways has done pr ecious little to improve the situation. This is
evident from the reply of the Department with regard to non-utilisation of funds that the
allocation for road sector is based on anticipated implementation of projects every year.
The Committee is of the opinion that the road sector plays a vital role in providing good
infrastructure in the country. The Committee feels that submission of routine replies for
under utilization of funds and poor performance in achieving the physical targets is not
going to yield any result. Instead of that the Department may list out the bottlenecks and
draw a strategy to overcome such bottlenecks. The Committee is of the opinion that
active involvement of project executing agencies such as PWD, NHAI and State
Governments would only help the Department in utilizing its fund to the maximum
extent. The Committee, therefore, recommends that proper co-ordination with all these
organizations should be maintained in order to achieve physical targets satisfactorily.
(Para 15)
ANNUAL PLAN 2008-09
The Committee notes that the growth of Motor Vehicles in India is unprecedented.
The process of liberalization and globalization augmented the numbet of vehicles to
beyond the imagination of our planners of road sector development. The infrastructure
available is not at all commensurate with the  growth of vehicles. The Committee also
notes that the allocation to the Department is almost static in real terms. There is an
increase of 20 per cent in Annual Plan outlay and 7.6 per cent in budgetary support. The
above increase in Annual Plan outlay and budgetary support may not be adequate even
to meet the increase in cost. Thus, it poses a great mismatch between the availability of
the road infrastructure and vehicular population, Inadequate development of road sector
will result in congested roads, traffic jams, recurring accidents and chaos in the road
sector. Moreover, the development of road infrastructure is immensely important to
sustain the growth of the economy. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Government should allot funds to the Department of Road Transport and Highways so as
to ensure the development of road infrastructure in proportion to the growth of vehicles.
The Committee further recommends that demands of the Department may be acceded to
at the RE stage. (Para 26 & 27)

NATIONAL HIGHWAYS AUTHORITY OF INDIA
The Committee notes that majority of the work of NHDP Phase-II which was
conceived in the year 2003 has yet to be completed. The Committee would like to know
the reasons for the delay and the agencies/officers responsible for it. The Committee
recommends that the Government may take immediate effective steps to complete the
project. The Committee also notes that the NHDP phase-IV is yet to be approved by the
Government however, no reasons therefor have been given. It is pity that 20000 km, a
major part of local NH, is yet to be brought to 2 lane standards under Phase-IV but the
project has not been approved till date by the Government. The Committee recommends
that NHDP Phase-IV may be considered by the Government for approval immediately. The
progress of the projects under other phases may be monitored regularly so as to ensure
their timely implementation. (Para 31)
ROADS OF INTER-STATE CONNECT IVITY AND ECONOMIC IMPORTANCE
The Committee notes that there is a gradual decrease in the allocation of
funds for improvement of State roads particularly during the year 2004-05 amount of
Rs.101.14 crore for projects under Economic Importance and Rs.232.94 crores for projects
under Inter-State Connectivity has gone down to Rs.27.53 crores and Rs.132.66 crores
respectively during 2007-08. The Committee hopes that the trend may be reversed and the
State Roads may get more and more allocation which may be properly utilized for the
improvement of various state roads connectivity.
The Committee is unhappy with the delay in finalizing the Detailed Project Report
on Mughal Road in Jammu and Kashmir as a result of which the competent authority
could not sanction the project. The Government, however, has not given any reason as to
why there was delay in finalizing the Detailed Project Report. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department to apprise the Committee about those reasons and
action taken in the matter. The Committee desires that the Department should fine tune
its systems and procedures to avoid such delays. (Para 40 & 41)
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS (ORIGINAL) WORKS: NH(O)
The Committee is not happy with the mere 15.90 per cent increase in allocation
for the maintenance of national highways. The Committee was informed that an amount
of Rs.2280 crores is required for maintenance and repair of roads annually. The
Committee is of the opinion that Government should give adequate importance for the
maintenance and repair of non-NHDP highways to ensure that investment made in the NH
does not go waste. The Committee recommends that one third of the cess collected for
the financing NHDP projects should be allocated for the maintenance and repair of
highways. The Committee desires that the Department should take up the issue with the
Planning Commission for getting one third portion of the cess collected for the
maintenance and repair of national highways as these non-NHDP national highways are
considered as lifeline of Indian transport sector. (Para 45)
DEVELOPMENT OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS IN NORTH-EASTERN REGION
The Committee notes that lot of programmes are on the anvil for the development
of National Highways in the North-East. The Committee hopes that the Department may
be able to utilize the entire allocated amount for the North-East. (Para 49)

The Committee feels that school children are the best messiahs for preaching road

general will keep watch on their activities. (Para 64)

The Committee feels that this is a welcome step. The Committee recommends that
the number of organization sponsored by the Ministry is very low and insufficient
compared to the vastness of our transport sector. The Committee, therefore, recommends

of the country for imparting refresher course to the drivers of Heavy Motor Vehicles. The
