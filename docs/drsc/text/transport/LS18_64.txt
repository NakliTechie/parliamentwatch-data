APPRAISAL OF 9TH FIVE-YEAR PLAN AND PROJECTION FOR 10TH FIVE-YEAR PLAN
 
RECOMMENDATION (Paras 13 & 14)
 
5.         The Committee is not satisfied with the performance of the Ministry of Shipping during 9
th Five Year Plan. The
 Ministry could utilise only Rs.4838.92 crores out of the approved outlay of Rs. 9428 crores in Port Sector. The
 Committee is of the view that the reasons given by the Ministry of Shipping for the heavy shortfall i.e. delays in
 sanctioning of the schemes, delays in tender finalization, award of contracts etc are not of such nature which were
 beyond the control of the Ministry. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government of India should review
 its working and come out with specific remedial measures (predictive and preventive) to eliminate the delays leading to
 under-utilisation of allocated funds. Agencies/Individuals should be made accountable as and when lapses occur.
 
6.         The Committee is also of the view that there has to be realistic approach while preparing any Five-Year Plan. 44
 Major Schemes of 9
th Five Year Plan are likely to spill over to the 10 th Five-Year Plan, which may affect the
 physical/financial targets of the Ministry fixed for the 10 th Plan. The funds proposed for Port Sector during 10 th Five
 Year Plan period are Rs.17416.40 crores. The Committee hopes that while preparing the 10 th Plan, the Ministry of
 Shipping would have visualized the possible problem and taken remedial action so that the approved plan outlay for the
 10th Five Year Plan are utilised optimally.
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
7.         The observations of the Committee have been noted.  It is however submitted that the allocation of Rs.9428
 crores for the Ninth Plan (1997-2002) is only indicative, to be operationalised subject to availability of resources,
 through the successive Annual Plan allocations for the Ninth Plan period.  The aggregate of the 5 Annual Plan outlays
 during the period was Rs.6963.92 crores.  Against this, expenditure incurred during the Ninth Plan period was
 Rs.4816.46 crores.  It is also pertinent to point out that the overall port capacity achieved in the major ports as on
 31.3.2002 is 343.95 million tones per annum, whereas the traffic handled by these ports during 2001-02 was 287.57
 MT.  Thus, the major ports have now reached the stage where the capacities available exceed the traffic.  There has also

TONNAGE ACQUISITION PROGRAMME DURING TENTH PLAN
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 23)
 
10.       The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Shipping failed to achieve the target of 9 million tonnes
 for the 9
th Five Year Plan. The achievement of 6.84 MT is at par with the target achieved during the 8 th Plan. This
 shows that the tonnage acquisition was static during the 9 th Five Year Plan. The Ministry could not identify the
 problem of shipping industry during the full five years of Ninth Plan and did not take adequate steps to improve the
 situation.
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
11.       It is not true that the Ministry did not take adequate steps to improve the situation.  Ministry of Shipping has
 been taking steps from time to time to facilitate the growth of Indian tonnage.  The following fiscal measures have been
 taken by the Government at the initiative of this Ministry.
 
a. Restoration of Section 33AC of the Income Tax Act from 50% to 100% tax relief on amounts earmarked for
 vessel acquisition in separate account;
b. Increase in depreciation rate on ships from 20% to 25%.
c. Necessary amendments in EXIM policy to rectify the anomaly which had necessitated procuring of special import
 license for the purpose;
d. Ministry of Shipping convinced Ministry of Finance to roll back imposition of 5% customs duty on acquisition of
 ocean going ships from abroad;
e. Expanding the scope of Section 33AC to stimulate Indian Ship owners to earmark higher funds for acquisition of
 tonnage.  Further, to make this section more attractive for Indian ship owners, Government has decided to allow
 deduction of funds transferred under Section 33AC while computing book profits for the purpose of Minimum
 Alternate Tax.
f. The Government has allowed private sector participation in providing training to seafarers, which would help in
 producing a higher number of quality seafarers to man Indian flag tonnage.
 
12.       In addition to the above, this Ministry constituted an Expert Committee under the Chairmanship of Shri Rakesh
 Mohan, Adviser to Finance Minister to review the Indian Shipping and suggest measures to address its problems.  The
 Committee in its report recommended introduction of tonnage tax as is followed by the leading Maritime Nations of the
 world.  The report of the Committee and recommendations thereon have been accepted by this Ministry and referred to
 Ministry of Finance for implementation.
 
13.       As regards the tonnage remaining static during 9
th Plan, while lack of conductive fiscal environment was the
 main reason, the fact that the Shipping Industry faced recession during the first half of the 9 th Plan period also did not
 help the cause of Indian Shipping.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
14.       The Committee notes the reply of the Government.  The Committee takes exception to the use of the word “it is
 not true” by the Ministry in their replies and directs that the Ministry should refrain from using such wording in future.

 economy. Therefore, it is necessary to provide conducive fiscal regime to Indian Shipping Sector so that it can become
 internationally competitive. The Committee notes with some satisfaction the fiscal concessions given to the Indian

DREDGING AT MAJOR PORTS
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 28)
 
18.       The Committee notes that Major Ports are spending large amount on the capital dredging either through their
 own resources or from the borrowed funds, which has definitely affected their financial performance.  It is also a fact
 that for the ports, it is difficult to raise borrowings for capital dredging because it may not result into commensurate
 incremental resources.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that capital dredging at all the Major Ports should be
 taken as national priority by the Government of India. The Government of India, as a matter of policy should provide
 adequate plan funds to all the Major Ports for carrying out Capital Dredging at the Ports.
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
19.       In the matter of capital dredging, the Government has already approved the River Regulatory Scheme of Kolkata
 Port Trust involving an outlay of Rs.350.84 crores, which will be financed entirely through budgetary support. 
 Budgetary support has also been earmarked for ‘Deepening of the Channel’ scheme of Paradip Port Trust.  A proposal
 for budgetary support for capital dredging for the Cochin channel for the proposed Vallarpadam Container Terminal
 and LNG Terminal at Cochin Port has also been taken up with the Planning Commission.  Further, a proposal for
 Japanese funding of the project ‘Deepening and Widening of Channel at Jawaharlal Nehru Port’ has been taken up with
 the Ministry of Finance (Department of Enonomic Affairs) and a token provision for budgetary support to this project
 has also been made in the Tenth Plan outlay.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
20.       While noting the reply of the Government, the Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Shipping
 should identify channels in other remaining Major Ports for capital dredging and Government of India should provide
 budgetary support for the same.
 
KOLKATA PORT TRUST (KoPT)
 
I – CAPITAL DREDGING
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 34)

21.       The Committee strongly disapproves the procedure adopted by the Government of India in the matter of
 providing funds to Kolkata Port Trust for Capital Dredging. The Committee is surprised to note that on one side, the
 Government of India has, in principle, decided to fully reimburse the cost on items directly related to dredging and
 maintenance of River Hooghly as well as cost incurred on items directly related to maintenance dredging of Haldia
 Shipping Channel, where as, on other hand, the allocation for capital dredging has been reduced in a arbitrary manner.
 The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that there should be no constraint of funds for the Kolkata Port so far
 as dredging is concerned. Silting at Kolkata Port is problem of recurring nature therefore the actual requirement of fund
 for dredging and maintenance of River Hooghly and Haldia Shipping Channel be identified and provision be made at
 Budget Estimate stage itself.
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
22.       Since the Budget Estimates for 2002-03 had already been finalized, the matter for full reimbursement of the
 expenditure  incurred by the Port Trust based on audit certificates will be taken up with the Ministry of Finance during
 Revised Estimates stage.  However, the matter will be pursued with the Ministry of Finance for allocation of the entire
 requirement based on audit certificates, at Budget Estimates stage itself during 2003-04 and onwards.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
23.       The Committee was further informed by the Ministry of Shipping vide O.M. No.PD-11013/5/2002-US(I) dated
 4.12.2002 that Capital Dredging Project under the scheme “River Regulatory Measures” for improvement of the
 draught in the Hooghly estuary of Kolkata Port Trust was sanctioned by the Government of India in November, 2001. 
 The project envisages cutting a channel of approximately 10 kms. of length for a width of 200 mtrs. (at bottom) upto a
 depth of 4.5 mtrs.  below chart Datum by Capital Dredging through Jiggerkhali Flat along an alignment favourable to
 the flow of the river in this region.
 
24.       Tender for the major component of the work (capital dredging) was issued on five occasions but could not be
 finalized because of unwillingness of intending bidders to accept maintenance dredging for one year in the newly
 dredge cut channel, apprehension of bidders that they would not be able to guarantee the required depth within the
 estimated limit of quantity of dredging as well as possibility of failure to dredge and sustain the channel within the
 proposed alignment etc.  Due to its inability in freezing the techno-commercial deviations put forward by the bidders,
 Kolkata Port Trust has engaged National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT), Chennai as consultant to review/refine
 the project in the light of present hydro-dynamic conditions of Hooghly estuary as well as to revise the technical part of
 the tender document after finalizing the recommendations.  NIOT have already started the work of reviewing the
 scheme and their report is expected by the end of December 2002.
 
25.       The Committee was further informed that Ministry of Shipping had a budgetary allocation of Rs.175 crores this
 year.  Unfortunately, on account of technical snag, Rs.175 crores has been surrendered at the R.E. stage.  The next
 meeting on Revised Estimate and Budget Estimate will take place after seven days.  The Ministry of Shipping have
 given proposal for about Rs.200 crores for raising the capital next year.  If the NIOT report comes, hopefully, by the
 end of December on schedule an entire technical tendering process would be facilitated.  Then there will be about 2-3
 months time when the operation can start.  Now, if the operations take about three months to start, then, probably the
 next financial year will come and, by that time, hopefully, the funds will not be problem. 
 
26.       The Committee is constrained to note that the Ministry of Shipping was unable to spend Rs.105 crores during
 this financial year and surrendered the said amount at Revised Estimate stage.  The Committee is of the opinion that
 capital dredging at Kolkata Port is a very important matter and needs time bound action plan.  Earlier, there was
 scarcity of the amount for the capital dredging at port, but now the Ministry is unable to spend the amount allocated for
 capital dredging, inspite of budgetary support from the Government of India.  The Committee, therefore, further
 recommends that the Ministry of Shipping should spend the allocated budgetary fund in a time bound manner and get
 prepared the action plan for capital dredging.

CONSTRUCTION OF SINGLE BUOY MOORING (SBM)
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 48)
 
37.       The Committee observes that despite of alternative proposals suggested by the Cochin Port Trust, the Kochi
 Refineries Ltd. is adamant to go ahead with its plan for construction of Single Buoy Mooring (SBM) without giving a
 positive thought to the proposals. The Committee is of the opinion that both Public Sector undertakings are under
 administrative control of the Government of India and in absence of agreement, any loss incurred to the Cochin Port
 Trust will be the loss to the Nation. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendation made in its 52
nd
 report on Demands for Grants (2001-02) of the Ministry of Shipping and recommends that Ministries of Shipping and
 Petroleum and Natural Gas should evolve an amicable solution to the problem. Kochi Refinery Ltd. should consider, in
 the national interest, the alternative proposals suggested by the Cochin Port Trust before taking up the construction of
 the Single Buoy Mooring.
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
38.       In order to resolve the issue amicably, a team comprising senior officials of Cochin Port Trust has been
 constituted to interact with the officials of Kochi Refineries Limited to study and work out a feasible alternative
 logistics solution.  In response, Kochi Refineries Limited has also constituted a high level Committee to interact with
 the port officials.  Based on the deliberations so far, Cochin Port Trust has expressed the hope that they would be able
 to arrive at a mutually acceptable solution.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
39.       The Committee was further informed in its meeting held on 27
th November 2002 by the Ministry of Shipping
 that acting on Committee’s recommendation to evolve an amicable solution to the SBM issue of Kochi Refineries,
 based on deliberations of the Committee of senior officials of the two organizations, an understanding has been reached
 that Kochi Refineries would arrange the preparation of Detailed Feasibility Report for developing SBM facility off
 Puthuvypeen within port waters and Cochin Port will go ahead with studies on their proposals.

COASTAL SHIPPING IN INDIA
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 84)
 
71.       The Committee is unhappy to note that during 9
th Five year plan no special attention was given on coastal
 shipping which is one of the most energy efficient, eco-friendly and cheap modes of transport for movement of bulk
 commodities over long distances. Considering the vast coastline and congestion on land routes, the costal shipping
 offers an effective alternative means of Transport. Despite this, no adequate policy measures have been taken by the
 Ministry. There are 184 minor/intermediate ports in the country along the 5560 kms coastline of India.
 Minor/intermediate ports can play important role in the coastal Shipping. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 Ministry of Shipping should frame a comprehensive policy and prepare plans to promote the coastal shipping in the
 country. 
 
ACTION TAKEN/PROPOSED TO BE TAKEN
 
72.       The Government has taken various measures to develop coastal shipping in the country.  As a measure to
 encourage its development, coastal ships and sailing vessels have been exempted from payment of lightdues. 
 Instructions have been issued to all Major Ports (except Calcutta Port Trust who has provided a separate birth for
 coastal ships) to grant priority berthing to coastal ships without priority berthing charges.  The Central Board of
 Exercise and Customs have issued instructions permitting sanction of drawback as the port of loading after shipment of
 goods.  The Coastal fleet in the country has increased.  It stood at 329 ships with GRT of 7,31,367 as on 31.12.2001.  A
 proposal to declare coastal shipping as an infrastructure industry was also taken up with the Ministry of Finance, but the
 same was not agreed to.
 
73.       It has since been decided by the Government to conduct a combined study on development of coastal shipping
 and minor ports.  The proposed study will identify cargo that can be diverted to coastal shipping from other modes of
 transport, evaluate future traffic potential for coastal movement in the next 10 years and explore the possibility of
 developing new routes to meet the existing and potential demands.  The study will identify minor ports for use by
 coastal shipping, keeping in view the rail/road connectivity and recommend the infrastructure needed at such ports.  It
 will also examine the possibility of establishing an integrated transport system between coastal ships and Inland Water
 Transport.  It will prepare a plan of action and give broad coast estimates thereof.  The proposal for award of the study
 is under process.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
74.
              While noting the reply of the Government, the Committee takes serious view over refusal by the Ministry of
 Finance to declare coastal shipping as an infrastructure industry.  The Committee is of the view that the
 coastal shipping is, at present, in the initial stage and requires comprehensive policy.  To declare, coastal
 shipping as an infrastructure industry is a first step towards its promotion.  The Committee, therefore,
 further recommends that the coastal shipping should be declared as an infrastructure industry.   The
 Committee further recommends that proposal for award of combined study on development of coastal
 shipping and minor ports be processed, expeditiously.
