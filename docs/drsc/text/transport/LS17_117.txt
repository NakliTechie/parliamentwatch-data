 the Shipping and Port Sectors, which include Shipbuilding and Ship-repair, Major Ports, National Waterways and Inland

 on these subjects and their implementation.

7.         The Committee enquired from the Department of Shipping about the shortfalls in the utilization of the outlay during the
 Plan and steps being taken for optimum utilization of funds, the Department of Shipping informed that there are procedural
 delays in the approval of the projects. The procedures to be followed for formulation, appraisal and approval of projects to be
 funded through public investment and also for Public Private Partnership (PPP) projects have been laid down by the Ministry
 of Finance.  All such projects are required to be appraised by the appropriate Appraisal Forum [Standing Finance Committee
 (SFC), Departmental Expenditure Finance Committee (Departmental EFC), Main Expenditure Finance Committee (Main
 EFC), Public Investment Board (PIB) or Public Private Partnership Appraisal Committee (PPPAC) as the case may be],
 depending upon the nature and estimated cost of the project before obtaining the approval of the Competent Authority.  As per
 instructions issued by the Planning Commission, a feasibility report is required to be prepared for obtaining their ‘In Principle’
 approval for any project/scheme to be funded from public funds costing Rs.50 crore and above. To ensure optimum utilization
 of funds, the pace of expenditure is being monitored at the highest level on a regular basis. Allocation of funds out of the
 budgetary support is examined and funds apportioned on the basis of progress and requirement of the scheme. To ensure
 optimum utilization of funds, at the time of preparation of BE 2007-08, token provisions have been kept for schemes for which
 approval of the Competent Authority is yet to be received.   For example, token provision of Rs. 1 crore has been kept for
 capital dredging at Tuticorin Port and also a token provision of Rs. 0.01 crores has been kept for River Regulatory Measures at
 Kolkata Port.

10.       The Committee is distressed to note that during the 10 th Plan, the Department of Shipping was allocated Rs.2570.89
 crore as budgetary allocation under the plan scheme which was reduced to Rs.1700.00 crore at the RE stage. The Committee
 does not see any merit therein and strongly disapproves the idea of reduction of plan outlay because once it is reduced by the
 Government at the beginning of the Five Year Plan itself it will certainly affect and jeopardise many of the plan activities of
 the Department. The Committee is further constrained to note the shortfall in utilization of funds in almost all the major
 activities of Department of Shipping i.e. Inland Water Transport, Shipping, Ports, Lighthouses and Lightships, Shipbuilding
 etc. During 2005-06, the utilization of funds was Rs.346.83 crore against RE Rs.405.00 crore, in 2006-07, the utilization of
 funds was Rs.510.21 crore against RE Rs.575.00 crore and Rs.759.00 crore has been allocated for BE 2007-08. The
 Committee is aware that the main reason for effecting reduction in funds at RE stage by the Ministry of Finance is the inability
 of the Department of Shipping to spend their allocated funds during the first two quarters of almost every  year.  The
 Committee observes that the major portions of the budgetary outlay had been utilized during the last two quarters of the
 financial year by the Department. The Committee disapproves the practice as the funds crucial for the development of
 Shipping and Port Sectors infrastructure are withheld in the process. The Committee notes that the reasons advanced by the
 Department for non-utilization are administrative in nature for which corrective action could have been taken in advance. The
 Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to plan and incur their expenditure in a proportionate manner so that it is
 evenly spread throughout the year so that the funds are utilised appropriately for the purposes and no alibi should be provided
 to the Ministry of Finance to reduce the allocations at the RE stage.
11.       The Committee also recommends that the Department of Shipping should do away with procedural hurdles and
 maintain close liaison for the purpose of review, monitoring, implementation and co-ordination of programmes/activities. The
 Committee hopes that Department of Shipping would take all corrective steps in this regard to avoid shifting of focus from the
 shipping sector in the next plan.

 constraints in transportation of materials. In one of the schemes viz. providing eastern side embarkation at Minicoy

17.       The Committee notes with concern that the budgetary allocation under the Plan scheme for port sector of the
 Department during the entire five years of the 10 th Plan had been slashed down consecutively and was subjected to heavy
 reduction at the RE stage. The Committee is perturbed to note that during the 10 th Plan, the Ministry of Finance imposed a cut
 of Rs.3344.20 crore or 57%  bringing it down from Rs.5954.84 crore to Rs.2610.64 or 43% at the RE stage, on the basis of
 poor utilization pattern of the Department of Shipping. In BE 2006-07, an outlay of Rs.1364.25 crore comprising of Rs.476.12
 crore as gross budgetary support was provided to the port sector and Department of Shipping had been able to utilize
 Rs.1024.53 crore or 75% of the budgetary allocation during the year. The Committee is constrained to note that the
 Department has given reasons which are routine in nature for non-utilization of funds which do not justify the slow pace and
 under-utilization by the Department. For example, schemes to provide infrastructure facilities at Minicoy, Agatti and Amini
 Islands could not progress mainly on account of constraints of transporting materials.   The Committee also notes that in order
 to ensure optimum utilization of funds, token provision have been kept in BE 2007-08 for schemes for which approval of the
 competent authority is yet to be received. The Committee feels that the underutilization of funds would have an adverse impact
 on the ongoing and future capacity building projects in the port sector. The Committee, recommends, the Department to adhere
 to strict fiscal discipline and fix the responsibility for persistent underutilization of allocated funds and take remedial measures
 thereon for optimum utilization of allocated funds during the year and inform the Committee about the measures taken by the
 Department in this regard.
18.       The Committee further recommends that learning from the experience of the 10
th Plan, planning for the 11 th Plan be
 done in such a way that there will be maximum utilization of funds allocated during each year of the Plan period.

20.       The Committee has also been informed that the traffic projected at the Major Ports in the terminal year of the 11
th Five
 Year Plan is 708.09 MT and the capacity that is required is projected to be 1001.80 MT. The Working Group on the Port
 Sector for the 11th Five Year Plan has projected that the Major Ports will handle 708 Million Tonnes of traffic in the final year
 of the 11 th Five Year Plan. The modernization of the Port infrastructure is essential to achieve this traffic projection. The
 Department of Shipping has formulated the National Maritime Development Programme under which specific projects are to
 be taken up for implementation over a defined period. The total investment involved under the Programme is Rs.1,00,339
 crore. Out of this, Rs.55,804 crore is for the Port Sector spread over 276 projects covering the entire gamut of activities,
 namely  construction/upgradation of berths, deepening of channels, rail/road connectivity projects, equipment
 upgradation/modernization schemes and other related schemes for certain backup facilities. These projects will be
 implemented over a period upto 2011-12. Even more schemes have been identified in the Working Group Report for the 11 th
 Five Year Plan, which is under finalization. The Major Port Trusts Act, 1963 permits private sector participation in port
 development. With a view to improving productivity and efficiency and promoting competition, the Government has put in
 place a scheme for private sector participation in Major Ports, including container terminals, specialized cargo berths for
 handling bulk, break bulk, multipurpose and specialized cargo.

TONNAGE TAX REGIME
36.       The Government has rationalized the fiscal regime for Indian Shipping Industry by introducing Tonnage Tax system
 from the financial year 2004-05, in order to provide Indian Shipping industry a level playing field vis-à-vis International
 shipping companies and also facilitate the growth of Indian tonnage. In response to introduction of Tonnage tax regime and
 also due to current shipping boom, Indian Tonnage has steadily grown in the last about 2 years. Indian tonnage as on
 01.06.2004 was 7.05 million Gross Tonnage (GT) which has increased to 8.42 million GT as on 03.12.2006. Dredgers have
 also been included in the Tonnage Tax regime as per Finance Act 2005-06.
37.       Inland Water Transport and Coastal Vessel Transport in the country do not have the critical mass to compete with the
 other mode of transport on its own and for its development requires fiscal support from the Government. The Committee in its
 105
th Report on Demands for Grants for 2006-07 of the Department of Shipping had recommended for inclusion of the vessels
 registered under Inland Vessels and Coastal Vessels Acts in the tonnage tax regime for development of the Inland Water
 Transport and Coastal Vessel Transport in the country. The Committee wanted to know about the progress made in the matter.
 The Department of Shipping in their written reply submitted that in the current year also the Department had taken up the
 matter with Ministry of Finance on various occasions, for inclusion of vessels registered under Indian Vessel Act and Coastal
 Vessel Act, under tonnage tax regime.  It was emphasized that these vessels are adversely affected as Section 33 AC benefit,
 which was available to them till 2003-04 (before introduction of tonnage tax) was withdrawn without extending the tonnage
 tax benefits.   However, this issue has not figured in the Budget of 2007-08 presented by Hon’ble Finance Minister in the
 Parliament.
38.       The Committee notes that with the introduction of the Tonnage Tax system and other fiscal incentives the Indian
 shipping companies have registered the growth of the Indian tonnage. However, the Committee is constrained to note that the
 vessels registered under the   Indian Vessels Act, 1917 and the Coastal Vessels Act, 1838 have not been included for the
 purpose of getting tonnage tax benefits. The Committee feels that Inland Water Transport and Coastal Vessel Transport in the
 country do not have the critical mass to compete with the other mode of transport on its own and for its development requires
 fiscal support from the Government. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to impress upon the Ministry of
 Finance for extending the benefits of tonnage tax to these vessels registered under the Indian Vessels Act and Coastal Vessels
 Act so as to harness the IWT potential in the country.

 30.11.2006 to recoup the full amount of Rs. 92.78 crore in the Seamen’s Provident Fund. As per decision of the Government ,

 crore out of which Plan outlay for IWAI is Rs.864.73 crore.  During the 10 th Plan, as against the outlay of Rs.864.73 crore the

 carrying capacity through expansion and modernization of IWT infrastructure/vessels.   Whereas the Inland Waterways
 Authority of India (IWAI) is responsible for the development of National Waterways, responsibility for the development of

 has been earmarked towards Plan Support to IWAI.

 routes identified in the Protocol on Inland Water Transport between India and Bangladesh.
75.       The Corporation is under the administrative control of the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways.
 CIWTC has its corporate office at Kolkata and various branch offices at Guwahati, Karimganj, Patna etc. The Corporation has
 at present 470 employees on its payroll and is headed by a full time Chairman-cum-Managing Director (CMD). There were
 two main divisions viz. River Service Division (RSD) and the Rajabagan Dockyard (RBD) headed by General Managers. The
 Rajabagan Dockyard stands transferred to Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers Limited (GRSE) w.e.f. 1
st July, 2006 as
 per decision of the Cabinet taken in its meeting held on 1.12.2005. The River Services Division is engaged in cargo movement
 in the riverine routes of NW-I and NW-2 besides export to Bangladesh. The existing fleet strength of the CIWTC is about 101
 vessels, out of which 51 vessels are operational, 23 vessels awaiting repair and 17 vessels are under repair.
76.       The Committee wanted to know the latest position about the implementation of Voluntary Retirement Scheme in the
 CIWTC, the Department of Shipping informed that the Government had decided to bring down the manpower level to 43 from
 the exiting level of 1080 by introduction of the Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS).  It was expected that by 31.3.2006 about
 323 employees would opt for VRS and the level of manpower will gradually come to the level of 43 by end of December,
 2006.  Accordingly, the provision of Rs.3.60 crore for payment of salary/wages of CIWTC during 2006-07 was made.  
 However, the VRS introduced did not yield the desired result. Hence, the amount of Rs.3.60 crore was found to be insufficient

 during 2007-08 to bring down the manpower strength further.

APPRAISAL OF THE 10TH PLAN AND THE ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2007-08
            The Committee is distressed to note that during the 10 th Plan, the Department of Shipping was allocated Rs.2570.89
 crore as budgetary allocation under the plan scheme which was reduced to Rs.1700.00 crore at the RE stage. The Committee
 does not see any merit therein and strongly disapproves the idea of reduction of plan outlay because once it is reduced by the
 Government at the beginning of the Five Year Plan itself it will certainly affect and jeopardise many of the plan activities of
 the Department. The Committee is further constrained to note the shortfall in utilization of funds in almost all the major
 activities of Department of Shipping i.e. Inland Water Transport, Shipping, Ports, Lighthouses and Lightships, Shipbuilding
 etc. During 2005-06, the utilization of funds was Rs.346.83 crore against RE Rs.405.00 crore, in 2006-07, the utilization of
 funds was Rs.510.21 crore against RE Rs.575.00 crore and Rs.759.00 crore has been allocated for BE 2007-08. The
 Committee is aware that the main reason for effecting reduction in funds at RE stage by the Ministry of Finance is the inability
 of the Department of Shipping to spend their allocated funds during the first two quarters of almost every  year.  The
 Committee observes that the major portions of the budgetary outlay had been utilized during the last two quarters of the
 financial year by the Department. The Committee disapproves the practice as the funds crucial for the development of
 Shipping and Port Sectors infrastructure are withheld in the process. The Committee notes that the reasons advanced by the
 Department for non-utilization are administrative in nature for which corrective action could have been taken in advance. The
 Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to plan and incur their expenditure in a proportionate manner so that it is
 evenly spread throughout the year so that the funds are utilised appropriately for the purposes and no alibi should be provided
 to the Ministry of Finance to reduce the allocations at the RE stage.

            Now, a large number of private ports have also been set up in the country. They should be asked to take over the
 responsibility of building the connectivity requirements and the Government should also explore the possibility of creation of a
 Corpus Fund to take care of the connectivity problems of all ports as suggested by the Secretary, Department of
 Shipping.                                                             (Para 28)
TSUNAMI REHABILITATION PROGRAMME
            The Committee notes with anguish that the Restoration, Rehabilitation Works as well as creation of additional facilities
 in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands under the Tsunami Rehabilitation Programme has not been given much attention by the
 Department of Shipping although an action plan for rehabilitation of port structures and creation of additional facilities has
 been initiated with an estimated cost of Rs.976.19 crore and the Government has already approved an amount of Rs.897.31
 crore. The Committee, however, is surprised to note the Department of Shipping could utilize only Rs.18.58 crore in 2005-06
 and Rs.24.15 crore in 2006-07. The Committee finds that there has been a large short fall in the utilization pattern with the
 causal pace of the progress of work. The Committee has a doubt whether any Action Plan was chalked out by the
 Government.  The Committee recommends the Department to have focused approach for providing the infrastructure facilities
 for the Andaman and Nicobar islands within the stipulated time frame and if need be a task force may be formed for
 monitoring the pace of implementation of the programme and keep the Committee informed about the progress in the
 matter.                                                                                  (Para 31)
CALCUTTA DOCK LABOUR BOARD
            The Committee notes that a proposal for release of one time grant of Rs.738.71 crore has been submitted to the Cabinet
 for financial assistance on account of payment of arrears of pensions and pensionary dues of CDLB and the  proposal is under
 consideration of the Committee of Secretaries. The Committee also notes that the Department is working out  a scheme for
 merger of CDLB with KoPT in consultation with the Department of Expenditure. The Committee is of the express opinion that
 liability on account of the payment of arrears of pensions and pensionary dues of CDLB solely lies on the Government and the
 problem of payment of pension and pensionary dues can be resolved only if, the Government approves self sustaining proposal
 for an initial corpus with appropriate policy and methodology for disbursement of pension and pensionary dues. The
 Committee welcomes the move of the Government for merging of CDLB with KoPT and financial restructuring of the KoPT
 to counter the adverse impact of the merger. The Committee recommends the Department that till the time the financial
 restructuring and merger of CDLB takes place, the Government should act upon releasing enhanced grant to CDLB for
 meeting its pensionary and other statutory liabilities.                                                                                                               (Para
 35)
TONNAGE TAX REGIME
            The Committee notes that with the introduction of the Tonnage Tax system and other fiscal incentives the Indian
 shipping companies have registered the growth of the Indian tonnage. However, the Committee is constrained to note that the
 vessels registered under the   Indian Vessels Act, 1917 and the Coastal Vessels Act, 1838 have not been included for the
 purpose of getting tonnage tax benefits. The Committee feels that Inland Water Transport and Coastal Vessel Transport in the
 country do not have the critical mass to compete with the other mode of transport on its own and for its development requires
 fiscal support from the Government. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to impress upon the Ministry of
 Finance for extending the benefits of tonnage tax to these vessels registered under the Indian Vessels Act and Coastal Vessels

file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/117thReport.htm[22-10-2018 12:22:07]
 Act so as to harness the IWT potential in the country.                                                         (Para 38)
CRUISE SHIPPING
            The Committee is constrained to note that despite the country having a large coast line, no policy for cruise shipping has
 been framed till date. The Committee notes that the Department of Shipping is formulating a cruise shipping policy for
 popularizing cruise shipping and development of infrastructure and other facilities in the country. The Committee also notes
 that in order to encourage the cruise shipping in the country, the Department has provided incentives to encourage private
 sector participation for development of cruise tourism facilities at ports on Eastern as well Western coasts. The Committee
 recommends the Department to expedite the process and bring out a comprehensive cruise shipping policy for the growth of
 the cruise shipping in the country within a time frame so that the boom in cruise shipping be tapped to the extent
 possible.                                              (Para 42)
MULTIPLICITY OF TAXES
            The Committee notes with concern that there exists a multiplicity taxes in the shipping sector due to which Indian
 tonnage is loosing out a competitive edge. The Committee is constrained to note that the Minimum Alternate Tax, Corporate
 Income Tax, Withholding Tax, Income Tax for Indian Seafarers, Custom and Central Excise Duties on Capital Investment and
 Service Tax etc. have deterring effects and are putting extra burden on the Shipowners whereas their foreign counterparts do
 not have to pay such taxes. This is hampering the growth of this vital sector in the country. The Committee, therefore,
 recommends the Department to pursue the matter of rationalization and reduction of taxes which have adverse impact on the
 growth of the Shipping Sector, with the Ministry of Finance more vigorously for suitable corrective measures to boost the
 shipping sector of the nation and inform the Committee of the outcome therefrom.                                     (Para 44)
HUMAN RESOURCES DEVELOPMENT
            The Committee is constrained to note that despite knowing fully well about the shortage of trained manpower in
 maritime sector, the Government have not taken adequate steps to meet the shortage. As per the estimates of the Indian 
 National Shipowners Association (INSA)  there was shortage of 834 officers during 2006-07 which is likely to increase by
 20% during 2007-08. Globally also as per BIMCO/ISF manpower Summary Report, there will be shortage of approximately
 27000 officers by 2015 which will further aggravate the problem of trained manpower in the country. The Government,
 however, is considering a proposal to allow foreign officers to work on Indian ships besides increasing the training facilities to
 increase the trained manpower. The Committee notes that there are four maritime training institutes being run under the
 Government sector apart from there are 124 maritime institutes  in the private sector imparting pre-sea and post-sea training in
 various disciplines. The Committee observes that since there are sizeable number of private institutes imparting maritime
 training all over the country, there is a need to provide institutional and integrated course in the country’s maritime human
 resources development. The Committee feels that the shortage of Indian officers can be made good by increasing training
 facilities in the country. The Committee recommends the Department to formulate the guidelines for affiliation of these private
 institutes and standardize the quality of such education and training through affiliation and academic supervision for achieving
 excellence in the maritime sector.                                                                       (Para 49)
RECOUPMENT OF SEAMEN’S PROVIDENT FUND
            The Committee is happy to note that as per its recommendations, the Government has approved the proposal for
 recoupment of Rs.92.78 crore in the Seamen’s Provident Fund. The Committee also notes that in order to ensure that such
 happenings are not repeated in future, a Bill to amend the Seamen’s Provident Fund Act, 1966 and to release the grant to
 Seamen’s Provident Fund is under consideration. However, the Committee disapproves the idea of linking the proposal for
 recoupment of Rs.92.78 crore with the amendment of Seamen’s Provident Fund Act. Because misappropriation of the amount
 belonging to the seamen was due to faulty rules/policy of the Government due to which the poor seamen were the sufferers.
 The Committee expects that the decision of Government to recoup the shortage of funds will certainly give relief to the
 Seamen whose precious savings were locked in the scam.                                                                                      (Para 53)
HINDUSTAN SHIPYARD LTD.
            The Committee notes with dismay that out of the approved 10
th Plan outlay of Rs.200 crore for HSL, a sum of
 Rs.137.50 crore has so far been released. The Committee notes that with the boom in the shipping industry, the HSL is
 changing its marketing strategy and utilizing the installed capacity for ship repairing turnover. The vigorous marketing efforts
 have brought the order book position of HSL to Rs.2000 crore. However, the Committee feels that at this juncture the
 Government support will be crucial for success of HSL. The Committee notes that a Rehabilitation-cum-Financial
 Restructuring proposal for Rs. 799.61 crore for the yard is under active consideration of the Government and recommends
 expeditious clearance of the proposal for the capital restructuring of the yard. The Committee expects that with the financial
 restructuring the yard would achieve a turn around and make sustainable profitable operations.                                                    
 (Para 58)
            The Committee notes with distress that the employees of the HSL who have taken voluntary retirement have not been
 paid their statutory dues and arrears and other benefits and have been denied of the benefits for long. The Committee is of the

file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/117thReport.htm[22-10-2018 12:22:07]
 opinion that liability on account of payment of statutory dues and arrears of salary and enhanced pensionary benefits entirely
 lies on the Government. The Committee is constrained to note the inconsiderate attitude of the Government in the matter. The
 Government neither approved the long pending revival package nor allowed HSL to raise the loan for payment of statutory
 dues and other retirement benefits.   The Committee, therefore, recommends the Government either to release the funds for
 payment of arrears to their employees forthwith or allow HSL to raise the loan as requested by the Department of Shipping on
 priority.                                (Para 61)
HOOGHLY DOCK & PORT ENGINEERS LIMITED,
            The Committee notes that during the 10 th Plan an outlay of Rs.41.86 crore for HDPEL had been provided and an outlay
 of Rs.163.00 crore has been proposed during the 11 th Plan period for Renewals and Replacement at Salkia Works and
 Nazirgunge Works. The Committee also notes that a Rehabilitation-cum-Revival Package proposal for Rs.393.61 crore has
 been prepared and is under active consideration of Board for Restructuring of Public Sector Enterprises.   The Committee also
 notes that in order to reduce the shortfall a token provision of Rs.1 lakh has been kept for revival package during 2007-08. The
 Committee recommends the Government to grant an early approval for revival package of HDPEL and write-off the
 Government loan and interest liability for improving the financial health of the company.   The Committee hopes that with the
 approval and implementation package, the Company would achieve a turn around and earn sustainable
 profits.                                                                                               (Para 67)
INLAND WATER TRANSPORT
            The Committee expresses its concern for underutilization of the budgeted amount by the Department of Shipping during
 the 10th Plan. Out of the approved outlay of Rs.864.73 crore, the Department of Shipping was allocated Rs.638.07 crore for
 development of IWT sector which was revised to Rs.560.65 crore and Department could spend only Rs.387.74 crore by the
 end of the 10
th Plan. In BE 2007-08, a sum of Rs.158.00 crore (including Rs.75.90 crore for NE Pool) has been earmarked  for
 IWT sector. On the one hand IWT required higher investment but on the other hand, the Department failed to utilize what
 meager allocation was made to them. The committee observed that the actual expenditure incurred has always been low as
 compared to the budgeted estimates for inland water transport sector. The Committee recommends the Department to intensify
 the monitoring mechanism for implementation and coordination of programme and activities of the Department besides
 addressing the procedural delays for proper utilization of the budgeted amount allocated at BE
 stage.                                                            (Para 69)
            The Committee notes that the Department has prepared an action plan with total projected financial implication of Rs.
 751.18 crore for making the existing 3 National Waterways fully operational for navigation and shipping purposes within a
 span of two years. The Committee feels that the inadequate interest and investment in the sector is aggravating the problems of
 inland water transport.  The Committee is of the strong opinion that the development of the inland water transport sector will
 ultimately depend on the flow of investments, both public and private, primarily public and a multi-pronged approach in which
 the Government takes the responsibility by putting in place the necessary infrastructure for the inland waterways. Therefore,
 the Committee recommends the Department to achieve a vibrant inland water transport and endeavour should be made to
 integrate it with National Maritime Development Programme for overall development of the IWT
 sector.                                                                                                                    (Para 73)
CENTRAL INLAND WATER TRANSPORT CORPORATION
            The Committee notes with dismay that though the Cabinet had decided to absorb all 371 employees of Rajabagan
 Dockyard (RBD) with Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers Ltd. (GRSE), however, only 314 employees were taken over
 by GRSE. The Committee is surprised to note that GRSE has not only ignored the decision of the Cabinet but also made the 57
 employees of the headquarters of the merged entity, the worst victim of non-implementation of merger process who had been
 acting-brain of RBD's operation since its nationalization. The Committee recommends the Government to take steps to ensure
 that the remaining 57 employees are also taken into the pay-roll of GRSE as decided by the Cabinet.
(Para 79)
            The Committee is constrained to note that inspite of its recommendation in the past, the Government has not come out
 with a complete blueprint for making full use of the existing assets of River Service Division (RSD). The Committee is of the
 opinion that CIWTC with its infrastructure and expertise can play an integral part for development and promotion of inland
 water and transport through NW-I and NW-II. The Committee recommends that possibility may also be explored to physically
 amalgamate the remaining CIWTC with Inland Water Transport Authority of India (IWAI), Kolkata Port Trust (KoPT) or
 Shipping Corporation of India (SCI), so that the infrastructure and expertise of CIWTC can be utilized for development of
 waterways transport.                                                                (Para 80)
