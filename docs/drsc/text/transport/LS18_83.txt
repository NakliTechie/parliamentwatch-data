REPORT
 
The Commission of Railway Safety deals with matters pertaining to safety in rail travel and operation.  For
 discharge of these functions, the Commission derives certain statutory powers from the Railways Act, 1989 and the
 rules framed thereunder.  While the Railway Board in the Ministry of Railways are the safety controlling authority and
 are responsible for laying down and enforcing safety standards for the Indian Railways, the main task of the
 Commission is to direct, advise and caution the railway executives through its regulation, inspection, audit,
 investigatory and advisory functions and thereby assist them in ensuring that all stipulated measures are taken in regard
 to the soundness of rail construction and safety in train operation.  The most crucial function of the Commission of
 Railway Safety is to ensure that any new Railway line to be opened for passenger traffic should conform to the
 standards and specifications prescribed by the Ministry of Railways and the new line is safe in all respects for carrying
 of passenger traffic.  Similar job is also performed in respect of other works such as gauge conversion, doubling of lines
 and electrification of existing lines.  Another important function of the Commission is to conduct statutory inquiry into
 serious train accidents occurring on the Indian Railways and to make recommendations for improving Railway Safety.  
 
2.         Since Railway safety is of paramount importance to all and the fact that there had been a spurt in train accidents
 during the recent past, the Committee decided to study the functioning of Commission of Railway Safety.  Since the
 Commission of Railway Safety is under the administrative control of the Ministry of Civil Aviation, the Committee
 asked the Ministry of Civil Aviation to furnish a status note on the functioning of Commission of Railway Safety for its
 consideration. 
 
3.         In the status note submitted by the Ministry of Civil Aviation, the Committee was informed about the genesis of
 the Commission of Railway Safety.  It was brought out that formerly the Commission of Railway Safety was called the
 Railway Inspectorate and it functioned under the control of the Railway Board till May 1941. In the wake of a serious
 accident in 1937, the Central Legislature considered that the Commission, which was charged with the responsibility
 for conducting inquires into serious railway accidents as also for the inspection of Railways, should not function under
 the administrative control of the same executive authority whose work it was required to inspect and comment upon. 
 The principle of separation of the Railway Inspectorate from the Railway Board was endorsed in 1940 by the Central
 Legislature who recommended that "Senior Government Inspectors of Railways should be placed under the
 Administrative control of some authority of the Government of India other than the Railway Board."  The Railway
 Inspectorate was then separated from the control of Railway Board to secure its independence from the authority
 administering the Railways.  After its separation, the Railway Inspectorate was placed under the administrative control
 of the then Department of Communications with effect from 12
th May 1941.  On 9 th July 1942, this Department of
 Communication was bifurcated into two Departments viz (i) Post & Air Department and (ii) War Transport
 Department.  The Railway Inspectorate was kept under the administrative control of Posts & Air Department and
 continuously thereafter under whichever Ministry that held the portfolio of Civil Aviation.  It was re-designated as the
 Commission of Railway Safety on 1.11.1961.  It came under the administrative control of the Ministry of Tourism and
 Civil Aviation in May 1967 subsequently re-designated as the Ministry of Civil Aviation.
 
ORGANISATIONAL SET UP & JURISDICTION
4.         The Commission is headed by the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety (pay scale Rs.24050-26000), who is

 also the Principal Technical Adviser to the Government of India in all matters concerning the Commission.  The
 headquarters of the Commission is located at Lucknow.  The Chief Commissioner directs the technical activities of the
 Commission and issues instruction for the guidance of Commissioners of Railway Safety in respect of holding statutory
 inquiries into serious railway accidents, inspection of new lines including electrification of existing lines prior to their
 opening for public carriage of passengers, and sanction for running of new locomotives/rolling stock.  He also co-

 specifications and procedures for construction, working and maintenance of assets in all branches of railway
 engineering and operation including civil, mechanical, electrical and signal engineering etc.  The Chief Commissioner
 is assisted by a Deputy Commissioner of Railway Safety (General) (pay scale Rs.14300-18300).  The Chief
 Commissioner of Railway Safety is assisted by a regular section of Ministry of Civil Aviation under a Section Officer
 who reports to Deputy Secretary in the Ministry of Civil Aviation as well as Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety.
 5.         There is a Technical Wing attached to the Chief Commissioner at his headquarters which has four Deputy
 Commissioners of Railway Safety (pay scale Rs.12000-16500) drawn from the mechanical, electrical, signaling,
 telecommunication and operating disciplines of Indian Railways.  The Technical Wing has two posts of Technical
 Assistant (pay scale Rs.4500-7000), two LDC, three stenographers and few group 'D' staff.  The Technical Wing has a
 post of Assistant Director (Official Language) (pay scale Rs.6500-10500).
 
6.         Working under the administrative control of Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety are nine Commissioners of
 Railway Safety (pay scale Rs.22400-24500), each one exercising jurisdiction over one of the 9 Zonal Railways, two
 with headquarters at Mumbai, 3 with headquarters at Kolkata and one each with headquarters at Bangalore, New Delhi,
 Lucknow and Secunderabad.  In addition, some of their jurisdictions cover railway establishments falling under their
 jurisdiction viz Metro Railway, Kolkata, DMRC, Delhi, MRTP, Chennai and Konkan Railway.  There are two Deputy
 Commissioners of Railway Safety (Signalling and Telecommunication) (pay scale Rs.12000-16500) headquartered at
 Mumbai and Kolkata.  Each Commissioner is an independent statutory authority under the Railway Act, 1989.  Each
 Commissioner of Railway Safety is assisted by a bare minimum staff of a stenographer/Sr. Private Secretary, one
 Office Superintendent (pay scale Rs.5500-9000), one Safety Assistant (pay scale Rs.5000-8000), one/two UDCs, one to
 four LDCs and a few Group 'D' staff.
 
APPOINTMENT TO THE CADRE OF COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
7.         The Deputy Commissioners of Railway Safety are appointed on deputation/absorption from the applicants drawn
 from Signaling, Electrical, Mechanical, Operating and Civil Engineering Departments of Indian Railways.  The period
 of deputation as per extant recruitment rules is five years.  During the period of deputation the officers are entitled to
 deputation allowance as per the instructions of Ministry of Finance.  However, in their case the restriction that pay plus
 deputation allowance shall not exceed the maximum of scale has been relaxed.  The officers are governed by Railway
 TA rules and are eligible for free passes and Privilege Ticket Orders (PTOs) on the same lines as other Railway
 Officers of corresponding status. 
 
8.         The Commissioners of Railway Safety are selected by the Union Public Service Commission on absorption basis
 from amongst the applicants who are senior officers drawn from Civil Engineering Department of the Indian Railways
 and once confirmed cannot return back to railway service, but are governed by Railway TA rules and are eligible for
 free passes and PTOs on the same lines as other Railway Officers of corresponding status. 
 
9.         The Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety is selected by the Union Public Service Commission from amongst
 the existing Commissioners who have put in a minimum of three years of service in the grade.
 
10.       The supporting staff of the Commission is appointed in the Commission itself through Employment Exchange
 and Staff Selection Commission etc. and are governed by civil rules for TA etc. and are not entitled for free passes and
 PTOs.
FUNCTIONS
11.       The responsibility for safety in the working and operation of Railway rests solely with Railway Board and the
 Zonal Railway authorities.  The main task of the Commission of Railway Safety is to direct, advise and caution the

 Railway executives with a view to ensure that all reasonable precautions are taken in regard to soundness of rail
 construction and safety of train operation.  The Railway Board refers to the Commission matters relating to
 modification or enhancement of standards in respect of operation of trains, track, locomotive, rolling stock and revision
 of rules embodied in the General Rules, Rules for the opening of New Lines, Manuals, IRCA Regulations, Schedules of
 Dimensions and other publications.  Suggestions made by the Commission of Railway Safety are duly considered by
 the Railway Board before necessary revisions are notified.
 
12.       The duties and functions of the Commissioners are laid down in the Railways Act, 1989 and rules framed
 thereunder, and executive instructions issued from time to time.  These are briefly:
·
              to conduct statutory inquires into serious railway accidents and suggest safeguards.
·              to inspect new railway lines with a view to determining whether they are fit to be opened for the
 public carriage of passenger, and to sanction their opening after inspection on behalf of the Central
 Government.
·              to sanction the execution of all new works and installations on the running track affecting the
 safety of the traveling public such as rebuilding of bridges, remodeling of station yards, line
 capacity works, resignalling works, etc.
·              to make periodical inspections of railways and report to the Central Government on any condition
 which may endanger the safety of the traveling public and make recommendations.
·              to examine the technical aspects of new rolling stock and advise on their sanctions and increase in
 speeds.
·              to authorize the carriage of oversized consignments stipulating the conditions for their movements.
·              to recommend and sanction infringements to the schedule of dimensions prescribed by the
 Government of India.
·              to grant dispensation from general rules under approved special instructions subject to stipulated
 safeguards.
·              to oversee the accident prevention efficacy of the zonal railway administrations by reviewing the
 reports of departmental enquiries into less serious accidents.
 
13.       Field duties of the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety consist of inspections of sections of Railways, visits
 to the Railway Headquarters and Divisional Offices, Railway installations and Circle Offices.  If considered necessary
 by him, he may himself hold inquiries into important accidents.   The Chief Commissioner submits an annual report on
 the working of the Commission which is laid on the table of both the Houses of Parliament.

ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL OF COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
16.       The Committee observed that the Commission of Railway Safety had been working under the administrative
 control of Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The Committee enquired from the Commission of Railway Safety as to whether

 the present arrangement of the Commission working under the Ministry of Civil Aviation was satisfactory or whether
 there were any difficulties in the existing arrangement.  The Committee also sought suggestion of Chief Commissioner
 of Railway Safety to make the working of the Commission more effective.   The Committee was informed that the
 Commission of Railway Safety is a high level statutory body charged with the responsibility for the inspection of new
 railway lines prior to their opening for passenger traffic, inspection of open lines, inquiries into serious Railway
 Accidents, and certain other duties connected with safety of railway operation as required under the Railways Act
 1989.  The Committee was also informed by the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety that during the course of
 working as Commissioners, they are required to take tough stands against the Railways and their working in many
 cases and hence it will not be in the interest of the impartial working of the Commission to keep it attached with the
 Ministry of Railways.  It is because of the independence the Commission enjoys from the Ministry of Railways that
 they are able to discharge their duties impartially.  In response to the query as to whether the Commission of Railway
 Safety should remain with the Ministry of Civil Aviation or should it be attached to any other organization, other than
 Ministry of Railways, Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety stated that for the past more than 50 years, the
 Commission of Railway Safety has been attached with that Ministry which deals with Civil Aviation.  The Chief
 Commissioner of Railway Safety stated that the Commission could as well be attached, with any other Ministry.  The
 Committee was further informed that the question regarding the Ministry under which the Commission of Railway
 Safety should function was considered by three high level Railway Accident Committees:
 
·
        Railway Accident Committee 1962 (Kunzru Committee )
·        Railway Accidents Enquiry Committee 1968 (Wanchoo Committee)
·        Railway Accidents Enquiry Committee 1978 (Sikri Committee)
 
17.       All these Committees went into great depth and after considering their recommendations, the Government every
 time decided that the Commission of Railway Safety should not be with the Ministry of Railways but remain outside it
 and decided to keep the Commission of Railway Safety under the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The Appointments
 Committee of the Cabinet re-considered this issue in 1998 and decided to maintain status quo i.e. to keep Commission
 of Railway Safety under administrative control of Ministry of Civil Aviation.
 
18.       As regard the suggestion to make the working of the Commission more effective, the Chief Commissioner of
 Railway Safety was of the view that the Commission should enjoy the autonomy as available with the Election
 Commission, Central Vigilance Commission, Union Public Service Commission or Comptroller and Auditor General. 
 In that event the Commissioners of Railway Safety will be able to assert themselves in ensuring that their
 recommendations and observations are enforceable and do not remain merely recommendatory.  The Commission
 needs to be given active support and assistance in the pursuit of its objectives.  Also the budget provisions for the
 Commission of Railway Safety should be delinked from the budget of Ministry of Civil Aviation and it should be on
 the lines of other autonomous bodies like Election Commission, Union Public Service Commission, Central Vigilance
 Commission, or Comptroller and Auditor General.
           
19.       During the evidence of the representatives of Ministry of Railways, the Committee sought the opinion of the
 Chairman, Railway Board as to whether the Commission of Railway Safety should be an independent body like the
 Election Commission of India.  The Chairman, Railway Board opined as under:-
 
"Sir, I personally feel that we should maintain the creditability of the Deputy Chief Commissioners of Railway
 Safety.  The general public still feels that they are under the Ministry of Railway; it may be directly or
 indirectly.  I personally feel that it should be an independent body.  I fully agree with you. ……. It will
 definitely enhance the confidence and credibility of the Commission.  There is no doubt about it."

21.       The Committee is of the opinion that the mandate of Ministry of Civil Aviation is altogether different from the
 mandate of the Commission of Railway Safety.  Therefore, the Ministry of Civil Aviation is not in a position to address
 the administrative problems of the Commission of Railway Safety adequately.  In order to ensure the impartial working
 of the Commission of Railway Safety, it is essential that the Commission works outside the control of both the
 Ministries of Railways and Civil Aviation.  In order to make the functioning of the Commission more effective, it is
 strongly felt that the Commission should be provided greater powers and independence.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Commission of Railway Safety should be made an autonomous statutory body like Election
 Commission and Union Public Service Commission.  The Committee also agrees with the contention of the Ministry of
 Railways that officers below the rank of Commissioner of Railway Safety should also not be sent on deputation, rather
 they should go on absorption terms to the Commission.   The Committee is of the firm view that this would make the
 Commission independent, powerful and vigilant to the fullest extent.   This would definitely go a long way in dispelling
 the general impression that the Ministry of Railways unduly influences the Commission in its functioning as it is rightly
 said that justice should not only be done but it should also appear to have been done.
EFFECTIVENESS OF RECOMMENDATIONS OF COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
22.       The Committee then enquired about the nature of the recommendations made by the Commission and whether
 the recommendations of the Commission were recommendatory or mandatory.  The Committee also enquired so to
 whether the Ministry of Railways had been accepting the advice given by the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety,
 particularly in matters relating to Railway Safety.  In response, the Committee was informed by the Commission of
 Railway Safety that their recommendations were recommendatory and not mandatory.  It was also informed that the
 recommendations were mostly honoured or accepted by the Ministry of Railways.  However, the Commission could not
 force the Railway Administration to accept the recommendations because they were only a recommendatory body.  
 The responsibility for acceptance and implementation of the measures recommended by the Commission rests solely
 with the Railway Board and the Zonal Railway Administrations.  The Commission, however, does not have the
 wherewithal to monitor the status of implementation of these measures by the Railways. 
23.       The Committee was further informed that the question regarding the nature of the recommendations of the
 Commission was also raised by the Appointment Committee of Cabinet (ACC) in 1998 when they asked the Ministry
 of Railways to categorically state whether the recommendations of the Commission should be mandatory or not.  In
 response, Appointment Committee of Cabinet was assured by the Ministry of Railways that  "Every recommendation of
 the Commission is scrutinized at various levels before taking a final decision.  In case, Railways have any reservation
 regarding a particular item, the same is discussed with the Commission by the Railway Board before a final decision is
 taken.  In case of non-acceptance of any of the recommendations, personal approval of the concerned Board Member is
 taken.  This ensures that before turning down any recommendation, it is thoroughly scrutinized from all aspects."  
 
24.       It was pointed out by the Commission that inspite of the above commitment, with the passage of time, the
 Ministry of Railways changed their stand from discussing the issues with the Commission before taking final decision
 on a particular recommendation of the Commission, to discussions only in serious cases of dispute, to no discussions on
 any serious issue raised by the Commission. Ministry of Railways had also communicated to the Commission of
 Railway Safety that if any recommendation of the Commission is not accepted by the Railway Board, the Commission
 may reflect such issues in their Annual Report.
 
25.       The Commission further pointed out that the commitment made by the Ministry of Railways to the Appointment
 Committee of Cabinet was not being honoured.   Many times their recommendations were neither accepted nor any
 reason was given for the non-acceptance.  The Ministry of Railways had not even been discussing with the Commission
 before rejecting their recommendations.  It was further informed that the recommendations once accepted by the
 Ministry of Railways were either diluted or revoked at a later stage unilaterally by the Ministry of Railways without
 prior consultation and approval of the Commission or without even informing the Commission.  In some cases the
 Commission is kept in the dark where the Railway Board withdraws or overrules some conditions insisted by the
 Commission.
 
26.       The Committee brought the observations made by the Commission of Railway Safety to the notice of Ministry
 of Railways and sought their comments thereon.  In response, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Railways

 that the recommendations made by the Commission relate to the whole spectrum of rail operation and maintenance. 
 While majority of the recommendations are accepted and implemented, some of them need thorough and detailed
 examination from various angles i.e. technical feasibility, operational significance, evaluation of extent of enhancement
 of safety, assimilation in the prevailing field environment and financial viability etc.  Some of them also require design

 practicability, feasibility and relative importance in enhanced safe operation.  Reasons for non-acceptance of
 Commission of Railway Safety recommendations are generally conveyed to Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety. 
 Some vital issues are also finalized during the meetings with the Commissioners of Railway Safety and Chief
 Commissioner of Railway Safety.  No recommendation has been rejected without assigning any reason at appropriate
 level, though in stray cases, detailed reasons were not conveyed, inadvertently.  As per rules and regulations, prior
 consultation or approval of the Commission is not necessary.  The Commissioner is normally informed of all such
 decisions, where his recommendations/observations are non-acceptable with specific reasons. 
 
27.       The Committee is of the view that although the recommendations of the Commission of Railway Safety are not
 mandatory for the Ministry of Railways yet the situation brought out by the Commission of Railway Safety is highly
 unsatisfactory.  The Committee is of the view that the recommendations made by the Commission of Railway Safety
 should receive utmost importance from the Ministry of Railways.  The Committee takes exception to the fact that, on
 certain occasions, the Ministry of Railways neither consults the Commission of Railway Safety before rejecting a
 recommendation nor furnishes reasons for not accepting the recommendations made by the Commission of Railway
 Safety.  The Committee strongly feels that the Commission of Railway Safety is a watch dog on behalf of the public at
 large.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Railways, should honour the commitment made by
 them to the Appointment Committee of Cabinet regarding consulting the Commission before rejecting a
 recommendation.   The Committee further recommends that it should be made mandatory for the Railway
 Administration not to reject or overrule any recommendations made by the Commissioners without a meeting between
 the Chairman, Railway Board and the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety, where the matters can be discussed
 threadbare and agreements reached.  The Committee further recommends that while expressing their inability to accept
 the recommendations, the Railway Board should give detailed reasons for not accepting the recommendations.  The
 Committee also recommends that any decision taken by the Railway Board in consultation with the Commission of
 Railway Safety must not be revoked unilaterally by the Railway Board or Zonal Railway, without prior consultation
 and approval of the Commission.
 
28.       The Committee enquired from the Commission of Railway Safety whether there have been any instances when
 the suggestions made by the Commission of Railway Safety were not considered by the Railway Board and, if so,
 details thereof.  The Commission of Railway Safety replied in affirmative and provided details of a few significant
 instances.  The Commission pointed out that one of their important concerns had been that before introducing a new
 passenger train, the Railway Board should seek concurrence of the Commission.  Running of new passenger trains
 involves creation of a lot of infrastructure including adequate number of platforms at every way side and terminal
 stations, adequate arrangement for inspections of undergear of the locos and coaches, availability of adequate number
 of staff to maintain the trains in safe condition and for running of them etc.  It was informed that the Railway Safety
 Review Committee (RSRC) 1998 had also recommended that the sanction of Commission of Railway Safety should be
 obtained before introduction of new passenger carrying trains.  This has not been accepted by the Railway Board.  Even
 the suggestion made by the Commission that the Railway Administration should make out a Check List and ensure its
 compliance before introduction of a new passenger train has not been complied with by the Railways.  This has serious
 implications on Railway Safety.
 
29.       The Committee brought the above position to the notice of Ministry of Railways and sought comments thereon. 
 In response, the Ministry of Railways replied that before any new train is introduced, certification of available
 maintenance infrastructure at either end is given by two SAG (Joint Secretaries) Officers, in-charge of operation and
 maintenance, after consulting all concerned agencies.  A checklist of items has been circulated to all concerned by the
 Railway Board.  The recommendation of the Railway Safety Review Committee 1998 regarding certification by the
 Commissioner of Railway Safety in cases of introduction of new trains was not found feasible for acceptance as the
 Railway Board is the final safety controlling authority and necessary certificate from respective Departments are

 concurrence of Commission of Railway Safety for every new train does not fall within the scope of the legal duties of

30.       Ministry of Railways further stated that it needs to be appreciated that the function of introducing new trains,

32.       The Committee observes that a large number of new passenger trains are introduced every year to meet the
 demand of the traveling public.  While it does fulfil the requirements of the public, it has to be examined as to whether
 such an action is in the interest of safety and convenience of the traveling public.  The present system in the country is
 already overstressed and it is important to ensure that all infrastructure facilities cater to the safe running of additional
 trains with due safety margins.  It is essential that such facilities are in position before adding any burden of additional
 trains.  The introduction of additional trains involves the safety of the passengers.   The Committee is not convinced
 with the reply furnished by the Ministry of Railways with regard to not seeking the concurrence of Commission of
 Railway Safety before introduction of a new passenger train.  The Committee gives due weightage to the reply of the
 Ministry of Railways that before any new train is introduced, certification of available maintenance infrastructure at
 either end is given by two SAG (Joint Secretaries) Officers, in-charge of operation and maintenance, after consulting all
 concerned agencies.  Ministry of Railway have also pointed out that concurrence of every new train does not fall under
 the legal duties of Commission of Railway Safety, as enumerated in the Railway Act, 1989.  The main task of the
 Commission is to direct, advise and caution the railway executives through its regulation/inspectional/audit and
 investigatory/advisory functions and thereby assist them in ensuring that all stipulated measures are taken in regard to
 the soundness of rail construction and safety in train operation.   The Committee is, therefore, of the view that the
 sanction of Commission of Railway Safety must be necessary for Ministry of Railways before introducing any new
 passenger train.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that it should be made mandatory for Ministry of Railways to
 seek the concurrence of Commission of Railway Safety before introduction of any new passenger train, so as to ensure
 that the safety of the passengers is not compromised by amending the existing statutory provisions in this regard.

 shall lie against the Central Government, any railway administration, a railway servant or any other person for anything

 The Commissioner of Railway Safety does not fit into this exception.  He is deemed to be a public servant within the
 meaning of Section 21 of the Indian Penal Code.  He is only entitled to the privileges which are available to a public

70.       The Committee observes that whenever a train accident occurs, the railway administration is quick to announce
 the prima-facie cause of the accident, even when the officials concerned are aware of the enormity of the accident and
 that, as provided in the Railway Act, Commissioner of Railway Safety would hold an enquiry to determine the exact
 cause of the accident.  However, if at a later date, the findings of the Commissioner of Railway Safety happen to
 coincide with the version earlier advanced by the railway officials, the Commission of Railway Safety is accused of a
 bias in favour of the Railways. Such actions affect the credibility of Commissioner of Railway Safety.
 
71.       The Committee is of the opinion that the unwritten convention that no one would give the cause of an accident,
 when an enquiry is pending, needs to be followed by the Railway administration.  The Committee recommends that the
 Railway administration should refrain from speculating and giving the prime-facie cause of an accident, which is
 required to be statutorily enquired by the Commissioner of Railway Safety.  This will ensure that the credibility of the
 Commission of Railway Safety is maintained and the Commissioners are not suspected of prejudice or bias in
 conducting their enquiries.
 
CONCLUSION
72.       The Committee is of the opinion that the Commission of Railway Safety is an important organisation of the
 Government and, in the prevailing circumstances, the Commission's hands need strengthening in all possible ways. The
 Committee recommends that the Government should bring a comprehensive legislation incorporating the suggestion
 made by the Committee in this Report so that the functioning of the Commission of Railway Safety could improve in
 the interest of the public travelling by Indian Railways.

The Committee is of the opinion that the mandate of Ministry of Civil Aviation is altogether different from the mandate
 of the Commission of Railway Safety.  Therefore, the Ministry of Civil Aviation is not in a position to address the
 administrative problems of the Commission of Railway Safety adequately.  In order to ensure the impartial working of
 the Commission of Railway Safety, it is essential that the Commission works outside the control of both the Ministries
 of Railways and Civil Aviation.  In order to make the functioning of the Commission more effective, it is strongly felt
 that the Commission should be provided greater powers and independence.  The Committee, therefore, recommends
 that the Commission of Railway Safety should be made an autonomous statutory body like Election Commission and
 Union Public Service Commission.  The Committee also agrees with the contention of the Ministry of Railways that
 officers below the rank of Commissioner of Railway Safety should also not be sent on deputation, rather they should go
 on absorption terms to the Commission.   The Committee is of the firm view that this would make the Commission
 independent, powerful and vigilant to the fullest extent.   This would definitely go a long way in dispelling the general
 impression that the Ministry of Railways unduly influences the Commission in its functioning as it is rightly said that
 justice should not only be done but it should also appear to have been done.
(Para 21)
 
EFFECTIVENESS OF RECOMMENDATIONS OF COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
 
The Committee is of the view that although the recommendations of the Commission of Railway Safety are not
 mandatory for the Ministry of Railways yet the situation brought out by the Commission of Railway Safety is highly
 unsatisfactory.  The Committee is of the view that the recommendations made by the Commission of Railway Safety
 should receive utmost importance from the Ministry of Railways.  The Committee takes exception to the fact that, on
 certain occasions, the Ministry of Railways neither consults the Commission of Railway Safety before rejecting a
 recommendation nor furnishes reasons for not accepting the recommendations made by the Commission of Railway
 Safety.  The Committee strongly feels that the Commission of Railway Safety is a watch dog on behalf of the public at
 large.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Railways, should honour the commitment made by
 them to the Appointment Committee of Cabinet regarding consulting the Commission before rejecting a
 recommendation.   The Committee further recommends that it should be made mandatory for the Railway
 Administration not to reject or overrule any recommendations made by the Commissioners without a meeting between
 the Chairman, Railway Board and the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety, where the matters can be discussed
 threadbare and agreements reached.  The Committee further recommends that while expressing their inability to accept
 the recommendations, the Railway Board should give detailed reasons for not accepting the recommendations.  The
 Committee also recommends that any decision taken by the Railway Board in consultation with the Commission of
 Railway Safety must not be revoked unilaterally by the Railway Board or Zonal Railway, without prior consultation
 and approval of the Commission.
(Para 27)
 
The Committee observes that a large number of new passenger trains are introduced every year to meet the demand of
 the traveling public.  While it does fulfil the requirements of the public, it has to be examined as to whether such an
 action is in the interest of safety and convenience of the traveling public.  The present system in the country is already
 overstressed and it is important to ensure that all infrastructure facilities cater to the safe running of additional trains
 with due safety margins.  It is essential that such facilities are in position before adding any burden of additional trains. 
 The introduction of additional trains involves the safety of the passengers.   The Committee is not convinced with the
 reply furnished by the Ministry of Railways with regard to not seeking the concurrence of Commission of Railway
 Safety before introduction of a new passenger train.  The Committee gives due weightage to the reply of the Ministry of
 Railways that before any new train is introduced, certification of available maintenance infrastructure at either end is
 given by two SAG (Joint Secretaries) Officers, in-charge of operation and maintenance, after consulting all concerned
 agencies.  Ministry of Railway have also pointed out that concurrence of every new train does not fall under the legal
 duties of Commission of Railway Safety, as enumerated in the Railway Act, 1989.  The main task of the Commission is
 to direct, advise and caution the railway executives through its regulation/inspectional/audit and investigatory/advisory
 functions and thereby assist them in ensuring that all stipulated measures are taken in regard to the soundness of rail
 construction and safety in train operation.   The Committee is, therefore, of the view that the sanction of Commission of
 Railway Safety must be necessary for Ministry of Railways before introducing any new passenger train.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that it should be made mandatory for Ministry of Railways to seek the concurrence

 of Commission of Railway Safety before introduction of any new passenger train, so as to ensure that the safety of the
 passengers is not compromised by amending the existing statutory provisions in this regard.

REVISION OF 'RULES FOR OPENING, 1933'
 
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Railways has, on the insistence of Commission of Railway Safety,
 decided to make necessary amendments in the present 'Rules of 2000' to include the 'one month notice' to the

 on his inspection should remain an exception, as envisaged under the said provisions, than becoming the rule.

PUBLICATION OF REPORTS ON RAILWAY ACCIDENTS
 
The Committee is constrained to note that the inquiry reports of the Commission of Railway Safety on accidents are
 either not published or published too late and the people at large do not come to know of the reasons for concurrence of
 a particular accident.  The Committee is of the view that the public needs to know the reasons for the railway accidents
 and it will serve the larger interest of the public if the accident reports are published at the earliest.  The Committee also
 notes the impediments faced by the Commission of Railway Safety in undertaking the enquiries.  The Committee feels
 that it is essential to provide legal protection to Commission of Railway Safety.  The Committee is in agreement with
 the recommendations made by Railway Safety Review Committee 1998, quoted at para 43 above.  The Committee
 recommends that these recommendations should be accepted and the relevant acts and rules should be amended at the
 earliest.  The Committee also recommends that when the Commission of Railway Safety proposes to take up an
 inquiry, inquiries initiated by police or magistrate should be stopped and the civil and police authorities should co-
operate in the Commission's inquiry.  The Committee feels that the implementations of the steps suggested by the
 Committee will enable the Commissioners of Railway Safety in undertaking their inquiries without any hindrances. 
 Thereby, the inquiry reports of the Commissioners on accidents could also be published as soon as the inquiry is over
 and the public at large would not be deprived of their vital right to information. 
(Para 52)
 
 
UPGRADATION OF STATUS OF OFFICERS OF COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
 
The Committee is constrained to note that vacancies continue to exist in the Commission of Railway Safety for fairly
 long periods because of non-availability of eligible officers from the Ministry of Railways.  The Committee
 recommends that any vacancy arising in the Commission of Railway Safety should be filled up at the earliest so that the
 functioning of the Commission does not suffer.  The Committee is also constrained to note that the recommendations
 made by Railway Safety Review Committee 1998 are yet to be implemented by the Government.  The Chief
 Commissioner of Railway Safety, being the Principal Technical Advisor to the Government of India on matters
 concerning rail safety, is the highest technical official in the Ministry of Civil Aviation for dealing with railway safety. 
 The Committee is of the view that upgradation of the post of Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety to the level of
 Secretary to the Government of India is absolutely essential so that he can effectively and forcefully interact with the
 nine Secretary level officers of Railway Board.  Lack of such a status to the Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety
 works as a serious deterrent to him in discharging his duties effectively in a hierarchy conscious bureaucracy.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that the status of Chief Commissioner of Railway Safety should be equal to that of
 the Chairman of Railway Board in rank and pay.  Likewise the status of Commissioner of Railway Safety should be
 equal to that of General Manager of a Zonal Railway in rank as well as pay.  The posts of Deputy Commissioners of
 Railway Safety should be manned by Senior Administration Grade Officers.   The Commissioners of Railway Safety
 should also be provided with the assistance of Deputy Commissioners. The Committee further recommends that, as
 recommended by the Fifth Central Pay Commission, the tenure of the Chief Commissioner should be five years, taking

EXPOSURE TO TECHNOLOGICAL ADVANCEMENTS
 
The Committee feels that there is a genuine need of the officers of Commission of Railway Safety to get exposed to the
 latest technological advancements taking place in the railway sector in India and abroad.  Otherwise there is a real
 danger that the technical knowledge of the officers of the Commission will get obsolete.  The Committee recommends
 that the officers of the Commission should be exposed to latest technologies through trainings in the country and
 abroad, so that they are in a position to perform their role effectively.  The officers of the Commission should also be
 given full facilities to become members of professional institutions, at par with the facilities extended by the railway
 administration to railway officers.  This will help in keeping the officers of the Commission updated in their technical
 knowledge.  However, for this purpose, the Commission should not depend upon the financial assistance of the
 Ministry of Railways.  The Committee recommends that the Government should ensure that the budget of the
 Commission of Railway Safety is suitably increased so that these requirements could be taken care of by the
 Commission itself.
(Para 63)
 
SUPPLY OF RAILWAY CIRCULARS ETC. TO COMMISSION OF RAILWAY SAFETY
 
The Committee is of the view that unless the Commission of Railway Safety is kept aware of the latest instructions with
 respect to railway safety issued by the Railway Board or Zonal Railways, it cannot discharge its duties effectively. 
 Ministry of Railways have admitted that copies of all important policy orders are endorsed to the Chief Commissioner
 of Railway Safety.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Railways should ensure that Commission of
 Railway Safety is on the permanent mailing list of Railway Board and Zonal Railways for all policy Circulars having
 bearing on safety and all Circulars/Orders affecting safety.  The Committee further recommends that the Commission
 of Railway Safety should be supplied with the proceeding of the Technical Committees of Railway Board so that the
 Commission remains informed of the safety problems encountered in the working of the railways and the measures
 taken to solve these problems.
(Para 67)
 
LACK OF OFFICE INFRASTRUCTURE
 
The Committee notes with concern the constraints faced by the officers of the Commission of Railway Safety in
 performing their duties due to lack of budgetary allocations and proper infrastructure and office machinery.  The
 Committee feels that the Commission of Railway Safety need adequate budgetary support from the Government so that
 it does not have to look upon the Ministry of Railways for being able to effectively discharge its duties.  The
 Committee recommends that the budget allocation of Commission of Railway Safety should be substantially increased
 so that the Commission could take care of its essential requirements such as funds for foreign training, air travel within
 India, nominations to various training courses, membership to professional institutions etc.
