I Brief Background 
 The tourism sector has huge potential as an instrument for economic 
development.  Tourism is also an important source of foreign exchange earnings.  Not 
only does the tourism sector spearhead growth, it also improves the quality of people‟s 
lives with its capacity to create large -scale employment of diverse kind.  It also 
supports environmental protection and champions diverse cultural heritage.  
2.  Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency to formulate national  policies  and  
programmes for the development an d promotion of tourism. In the process, the 
Ministry consults and collaborates with other stakeholders in the sector including 
various Central Ministries/ Agencies, State Governments/ Union Territory 
Administrations and the representatives of the private sector. 
3. Secretary (Tourism) is the executive chief of the Ministry. The Directorate 
General of Tourism has 20 Domestic field offices and an Indian Institute of Skiing and 
Mountaineering within the country and 08 overseas offices. The overseas offices 
promote Indian tourism in the markets abroad. 
4. The domestic field offices also play a vital role for promotion of tourism sector 
in the country. They are also involved in monitoring the progress of implementation of 
projects sanctioned by the Ministry to the Stat e Governments/Union Territories. India 
Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) is the only public sector undertaking under 
the charge of the Ministry of Tourism.  
5. The Ministry also has the following autonomous institutions: 
(i) Indian Institute of Tourism and Travel Management (IITTM). 
(ii) National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology 
(NCHMCT); and the Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs). 
(iii) Indian Culinary Institute (ICI).

 18. The Committee notes from the information furnished based on the 3 rd Tourism 
Satellite Account for India (TSAI) prepared in 2018 by Ministry of Tourism  that the 
contribution (direct and indirect) of Tourism to the GDP was estimated to be 5.06% in 
2016-17 and the total jobs (direct and indirect) in the country accounted for 12.75% of 
total employment in 2018-19. The annual growth rate of Foreign Tourist Visits  during the 
year 2918 was 5.2%. The annual growth in Foreign Exchange Earnings during the 
corresponding period was 4.7%. During his deposition before the Committee , the 
Secretary, Ministry of Tourism informed that  Domestic Tourist Visits during 2018 were 
1854.9 million as compared to 1657.5 million during 2017 registering a growth @ 11.91% 
over previous year.  
 
19. The Ministry has also furnished the following information:-  
Arrivals of Non Resident Indians (NRIs) 
 From 2014 onwards, Ministry of Tourism has  started to 
compile the arrivals of NRIs on annual basis and there 
were 6.87 million arrivals of NRIs in India during 2018. 
International Tourist Arrivals (ITAs) 
 In concordance with UNWTO, ITAs include both FTAs 
and Arrivals of NRIs. In the year 2018, there were 
17.42 million ITAs in India.  
20. The Committee has noted that some of India's smaller neighbouring countries like 
Myanmar, Bhutan and Sri Lanka have performed remarkably well in terms of 
international tourist arrivals . For example during 2018, there were 35,51,428 international 
tourist arrivals in Myanmar and 2,74,097 in Bhutan. International tourist arrivals in 
Bhutan and Sri Lanka registered a growth of 7.61 % and 5.6% respectively during 2018.

25. The Committee observes that tourism being a labour -intensive multi -
sectoral activity characteri sed by multiple services and industries is a key driver 
of socio -economic progress as well as employment generation and economic 
development.  The tourism sector has the potential to stimulate other economic 
sectors through its backward and forward linkage s and cross -sectoral synergies 
with sectors like agriculture, handicrafts, transport, construction, to name a few.  
It is thus evident that any higher investment made in the tourism sector can lead 
to large sale employment generation, alleviate poverty and  propel India to a 
faster growth trajectory.   
26. The Committee is dismayed to note that despite its unique endowments of 
biodiversity and its rich culture and heritage, India‘s total share in global 
international tourist arrivals remains a modest 1.24% a gainst the target of 3% 
while, as per the Report of the Working Group of Tourism of the 12 th Five Year 
Plan, in about 150 countries of the world, tourism is one of the 5 top export 
earners and number one export earner in about 60 countries. When measured by 
the yardstick of its vast tourism resources, India‘s low share of tourism arrivals 
is certainly below its potential.  Similarly, the contribution (direct and indirect) 
of tourism in the GDP and the total jobs in the country is estimated to be 5.06% 
and 12.75% respectively, which, in the considered opinion of the Committee, is 
inadequate and way  below the massive potential that exists. The Committee 
wishes to point out that in many smaller and developing countries , the entire 
economy of the country depends on tourism for its survival and tourism remains 
a major source of foreign exchange earnings . That being the case , the world's 
second largest country in terms of population, seventh largest by area, having 
7500 km of coastline and one of the most endowed regions of the world in terms 
of its tourism attractiveness, India receives less internationa l tourists than its full 
potential.  Taking all these factors into account, the Committee apprehends that 
the shortfall of Rs. 147.42 crore in BE 2020 -21 against the projected demand of 
Rs. 2647.25 crore would seriously jeopardize the targeted tourism outc omes 
during the Financial Year 2020-21.  

 
 
14 
 
27.    The Committee notes the submission of the Ministry that the difference in 
the projection made by it and the allocation given for the financial year 2020 -21 
is not high and that if additional funds are require d, it would move the Ministry 
of Finance for additional allocation at the RE 2020 -21 stage.  The Committee 
observes that availability of adequate resources to the tourism sector is sine qua 
non for achieving the tourism growth as well as inclusive growth.  Since 
development of tourism infrastructure is capital intensive, the Committee 
apprehends that with the budgetary allocation of Rs.2499.83 crore for 2020 -21, 
which, in the opinion of the Committee, is low, it would be difficult for the 
Ministry to take u p significant activities related to on -going infrastructure 
development of the sanctioned tourism circuits/destinations and also 
development of new tourism products of global standards.  If the tourism sector 
has to play a major role in faster and inclusiv e growth, the infrastructure 
bottlenecks need to be unleashed.  The Committee notes that more than half of 
the allocation of the Ministry for 2020 -21 is devoted for the schemes relating to 
infrastructure development.  In most of the States/UTs, the outlay for tourism is 
meagre and the Central Government has dominance in the domain of public 
resource mobilization by w ay of collection of tax revenue.  The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry be provided a higher financial outlay at the RE 
2020-21 stage.  The  Committee would like the Ministry of Tourism to bring the 
above recommendation to the notice of Ministry of Finance and also intimate the 
Committee of their response in this regard.    
28.    Since the consumption demand arising from tourist expenditure h as a 
multiplier effect on the economy, the expansion of the tourist sector can lead to 
large scale employment gene ration and poverty alleviation. Needless to mention, 
tourism has huge potential to stimulate overall economic growth and any 
increase in the o utlay for tourism sector can propel our country to a faster 
growth trajectory and will lead to large scale employment generation and 
alleviation of poverty. If our country can tap its full potential of the tourism 
sector, it can play a major role for faste r and inclusive growth;  but for this to 
happen, the budgetary provisions for the Ministry has to be enhanced 

 
 
15 
 
substantially.  By doing so, tourism infrastructure could be strengthened/ 
upgraded, conservation and development of all heritage sites could be 
undertaken and completed and last mile connectivity to tourist de stinations could 
be improved. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government 
should accord priority status to the tourism sector and enumerate a fiscal 
roadmap for generating and allo cating more financial resource for tourism so 
that the full potential of a vibrant sector could be tapped.

32. The Committee takes note of the fact that the annual growth rate of the 
Tourism Sector in India is 5.2% and its contribution to GDP is 5.06%. According 
to UN World Tourism Organisation Report (2011), for diversified economies, the 
contribution of tourism to national GDP is over 10% for countries where tourism 
happens to be the main driver of national economy. Quite apparently, despite 
having a strong domestic tourist base which is likely  to grow further on the back 
of a rapidly rising middle class with increased disposable incomes and having 
many World Heritage sites and rich fauna, our country has not been able to 
exploit its tourism potential to the fullest possible extent and the touri st sector 
has not proved to be the proverbial engine of growth for our economy. For a 
country of our size, with wealth of attractions and experiences, this 
underperformance represents significant foregone economic value. Since tourism 
in the country has th e potential to emerge as a key economic driver through 
economic growth and employment generation, the country can make a giant leap 
towards achieving 5 trillion dollar economy by tapping the enormous potential of 
the tourism sector. The Committee, therefor e, recommends that the strategies 
adopted for promotion and development of the tourist sector should be 
recalibrated to harness the full potential of Indian tourism and double the 
current contribution of tourism to GDP in the next 5 years. The Committee 
would like the Ministry to prepare a vision document, chalking out a road map 
for accelerating the expansion of the tourism sector and helping achieve a 5 
trillion dollar economy by 2025 by way of a substantial contribution from the 
tourism sector. The Commi ttee, therefore, desires that policy measures may be 
initiated by the Ministry to accelerate the growth momentum of the tourism 

 
 
17 
 
sector and raise the contribution of tourism from the current 5.06% to 10% of 
GDP.

56. The Committee is constrained  to observe that under the Swadesh Darshan 
Scheme (SDS), out of the 77 projects sanctioned so far for Rs. 6035.70 Crore, 
only 9 Projects have reached their logical conclusion and the remaining projects 
have witnessed physical progress ranging from 50% to 90% and 10 projects have 
not even  taken off due to non -release of funds. The Committee also notes that no 
new projects have been sanctioned during 2019 -20, though the Ministry has not 
adduced any reasons for this.  The Committe e further notes with serious concern 
that against the sanctioned provision of Rs. 6035.70 Crore under the SDS, only 
Rs. 3744.00 crore have so far been released.  It is a matter of anguish for the 
Committee that such substantial variations have occurred bet ween the 
sanctioned provisions and the actual release and expenditure.  Obviously, these 
instances betray absence of a sound budgetary mechanism for assessing the 

 
 
25 
 
actual requirements of funds.  The Committee , therefore, recommends that the 
Ministry should address this issue squarely and devise a foolproof mechanism to 
overcome the deficiencies in the existing system of assessing the requirement of 
funds under the Swadesh Darshan Scheme. 
57. The Committee takes note of the remedial measures formulated by the  
Ministry for effective implementation of the projects sanctioned under the SDS 
and would like the Ministry to carry out an appraisal of their efficacy so that the 
avowed objective of developing world class infrastructure in the circuit 
destinations, promoting cultural and heritage value of the country and providing 
complete tourism experience with varied thematic circuits under the Scheme 
could be achieved. 
58. The Committee is of the view that though the Ministry reviews the 
progress of the projects const antly under the SDS, the absence of a dedicated 
monitoring mechanism at the State level is hampering the performance and goals 
of the projects sanctioned under it.  The Committee , therefore, recommends that 
the Ministry should , without further loss of time , take up the issue with the 
concerned State Governments at the highest level to understand their constraints 
in implementation of tourism projects and put in place a dedicated mechanism 
for implementation of the projects as it has a direct bearing on the timelines set 
for these projects.  Such a focused monitoring would go a long way in speeding 
up long pending SDS projects crucial for enhancing the tourist attractiveness and 
realising the tourism potential of India‘s assets. 
59. The Committee observes that rural tourism is among a few emerging areas 
of tourist interest world -wide.  Rural tourism is important because it helps in 
inclusive development of remote and backward areas by way of enhancing 
earning capacity and better livel ihood for the rural population.  As of now, two 
projects with a sanctioned outlay of Rs. 125.02 crore have been approved under 
Rural Circuit out of which only Rs. 21.24 Crore has been released for a project in

Kerala which is indicative of the snail‘s pace of progress in the matter.  The 

 
 
26

the State Government of Kerala , the handicaps in implementing the project 
could have been ironed out and the project could have kick started. 
60. The Committee recommends that for better promotion of rural  tourism, a 
cluster approach may be pursued rather than a stand -alone approach and more 
and more Rural Circuit P rojects may be sanctioned and implemented for 
promotion of niche tourism and more funds may be earmarked for rural circuits 
under the SDS. 
61. The Committee is dismayed to note that all 5 Buddhist Circuit Projects 
costing Rs. 355.26 Crore have witnessed ti me overruns.  The reasons attributed

view that had the Ministry completed pre -project activities  including clearance 
before sanction of these projects or before award of contract, persistent problem 
of time overrun could have been easily avoided.  Now that the Ministry has 
revised the guidelines of the scheme in 2017, the Committee expects that the 
sanctioned Buddhist Circuit Projects would be implemented expeditiously and 
the issues related to time overrun would be resolved.  The Committee desires to 
be apprised of the outcome in this regard.

127. The Ministry of Tourism provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) to the

128. The Committee has been informed that to harness the potential of cruise tourism in 
the country, a Task Force had been constituted with the Secretary (Tourism) as the 
Chairman and Secretary (Shipping) as the co -Chairman.  On the recommendations of 
Task Force, the following measures were taken for the development of cruise tourism:- 
 
 Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) have been formulated to be followed by the 
various agencies for handling cruise ships. 
 The facility of e-tourist visa has been extended to passengers arriving at the ports 
of Mumbai, Mormugao, Mangalore, Kochi and Chennai. 
 The Ministry of Home Affairs has waived off biometric requirements for cruise 
passengers arriving on e-via for a period of three year, i.e. upto 31.12.2020. 
 To address manpower, co-ordination and logistical issues for handling cruise 
vessels at ports, “Port Level Facilitation Committees” have been formed under the 
respective Chairman of the Major  Ports to facilitate seamless handling of cruise 
ships and passengers. 
 Foreign flag vessels carrying passengers have been allowed to call at Indian ports 
without obtaining licence from Director General of Shipping. 
On being asked as to why the Ministry has not utilized any other waterways, except 
for National Waterway 1 and Natio nal Waterway 2, for developing cruise circuits 
in the country and what are the difficulties faced by the Ministry in developing 
cruise tourism projects in And aman and Nicobar Islands, the Ministry has stated 
the following: 
The mandate of Inland Waterways A uthority of India (IWAI) is to develop 
and regulate National Waterways for cargo shipping & navigation.  
 
The infrastructure developed for transportation on National Waterways by 
IWAI including fairway, terminals, jetties, navigation aids etc. are used by 
the river cruise tourism operators. IWAI plays the role of a technical 
facilitator for river cruise tourism on National Waterways. 
 

 
 
51 
 
River cruise tourism has been a regular feature on National Waterway 
(NW)-1(Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hooghly river system) and NW -2 (river 
Brahmaputra) since several years. Various cruise operators viz., M/s 
Heritage River Cruises Pvt. Ltd., M/s Vivada Inland Waterways and M/s 
Assam Bengal Navigation Company operate their cruise vessels on NW -1, 
NW-2 and Sunderbans waterways. 
 
National Waterway (NW) -1 (Ganga - Bhagirathi-Hooghly river system 
from Allahabad to Haldia), NW – 2 (River Brahmaputra from Dhubri to 
Sadiya), NW -3 (West Coast Canal from Kottapuram to Kollam along with 
Udyogmandal and Champakara Canals) have already been develo ped with 
fairway navigational aids, jetties and terminals with mechanized equipment 
handling facilities for loading and unloading of cargo. 
 
In addition to NW -1 & NW -2; NW -3 Kerala is a world renowned tourist 
destination for domestic and foreign tourists w here house boat tourism with 
food and accommodation facilities are available. The area of operation of 
these boats include Vembnad lake of Alapuzha, Kuttanad area and 
Astamudi lake of Kollam area. 
 
129. The Committee notes from the information made availab le to it that in the meeting

132. The Committee observes that Cruise Shipping is one of the fastest growing 
components of the leisure industry world -wide.  The Indian economy is 
developing at a steady pace and became the fifth largest economy in 2019 
surpassing the U.K. and France (as per IMF‘s October 2019 World Economic 
Outlook).  India‘s middle class possessing disposal incomes is also growing in 
number. Despite all these advantages, cruise tourism in India is still in its nascent 
stage and India does not even figure in the top ten in Asia in terms of Cruise line 
port calls.  India has a mere 285 cruise calls in FY 2018 -19 whereas Thailand and 
Malaysia get about 500 cruise calls a year.  The fact remains that India‘s 
enormous cruise potential remains untapped despite being endowed with 7,500 
km of coastline.  To meet India‘s untapped cruise potential, a high level Task 
Force was constituted in the year 2015 with Secretary (Tourism) as Chairma n 
and Secretary (Shipping) as co -Chairman.  Five years have elapsed since then.  
The Task Force has reviewed and updated t he Standard Operating Procedure  
and a consultant has been jointly appointed by Ministry of Tourism and Ministry 
of Shipping for prepar ing an Action Plan and detailed blue print for the 
development of cruise tourism in the country.  It is really intriguing that despite 
acceptance of the report of the Consultant, no projects have been sanctioned for 

 
 
53 
 
development of cruise tourism to the States during 2017-18, 2018-19 and 2019-20.

for that to happen, stable and consistent operating conditions will have to be 
concretized because cruise line companies which ty pically plan two to three years 
ahead, need to know that the basic conditions of operating are stable and 
consistent.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism 
in collaboration with Ministry of Shipping should expedite appropriate policy 
measures for developing India‘s largely untapped and hitherto small cruise 
market and pull cruise vessels to its shores.  The Committee desires to be 
updated on the progress made in this regard. 
134. The Committee also observes that many of India‘s destinations are in its

be kept apprised of the steps taken and the achievement made towards 
developing river cruise in the country.

XI India Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) 
 
158. India Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) is a Public Sector Undertaking 
under administrative control of the Ministry of Tourism. Incorporated on October 1, 1966, 
ITDC played a key role in the development of tourism infrastructure in the country. The 
Corporation is running hotels, restaurants at various places for t ourists, besides providing 
transport facilities. In addition, the Corporation is engaged in production, distribution and 
sale of tourist publicity literature and providing entertainment and duty -free shopping 
facilities to the tourists. The Corporation als o has its presence in engineering related 
consultancy services. ITDC was set up with a view to undertake development and 
promotion of tourism related activities. ITDC has been mandated to promote tourism in 
the country in form of infrastructure development , hotels & accommodation, travel & 
tourism promotion, printing & publicity, event management, hospitality education, Sound 
and Light Shows and other related activities. It went ahead with a view to promote 
tourism by providing quality infrastructure in pla ces where the tourism potential was 
untapped due to non-availability of accommodation, travel arrangements, etc. 
 
159. The Corporation also takes up work of execution of Engineering Projects and 
preparation of DPRs for tourism related projects. ITDC has pl ayed a committed and 
pivotal role in the development of tourism infrastructure in backward areas, thereby trying 
to promote regional balance. After the disinvestment of 09 hotels and one incomplete 
hotel project, ITDC consolidated its remaining activities and restructured itself to take up 
diversified service -oriented business activities. As per Disinvestment Policy of the 
Government of India, 9 hotel properties including 3 Joint Venture Hotel properties (viz 
Hotel Lake View Ashok, Bhopal; Hotel Brahmaputra  Ashok, Guwahati, Hotel Bharatpur 
Ashok, Bharatpur, incomplete hotel project at Gulmarg, Hotel Janpath, New Delhi, Hotel 
Jaipur Ashok, Jaipur, Lalitha Mahal Palace Hotel, Mysore, Hotel Pataliputra Ashok, Patna 
and Hotel Donyi Polo Ashok, Itanagar) have bee n transferred/ handed over to the 
respective State Governments. Disinvestment/Divestment process of remaining properties 
i.e. Process for joint leasing in respect of Hotel Pondicherry Ashok, Puducherry; Hotel 
Nilachal Ashok, Puri, process for giving Hotel Kalinga Ashok, Bhubaneswar on 
Operation & Maintenance Contract is underway. Hotel operations at Hotel Ranchi Ashok, 
Ranchi and Hotel Nilachal Ashok Puri are closed. Incomplete Project at Anandur Sahib, 
Puri is under way to be transferred to State Government. 
 
160. With the changing economic and business scenario, the Corporation is in the 
process of strengthening all business verticals to increase its turnover and profitability and 
to position itself as one stop solution for travel, tourism and hospitality needs.  

 
 
64 
 
 
161. ITDC is striving to achieve the desired position by leveraging on inherent strength 
of Corporation as a well -known, established and trusted brand and by strengthening and 
enlarging other potential SBUs by adopting SBU specific strategy, incre asing customer 
base from B2G to B2C and  B2B, achieving customer delight and by offering value for 
money, quality services at par with best in industry.

CRUISE TOURISM 
 
 The Committee observes that Cruise Shipping is one of the fastest growing 
components of the leisure industry world -wide.  The Indian economy is 
developing at a steady pace and became the fifth largest economy in 2019 
surpassing the U.K. and France (as per IMF‘s October 2019 World Economic 
Outlook).  India‘s middle class possessing disposal incomes is also growing in 
number. Despite all these advantages, cruise tourism in India is still in its na scent 
stage and India does not even figure in the top ten in Asia in terms of Cruise line 
port calls.  India has a mere 285 cruise calls in FY 2018 -19 whereas Thailand and 
Malaysia get about 50 0 cruise calls a year.  T he fact remains that India‘s 
enormous cruise potential remains untapped despite being endowed with 7,500 
km of coastline.  To meet India‘s untapped cruise potential, a high level Task 
Force was constituted in the year 2015 with Secretary (Tourism) as Chairman 
and Secretary (Shipping) as co -Chairman.  Five years have elapsed since then.  
The Task Force has reviewed and updated the Standard Operating Procedure 
and a consultant has been jointly appointed by Ministry of Tourism and Ministry 
of Shipping for preparing an Action Plan and detailed blue  print for the 
development of cruise tourism in the country.  It is really intriguing that despite 
acceptance of the report of the Consultant, no projects have been sanctioned for 
development of cruise tourism to the States during 2017 -18, 2018-19 and 2019-20. 
This, in the opinion of the Committee, speaks volumes of the Government‘s 
inertia and lethargy in the matter.                             (Para:132) 
 Cruise Tourism is a supply -driven market and cruise lines are always in 
search of new markets and des tinations.  India‘s enchanting and exotic 
destinations could catapult India as one of the leading cruise destinations.  But 
for that to happen, stable and consistent operating conditions will have to be 
concretized because cruise line companies which typic ally plan two to three years 
ahead, need to know that the basic conditions of operating are stable and 
consistent.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism 
in collaboration with Ministry of Shipping should expedite appropriate pol icy 
measures for developing India‘s largely untapped and hitherto small cruise 

 
 
88 
 
market and pull cruise vessels to its shores.  The Committee desires to be 
updated on the progress made in this regard.               (Para:133) 
 The Committee also observes tha t many of India‘s destinations are in its 
interiors and water -based tourism is well -developed in some States like Kerala, 
Goa, West Bengal etc. However, a lot remains to be done to develop river cruise 
that connects interior tourist destinations. The Commi ttee, therefore, desires to 
be kept apprised of the steps taken and the achievement made towards 
developing river cruise in the country.                (Para:134)
