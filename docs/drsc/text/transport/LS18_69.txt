INTRODUCTION
 
            The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation and implementation of national policies and
 programmes in the civil aviation sector.  The Ministry oversees the development and regulation of civil aviation in the
 country including inter-alia establishment of new aerodromes, maintenance and upgradation of the existing aerodromes,
 regulation of carriage of traffic by air and ensuring civil aviation safety and security.  Railway Safety, including
 enquiries into serious railway accidents, also constitutes one of the allied functions of the Ministry.
 
2.         The civil aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organisations under the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation i.e. Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation Security.  The functions pertaining to
 Railway Safety are entrusted to a Commission.  The Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following Public Sector
 Undertakings/Companies/Autonomous Bodies under its administrative control:-
 
Air India Limited and its wholly owned subsidiaries viz. Hotel Corporation of India Limited and Air India Charters
 Limited.
Indian Airlines Limited and its wholly owned subsidiary viz. Airlines Allied Services Limited.
Airports Authority of India.

9.         The Committee brought to the notice of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that the approved outlay for 2001-02
 included a capital expenditure of Rs.150 crores on non-aircraft projects.  The anticipated expenditure in Performance
 Budget 2002-03 was Rs.90 crores.  However, the actual expenditure had been to the tune of Rs.50 crores only.  The
 reason for shortfall in expenditure mentions deferment of expenditure on certain schemes due to financial constraints
 faced by the company. Again the anticipated expenditure on the projects during 2002-03 has been stated to be Rs.67
 crores whereas the approved outlay was Rs.150 crores.  The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation
 whether the non-utilisation had not affected the performance of the company.
 
10.       In response, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Civil Aviation that in Performance Budget 2002-
03, the estimated expenditure on non-aircraft projects for Annual Plan 2001-02 was estimated at Rs.90 crores vis-a-vis
 estimate of Rs.150 crores due to deferment of expenditure due to financial constraints. In Performance Budget 2003-04,
 as against the estimated outlay of Rs.90 crores, the actual outlay for 2001-02 was stated at Rs.50 crores only due to
 deferment of planned expenditure on Ground Services Requirement, Computer & Communication Equipment,
 Engineering Workshop Equipment etc. due to financial constraints.
 
11.       The Capital Expenditure of Rs.67 crores for 2002-03 (RE) include Rs.32 crores for seat upgradation/DVD
 modernization programme as funds were prioritized for the project and deferment of Rs.107 crores of capital
 expenditure to 2003-04 under continuing schemes.
 
12.       The Committee was further informed that non-utilization of approved outlay has not affected the performance of
 the company in any manner.  The company monitors and incurs capital expenditure that is absolutely essential as a part
 of economy measures and considering the financial constraints, without compromising on the levels of services and
 safety.
 
13.       The Committee is not satisfied with the reply furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation for non-utilisation of
 plan outlay prescribed for non-aircraft projects of Air India.  The Committee feels that there is lack of proper planning
 in Air India, as a result of which the Company fails to make financial provision for the schemes envisaged by it.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that Air India should make a realistic assessment of its requirements and the ability
 of the company to make financial provisions for the same.

18.       The Committee noted that the Performance Budget 2002-03 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation projected a loss of
 Rs.98.25 crores for Indian Airlines for 2002-03.  However, the Performance Budget 2003-04 indicated an estimated
 loss of Rs.417.80 crores.  The Committee enquired about the reasons for the huge difference in the projected and the
 estimated loss of the Company.
 
19.       In response, the Committee was informed that the Performance Budget 2002-03 was formulated sometime
 around January 2002 and it was expected that the effect of 11 September 2001 incidents in USA would start tapering
 off in the year 2002-03.  These expectations, however, did not materialize and there was increase in input costs also
 mainly in the ATF prices. At the time of Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) exercise in
 October/November, loss for the year 2002-03 was, therefore, projected to be Rs.417.80 crores.  The revised estimates
 of the Company for the year 2002-03 have since been approved by the Board of Directors.   As per the revised
 estimates, the loss for the year 2002-03 is likely to be Rs.245.50 crores.   The revised estimates are formulated in the
 month of March and reflect a close to actual assessment of the profitability for the year.

 Ministry of Finance and others have raised some objections.  Now, we hope that we would be able to satisfy them.  One

31.       The Committee notes that the fleet expansion programme of Indian Airlines has been held up for the last one
 year since the company was rostered for disinvestment.  The Committee is aware that Shipping Corporation of India,
 Mumbai, a Public Sector undertaking under the Ministry of Shipping, which is also proposed to be disinvested, has
 placed orders for acquiring 9 vessels in October 2002.  The Committee fails to understand as to why there are two sets
 of rules for the Public Sector Undertakings of the Government of India.  On the one hand Indian Airlines is not being
 allowed to go in for fleet expansion since it is rostered for disinvestment whereas, on the other hand, another Public
 Sector Undertaking, namely Shipping Corporation of India, is under no such constraints.   The Committee had
 highlighted in its 59
th Report on Demands for Grants 2002-03 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that the Government’s

NON-UTILISATION OF ILS CAT-IIIA AT IGI AIRPORT, DELHI
 
38.       During the course of an earlier interaction with the Committee the Ministry of Civil Aviation had informed that
 as part of the ongoing modernisation and development of airports, state-of-the-art Instrument Landing System (ILS)
 CAT IIIA had been installed at Indira Gandhi International Airport, New Delhi to enable the operation of flights and
 ensuring least inconvenience to the passengers due to extreme fog.  All directional, location and runway vacated signs
 associated with CAT IIIA operations had also been installed at the airport.  However, it was noted that even afterwards
 the system was not being utilised and the flights were delayed/diverted to other locations during foggy situations at
 Delhi.  In reply to a related Unstarred Parliament Question, the Ministry had replied that the system was not being
 utilised since the pilots had not been trained to use the system.  The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation as to why pilots had not been trained so as to ensure proper utilisation of the system.
 
39.       In response, the Committee has been informed that in order to help pilots while landing in bad weather, Air India
 and Indian Airlines are training Pilots in accordance with civil aviation requirements to utilize ILS CAT-II/CAT-IIIA
 System.
 
40.       In case of Indian Airlines, only Airbus A-320 aircraft has been approved by the manufacturers to undertake ILS
 CAT-II and CAT-IIIA Operations.  Efforts are made to schedule CAT-II/CAT-IIIA cleared pilots for flights depending
 early morning/arriving in the late evenings during winter months.  Central Training Establishment (CTE), Hyderabad is
 imparting training to the Pilots on ILS CAT-II and CAT-IIIA on A-320 aircraft and all the Pilots are programmed in
 batches for this training.  Effort is being made to train all the eligible pilots gradually for CAT-II and then CAT-IIIA
 Operations.

RATIONALISATION OF SALES TAX ON AIR TURBINE FUEL
 
53.       Fuel is the largest component of airline cost.  The domestic air transport industry in India has been facing serious
 problems due to high cost of operations for which one of the major reasons is the heavy cost of Air Turbine Fuel
 (ATF).  Even though the pricing of ATF is now on import parity basis, the rates applicable for domestic operations
 continue to be significantly higher than that of international operations.  The cost of ATF goes up even further due to
 high rates of sales tax levied by the State Governments/Union Territory administration varying from 20 per cent to 36
 per cent which affects the viability of domestic airline operations.  The Committee observed that both Indian Airlines
 and Air India had cited the high cost of ATF as one of the major factors for increase in expenditure.  During the course
 of his evidence, Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation commented as under:-
 
“…. So far, we have not got the relief which we wanted to have with regard to the ATF.  Apart from the high price
 charged by the oil companies, there are two components.  One is the excise duty which is charged by the Government
 of India and the other is the sales tax, levied by various States.  In Kerala, in spite of the fact that sales tax on ATF has
 been reduced from 39 per cent to 25 per cent, a large amount on account of sales tax is incurred by the airlines…. ”
 
54.       The Committee was also informed by the Ministry of Civil Aviation that the Minister of Civil Aviation had
 written to all the Chief Ministers to consider an appropriate reduction in the rate of sales tax on ATF in the states. 
 Further, the Committee was informed that State Government of Andhra Pradesh has reduced the sales tax on ATF to 4
 per cent and the State Government of Karnataka had committed to reduce the same. 
 
55.       Fuel plays a crucial role in the cost structure and profitability of any airline.  The Committee is of the view that
 the high cost of ATF for domestic air transport increases the cost of operation and makes it unviable even in areas
 where it has comparative advantage over other modes of transport.  The Committee appreciates the initiative taken by
 the State Government of Andhra Pradesh in reducing the rate of sales tax on ATF to 4 per cent.  The Committee hopes
 that this step of the Government of Andhra Pradesh would encourage the other State Governments to follow suit.  The
 Committee recommends that Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress upon the State Governments to come forward
 and reduce the sales tax on AFT to the minimum level, preferably 4 per cent.
 
56.       The Committee has also been informed that some of the states are of the view that there is not much by way of
 airport infrastructure or flight availability in their states and hence, they would not like to lose revenue by reducing rate
 of sales tax on ATF.  In this context, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take up the
 matter with the State Governments with respect to the requirements of air connectivity etc. for their states and try to
 evolve a solution whereby decision on all these problems could be taken as a package deal.

SCHEDULE OF VARIOUS DOMESTIC AIRLINES
 
70.       The Committee also drew the attention of the Ministry of Civil Aviation to the fact there are a number of flights,
 both Indian Airlines and private airlines, operating between different stations, both in the morning as well as in the
 evening.  Also, there is very little difference in the schedule of Indian Airlines and the private airlines.  However, there
 were not many flights during the intervening period of the day.  Many Members of the Committee also stated that there
 was a general impression that prime time flights on various sectors are being given to private operators where Indian
 Airlines could have operated its own flights and cited instances in support of their apprehension.  The Committee then
 enquired about the procedure followed for approval of schedule of various domestic airlines. 
 
71.       In reply the Ministry of Civil Aviation informed that in accordance with the International practice, DGCA is
 approving two flight schedules in a year, namely, winter schedule effective from Monday of the last week of October
 and summer schedule effective from Monday of last week of March.  All domestic scheduled operators file their
 proposed flight schedule in the prescribed application form 45 days before the commencement of the schedule.  After
 receipt of the proposed flight schedule from all operators, a combined airport movement report in respect of all
 operators from all airports is generated within a week at DGCA. It is ensured by DGCA that flight schedules of the
 operators meet the Route Dispersal Guidelines of Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The combined airport movement report is
 forwarded to various concerned agencies like Airports Authority of India, Air Headquarters, Naval Headquarters, HAL,
 Bangalore, Cochin International Airport Ltd for time slot approval and also to Bureau of Civil Aviation Security for
 security clearance.  The combined airport movement report is also forwarded to all scheduled operators.  
 

69th Report
file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/69threport.htm[28-08-2018 13:04:26]
72.       Airports Authority of India convenes a meeting about 3 weeks before the commencement of the schedule with
 all operators and all concerned agencies to discuss the flight schedule and allocation of time slots desired by the
 operators.  A revised airport movement report is then generated by DGCA incorporating all the changes in the time
 slots and sent to all concerned agencies for their approval.  The flight schedules of all operators are finally approved by
 DGCA based on the approvals received from various concerned agencies 15 days before the commencement of the
 schedule. 
 
73.       The schedule is the primary product attribute in the airline business.   The inherent nature of domestic market is
 such that demand, on most routes, tends to be concentrated around peak periods, and all airlines, therefore, tend to fix
 their timings during this peak period in order to be able to attract maximum number of passengers.   For this reason
 flight timing of airlines tend to be close to each other.   For example, on major metros mornings and evenings are the
 preferred time slots for all airlines operating on the route.  The process of approval of the schedules has to be such as to
 avoid allegations of lack of transparency and motivated fixation of timings.
 
74.       Although Ministry of Civil Aviation have stated in their reply that the process of approval of the schedules has to
 be such as to avoid allegations of lack of transparency and motivated fixation of timings, the Committee is constrained
 to note that prime timings are being given to the private airlines and Indian Airlines operates its flights at odd hours
 which not only results in inconvenience to the passengers but also most of the passengers load is diverted towards
 private airlines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that this issue should be seriously looked into and flight
 timings of Indian Airlines flights should be rescheduled for better utilisation of its seat capacity.  The Committee also
 recommends that there should be coordination between the Indian Airlines and the private airlines at the time of
 finalisation of their schedule so that there is a staggering of the multiple flights existing at peak hours which would
 result in improved air services to the passengers during the non-peak hours also.

 that many State Governments are willing to contribute to the development of such airports.   The Committee
 recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take initiative in this regard and get the opinion of the State
 Governments as to whether such airfields can be run with their contribution, in the respective States.  The Committee
 feels that the development of such airfields will certainly pave the way for providing inter-state air connectivity in those
 states. 
(Para 69)
 
SCHEDULE OF VARIOUS DOMESTIC AIRLINES
 
Although Ministry of Civil Aviation have stated in their reply that the process of approval of the schedules has to be
 such as to avoid allegations of lack of transparency and motivated fixation of timings, the Committee is constrained to
 note that prime timings are being given to the private airlines and Indian Airlines operates its flights at odd hours which
 not only results in inconvenience to the passengers but also most of the passengers load is diverted towards private
 airlines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that this issue should be seriously looked into and flight timings of
 Indian Airlines flights should be rescheduled for better utilisation of its seat capacity.  The Committee also recommends
 that there should be coordination between the Indian Airlines and the private airlines at the time of finalisation of their
 schedule so that there is a staggering of the multiple flights existing at peak hours which would result in improved air
 services to the passengers during the non-peak hours also.
