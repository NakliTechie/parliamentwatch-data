REPORT
The Ministry of Culture is responsible for the preservation and conservation of the country's
rich cultural heritage and promotion of art and culture. The Ministry functions through two attached
offices, six subordinate offices and thirty five autonomous organisations. There are seven Zonal
Cultural Centres for promoting folk and traditional arts of different regions of the country. There are
also four National Missions viz., National Mission for Manuscripts, National Mission for Monuments
and Antiquities, National Mission on Libraries and Gandhi Heritage Sites Mission.
2. The vision of the Ministry has been to protect the cultural diversity of the country and its
heritage. The mission of the Ministry is to implement sustainable solution through which India's
diverse tangible and intangible culture and ancient heritage will remain universally acceptable.
3. The mandate of the Ministry is to preserve, promote & disseminate all forms of Art & Culture
through (i) Maintenance & Conservation of Heritage, Historical Sites & Ancient Monuments; (ii)
Promotion of Literary, Visual and Performing Arts through various organizations; (iii) Promotion of
Institutional & Individual Non-official Initiatives in Art & Culture; (iv) Administration of Museums to
ensure Preservation, Conservation and to facilitate Public Access to Heritage; (v) Administration of
Public Libraries; (vi) Maintenance of Manuscript Records and their digitization; (vii) Promotion of
Institutions & Organisations of Buddhist & Tibetan Studies; (viii) Preservation of Gandhian Heritage
Promotion of Research and Awareness in field of Art & Culture; (ix) Promotion of Research in
Anthropology & Ethnology; (x) Ensuring Equitable Access to Cultural Goods & Services for benefit
of all Members of the Community; (xi) Promotion of Cultural Industries including Traditional Artisanal
Enterprises; (xii) Catalysing Partnerships at every level to ensure Sustainable Development &
Management of Cultural Resources; (xiii) Promotion of International Cultural Relations; and (xiv)
Observation of Centenaries and Anniversaries of Important National Personalities & Events.
4. The Ministry is the custodian of all the major libraries in the country. It extends grant-in-aid
for library development and is also responsible for all policy matters regarding library development.
Through National Archives of India, the Ministry is responsible for maintenance of all archival records
of the country. The Ministry is involved in protection and promotion of Buddhist and Tibetan Culture
through various institutions located at Sarnath, Varanasi and Leh.
5. The Ministry has a capacity-building programme for all those who wish to excel in the field
of art and culture. Institutions like the School of Archaeology, School of Archives, National Museum
Institute, the Asiatic Society, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Institute of Asian Studies are mentionable in
this regard. Various courses offered by National School of Drama and Kalakshetra Foundation are also
some of the examples of capacity building programmes of the Ministry.
6. The Ministry marks its international presence by way of organising Festivals of India abroad
and is also responsible for implementation of various UNESCO Conventions in the field of culture and
for inscribing world heritage sites.

Spending pattern during the Twelfth Plan
17. As per the data furnished by the Ministry, during the first year of the 12th Five Year Plan, as
against Rs. 864.00 crore approved by the Planning Commission, the Ministry had incurred an
expenditure of Rs. 803.79 crore during the year 2012-13, which is about 93.03% of RE 2012-13.
18. During the year 2013-14, (the 2nd year of the 12th Five Year Plan - 2012-17, a sum of
Rs. 1537.00 crore was allocated for various developmental activities/programmes of the Ministry of
Culture, as against the outlay of Rs. 864.00 crore for the Annual Plan 2012-13. This plan allocation
included Rs. 102.00 crore (Rs. 100.00 crore for INTACH as corpus fund and Rs. 2.00 crore for
Kedarnath Temple) obtained in the 1st Batch of Supplementary Demands for Grants- 2013-14. The
Plan allocation showed an increase of 77.89% in for the Annual Plan 2013-14 over the year 2012-
13. The Annual Plan allocation was reduced to Rs. 1470.00 crore at the Revised Estimates stage
against which an expenditure of Rs. 1380.13 crore (93.89%) was incurred.
19. During the year 2014-15, an amount of Rs. 1835.00 crore was allocated to the Ministry for
the Annual Plan 2014-15, as against Rs. 1537.00 crore allocated for the year 2013-14. The increase
in plan allocation in 2014-15 was about 19.39% over the year 2013-14, against which expenditure
of Rs. 1285.84 crore (85.72%) as on 09/03/2015 has been incurred.
20. The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry was able to spend more than 90%
of its allocation during the first three years of the Twelfth Plan. At the same time, the
Committee observes that better planning and supervision could have definitely improved the
pace of utilization.

Ministry's Projected Demand vis-a-vis Budgetary allocation
28. The Ministry of Culture has stated that the Ministry had projected demand of an outlay of
Rs. 2217.56 crore for the Annual Plan 2015-16 on the basis of various activities/schemes proposed
to be taken up by the cultural organizations/divisions under the Ministry. The Ministry of Finance has
allocated a sum of Rs. 1455.00 crore for developmental activities of the Ministry of Culture for
Annual Plan 2015-16, thereby resulting in a substantial reduction vis-a-vis the projections(Rs. 762.56
crore) on account of resource constraints. The Plan allocation for 2015-16 pegged at Rs. 1455.00
crore is about 65.61% of the outlay proposed by the Ministry of Culture for the year 2015-16.
Ministry of Finance has not intimated any reason for this reduction in Annual Plan allocation for the
year 2015-16.
29. The Ministry has stated that reduced allocation would adversely impact on almost all sectors/
organizations/schemes of the Ministry including Archaeological Survey of India, Museums, Libraries,
Scheme of Art & Culture and Centenary Celebrations, etc. Ministry of Culture has, accordingly,
earmarked Plan funds among various cultural organizations/schemes keeping in view their minimum
requirements and overall Plan funds allocated by Ministry of Finance.
30. The financial year 2014-15, Ministry of Culture had achieved more than 50% of the total Plan
allocation. The Ministry has stated that the expenditure level was slowed down to 64% during the
period October - December, 2014 due to the proposed reduction in the allocation at the Revised
Estimates stage. As against RE 2014-15 of Rs. 1500.00 crore, Ministry of Culture has incurred Plan
expenditure of Rs. 1292.37 crore (as on 15/03/2015) during the year 2014-15 which is about 86.16
% of RE 2014-15.
31. The Committee is at a loss to note that allocations had been reduced in almost all
sectors, which has adversely impacted Archaeology, Museums, Libraries and other important
Organizations under the Ministry. Culture imparts important identity to a nation. In the
development paradigm, culture plays a crucial role in the life of a nation. Therefore,
preserving, protecting and promoting our rich cultural heritage assume greater significance,
for which the Ministry must get adequate allocation. The Committee is of the firm view that
with the existing scale of allocation, even the existing schemes and activities of the Ministry
will be difficult to run. Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry
should actively pursue the matter of increasing the allocation at the RE stage in the current
financial year, with the Ministry of Finance.

Issues taken up/pending with Ministry of Finance
35. The Ministry of Culture has informed the Committee that the Ministry has taken up the issue
regarding five World Heritage Sites declared by Minister of Finance in his Budget Speech (2015), with
Ministry of Finance. Besides, a monument  viz.,  Qutub Shahi Tombs, Hyderbad (Telangana) is on the
tentative World Heritage List. ASI is responsible for the restoration, conservation, preservation and
supportive infrastructure work for these protected monuments/sites. In addition, ASI proposed to
implement a project entitled Adarsh Smarak in which 25 ASI protected monuments will be converted
into model monuments with providing all the modern facilities including audio visual guides. Ministry
of Culture has also pointed out the requirement of additional Plan funds for implementing the Dandi
Projects (Memorial and Heritage Path) announced by the Hon'ble PM on the anniversary of Dandi
March. Accordingly, Ministry of Finance has been requested to consider an additional allocation of
Rs.60.00 crore (Rs.35.00 crore for ASI protected World Heritage Sites and Rs.25.00 crore for Adarsh
Smarak Project) and Rs.60.00 crore for Dandi Memorial project during 2015-16. The Ministry has
also stated that the response in this regard is awaited from the Ministry of Finance.

36. The Committee notes that the sums demanded by the Ministry of Culture are required
for not only existing schemes, but also for new ones declared mid way by the Prime Minister
as well as Finance Minister. The Committee, therefore, strongly endorses the request put

reduced allocation for vital Schemes of the Ministry would adversely affect the core activities

Savings/under-utilizations during 2014-15
37. As against total budgetary provision of Rs.2511.00 crore at BE 2014-15, it was reduced to
Rs. 2159.00 crore at RE stage 2014-15 on account of massive cut imposed by the Ministry of
Finance under Plan and Non-Plan heads. The Ministry has incurred an expenditure of Rs.1880.82
crore upto February, 2015 as against RE of Rs. 2159.00 crore during the current financial year 2015-
16. The Ministry has informed that the total expenditure level upto February, 2015 is about 87.12%
of RE 2014-15.
38. The Committee notes that as per the details furnished by the Ministry, savings during
2013-14 was due to varied reasons such as non-filling up of vacant posts, delay in completion
of works, postponement of some programmes etc.
39. The Committee impresses upon the Ministry that every possible step should be taken
to ensure that under- utilization of funds does not occur. The Committee is of the view that
periodic monitoring by the Ministry, of the progress of schemes/programmes would help in
identifying the reasons for under-utilization at an early stage and in taking remedial action.
Further, the Committee stresses upon the need for strict monitoring and supervision of the
utilization of the grants given to the Organisations under the Ministry.
40. Filling vacant posts should also be expedited or outsourcing some activities may also be
considered.
41. The Committee could understand from the Outcome Budget that under the 'Grants-in-aid' Head
of various organizations, significant amounts were surrendered to Ministry of Finance due to receipt
of less number of eligible applications.
42. The Committee may be apprised of the reasons for the failure in properly assessing the
prospective number of applications and why such a pattern is repeated. The Committee also
desires that the Ministry may take serious note of this and also inform the Committee about
the steps taken to reduce the Savings.

58. The major activities of the Archaeological Survey of India are:
1. Survey of archaeological remains and excavations;
2. Maintenance and conservation of centrally protected monuments, sites and remains;

4. Architectural survey of temples and secular buildings;
5. Establishment, Maintenance and running of Archaeological museums;
6. Development of epigraphical research and numismatic studies;
7. Expeditions abroad;
8. Training in Archaeology; and

Encroachment of Monuments
69. The Committee appreciates the significant role played by ASI in protecting the monuments and
heritage sites of our country. It is an undisputed fact that the encroachment of ASI protected
monuments can be prevented only by better co-ordination with and co-operation from State
Governments.

Secretaries of all State Governments/ Union Territories requesting formation of State level Co-

Government has recently constituted State level Co-ordination Committee.
72. In addition to the 3685 Centrally protected monuments, there are large number of monuments
of archaeological value under the control of the State Governments.
73. The Ministry, in its reply furnished to the Committee, has stated that the monuments under the
jurisdiction of respective States are taken care of by the Archaeology Departments of the concerned
State. The budgetary allocation is made for conservation/preservation of the State protected
monuments by the concerned State Governments. Any technical help requested by the State

13
Governments for conservation work of the monuments is provided by the Archaeological Survey of
India. In some cases where the request is received for conservation work of State protected
monuments on Deposit Work basis, the ASI takes up necessary execution of the work on deposit of
the funds by the State Governments.
74. The Committee emphasizes the need for greater co-ordination between ASI, State
Governments and the local authorities in the matter of prevention and removal of
encroachments at our monuments. The Committee impresses upon the Ministry to
aggressively pursue the matter of setting up the State level Co-ordination Committees, with
the remaining State Governments.
75. The Committee also urges that the field offices of ASI should pursue such cases of
encroachment persistently with the District Authorities on urgent basis and chalk a way out
in such cases. The Committee also underlines the need for adequate follow up of the cases
filed in Court of law against the encroachers.
76. As a preventive measure, the Committee likes the Ministry to consider the idea of
having a Central Force specially for protecting/ guarding our cultural heritage sites/
monuments on the lines of CISF and BSF etc. It can be' utilized both for Central and State
sites.
77. The Committee is unhappy to note that even though ASI issued 1950 demolition orders during
the last 4 years, only 32 such orders were executed. The Ministry's reply states that the
implementation of the demolition orders issued under Section 38(1) and 38 (2) of the Ancient
Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act by the Director General of ASI is a
responsibility of the District Administration.
78. The Committee understands the difficulties faced by ASI in this regard. The Committee is also
aware that there are a number of Court cases against the demolition orders issued by ASI.
79. In this regard, the Committee is of the view that ASI can fulfill its mandate only when
it has an adequate statutory backing to tackle encroachments. The Committee notes that at
present, ASI does not have inherent powers to execute the demolition orders issued by it.
Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that adequate powers should be granted to
ASI under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958 which
would equip ASI to execute its demolition orders. The Committee is also of the considered
view that the Act should stipulate the action that can be taken against the District Authorities
in case of laxity or non-co-operation on their part in executing the demolition orders.
80. The Committee also recommends that a Task Force may be constituted in the States
wherein encroachment of monuments situated in communally sensitive areas are to be
removed. The specialized Task Force can co-ordinate with the District Authorities and the ASI
to remove the encroachers.
81. The Committee feels that keeping in view the humanitarian aspect of encroachments,
creation of a separate fund for removing of illegal encroachment & for rehabilitation would
ensure that the monument is protected and also that the encroachers are suitably
rehabilitated, without adversely affecting their lives. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its
earlier recommendation that a separate fund is needed for removing of illegal encroachments
and for rehabilitation.

OBSERV ATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE — AT A GLANCE
Assessment of the Demands for Grants (2015-16) of the Ministry
The Committee notes that the present Budget Estimates shows an increase of just Rs.10
crores in comparison to the Revised Estimate last year i.e., 2014-15. The Committee is also
surprised to note that the Ministry had to face a sharp budgetary cut at RE stage in the last
financial year. The Committee failed to get a convincing reason for this cut. Given the
importance of the mandate of this Ministry i.e., to preserve, protect and promote our vast
cultural heritage, both tangible and intangible, spread across the length and breadth of the
country, the Committee is at a loss to see that Plan allocation to this Ministry has come down
from Rs.1835 crore in BE 2014-15 to Rs.1500 crore in RE 2014-15 and to Rs.1455 crore in BE
of 2015-16. Such allocation are highly insufficient and the Committee recommends that Plan
allocation for the Department be increased adequately at RE stage. (Para 13)
Twelfth Five Year Plan
The Committee notes that against the proposed outlay of Rs.18608.94 crore, only
Rs.7275.00 crore were earmarked for the Twelfth Five Year Plan. Due to this severe
downsizing, many important Schemes mentioned above, could not be taken up by the Ministry.
Since three years of the plan period have been over, the Committee recommends that for the
remaining two years, the Plan allocation for the Ministry of Culture should be hiked so that
important schemes/projects should not suffer. The Committee also would like to know the
rationale for abandoning the three schemes in the Museum Sector by the Ministry along with
an impact-assessment Report. (Para 16)
Spending pattern during the Twelfth Plan
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry was able to spend more than 90%
of its allocation during the first three years of the Twelfth Plan. At the same time, the
Committee observes that better planning and supervision could have definitely improved the
pace of utilization. (Para 20)
2015-16

funds among the Schemes subsumed within the group, when the need arises. The Committee
hopes that this financial arrangement would impart more flexibility to the Ministry in terms

that due care and caution should be exercised to ensure that the new scheme of allocation
does not affect the progress of existing schemes/ programmes of the Ministry and also the
