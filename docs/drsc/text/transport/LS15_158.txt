REPORT  
  Civil Aviation sector is one of the major sectors  which have seen remarkable growth in the 
recent past. The economic melt down and increased A TF , prices have had some negative impact on 
civil aviation during the last year, but the sector  was able to regain some of its lost ground. India has 
become the ninth largest civil aviation market in t he world as well as major aviation hub in the Asia 
Pacific market, which is having 647 million air passengers this year. The country is expecting to reach 
500 million passengers in 2030 and in the coming th ree to five years it will reach the target of 200 
million passengers. Due to the open sky policy and liberalization the number of aircraft has increased  
manifold in recent years. As such the civil aviation sector has become a much happening one. 
2.  The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible f or the formulation of national policies and 
programmes for the development and regulation of ci vil aviation in the country and for devising and 
implementing schemes for orderly growth and expansi on of civil air transport. Its functions also 
extend to overseeing the provision of airport facil ities, air traffic services, carriage of passengers  and 
goods by air, safeguarding civil aviation operation s, regulation of air transport services, licensing of 
aerodromes, air carriers, pilots and aircraft maintenance engineers. In addition to framing policies, the 
Ministry provides guidance to the organizations in the implementation of policy guidelines. 
3.  The Ministry of Civil Aviation has the followin g organizations under its administrative control:– 
(i) Attached/Subordinate Organizations

300ER) and 1 Airbus (A-321) aircraft were projected  to be received during the year. Further, as per 
the approval of the Annual Plan 2010-11, the outlay  for the year has been projected at Rs. 5634.80

that out of the total 43 aircraft to be received from Airbus Industries, 42 aircraft will be received up to 
31st March, 2010 and the remaining 1 aircraft shall be received in 2010-11 thus completing the Airbus 
aircraft project.

GPS AIDED GEO AUGMENTED NAVIGATION (GAGAN) SYSTEM  
32.   The Committee was informed that GAGAN is an a ugmentation system to enhance the accuracy 
and integrity of GPS signals to meet precision approach requirements in Civil Aviation. The project is 
being jointly implemented by Airports Authority of India and Indian Space Research Organization 
(ISRO). GAGAN overlay covers from Africa to Austral ia. It can also be extended to neighbouring 
‘countries. Technology demonstration successfully c arried out and system likely to be fully 
operational by June, 2013. GAGAN would provide prec ision approach and landing guidance up to 
APV 1.5 leading to CAT-I to aircraft hitherto not a vailable due to terrain conditions precluding 
provision of ILS. A provision of Rs. 205.00 crores has been made during 2010-11 for GAGAN.

SCHEMES  UNDERTAKEN  IN  NORTH  EASTERN  REGION 
  The Committee feels that the airports in North-Ea stern Region need more attention from 
the AAI. Development of airports in Guwahati, Dibru garh and Teju should be given due 
consideration for improvement of facilities at these airports. (Para 46)  
PAWAN  HANS  HELICOPTERS  LIMITED 
  The Committee observes that Pawan Hans Helicopter  Limited has performed reasonably 
well in the last year without having additional man  power and capacity addition. The fleet 
serviceability has improved from 66% in 2006-07 to 83% in 2009-10. The operating profit has 
remarkably increased from mere Rs.2.78 crore in 200 6-07 to Rs. 34.00 crore in 2009-10. The 
Committee appreciates the performance of Pawan Hans  Helicopters Limited despite the fact 
that the overall performance of helicopter industry  has not been good. The Committee hopes 

that PHHL will keep up with this performance and wi ll not be complacent in future. The 
Committee expects a major role from the reliable he licopter service provider during the 
Commonwealth Games 2010, Delhi for a better connectivity to tourists and business community 
and also for emergency/disaster management. The Com mittee is of the opinion that PHHL 
should lend their hands in promotion of unexplored tourist destinations by expanding their 
commercial operations to such places in consultation with AAI. 
(Para 51)  
HOTEL CORPORATION OF INDIA LIMITED 
  The Committee feels that the handing over of Cent aur Lake View Hotel, Srinagar and 
Chefair Flight Catering, Mumbai to professionals on  Management Contract will strengthen the 
infrastructure and performance of these units. The Committee is of the opinion that Centaur 
Lake View Hotel, Srinagar requires much needed face lift and renovation to attract more 
domestic and international tourists to the valley. The Government has offered certain benefits to 
hospitality sector in connection with the Commonwea lth Games 2010. The Committee 
recommends that Government should explore the possi bility of extending some financial 
assistance to HCI for the renovation of Centaur Hotel, Delhi Airport.   
 
INDIRA  GANDHI  RASHTRIYA  URAN AKADEMI 
  The Committee feels that IGRUA being the pioneer pilot training institute plays a major 
role in the country’s requirement of pilots. The Co mmittee hopes that handing over its 
management to professionals has some positive effec ts on its performance. The Committee is of 
the opinion that after the modernization, IGRUA should be in a position to meet the shortage of 
trained manpower in the aviation industry adequately. (Para 62)  
DIRECTORATE  GENERAL  OF  CIVIL  AVIATION 
  The Committee notes that against Rs. 2 crore plan  outlay in 2008-09, the DGCA has been 
allocated Rs.86 crores in 2010-11. This manifold in crease in the allocation is in keeping with the 
need for strengthening of this aviation regulatory body in the country. The Committee hopes 
that the proposed Safety Programme will be develope d as per the schedule, so that airlines and 
airports follow the uniform safety programmes serio usly. The Committee hopes that long 
pending construction of new building for DGCA will get its final shape as shortcomings in 
infrastructure should not make the regulator handicapped in performance of its designated role. 
The Committee is constrained to note that a number of critical posts were kept vacant for years 
together. The Committee is of the view that fiscal restrictions should not be imposed 
mechanically and operational requirement of the pos t must be considered before deciding to 
abolish or keeping the post vacant. The Committee f urther feels that recruitment for filling up 
the newly created posts will be completed as per sc hedule to ease the pressure on the existing 
staff. The Committee has decided to examine the who le gamut of issue relating to DGCA 
including its authority, manpower, etc. separately. (Para 67)  
BUREAU  OF  CIVIL  AVIATION  SECURITY 
  The Committee finds that there is a major increas e in allocation of funds to BCAS under 
the plan head which clearly indicates the role of B CAS to play in the coming years. The 
Committee hopes that issues revolving around the construction of its headquarters building will 

be sorted out at the earliest. The Committee is of the opinion that introduction of biometric 
passes will strengthen the security apparatus of th e airports and will restrict the entry of 
unauthorized persons in sensitive areas.    
AERO  CLUB  OF  INDIA 
  The Committee finds that enhanced Grants-in-aid t o Aero Club of India, will be expended 
judiciously as strengthening of this institution wi ll meet the demands of qualified pilots and 
maintenance engineers for aviation industry. (Para 74)  
REGULARIZATION  OF  CASUAL  EMPLOYEES 
  It was brought to the notice of the Committee that in Jammu & Kashmir there are certain 
categories of casual employees working with NACIL a nd its predecessor Indian Airlines, Hotel 
Corporation of India and Airports Authority of India for the last so many years. They are not given the 
benefits of regular employees. They worked with the  organizations for decades together braving all 
odds in their career. The Committee, therefore, recommends that these emp loyees who worked 
with Indian Airlines/NACIL, Hotel Corporation of India and Airports Authority of India for so 
many years braving all odds may be regularized at the earliest. (Para 75)  
ROLE  OF  CIVIL  AVIATION  IN  PROMOTION  OF  TOURISM 
  The Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry  of Civil Aviation can play an anchor 
role in promoting such unexplored tourist places wi th the coordination of State governments, 
tourism department and local bodies by providing re quisite airport infrastructure. Places in 
North East and Leh region could be easily accessibl e by air rather than a taxing road journey. 
The Committee feels that the Ministry should strengthen the airport infrastructure and enhance 
air services to these areas. The Committee came to know about the hardship faced by 
government employees in getting tickets under L T80  scheme for air travel using the Leave 
Travel Concession. Limited availability of seats ha s made getting confirmed tickets under this 
scheme a hard proposal. The Committee feels that NA CIL should take steps to increase the 
number of seats under the category to alleviate the present hardship of getting tickets under the 
L T80 scheme. The Committee is of the opinion that special tourist packages for air travel 
should be made available region-wise to cater the needs of air travelers.   
 
GENERAL  OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS 
  The Committee on scrutinizing of budget papers of  Ministry of Civil Aviation finds that 
the over all performance of the Ministry during the  last year has been almost satisfactory. The 
Committee feels that strengthening of aviation infrastructure, inculcating corporate governance 
in NACIL, promotion of helicopter tourism, strength ening and restructuring of DGCA and 
BCAS, empowering AAI and providing it a level playi ng field in development and 
modernization of airports, etc. are some of the major areas where the Ministry has to take some 
initiatives. Shortage of pilots, Air Traffic Contro llers, Aerodrome Inspectors, Helicopter 
inspectors is also major cause of concern. The Comm ittee feels that unless the Ministry 
addresses these issues with a sense of urgency, it cannot keep the present momentum of the 
industry for long. The Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry will take all the necessary 
steps to meet shortage of man power so that the mis match in demand and supply in aviation 

market is addressed success fully. The Committee further hopes that the Ministry will take more 
steps to make air travel an affordable, reliable and safe one. 
(Para 77)
