 REPORT
 
            The Department of Culture is the custodian of India’s rich cultural heritage.  In a broader perspective, the
 Department addresses issues relating to national identity in conjunction with several other Ministries and Departments
 such as Tourism, Education, Textiles and External Affairs.  The Department is responsible for promoting art and culture,
 mutual understanding and goodwill and to foster closer relations with foreign countries. Preservation of ancient and
 historic monuments and records, exploration and excavation of archaeological sites and remains, maintenance and
 expansion of libraries, museums of national importance and observance of centenaries and anniversaries of eminent
 personalities are among its other responsibilities.
 
2.         The prime objectives of the Department, i.e. promotion, preservation and conservation of art and culture have been
 implemented under 16 schemes through funding of 26 autonomous organizations and well developed infrastructure of 2
 attached and 6 subordinate offices.
 
3.         The activities and programmes undertaken by the Department of Culture can be broadly classified under the
 following heads:-
 
A.            Archaeology
B.            Museums
C.            Anthropology and Ethnology
D.            Archives & Archival Libraries
E.            Tibetan/Buddhist Studies
F.            Libraries
G.            Akademies & National School of Drama
H.            Promotion and Dissemination of Culture
I.            Memorials
J.            Centenaries & Anniversaries
K.        Cultural Relations & Festival of India
L.            Secretariat
M.        North East Region
 
THE APPROACH IN THE 10
TH PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2003-04
 
4.         The 10
th Plan has commenced from April 2002.  In the context of 10th Five Year Plan culture has been redefined as
 a vehicle for providing employment opportunities and to spur economic growth apart from strengthening its role as an
 expression of the creative urges of the people.  The focus has also been laid for preservation of Culture at the grass root
 level; implementation of a comprehensive plan for preservation of archeological heritage and development of the
 monument complexes and museums.  The action plan can broadly be summarized as under: -
 (i)         Efforts are to be made for the preservation of the country's archaeological heritage and development of monument
 complexes and museums, besides the archival heritage. Efforts will also be made to promote classical, folk and

10.       The Committee observes that for the 10 th plan an amount of Rs.1720 crores inclusive of Rs.180 crores for Capital
 Component has been allocated for plan expenditure of the Department of Culture.  Out of which only Rs.250 crores had
 been allocated during the first year of the 10
th Plan i.e. 2002-03 and an amount of Rs.250 crores has been allocated in B.E.
 2003-04 as the plan outlay of Department of Culture against the projected requirement of Rs.399 crores.  The Committee
 is constrained to note that the share of the Department in terms of total plan allocation of the Government is only 0.2 per
 cent, which is abysmally low.  The Committee finds that the plan outlay approved by the Government of India is on lower
 side in comparison to the amount proposed by the Department of Culture for 2003-04.  The Committee feels that the
 Government has not paid adequate attention to this Sector. The Committee disapproves the low pace of allocation of
 outlay to the Department of Culture out of the total approved allocation outlay, i.e. of Rs.1720 crores for the plan
 expenditure to be made during the 10
th five year plan.  The Committee feels that any reduction in the outlay would have
 serious implications in the development activities of the Department.  The Committee, recommends that in future the
 Department should be allocated with more funds in order to enable them to fulfill the targets and the Government of India
 should address the financial requirements of the Department of Culture more realistically and allocate the amount more
 judiciously while sanctioning the plan outlay for the remaining periods of the 10
th Plan.
 
REDUCTION OF CAPITAL WORKS ALLOCATION OF THE DEPARTMENT OF CULTURE AT R.E. STAGE BY
 MINISTRY OF URBAN DEVELOPMENT
 
11.       The Committee observed that amount for capital works allocation of the Department of Culture was reduced from
 Rs.45 crores to Rs.25.51 crores at R.E. stage during the year 2002-03 and desired to know the reasons for such reduction. 
 The Department of Culture informed that the Budget Estimates of the Department of Culture for the year 2002- 03 was
 Rs.250.00 crores under Plan. Out of this a sum of Rs.45.00 crores was earmarked for Capital Works and the Capital
 Works Outlay of the Department of Culture is at present being shown in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Urban
 Development and Poverty Alleviation. This money is spent by the CPWD on the projects of the Department of the Culture
 without taking them into confidence. However, the control over the funds and the pace of progress of the projects (both
 physical and financial) remains with the CPWD due to which progress in number of projects has been tardy e.g. Bhasha
 Bhavan and National Gallery of Modern Art.  The Ministry of Urban Development unilaterally reduced this amount from
 Rs.45.00 crores to Rs.25.51 crores during 2002-03 at R.E. stage.  The Capital Works allocation for the year 2003-04 is
 Rs.24.50 crores.  No money has been allocated for the completion of Bhasha Bhawan Project. The Department has
 requested the Ministry of Urban Development and CPWD to revert back the money diverted in earlier years for
 completion of the project Bhasha Bhawan and National Gallery of Modern Art.  In this context the Secretary, Department
 of Culture has deposed before the Committee that:-

ARCHEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA 
 
15.       The ASI, a pioneer institute in the field of the Archeology oriented activities, was established in 1861.  The major
 activities of the ASI are as under:-
 
(i)
                  Maintenance, conservation and preservation of centrally protected monuments/sites and remains;
(ii)                Conducting archaeological explorations and excavations;
(iii)               Chemical preservation of monuments and antiquarian remains;
(iv)              Architectural survey of monuments;
(v)                Development of epigraphical research and publications;
(vi)              Setting up and re-organisation of site museums;
(vii)             Training in Archaeology;
(viii)           Bringing out archeological publications;
(ix)              Archeological expeditions outside India (Excavation & conservation);
(x)                Implementation of Antiquity & Art Treasure Act, 1972;
(xi)              Maintenance of archeological libraries.

18.       The Committee was informed that an increase of Rs.4 crores over the B.E. 2002-03 has been provided to ASI in the
 B.E. 2003-04. The funds have been provided for the creation of monument- specific cultural hubs for the purpose of
 enhancing tourist potential which would also help in generating employment and contribute to poverty alleviation.  The
 funds will also be utilized for conservation and development of World Heritage Monuments and development of protected
 Forts and annual repairs of other monuments and sites.
 
19.       The Committee was also informed by the Secretary that the ASI generates a annual revenue collection of Rs.30
 crores by way of charging admission fee from the visitors from 124 ticketed monuments.  On the observations of the
 Committee that the entire receipt through tickets be given back to Department, the Ministry of Finance has enhanced
 budget provision by 50 per cent at the Revised Estimate stage during 2001-02.  The Ministry of Finance has been
 requested to permit the Archaeological Survey of India to utilize 100 per cent of the revenue collected through entry fee. 
 The Finance Ministry has released 75 per cent of the revenue to the Department of Culture at the Revised Estimate Stage
 during 2002-2003 for maintenance activities.
 
20.       During the course of evidence the Committee desired to know whether ASI proposes to raise number of
 monuments for ticketing purposes for generating revenue.  The Department of Culture informed that a proposal is being
 mooted to identify more monuments/sites to attract large number of tourists and to introduce entry fee.
 
21.       The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Finance has released 75 per cent of the revenue collected
 through entry fee to the Department of Culture at the revised estimate stage during 2002-03 for conservation and
 maintenance of monuments.  The Committee feels that execution of conservation and preservation works need an
 excellent expertise and timely funding of projects.  But with its meager resource, it was difficult for the ASI to protect and
 maintain all the 3616 monuments/sites.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that 100 per cent of the revenue collected
 from admission fees should be released to ASI for stepping up the activities and to augment resources of ASI besides
 coordinated efforts should be taken by the ASI to bring more monument under central protection and also raising the
 number of monuments for ticketing purpose to generate more revenue.
 
22.       As regards opening of three more circles and filling up of vacant posts under ASI, the Department of Culture
 informed that three new Circles under Archaeological Survey of India for the States of Uttaranchal, Jharkhand and
 Chhatisgarh have been created and the process of filling up of the posts and identification of staff for the same is in
 process.  The new Circles are likely to be made functional from 1
st April 2003.
 
23.       The Department of Culture also informed that the process of filling up of vacant posts in the Archaeological
 Survey of India is already in progress.  The vacancies could not be filled mainly because the Recruitment Rules had to be
 revised.  The rules have since been revised and notified.  All efforts are being made to fill up the vacant posts as per the
 prescribed procedures at the earliest.  Regarding fixing of time limit for filling up these vacant posts in ASI, the
 Committee was informed that it will take at least one year. 
 
24.       The Committee is happy to note that three new Circles in the States of Uttranchal, Jharkhand and Chattisgarh have
 been created under ASI and will be operational from April 2003 which will result in intensive preservation and
 maintenance of monuments in more sites.  The Committee is not satisfied with the efforts of the Department of Culture
 with regard to filling up of vacant posts in ASI and recommends that ASI should fill up these vacant posts with in a
 definite time frame, so that execution of Conservation, Preservation and Developmental work of monuments do not suffer
 for want of shortage of manpower.

MUSEUMS
 
The Committee feels that museums are major attractions for tourists, foreign students and scholars and is a link between
 culture and education.  The success of a museum or Art Gallery depends, to a large extent on its ability to project “Unity
 in Diversity’ in the cultural matrix of the nation.  This could be better achieved if an integrated approach has been adopted
 for linking art with science, craft with technology, man made treasures with natural resources and strengthening of basic
 functions of museums in collections, conservation, documentation, research, publication, presentation and education.  The
 Committee recommends the Department of Culture to strengthen and provide better infrastructure to improve the
 standards of museums in the country.  In this connection, the Committee observes that large-scale excavations have been
 undertaken at the sites of Dholavira and Hatab in Gujarat, Govishan, Kashipur in Uttaranchal, Sravasti in UP and Udaigiri
 in Orissa.  The Committee recommends that items found during excavation at Dholavira should be exhibited and
 conserved in a museum at the site itself.

 recommends that a plan funding should be done for the Zonal Cultural Centres to create additional art infrastructure
 facilities and improve the existing facilities by the Department of Culture besides setting up of uniform facilities across all
 the Zonal Cultural Centres so that activities of Zonal Cultural Centres should be carried out in accordance with the
 objective of promotion and dissemination of the arts of the respective zone.  The Committee also recommends that
 shilpgrams should be setup by all Zonal Cultural Centers for providing promotion and marketing facilities to artisans.
(Para 94)
 
SETTING UP OF MULTI PURPOSE CULTURAL COMPLEXES INCLUDING THOSE FOR CHILDREN
           
The Committee notes that the plan allocation during 2003-2004 has been increased for setting up of Multi Purpose
 Cultural Complexes for Children in coordination with autonomous body created by State Government but still it falls
 short of the requirement of Department of Culture.  As a result, the Department could not take up its proposal for revising
 the guidelines for multipurpose complex.  The Committee is of the view that these complexes will foster and coordinate
 activities in the State in the different cultural field such as music, drama, dance, literature, fine arts etc. and promote the
 cultural unity of the country.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government of India should provide
 sufficient outlay for timely completion of Multi Purpose Cultural Complexes and to enable the Department of Culture to
 take up the work of revision of guidelines of the scheme.
(Para 101)
 
NORTH EAST REGION
 
The Committee is happy to note that the Department of Culture has been able to utilize 100 per cent plan outlay allocated
 for North East Region during 2002-2003 to highlight and popularize the rich and diverse art and culture of North East
 India including Sikkim.  The Committee notes that a budgetary allocation of Rs.22 crores has been made during 2003-
2004 for various activities and programme of the Department of Culture in North East Region.  The Committee
 recommends that the Department of Culture should adopt proactive approach in initiating activities so that rich cultural
 wealth of North Eastern Region will come into limelight.
(Para 104)
 
 
 
COORDINATION BETWEEN THE DEPARTMENT OF TOURISM AND DEPARTMENT OF CULTURE 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Culture and Department of Tourism should develop some
 institutional mechanism with regard to the formulation of policies and projects for integrated development, conservation
 and preservations of monuments. Coordinated efforts should be taken by the Department of Culture in consultation with
 the Department of Tourism in order to ensure integrated development of clusters of monuments.
(Para 106)
