REPORT  
 
 
The Indian Shipping Sector, with about 212 ports (1 2 Major and 200 Minor ports)  
along its 7,517 km coastline, has strengthened subs tantially in the last two decades. Over the 
years, the development of other aspects of shipping , such as containerization, multi-modal 
transport services, advancement of marine engineeri ng technology etc. has enabled  
infrastructural improvement in the Indian shipping industry. Roads, rails, airports and seaports 
together with the optimum storage capacity to move large shipments have placed the industry in 
a very advantageous position. The Ministry of Ports , Shipping & Waterways has been entrusted 
with the responsibility of formulation and implemen tation of various policies and programmes  
on development of Major & Minor Ports and harbours,  maritime safety, training & skill 
development, human resource, finance etc. Approximately 95% of the country /uni201Fs trade by volume 
and 68% by value, is moved through maritime transport.

4. As is evident from the above Table, against the pro jected demand of Rs. 2542.43 crores, 
net allocation made for the Ministry of Ports, Ship ping and Waterways under Demand No. 78 in 
the Budget 2022-23 is Rs. 1709.50 crore (Rs. 1135.1 9 crore under Revenue head and Rs. 574.31 
crore under Capital head) and there is a shortfall of Rs. 832.93 crores. The BE 2022-23 provision 
is higher than the RE 2021-22 of Rs. 1585.37 crore but only slightly higher than BE 2021-22 of 
Rs. 1702.35 crore. 
5. The Ministry submitted that in respect of Sagarmala  Programme, against the projection 
of Rs. 500 Cr. For BE 2022-23, it has been allocate d funds of Rs. 412.79 Crore under Revenue 
Section for development and implementation of proje cts viz. port infrastructure projects, coastal 
berth projects, road & rail projects, fishery harbo urs, floating jetties, skill development projects, 
Coastal community development, International standa rd cruise terminal and unique and 
innovative projects such as Ro-Pax ferry services, National Maritime Heritage Museums. The 
allocation of Rs. 412.79 Cr. Includes the allocatio n of Rs. 52.789 Cr. For ALHW which was 
earlier a separate scheme with title „Development o f Minor Ports /uni201F and Rs. 50 cr. which was 
separately being shown under the head - „Other Sche mes under Sagarmala /uni201F. It further submitted 
that consequent upon recommendation of the Expendit ure Finance Committee (EFC) headed by 
Secretary (Expenditure), Ministry of Finance, the S cheme of „ Development of Minor Ports /uni201F 
under which expenditure is done by ALHW for develop ment of jetties in UTs of Andaman and 
Nicobar and Lakshadweep, has been merged with Sagarmala Scheme. 
6. The Committee notes a significant gap in the overall budgetary allocation of the Ministry in 
the Financial Year (FY) 2022-23 as compared to its projected requirements. Against the 
proposed demand for /Rupee/Rupee /Rupee/Rupee2542.43 Crores in B.E. 2022-23, only /Rupee/Rupee /Rupee/Rupee1709.50 Crores have been 
allocated, registering a shortfall of /Rupee/Rupee /Rupee/Rupee832.93 Crores i.e. 32.76% of the overall budgetary 
requirement of the Ministry for the FY 22-23, and i s certain to have an adverse bearing on 
the implementation of various port projects of the Ministry. The Committee observes that 
under several crucial heads such as Sagarmala Devel opment Company, Indian Maritime 
University, Kolkata Port Trust, Inland Water Transp ort etc., significantly lesser 
allocation/no allocation has been made against the projected demand of the Ministry. The 
Committee desires to be apprised of the different s chemes/ projects under its ambit which 
will be adversely affected because of the shortfall  in B.E. 2022-23 along with the course of 
action planned by the Ministry to deal with this sh ortfall. The Committee observes that the 
growth of India /uni201Fs maritime sector is of paramount importance and ne eds to be accelerated 
to strengthen our position of eminence in the globa l maritime sector.  Since the Ministry 
has to play a vital role in shaping India /uni201Fs maritime prowess into an 

8  
engine of economic development, availability of ade quate financial resources is a 
prerequisite towards propelling India to the forefr ont of the global maritime sector. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should ensure a good pace of 
utilization of allocated funds in initial quarters of FY 2022-23, deploy their resources in a 
more efficient way and request the Ministry of Fina nce to enhance budgetary allocation at 
the R.E. 2022-23 stage so that the various programm es and projects that the Ministry has 
planned for FY 22-23 can stay on course. 
7. The Committee notes that the Ministry had projected a total requirement of Rs.  690 crores 
under the broad head „Sagarmala /uni201F against which an allocation of Rs. 412.79 crores h as 
been made under the head. It is pertinent to mentio n here that this allocation of Rs. 412.79 
crore includes the allocation of Rs. 52.789 crores for ALHW, allocation for  which was 
demanded by the Ministry under a separate head. Thi s means that effectively, against  
projected  demand  of  Rs.  690  crores,  the  allo cation  made  under  the  head 
„Sagarmala /uni201F is merely 360 crores i.e., 52.17 percent of its projected requirement. The 
Committee is disappointed to note this huge mismatc h between the projected requirement 
of the Ministry and BE 2022-23 for this major progr amme and fails to understand how the 
Ministry would be able to make the progress it plan ned to make under this head given this 
significant shortfall. Sagarmala is the flagship programme of the Ministry to promote port- 
led development in the country and therefore, reduc ed allocation under the same is certain 
to have a direct impact on the pace of growth of th e maritime sector in the country. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry may approach the Ministry of 
Finance for higher allocation under this head at th e R.E. stage so that the progress 
envisaged by the Ministry under the Sagarmala Programme for the Financial Year 2022-23 
can be achieved as per targets. 
8. The Committee notes that against the projected dema nd of Rs. 128.59 crore (Rs. 
75.70 crore under the revenue account and Rs. 52.78 9 crores under the capital account) of 
the Ministry under the head – „Development of Minor  Ports /uni201F for FY 2022-23, an allocation 
of Rs. 52.79 crores has been made and the head - „D evelopment of Minor Ports /uni201F has been 
subsumed under the head – Sagarmala. However, the C ommittee finds no explanation for 
there being no budgetary allocation for the revenue  head „Development of Minor Ports /uni201F at 
BE 2022-23 stage, even though the Ministry had proj ected a demand of Rs. 75.70 crores for 
the same. The Committee is well aware that the priv ate ports have been more successful in 
attracting higher private investment because they a re perceived to be more business 
oriented, customer-friendly, cheaper and in general more efficient.        However, the minor 

9  
ports owned by State Governments or State Maritime Boards are facing stiff competition 
from the private minor ports which are on a differe nt growth trajectory due to superior 
infrastructure. The allocation of Rs. 52.789 crore under ALHW for Development of Minor 
ports against the proposed BE 2022-23 of Rs. 128.49 crore appears quite insufficient for the 
upgradation of infrastructure to face the challenge s from private players. The Committee, 
therefore, desires to be apprised of the reasons be hind no allocation under the revenue  
head of the former-head „Development of Minor Ports /uni201F at BE 2022-23 stage and how the 
Ministry plans to offset the impact of this shortfall.

39. Several policy measures have been taken to overcome  structural and organizational impediments 
to increase port production and productivity. Major Ports in India are moving towards a complete 
„Landlord Model /uni201F through PPP mode by strengthening institutional fr ameworks, model 
agreements and legislations to bring in the best te chnology and increase efficiency and 
productivity of the ports. Major steps taken for PPP enhancement in port sector are as follows: 
/uni25CF MPA Act 2021, enacted by the Parliament in February  2021, will provide more  
autonomy to ports and help them take faster decisio ns in infrastructure development and 
changing market conditions. 
/uni25CF TAMP /uni201Fs role for fixation of the tariff has been done awa y with in the new MPA Act, 
2021 as a tariff regulator and ports would be free to set their tariff based on market  
forces. The impact of the changes in Tariff fixatio n will be enormous in generating inter- 
port competition and empowering ports to take on ma rkets with nearby non major ports 
handling similar cargo. 
/uni25CF The MPA Act, 2021 also provides for formulation of an Adjudicatory Board to adjudicate 
disputes among Major Ports, PPP concessionaires and  captive use. This will have an 
enormous impact on overall maritime trade as focus would be on meeting the market 
demand and providing high level of services to the customers, leading to adoption of 
high-tech in overall port operations.

/uni25CF SAROD Ports: To take care of the pending or future disputes between the ports and the 
concessionaires, the Society for Affordable Redress al of Disputes (SAROD-Ports) has

Ports & Terminals Association (IPPTA) under the Soc ieties Registration Act, 1860. The 
Society has been registered in Delhi on 30.01.2020 and shall be functioning as per the 
Rules and Regulations framed and amended from time to time. The purpose and 
objectives of forming SAROD-Ports are:- 
(i) Resolution of disputes at affordable cost; 
(ii) Timely resolution of disputes in just and fair manner; and 
(iii) Enrichment of Dispute Resolution Mechanism with association of technical 
experts. 
/uni25CF Model Concession Agreement (MCA 2021): This has bee n notified after revising the 
existing MCA 2018, considering the change in policy  environment due to enactment of 
MPA Act 2021 and exploring provision of further fle xibility to respond to dynamic 
market and regulatory conditions. Many provisions h ave been provided or modified to 
cater to changing environment and develop a conduci ve atmosphere around PPP to boost 
investment. 
/uni25CF Tariff Policy (2021): New Tariff Guidelines, 2021, for framing of Scale of Rates for PPP 
projects under bidding stage and all future concess ion agreements to be entered by Major 
Ports  as  per  MPA  Act,  2021  has  been  notifie d  on  21 st    December,  2021.  PPP 
Concessionaires for projects after commencement of the MPA Act are free to fix the  
tariff based on market conditions and on such other  conditions as may be notified by the 
Government. MoPSW has also issued a Policy to the M ajor Ports Authorities on 3rd 
November, 2021 as a policy direction u/s 53 of the MPA Act 2021 for fixation of rates  
for assets and services offered by the Major Ports.  New Tariff Policy for Major Ports 
empowers the board of the ports and provides flexib ility to fix tariff for assets and 
services operated by Major Ports themselves as per the market conditions.

RESPONSE TO OIL SPILL HAZARDS 
45. Upon the Committee /uni201Fs query on oil spill hazards, the Ministry apprised  that a National Oil Spill 
Disaster Contingency Plan (NOSDCP) has been in plac e in order to prevent oil spill generated 
impacts on marine species and ecosystem. The NOSDCP  works with different stakeholders such 
as Ports, State Governments. The Indian Coast Guard  has been declared as National  
Coordinating Agency for coordination of oil spill r esponse action. Under NOSDCP, spills 
occurring within Notified Port areas are dealt by i ndividual ports, and in turn MoPSW is taking 
action to strengthen oil spill response capability of ports in the country. In this regard, the 
Ministry had already executed a scheme of 50% finan cial assistance for procurement of Tier-1 
Oil Spill Response Equipment to Oil handling Major Ports & Non -Major Ports which continued 
till December 2020. Most of the Oil handling ports are equipped with Tier -I Oil Spill Response 
Facility. 
46. Subsequently, the Ministry has come out with the co ncept of developing 5 Regional Oil  
Response Centers (ROSRC) along the coastline coveri ng all major and non-major ports. 
ROSRCs shall provide enhanced capability and bring domain expertise and professional 
competence required to deal with oil spills in shor test possible time and minimizing the Overall 
Impact on ecosystem. The Ministry also provided the following information: 
 
a) Annex 1 of the International Convention for the Pre vention of Pollution from Ships 
(MARPOL) convention to which India is party to, deals with Prevention of Pollution by Oil. The 
aforesaid convention besides mandating the construction and operational requirement for ships to 
prevent pollution by oil, also specifies the requir ements for emergency response in case of oil 
spill. Indian ships are surveyed at periodic interv als to ensure compliance with the requirement  
of the MARPOL Convention. 
 
b) Part XI-A of the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, and M erchant Shipping (Prevention of 
Pollution by Oil from Ships) Rules, 2010, deals with aspects related to prevention of oil pollution 
by ships. 
 
c) In addition, to discourage operation of sub-standar d vessels in Indian water, Flag State 
Inspections (FSI) and Port State Control (PSC) Insp ections of ships in Indian ports are carried  
out to ensure compliance of ships with applicable regulation for prevention of pollution by oil. 

27   
d) Merchant Shipping (Regulations of Entry of Ships in to Ports, Anchorages and Offshore 
Facilities) Rules, 2012 has provisions that require  older vessels to conform to standards of 
members of the Association of International Classif ication Societies, so that possibility of oil- 
spill from such vessel is minimized. 
 
e) National response mechanism to deal with oil spill incidents has been prepared (NOS-DCP). 
In addition, to deal with compensation matters in c ase of an oil-spill from certain types of oil, 
India has become a party to the International Civil  Liability Convention (CLC-92) and the 
International Fund Convention (known as 1992 Fund). 
 
47. The Committee appreciates that a specialized agency  viz., National Oil Spill Disaster 
Contingency Plan (NOSDCP) has been established to p revent oil spill, which coordinates 
with ports, state governments and the Indian Coast Guard and that the Ministry is 
implementing sufficient measures to prevent oil spi ll hazards and their impact on marine 
species. The Committee desires to know the financia l allocation provided to NASDCP and 
whether it is sufficient to provide for purchasing oil spill prevention equipments of 
international standards. The Committee would also like to be apprised of the recent oil spill 
incidents and whether the response of the NOSDCP wa s found to be adequate to address 
such disaster.

PERFORMANCE OF PORTS 
The Committee observes that the cargo handling capa city of Major Ports in the 
country has increased by about 79 percent between t he years 2014-15 and 2020-21 
(ignoring the incomplete data for the year 2021-22 since it is yet to be finalized), which is 
an annualized growth rate of about 10.2 percent. As  compared to this, the gross cargo 
handling capacity of Non-Major Ports has increased by only about 52 percent, with an 
annualized growth rate of just 7.2 percent. In term s of Traffic handled, however, Non- 
Major Ports registered a growth of about 30 percent  ( ∼∼ ∼∼6.7 percent annualized) between 
the years 2015-16 and 2019-20 (excluding the Covid affected year 2020-21 and ongoing 
year 2021-22) whereas Major Ports in the country on ly managed a minor increase of 
about 16 percent ( ∼∼ ∼∼3.8 percent annualized) during the same period. Thi s data points to 
the fact that while Major Ports across the country have managed to increase their 
capacity at a satisfactory pace, which is higher th an that of Non-Major Ports, the 
statistics pertaining to traffic handled at ports across the country do not portray a robust 
picture for the Major Ports in the country. While N on-Major Ports have managed to 
increase the volume of traffic handled at nearly th e same pace at which their capacity is 
increasing, Major Ports paint a contrasting picture  with annual growth in traffic 
handled at a mere 3.8 percent as against the capaci ty growth rate of about 10.2 percent. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry t o review the possible reasons 
behind the comparative stagnancy in the volume grow th of traffic handled at  Major 
Ports in the country. The Ministry may also come up  with an effective plan of action to 
further improve the growth rate of cargo handling c apacity of Non-Major Ports in the 
country. The Committee desires to be apprised of the action taken by the Ministry in this 
regard.
