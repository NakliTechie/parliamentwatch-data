AIR INDIA LIMITED
(Recommendation at Para 13)
 
5.         The Committee is not satisfied with the reply furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation for non-utilisation of
 plan outlay prescribed for non-aircraft projects of Air India.  The Committee feels that there is lack of proper planning
 in Air India, as a result of which the Company fails to make financial provision for the schemes envisaged by it.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that Air India should make a realistic assessment of its requirements and the ability
 of the company to make financial provisions for the same.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
6.         Air India has noted the recommendations of the Committee.  AI had provided Rs.150 crores as capital
 expenditure outlay towards non-aircraft project for the year 2002-03.  Considering the economy measures, capital
 expenditure was committed at Rs.67 crores without compromising on levels of service and safety.  The unspent amount
 has been carried forward to 2003-04 under Continuing Schemes of the Capital Budget of 2003-04.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
7.         The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry of Civil Aviation.
 
INDIAN AIRLINES LIMITED
(Recommendation at Para 21)
 
8.         The Committee is not convinced with the reply furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation for not being able to
 correctly estimate the loss of Indian Airlines during 2002-2003.  The Committee notes that the estimated loss of Indian
 Airlines was more than four times of the loss projected by the Company, which clearly indicates the inability of the
 Company to make a correct assessment of its financial performance.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 Indian Airlines should evolve a mechanism whereby they could make a realistic assessment of its financial performance
 keeping in view the prevalent market scenario and economic conditions.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
9.         The recommendation has been noted for compliance.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
10.       The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry of Civil Aviation, However, the Committee would like to know
 the steps taken by the Ministry of Civil Aviation/Indian Airlines to make a realistic assessment of its financial
 performance.
 
EXPANSION OF FLEET BY INDIAN AIRLINES/AIR INDIA
(Recommendation at Para 31)
 
11.       The Committee notes that the fleet expansion programme of Indian Airlines has been held up for the last one
 year since the Company was rostered for disinvestment.  The Committee is aware that Shipping Corporation of India,
 Mumbai, a Public Sector Undertaking under the Ministry of Shipping, which is also proposed to be disinvested, has
 placed orders for acquiring 9 vessels in October 2002.  The Committee fails to understand as to why there are two sets
 of rules for the Public Sector Undertakings of the Government of India.  On the one hand Indian Airlines is not being

 allowed to go in for fleet expansion since it is rostered for disinvestment whereas, on the other hand, another Public
 Sector Undertaking, namely Shipping Corporation of India, is under no such constraints.  The Committee had
 highlighted in its 59th Report on Demands for Grants 2002-03 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that the Government’s
 decision not to procure any new aircraft for Indian Airlines/Air India pending disinvestment of the two national carriers

 encroach upon the market share of Indian Airlines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that all necessary
 clearances/approvals for fleet expansion by Indian Airlines and Air India should be granted at the earliest.  The
 induction of new aircraft and the optimum use of the existing fleet by Indian Airlines would go a long way in
 increasing its market share, efficiency and profitability.

 improve its balance sheet by reducing huge expenditure on ageing fleet, Ministry of Civil Aviation approached the

 15.4.2003 approved the proposal of Ministry of Civil Aviation.  Accordingly the project is being processed further for
 approval of Competent Authority.

NON-UTILISATION OF ILS CAT-IIIA AT IGI AIRPORT, DELHI.
(Recommendation at Para 44)
 
20.       The Committee is constrained to note that the progress made with regard to the training of pilots for effectively
 utilizing the ILS CAT IIIA system is highly unsatisfactory in respect of both Indian Airlines and Air India.  The
 Committee wishes to express its anguish over the fact that after making huge investment for the installation of the
 modern technologies at the airports, the instruments remain lying idle for want of trained personnel.  Also, the
 operational capability of the system cannot be judged without effectively utilizing the system.  The Committee feels
 that the pilots should have been given training simultaneously at the time of the installation of the system.  The
 Committee hopes that the system does not become obsolete by the time our national carriers have trained personnel to
 utilize the system.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take some
 immediate steps so that the training of the pilots can be completed in a time bound manner and the modernized system
 installed at the India Gandhi International Airport could be put to optimum use, thereby reducing the hardships of the
 passengers during the foggy periods.

30.       The Committee is of the opinion that the dimensions of aviation security have changed significantly during the
 last few years.  The Committee feels that in view of the huge costs incurred towards the deployment of CISF at the
 airports, there is a need to have a re-look into the proposal.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation should explore the possibility of setting up of a dedicated Civil Aviation Security Force, under the
 administrative control of the Ministry.  This would not only be beneficial from the point of view of administrative
 control but also result in employment generation in the various parts of the country.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
31.       The issue regarding creation of a separate Aviation Security Force was under active consideration of the
 Government till December 1999.  In the wake of hijacking of Indian Airlines flight IC-814 on 24
th December, 1999, the
 Government of India decided to induct CISF at all operational airports in the country, after imparting necessary
 training.  CISF has already been deployed at 46 operational airports of the country and is being deployed at the
 remaining operational airports in a phased manner.
 
32.       Since CISF has been functioning quite satisfactorily and have gained sufficient experience in the field of
 aviation security; no separate civil aviation security force is proposed to be set up.
 
33.       While it is true that expenditure is being incurred on CISF it appears that the creation of a separate AVSEC force
 will be more expensive as in that case AAI will become totally responsible for the upkeep of the force.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
           
34.       The Committee notes the reply of Ministry of Civil Aviation.  However, the Committee feels that the question of
 cost incurred has to be studied as compared to the cost, which was earlier being incurred by the deployment of state
 police at the airports.  The Committee would like to revisit the entire question of deployment of CISF both in terms of
 cost as well as impact of the present salary and perquisites and their modalities.

39.       Fuel plays a crucial role in the cost structure and profitability of any airline.  The Committee is of the view that
 the high cost of ATF for domestic air transport increases the cost of operation and makes it unviable even in areas
 where it has comparative advantage over other modes of transport.  The Committee appreciates the initiative taken by

 that this step of the Government of Andhra Pradesh would encourage the other State Governments to follow suit.  The
 Committee recommends that Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress upon the State Governments to come forward
 and reduce the sales tax on ATF to the minimum level, preferably 4 per cent.

 the State Governments with respect to the requirements of air connectivity etc. for their states and try to evolve a
 solution whereby decision on all these problems could be taken as a package deal.

 States at Civil Aviation Minister’s level.  Representatives of Ministry of Civil Aviation/Indian Airlines/Private Airlines
 have also met Chief Ministers/Chief Secretaries of State Governments in this regard.  The difficulties being faced in
 providing air connectivity to various locations due to commercial unviability of operations on account of high ATF
 prices have also been impressed upon the State Governments.

DEVELOPMENT AND MAINTENANCE OF AIRPORTS BY AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA.
(Recommendation at Para 63)
 
43.       The Committee is not convinced with the replies furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation with regard to
 making investments at certain airports while withdrawing the flights to/from such stations.  The Committee is of the
 view that there is no coordination between the various executive wings of the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The
 Committee feels that while improving the infrastructure at the airports, there is a simultaneous need to provide air
 connectivity to such places, in absence of which the investments made forwards development of airports go waste.  The
 Committee also feels that Airports Authority of India is not taking due care of its assets as is evident from the incident
 regarding illegal sale of the land of the Airports Authority of India.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that there
 should be better coordination between the various agencies of the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The Committee also
 recommends that Airports Authority of India should evolve some mechanism to take better care of vast lands at its
 disposal.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
44.       Airports Authority of India has evolved major initiatives for protecting its land resources from encroachment and
 illegal occupation. Apart from identification of the lands belonging to AAI and classification of the land records in a
 scientific way, the Land management Department has been strengthened by posting senior officers at the international
 airports as well as the Regional Headquarters. This Department has completed identification of the land holdings
 including land encroachment at all the airports. The process of building of the land records is in progress by employing
 specialized personnel like Tehsildars, Patwaris, Revenue Officers, at respective unit level. AAI has also identified a
 total of 682 acres of land encroached at 20 airports and action has been initiated to remove these encroachments by
 taking up the matter with the State Governments. Action has also been initiated to protect airport lands from further
 encroachments and to provide security by construction of boundary walls, barbed wire fencing along the perimeter
 road, deploying Security Guards, etc. Such investments are unavoidable to protect the assets of AAI. Investments are
 also made for maintaining the facilities in operational status at operational airports irrespective of whether scheduled
 flights take place or not. Some airports are maintained in operational condition since casual operations take place at
 these airports. In addition, AAI also undertakes periodical maintenance of all operational airports, which include

 discharge their responsibilities in this regard.

DEVELOPMENT AND MAINTENANCE OF AIRPORTS BY AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA.
(Recommendation at Para 69)
 
49.       The Committee feels that in the present day context, there is a need of upgradation of small airfields located in
 various states such as Cooch Behar and Balaghat in West Bengal and Ajmer in Rajasthan.  The Committee also
 understands that many State Governments are willing to contribute to the development of such airports.  The
 Committee recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take initiative in this regard and get the opinion of
 the State Governments as to whether such airfields can be run with their contribution, in the respective States.  The
 Committee feels that the development of such airfields will certainly pave the way for providing inter-state air
 connectivity in those states.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
50.       In 1989, Airports Authority of India had written to various State Governments to take over the non-operational
 airports in the States for upkeep and maintenance and also for use by the respective State Governments. None of the
 State Governments excepting the Government of Maharashtra had responded to our request for takeover and
 maintenance of the airports. Only the Government of Maharashtra has responded and two airports namely Sholapur and
 Kohlapur have been leased out to the State Government of Maharashtra for a period of 15 years for upgradation,
 maintenance and upkeep of the facilities. None of the other State Governments have responded to the request of AAI
 for taking over these non-operational airports.

Balurghat and Cooch Behar airports in West Bengal
 
51.       The airstrip at Balurghat and Cooch Behar in West Bengal are suitable for operation of 20-seater type of aircraft.

 has no plan to construct an airport at Ajmer, as this airport will be a loss-making airport.

SCHEDULE OF VARIOUS DOMESTIC AIRLINES
(Recommendation at Para 74)
 
54.       Although Ministry of Civil Aviation have stated in their reply that the process of approval of the schedules has to
 be such as to avoid allegations of lack of transparency and motivated fixation of timings, the Committee is constrained
 to note that prime  timings are being given to the private airlines and Indian Airlines operates its flights at odd hours
 which not only results in inconvenience to the passengers but also most of the passengers load is diverted towards
 private airlines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that this issue should be seriously looked into and flight
 timings of Indian Airlines flights should be rescheduled for better utilization of its seat capacity.  The Committee also
 recommends that there should be coordination between the Indian Airlines and the private airlines at the time of
 finalisation of their schedule so that there is a staggering of the multiple flights existing at peak hours, which would
 result in improved air services to the passengers during the non-peak hours also.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
55.       Most of the flights of Indian Airlines have the historical time slots based
on the market requirements experienced over a period of time.  The private
airlines have been scheduling flights close to Indian Airlines timings.
 
56.       The schedule (flight timings) are the primary product attribute in the airline business.  The inherent nature of the
 domestic market is such that demand  on most domestic routes tends to be concentrated around peak periods, and all
 airlines therefore, tend to fix their timings during this peak period in order to be able to attract the maximum number of
 passengers.  For this reason, flight timings of airlines tend to be close to each other.  For example, on major metros,
 morning and evening time slots are preferred by all airlines.
 
57.       Further, flight timings depend on a number of factors like availability of aircraft crew and other resources,
 airfield characteristics etc. Indian Airlines accordingly schedules its flights taking into these factors and the deployment
 guidelines issued by DGCA.
