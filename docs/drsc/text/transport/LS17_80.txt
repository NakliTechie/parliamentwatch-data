                                                                                          R E P O R T
            The Ministry of Culture is the apex authority to preserve and protect India’s rich cultural heritage.  The Ministry of Culture
 undertakes activities that follow from the subjects allocated under the Government of India’s Allocation of Business Rules 1961, which
 include:-
                           i.         Maintenance and conservation of the country’s heritage ancient monuments and historic sites;
                         ii.         Promotion of literary, visual and performing arts;
                        iii.         Administration of libraries, museums and institutions of anthropology;
                       iv.         Maintenance, preservation and conservation of archival records and archival libraries;
                         v.         Research and development in the conservation of cultural property;
                       vi.         Observation of centenaries and anniversaries of important national personalities and events;
                      vii.         Promotion of institutions and organisations of Buddhist and Tibetan studies;
                    viii.         Promotion of institutional and individual initiatives in the field of art and culture; and
                       ix.         Entering into and implementation of cultural agreements with foreign countries.
 
2.         The functional spectrum of the Ministry is rather wide, ranging from generating cultural awareness at the grassroots level to
 promoting cultural exchanges at an international level.  Along with programmes for the preservation of India’s ancient heritage, the
 activities of the Ministry encourage and disseminate a variety of contemporary creative arts as well.  The aim of the Ministry is to
 develop ways and means through which the basic cultural and aesthetic sensibilities of the people remain active and dynamic.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA

8.         The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) was established in 1861.  It is a large organisation having its network throughout the
 country.  So far, ASI has declared 3648 monuments to be of national importance in the country.  The major activities of the ASI are
 maintenance, conservation and preservation of centrally protected monuments/sites and remains.  It also keeps developing epigraphical
 research, setting up and reorganisation of site museums and training in archaeology.  The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) has 21
 circles and 2 mini circles at present, whose jurisdiction are by and large co-terminus with the States.  With the creation of 3 new states
 viz. Uttaranchal, Jharkhand and Chattisgarh, three new circles are being opened with effect from 1
st April 2003.  The Ministry has also
 submitted that the Archaeological Survey of India is bringing more monuments under central protection.

10.        The Committee was informed that Archaeological Survey of India has been allocated Rs.70 crore for the year 2004-05 at B.E.

11.        The Ministry has informed about the policy regarding archaeological exploration and excavation.  It says that the provisions
 made in the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958, govern excavations in India.  The Director General
 of the Archaeological Survey of India  grants licenses to various excavation agencies, both government and autonomous, for conducting
 excavations on the recommendations of the Standing Committee of the Central Advisory Board of Archaeology (CABA).
            Criteria for selection of a site for excavation are:-
(i)
            bridging of missing gaps(s) in the cultural sequence of Indian archaeology;
 
(ii)
          throwing more light on lesser known culture; and
 
(iii)
         Salvage archaeology in the event of a site being submerged by a dam or being destroyed or damaged due to
 absolutely necessary development works, natural calamities.
 
12.        The archaeological Survey of India conducts excavations through six excavation branches located at Nagpur, Delhi, Patna,
 Bhubaneswar, Vadodara and Mysore besides the Institute of Archaeology, Prehistory Branch and 21 Circles.  An amount of Rs.7.5 crore
 has been allocated in B.E. 2004-05 under the head Archaeological Exploration and Excavations as against Rs.4.5crore allocated in B.E.
 for 2003-04.  The Ministry has informed that the increased funds of Rs.3 crore will be utilised for:-
                        i.         Carrying out multidisciplinary studies under the Sarasvati Heritage Project in respect of sites already undertaken for
 excavation;
 
                      ii.         Technical upgradation of Excavation Branches. Underwater Archaeology Wing, Building Survey Project, Epigraphy
 Branches, Prehistory Branch, Temple Survey Projects;
 
                     iii.         Carrying out simultaneous conservation of excavated remains:
                    iv.         Procuring satellite imageries; and
                      v.         Radio carbon dating various samples recovered from the excavation by Accelerator Mass Spectromepry Method now
 available at Institute of Physics, Bhubaneswar.
 
13.        The increased budgetary allocation will also be utilised for excavations and simultaneous conservation of the major excavated
 sites like (a) Dholavira (Gujarat), (b) Hatab (Gujarat), (c) Govishan, (d) Kashipur (Uttaranchal), (e) Sravasti (UP) and (f) Udaigiri
 (Orissa).
 
14.        The Underwater Archaeology Wing which has already been constituted in a nucleus form will also be expanded and suitably
 equipped/augmented.  Expeditions in collaboration with the Indian Navy have been taken up at Bangaram Island, Lakshadweep. The
 Ministry has informed that they have availed the expertise from various institutions like Indian Institutes of Technology; Central
 Building Research Institute, Roorkee; Geological Survey of India; Water and Power Consultancy  Services etc. from time to time and
 the help of Indian Navy has also been invited as and when required.
 
15.      The Committee is of the view that excavation is a highly sensitive matter and given a multi-religious, multi-ethnic context, the
 Archaeological Survey of India is playing a crucial role in strengthening our cultural harmony.  Therefore, the Committee feels that
 proper scientific consideration should be given in selection of excavation sites and no other extraneous factors should be given
 importance.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Archaeological Survey of India should also take into account other
 important researches being carried out by the universities and scholars of national eminence.  Before excavation the Archaeological
 Survey of India  should ensure that the site on which the work has to be started is well protected and during excavations the antiquities
 and other materials found remain secured.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry should play proactive role in preserving the

VACANT POSTS IN ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
 
17.        The Ministry has informed that at present 322 posts in various categories are lying vacant in the Archaeological Survey of India
 out of the sanctioned posts of 8376.  The Ministry has further added that for some of the posts interviews and selection to Group ‘A’
 posts are under active consideration of U.P.S.C.  The Ministry has informed that in various categories posts are lying vacant e.g. Sr.
 Archaeologist-10, Dy. Sr. Archaeologist-30, Asstt. Archaeologist-56, Asstt. Archaeological Chemist-56, Dy. S.A. Engineer-11,
 Conservation Asstt. Gr-I-14, Conservation Asstt. Gr-II-12, UDC & LDC-28 and also some in other categories. 
 
18.        The Committee is unable to understand as to why such a large number of posts have been kept vacant.  The Committee is of the
 view that keeping vacant posts in crucial cadres such as, Sr. Archaeologist, Dy. Sr. Archaeologist, Asstt. Archaeologist, Asstt.
 Archaeological Chemist, Dy. S.A. Engineer, Conservation Asstt. Gr-I, Conservation Asstt. Gr-II will certainly affect the functioning of
 the Archaeological Survey of India.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry should expedite the process of
 filling the vacant posts within a definite time frame so that the execution of Conservation, Preservation and Development work of
 monuments do not suffer due to lack of manpower.   The Committee should be apprised of the progress in the matter.
 
ORIGINAL WORKS AND CONSERVATION OF MONUMENTS UNDERTAKEN BY A.S.I.
 
19.        With the commencement of the 10
th plan period, perspective plans have been prepared by all the 21 circles & two mini-circles,
 science and horticulture branch of the ASI covering over 500 monuments.  The conservation problems of the monuments under the
 protection of Archaeological Survey of India are enormous and multifarious.  Conservation work needs expertise as well as timely
 funding. The necessary funding for conservation programmes are met out of various plans and projects like, Perspective Planning,
 National Culture Fund (NCF) and Loan funding by the Japan Bank of International Co-operation (JBIC).
 
20.        The Ministry has informed that during the 10 th Five Year Plan Period over 800 schemes in various monuments and the work on
 678 schemes has already been started.  Development of cultural and eco-tourism is emphasised upon by integrating it with monuments.  
 For this purpose, cultural hubs encompassing facilities for tourists viz. Interpretation Centres, public conveniences and modern ticket
 counters would be created keeping the prominent monuments as a focal developmental point and incorporating all minor monuments
 and sites in the near vicinity.  Certain cultural hubs thus identified are:-
                           i.         Red Fort and Humayun’s Tomb in Delhi.
                         ii.         Jaisalmer and Kumbhalgarh Fort in Rajasthan
                        iii.         Sun Temple, Modhera in Gujarat.
                       iv.         Khajuraho temples, Sanchi, Bhimbetaka rock-shelters in Madhya Pradesh.
                         v.         Taj Mahal and Fatehpur Sikri in Uttar Pradesh.
                       vi.         Udaigiri and Khandagiri caves in Orissa.
                      vii.         Ajanta and Ellora Caves in Maharashtra.
                    viii.         Hampi and Pattadakal temples in Karnataka.
                       ix.         Churches and Convents, in Goa.
                         x.         Mahabalipuram, Kanchipuram and Tanjavur temples in Tamil Nadu.
 
21.        Besides, 60 Jaina monuments, under protection of Archaeological Survey of India have been identified for the conservation and
 development of monuments under Jaina Deposit funds to the tune of Rs.1286 lakh for the celebration of 2600 th years of Tirthankara
 Mahaviras Janam Kalyanak.  The agreement for the Phase-II of the Ajanta-Ellora Conservation and Tourism Development Project with
 a loan outlay of 7331 Million Yen (Rs.300 crore) is finalised, the ASI's component being 901 Million Yen (Rs.36.77 crore).  Besides
 Ajanta and Ellora Caves, other monuments such as Daultabad Fort, Pitalkhora Caves, Patnadevi temple, Aurangabad Caves, Bibi-ka
 Maqbara, Lonar temples etc. are included in the Phase-II of the project.
 
22.      The Committee appreciates various ongoing projects for conservation and development of monuments undertaken by the
 Archaeological Survey of India as a result of which a large number of tourists are visiting these monuments. The Committee agrees to
 the view that conservation problems of the monuments are enormous and multifarious. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 restoration and renovation work of any monuments should be done as per the prescribed scientific norms and the Archaeological

PUBLICATIONS OF ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
23.        The Archaeological Survey of India ’s publications are being brought out under three categories i.e. Academic, Informative and
 Awareness. Under the Academic category, the series entitled Indian Archaeology- A review, which contains the reports on the various
 archaeological activities of a particular year not only of the Survey, but of all the institutions and departments concerned with
 archaeology as a specialized discipline, in the country has been taken up for publication.  The issues for the year 1998-99, 1999-2000
 and 2000-01 are in press.  The ASI has also taken up (1) Excavation Reports, (2) Epigraphical publications and reprints of out-of-stock. 
 Annual Reports and Inventory of Delhi Monuments for publication and these are at various stages of printing.  Under the informative
 series, the ASI has taken up publication of Guide Books and picture post cards on the World Heritage Monuments.  During the period
 under review Sanchi Guide book and picture post cards on Ajanta, Mahabalipuram,  Old Goa and Hampi were brought out in addition to
 the reports on Bhimbetka Rock Shelters.  Under the Cultural Awareness Programme, exhibitions workshops and seminars were
 organised in collaboration with INTACH and NGO’s.  The circle and branch offices of the Archaeological Survey of India also
 celebrated World Heritage Day/Week.
 
24.        The Committee expresses its displeasure over the fact that various reports on the archaeological activities are pending for almost
 last six years.  The Committee is of the view that in absence of timely publication of these reports, achievements of Archaeological
 Survey of India remains largely unattended and unnoticed by the public.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry
 should take steps to update its publication at the earliest.  The Committee also recommends the Ministry to get the publication of reports
 of various archaeological activities to be digitalised as in this form it can be preserved for longer period.

27.        Indian Museum, Kolkata is the oldest and largest institutions of its kind in the country.  It houses unique treasures of Indian and
 foreign art representing centuries of cultural ethos and traditions.  The museum has a vast repository of paintings, sculptures, bronzes,
 metals, coins textiles and decorative art.  It is felt that there is a need for strengthening and modernising security arrangements in the

28.        Museum Security is a major concern today since a museum is a repository of so many invaluable things which have high market
 price.  In view of recent thefts at some major museums in the country museum security has become a matter of great concern.  Giving
 the details of measures taken for the security of museums in the country, the Ministry informed that the security in National Museum,
 New Delhi and Salar Jung Museum, Hyderabad is being looked after by Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), while in Indian
 Museum, Kolkata and Allahabad Museum, Allahabad, the State Police is providing outer security and inner secuirty is being managed
 by the personnel engaged by Archaeological Survey of India.  The security situation of the museums is being reviewed and assessed
 from time to time and necessary instructions issued.  Advanced electronics devices have also been installed.  The heads of museums and
 galleries have recently been instructed to undertake comprehensive review of the security systems in their organization and prepare a
 plan of action to enhance the security and also to use the latest technology, wherever applicable.  During the current financial year, a
 provision of Rs.40.00 lakh has been made for the National Museum to acquire more electronic security gadgets.  Whereas, for other
 museums there is no such specific mention regarding allocation for security of museums.
 
29.        The Committee expresses its concern towards the prevailing scenario of museum security all over the country especially, the
 National Museum.   It is a matter of urgent need to have full proof security to guard the invaluable antiquities preserved at museums.

NATIONAL GALLERY OF MODERN ART
30.        National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA) was established for preservation of our contemporary art heritage, engaged in
 organising exhibitions in India and abroad, publications, acquisition of new works of art etc.  It has been implementing a number of
 schemes for strengthening its activities and spreading art education among the public.  It represents the evolution of changing art forms
 through the passage of time and is the only institution of its kind in India at par with its counterparts in other advanced countries.      
 The Ministry has informed that NGMA proposes to build a large extension comprising around 24,700 square meters of new museum
 space to its existing infrastructure.  This is going to be a state of the art museum comparable to any other international museum of the
 world with additional museum space academic block, preview theatre, restoration labs, storage space etc.  Construction work is likely to
 be executed during 2004-05.  It also proposes to make all galleries of the building of NGMA, Jaipur House, renovated and modernised
 to meet exacting international standards during the current year.  During the year 2004-05 NGMA proposes to organise 9 important and
 prestigious exhibitions of eminent at various places.  For all these development work of the Art Gallery, an outlay of Rs.30 crore was
 proposed, but only Rs.4 crore has been sanctioned.
 
31.        The Ministry has informed that Rs.4 crore which has been sanctioned for NGMA constitute the revenue part only.  The
 requirement of Rs.26 crore was mainly of capital nature for construction of additional block in New Delhi and for renovation of the
 building in Bangalore.  This requirement would be met out of an amount of Rs.45.50 crore kept for building projects under the capital
 outlay.
 
32.        The Committee is satisfied to note the development work undertaken by the National Gallery of Modern Art which is engaged in
 preservation of contemporary art heritage, organising exhibition in India and abroad, acquisition of new art works etc.  The Committee
 hopes that these development works will be completed as per the schedule.  The Committee recommends the National Gallery of
 Modern Art should hold such exhibitions of eminent artists at more places in the country to spread the art education among the public.

NATIONAL ARCHIVES OF INDIA
56.        National Archives was set up an Imperial Records Department in 1891 in Calcutta and shifted to Delhi in 1937 and renamed as
 National Archives of India after independence in 1947.  This archive is the custodian of non-current records of enduring value of
 Central Government Ministries/ Department/Offices and their predecessor bodies.  The main functions of National Archives of India
 include accession of public records for permanent preservation, care and conservation of records in its custody for posterity; preparation
 of reference media, dissemination of information; acquisition of microfilm copies of records of Indian interests from abroad; regulation
 of access to records for research and other purposes.  National Archives of India is the nodal agency for records management activities
 of the Central Government Ministries/Departments/Offices as also of PSUs.  Statutory Bodies, Corporations, Committees and
 commissions set up by the Union Government and ultimate transfer of records of enduring value to National Archives of India for
 permanent preservation and use by the posterity.  There are various ongoing schemes, viz. expansion of Records Management
 Programme, National Register of Private Records, Repair and Reprography, Administration and Library, the School of Archival Studies,
 setting up of record centre in the Eastern Zone etc.             Under the scheme of Financial Assistance for Preservation of
 Manuscripts/Rare Books, the archives provides financial assistance to voluntary organisations, educational institutions, libraries,
 museums and universities etc.  The National Archives of India has also set a target under various schemes for expansion and
 modernisation programmes.  An outlay of Rs.3 crore has been approved by the Planning Commission for the year 2004-05 against the
 proposed outlay of Rs.7.80 crore.
 
57.        The Committee appreciates the role of National Archives of India in the field of acquiring and conserving the records for
 posterity.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that for the year 2004-05 an amount of Rs.3.00 crore has been approved
 against the requirement of Rs.7.80 crore.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should allocate more funds to the
 National Archives of India to carry out its various schemes and projects in the current year and all ongoing schemes should be
 completed in a time bound manner.

TENTH PLAN OUTLAY AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2004-05
 
The Committee notes that the plan allocation of the Ministry of Culture has been increased substantially to Rs.400.00 crore.  The
 Committee hopes that the Ministry will ensure that the enhanced allocation will be utilised optimally with due emphasis on the new
 projects undertaken during the year viz., National Mission on Monuments, Intangible Heritage and Antiquities.
 
ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
 
The Committee is of the view that excavation is a highly sensitive matter and given a multi-religious, multi-ethnic context, the
 Archaeological Survey of India is playing a crucial role in strengthening our cultural harmony.  Therefore, the Committee feels that
 proper scientific consideration should be given in selection of excavation sites and no other extraneous factors should be given
 importance.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Archaeological Survey of India should also take into account other
 important researches being carried out by the universities and scholars of national eminence.  Before excavation the Archaeological
 Survey of India  should ensure that the site on which the work has to be started is well protected and during excavations the antiquities
 and other materials found remain secured.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry should play proactive role in preserving the
 excavation findings against natural forces, thefts and other related crimes as well as communal elements who try to incite public,
 thereby creating controversies around excavations sites.  Hence, the Committee emphasises that it is better to take preventive measures
 to check such incidents rather than taking corrective measures later on.
The Committee notes that the underwater archaeology is in a nascent stage.  However, the Ministry should also actively involve
 itself in the under water excavations going on at Dwarka by Indian Navy as they might not have necessary scientific expertise for
 proper archaeological interpretation.
(Paras 15 and 16)
 
 
VACANT POSTS IN ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
 
The Committee is unable to understand as to why such a large number of posts have been kept vacant.  The Committee is of the view
 that keeping vacant posts in crucial cadres such as, Sr. Archaeologist, Dy. Sr. Archaeologist, Asstt. Archaeologist, Asstt. Archaeological
 Chemist, Dy. S.A. Engineer, Conservation Asstt. Gr-I, Conservation Asstt. Gr-II will certainly affect the functioning of the
 Archaeological Survey of India.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry should expedite the process of filling
 the vacant posts within a definite time frame so that the execution of Conservation, Preservation and Development work of monuments
 do not suffer due to lack of manpower.   The Committee should be apprised of the progress in the matter.
(Para 18)
 
ORIGINAL WORKS AND CONSERVATION OF MONUMENTS UNDERTAKEN BY A.S.I.
 
The Committee appreciates various ongoing projects for conservation and development of monuments undertaken by the

 Archaeological Survey of India as a result of which a large number of tourists are visiting these monuments. The Committee agrees to
 the view that conservation problems of the monuments are enormous and multifarious. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 restoration and renovation work of any monuments should be done as per the prescribed scientific norms and the Archaeological
 Survey of India  should try to maintain the originality of the monuments/sites of national importance while renovating these
 monuments.  The Committee also notes the guidelines issued by the Hon’ble Supreme Court related to the renovation work carried out
 in the Red Fort, wherein, the Hon’ble Court had directed the Archaeological Survey of India  to consider the opinion of various experts
 in the field.  On the basis of the directions of the Court a Committee of Experts was also constituted by the Archaeological Survey of

 should also evolve institutionalised mechanism for eliciting opinion of independent experts in the field of archaeology.

PUBLICATIONS OF ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
 
The Committee expresses its displeasure over the fact that various reports on the archaeological activities are pending for almost
 last six years.  The Committee is of the view that in absence of timely publication of these reports, achievements of Archaeological
 Survey of India remains largely unattended and unnoticed by the public.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry
 should take steps to update its publication at the earliest.  The Committee also recommends the Ministry to get the publication of reports
 of various archaeological activities to be digitalised as in this form it can be preserved for longer period.
(Para 24)
 
MUSEUMS AND MUSEUM SECURITY
 
The Committee expresses its concern towards the prevailing scenario of museum security all over the country especially, the
 National Museum.   It is a matter of urgent need to have full proof security to guard the invaluable antiquities preserved at museums.
 The Committee, therefore, recommends to have modern security gadgets to strengthen the museum security besides training of security
 personnel to operate on these security gadgets.  The Committee further directs the Ministry to provide museums with adequate funds to
 strengthen their security as it has been observed that except for national museum, no specific allocation has been made for the security
 of museums.
(Para 29)
 
NATIONAL GALLERY OF MODERN ART
 
The Committee is satisfied to note the development work undertaken by the National Gallery of Modern Art which is engaged in
 preservation of contemporary art heritage, organising exhibition in India and abroad, acquisition of new art works etc.  The Committee
 hopes that these development works will be completed as per the schedule.  The Committee recommends the National Gallery of
 Modern Art should hold such exhibitions of eminent artists at more places in the country to spread the art education among the public.
(Para 32)
SCIENCE CITIES
 
The Committee is of the view that Science is the key to development of any nation and Science Cities can play an active role in
 popularising science in the country.  Therefore, the Committee is of the view that more and more Science Cities should be set up in the
 country.  The Committee is not happy with the pace of development of setting up of Science Cities of Kapurthala (Punjab) and
 Ahmedabad and would like to apprise itself about the reasons for delay in these projects.  The Committee also recommends that due
 priority should be given to Science City projects and the Ministry should actively co-ordinate with the Members of Parliament of
 respective states so as to ensure coordination and cooperation of local bodies.  The Committee directs the Ministry to generate interest
 among the private entrepreneurs since, they will be bearing major cost of these projects as per the norms prescribed by the
 Government.  
(Para 38)

 the ongoing schemes at R.E. stage.
