REPORT
Road and Highways is a critical infrastructure for economic development of a country. In
India, road infrastructure is utilized to transport over 60% of total goods and 85% of total
passenger traffic. Therefore, India has taken up ambitious programmes for developing National
Highways and other roads in a big way. Our focus is on developing roads of international standards
to create facilities for faster and smooth flow of traffic with enhanced safety features including
better riding surface, better road geometry, better traffic management, adequate signage, divided
carriageways, service roads, grade separators, over bridges and underpasses, bypasses and wayside
amenities as well as medical facilities.
2. The National Highways in India, although comprise only 1.7% of the entire road network,
carry about 40% of the total road traffic. India has one of the largest road networks consisting
of National Highways, Expressways, State Highways, Major District Roads, Other District Roads
and Village Roads.
3. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is responsible for the development and
maintenance of National Highways. Out of the total length of 79,116 km. of National Highways,
38,800 km. is with the State Public Works Departments (State PWDs), 30,046 km. with the
National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) and 2,905 with Border Roads Organization (BRO),
balance length of 7,365 km. is yet to be entrusted to the executing agencies.
4. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways primarily follows the agency system to
develop and maintain the National Highways. Besides, the State Governments, the Border Road
Organization and National Highways Authority of India, an autonomous organization under this
Ministry, act as agencies of the Central Government. The Ministry has undertaken the National
Highways Development Project which is being implemented in phases and envisages the
improvement of more than about 54,500 km of arterial routes of National Highways network to
international standards.

8. When asked about the performance of road sector during the year 2012-13, the Secretary,
Ministry of Road Transport and Highways informed the Committee that it was a difficult year for
the sector. The slide back in the GDP had a very direct impact on the road construction activity.
The market sentiment was extremely poor. There was serious distress in the market, both in terms
of equity and debt. The road building sector witnessed obstacles in terms of environment and
forest clearances. As a result, 13 projects bid out by NHAI did not receive any bid.
9. The Committee finds that funds under both plan and non-plan outlays have seen
substantial reduction at RE stage for the year 2012-13. The Committee is not convinced of
the reasons for such drastic reduction of funds especially under plan head from Rs.23000
crores at BE stage to Rs.17773.54 crores at RE stage. The Committee found that reduction
of funds at RE stage is happening every year due to one reason or the other. The
Committee feels that the Ministry should strive to utilize the allocated money. The
Committee also feels that major modification of allocation at the stage of revised estimate
should be avoided as it delays the implementation of sanctioned projects.

13. The Committee notes that the total GBS outlay provided during the Twelfth plan is
Rs.1,44,769 crore including central transport sector as against the projected requirement of
Rs.2,64,080 crore excluding central transport sector. The Committee feels that this
reduction in allocation will certainly deter the Ministry in taking-up the new projects/
schemes envisaged for the Twelfth plan period. Projects like connectivity to 50 minor ports,
connectivity to 24 airports and implementation of special packages for J&K and North-
Eastern Region have to be dropped by the Ministry for want of funds from the Government.
The Committee also notes that Rs.6,400 crore has been allocated against the requirement
of Rs.37,540 crore for works NH(O) for the entire plan period, which is unsustainable
considering the length of roads taken-up for improvement under this scheme. This reduced
allocation for the NH(O) will affect the NH (O) works adversely and ultimately result in
diversion of cess towards development of non-NHDP projects from NHDP projects. The
Committee is concerned to note that such diversion of cess meant for NHDP projects has
been taking place for the last three years which is alarming. The Committee feels that the
Ministry should take up the enhancement of GBS during mid-term plan review so that new
projects/schemes apart from the on-going ones do not suffer.

21. When it was asked about the NHAI not fully utilizing money raised through tax bonds in
last fiscal, it was informed that as regards the issuance of tax-free bonds authorized by
Government of India in 2012-13, NHAI had started the preparatory work towards this public issue/
private placement. But considering the pace of the expenditure incurred during financial year 2012-13,
it is noted that the anticipated expenditure levels for 2012-13 would not be achieved broadly due
to reasons stated under:
(a) Many projects awarded during 2010-11 and 2011-12 could not start due to financial
closure issues and environmental/forest clearance problems, etc. Hence, expenditure on
account of VGF, utility shifting for many of these projects was negligible.
(b) Against the original projection of 4000 kms award of phase-IV under EPC mode,
scheduled for the current year, no EPC projects could be awarded/started and hence
expenditure on construction under EPC was nil.
(c) Even for projects under execution, expenditure on VGF, utility shifting etc. due to land
acquisition issues was lesser than estimated as most of these projects started late due
to issue of environmental clearances.
22. In view of the above, NHAI has decided not to raise the funds through Tax free bonds in
the financial year 2012-13, thereby deferring the fund raising. Nevertheless, it needs to be taken
note of that these capital expenditure merely shifts to subsequent periods and a proper phasing of
fund raising will be necessary to ensure that cost thereof remains within acceptable limits.

27. When it was asked about the measures being taken to retain/attract bidders for highway
projects, it was informed that though the response so far is not encouraging all possible options
were being explored by the Government to bring back investor’s confidence/interest. The
Government is regularly interacting with all the stakeholders including contractors, concessionaires,
banks and other financial institutions to understand the risk which they are apprehending in the
projects, to work out the strategies to minimize those risk. The issue of non-availability of equity
with promoters has been already taken-up with the Ministry of Finance with a suggestion to
remedy this and to ensure that the equity-capital deficit does not adversely affect the health of the
road sector. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has already moved a Cabinet Note
proposing amendment to the provision in the Concession Agreement by which complete divestment
by the existing developers will be possible.
28. As far as the amendment in policy guidelines by Reserve Bank of India is concerned, the
RBI after deliberations at various levels vide their circular dated the 18
th March, 2013 have advised
all scheduled commercial banks that in the case of PPP projects, the debts due to the lenders may
be considered as secured to the extent assured by the project authorities in terms of the Concession
Agreement. With this revision in policy, loans given to the road sector by the scheduled commercial
banks to the extent of the value of debt due will be classified as secured. Earlier, these were being
classified as unsecured. Due to this amendment, the overall availability of finances will increase and
road sector borrowings also will become less expensive.
29. It was further informed that issues pending with Ministry of Environment and Forests
which delay road projects have also been resolved. After the delinking of forest clearance with
environment clearance, the Ministry of Environment and Forest will issue formal clearance letter
for 25 projects which were pending with them since long. The Ministry of Environment and
Forests has issued an OM dated the 5
th February, 2013 by which linear stretches have been
exempted from obtaining Gram Sabha NOC under the Forests Rights Act. This exemption will cut
down delays significantly in processing of the cases of forest clearance.

30. On the issues of mandatory environment clearance for borrow areas, it was informed that
Ministry of Environment and Forests has issued a circular by which separate environment clearance
will not be required for the borrow areas once environment clearance is granted for the complete
project. NHAI will have to provide broad coordinates of the area from where the soil will be
borrowed at the time of submission of proposal for environment clearance for the project.
31. To a further query on the major problems being faced in executing highway projects in
different states, from fixing terms and conditions, inviting and deciding bids, awarding the work,
its progress and completion, it was further informed that issues in land acquisition, delay in handing
over Defence/Railway land, lack of State Support Agreement, issues with State government, issues
with Railway Ministry related with ROB/RUB, non-availability of aggregates, delay in shifting of
utilities and delay in tree cutting are considered as major problem in road projects.
32. When asked about review done, if any, of existing PPP model for NH projects, it was
informed that development of NHs on PPP was mainly from the consideration of augmentation of
resources which were not adequate. The PPP mode mainly depends upon the financial viability of
the project. It appears that most viable projects on PPP mode have already been bid-out. As a
result, the response of the bidders now is very poor. When project fail to evoke response from
the private sector, Government has no option but to switch over to budgetary funding of those
projects. All the documents whether for PPP projects or for the budgetary projects are evolutionary
in nature and undergo change based on the feedback received from the stakeholders.
33. During the discussion the role of the Planning Commission in certain crucial aspects of
highways construction/development also came up. The Committee was informed that the Ministry
of Road Transport and Highways and NHAI have to go for approval to the Union Cabinet on many
of the issues vexing the road sector. The Chairman NHAI submitted before the Committee
“the Planning Commission has got the approval of the Cabinet for everything. It is
surprising that model concessionaire agreement which is evolving over a period of time and
we do not have much of experience on that, you straightaway get the stamp of the Cabinet.
If any minor tweak is required, you have to go to Cabinet. People are generally averse to
go to the Cabinet for minor things. These things nobody would have read while approving
MCA like that. It is an uncalled for thing.......... I am doing the contract management
through a document which is evolved in the Planning Commission based on experience of
some Western countries”.
34. The Committee, in view of the complicated and long procedure involving multiple
agencies at different levels coupled with strict norms, legal, administrative, financial etc.
that are required to be followed at different stages, appreciates the constraints under
which highways projects have to be executed. Then there are problems with land
acquisition and environmental clearances that are time taking. But, the Committee feels
that such infrastructure projects as highways have to be taken up and completed in this
setting only; there in no escape from the constraints as these are built into the system.
Real challenge, therefore, before the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways is to
expedite the projects in minimum time and to maximum possible extent, by having a
robust monitoring and coordination mechanism at different level/stages. Every important
agency involved in the process like financial institutions such as Banks and RBI, State
Governments, other Ministries and particularly Planning Commission will have to realize
the urgency and importance of faster development of Highways and will have to make it
a joint effort.
35. The Committee hopes that certain measures taken recently in respect of making
loans from Banks as secured loans; separating forest clearance from the environmental and

9
efforts made to revise Concession Agreement will certainly go a long way in expediting our
highways development programmes.
36. In this regard, a disturbing aspect about Planning Commission’s approach towards
highways construction came to notice of the Committee. To Committee’s utter dismay, the
Planning Commission instead of promoting and facilitating highways construction is alleged
to have been exceeding its brief. The Committee feels that the Commission’s overbearing
attitude was causing unease amongst the line Ministries/Departments. It is the considered
opinion of the Committee that documents/papers prepared by the line Ministries for
implementing a project may be seen by the Commission just as is done by the Department
of Law and Department of Expenditure; it should refrain from commenting upon
engineering & technical aspects and should confine itself to planning and allocation/
management of resources only. Moreover, the ultimate accountability for anything done or
not done rests with Ministry and not with the Planning Commission. For example, in the
case of Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, the Committee learnt that even
Concessionaire Agreements are being finalized in the Commission on the basis of models
being followed in Western Countries. If it is true, the Committee feels that Planning
Commission by doing so was going beyond its original mandate visualized by Pandit
Jawaharlal Nehru, whose idea was to have such a body which could facilitate faster growth
and development in planned manner. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Concessionaire Agreement devised by the Planning Commission should be reformulated in
concurrence with the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways to address the local
conditions and changing circumstances. The Committee feels the Western models will not
work in our country because we have our own peculiarities and constraints.
37. The Committee has strong reasons to conclude that PPP as a model for the
development of road projects needs to be reviewed seriously in the light of our experience
so far in this regard. That the private capital is mainly for profit is borne out by the fact
that the profitable projects are being bid out fast. A vast country which needs faster
connectivity to its length and breadth has to adopt an alternative model of project
development which is development driven and not profit driven. Since profitable projects are
sold out first, the Government is facing problem in getting bidders for such non-profitable
road projects as those in LWE and North-East Areas. India needs faster construction of
roads and the Committee, therefore, recommends that all such projects without bidders
should be developed with the budgetary support from the Government and Planning
Commission needs to be more practical and careful about its role and function.
National Highways Development Project (NHDP)
38. National Highways Authority of India is mandated to implement National Highways
Development Project (NHDP) which is India’s largest ever Highways Project in a phased manner.
Rapid expansion of passenger and freight traffic makes it imperative to improve the road network
in the country. Accordingly, Government of India launched major initiatives to upgrade and
strengthen National Highways through various phases of National Highways Development Project
(NHDP). NHDP consists of the following components:
• NHDP Phase-I: Four laning of 7498 km. at an approved cost of Rs. 30,300 crore in
December, 2000 and NHDP Phase-II: Four laning of 6,644 km. at an approved cost
of Rs. 34,339 crore in December, 2003. These two phases comprise of Golden
Quadrilateral (GQ), North-South and East-West Corridors (NS-EW), Port Connectivity
and other projects. The GQ (5,846 km.) connects the four major cities of Delhi,
Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata. The NS-EW Corridors (7,300 km) connect Srinagar in

the North to Kanyakumari in the South, including spur from Salem to Kochi and
Silchar in the East to Porbandar in the West.
• Government has approved upgradation of 12,109 km under NHDP Phase-III at an
estimated cost of Rs. 76546 crore.
• Government on June 18, 2008 has approved upgradation/strengthening of 5,000 kms. of
national highways to two lane with paved shoulders on BOT (Toll/Annuity) basis under
NHDP Phase–IV A at a cost of Rs. 6950 crore. Moreover, a unified programme of the
entire length of 20000 Kms envisaged in NHDP Phase-IV has also been approved by
Cabinet Committee on Infrastructure (CCI) in its meeting dated 2
nd Feb., 2012.
• Government on October 5, 2006 has approved six laning of 6,500 km. of national

• Government on November 2, 2006 has approved construction of 1000 km. of

• Government has approved construction of 700 kms. ring roads and bypasses besides
grade separators, flyovers, elevated roads and tunnels etc. at a cost of Rs.16,680 crore
under the NHDP Phase-VII.
39. NHAI is implementing National Highway Development Programme (NHDP) and some other
allied projects in accordance with the mandate approved by the Government. The development and
maintenance of National Highways is financed by following modes:–
(a) Government of India’s Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) and Additional Budgetary
Support (ABS).
(b) Dedicated accruals under the Central Road Fund (share in the levy of cess on fuel)
allocated through Union Budget.
(c) External Assistance in respect of International Institutions (World Bank; ADB; JBIC)
funded projects allocated through Union Budget.
(d) Ploughing back of toll revenue including toll collection, negative grant, premium and
revenue share deposited by NHAI into Consolidated Fund of India and in turn
equivalent amount released to NHAI for development, maintenance and management of
National Highways entrusted to it.
(e) Private financing under PPP frameworks
(i) Build Operate and Transfer (BOT)-Toll/Design Build Finance Operate and Transfer
(DBFOT)-Investment by private agencies and return through levy and retention of
user fee;
(ii) BOT (Annuity)-Investment by private agencies and return through semi-annual
pre-determined payments from NHAI as per bid; and
(iii) Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)-with equity participation by NHAI.s
(f) Funding of the NHDP Projects under SARDP-NE and in J&K with Additional
Budgetary Support (ABS) over and above cess allocation; and
(g) Market borrowings (including funds raised through Capital Gain Tax Exemption Bonds
under section 54 EC of Income Tax Act and Tax free Bonds) as authorized by
Government of India on year to year basis.

on highways shall be consistently growing at 15% annually, GDP growth shall remain robust at
8%-9%, 20 km. per day highway construction announced in the year 2009 shall have to be
achieved, cost escalation will stop and environmental clearances shall be coming with a dispatch.
Most of the long stretch national highway development projects in previous two years were
cornered by leading road infrastructure developers of the country by offering premium to the NHAI
because the expert international rating agencies hired by road developers have validated their
expectation and targets of financial closure and revenue recoveries through Toll collections over a

46. However, most of the road developers, even after grave economic slow down of year
2008-09, have gambled to quote high premium in acquiring national highway road development

there is a large scale under reporting of the traffic and therefore, the lower revenue recovery at
Toll plazas by the concessionaires. Fact of the case is that these big construction firms had
wrongfully offered premium in their road construction bidding process before NHAI and at the
same time the policy makers have forgotten that the burden of so called premium shall ultimately
be borne by the road users and now since the excitement has fizzled out the sympathies and noises
for the infrastructure players are being blow out of proportion by their well entrenched interest in
Government and outside.
47. The bankers are now weary of backing new projects because exposure to these big
infrastructure players has far exceeded capital adequacy ratio as they have plunged into non-road
construction projects like power, which are not taking up due to various reasons. As a matter of
fact, a sound long term investment project like road building always discounts the fluctuation in
traffic growth, cost overrun, shrinking GDP and passing through policy/environmental bottlenecks
rather than using them a ‘fig leaf’ to back out of their legal commitment.
48. It seems that ground work is being undertaken by NHAI and other official agencies to work
out so called settlement and conciliation mechanism to bail them out and holding back any penal
action against them for backing out of their commitment to start work on highway construction
in which they have committed through tender process premium payment to the NHAI in acquiring
the work. It is feared that in the end, as has been happening, it will be the road users, hapless
stakeholder, who shall be paying the price for acts of omissions and commissions committed by
road developers, NHAI and policy makers.

II. Road Transport
96. The Committee was informed that more than 60 per cent of freight and 85 per cent
passenger traffic are carried by road. Although National Highways constitute only about 1.7 per
cent of the road network, it carries 40 per cent of the total road traffic. Ministry claimed that easy
availability, adaptability to individual needs and the cost savings are some of the factors which go
in favour of road transport. Road transport also acts as a feeder service to railway, shipping and
air traffic. The number of vehicles has been growing at an average pace of around 10 per cent
per annum. The share of road traffic in total traffic has grown from 13.8 per cent of freight traffic
and 15.4 per cent of passenger traffic in 1950-51 to an estimated 62.9 per cent of freight traffic
and 90.2 per cent of passenger traffic by the end of 2009-10. The rapid expansion and
strengthening of the road network, therefore, is imperative, to provide for both present and future
traffic and for improved accessibility to the hinterland. In addition, road transport needs to be
regulated for better energy efficiency, less pollution and enhanced road safety.

OBSERV ATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS/CONCLUSIONS— AT A GLANCE
Annual Plan 2012-13
The Committee finds that funds under both plan and non-plan outlays have seen
substantial reduction at RE stage for the year 2012-13. The Committee is not convinced of
the reasons for such drastic reduction of funds especially under plan head from Rs.23000
crores at BE stage to Rs.17773.54 crores at RE stage. The Committee found that reduction
of funds at RE stage is happening every year due to one reason or the other. The
Committee feels that the Ministry should strive to utilize the allocated money. The
Committee also feels that major modification of allocation at the stage of revised estimate
should be avoided as it delays the implementation of sanctioned projects. (Para 9)
Twelfth Plan (2012-17)
The Committee notes that the total GBS outlay provided during the Twelfth plan is
Rs.1,44,769 crore including central transport sector as against the projected requirement of
Rs.2,64,080 crore excluding central transport sector. The Committee feels that this
reduction in allocation will certainly deter the Ministry in taking-up the new projects/
schemes envisaged for the Twelfth plan period. Projects like connectivity to 50 minor ports,
connectivity to 24 airports and implementation of special packages for J&K and North-
Eastern Region have to be dropped by the Ministry for want of funds from the Government.
The Committee also notes that Rs.6,400 crore has been allocated against the requirement
of Rs.37,540 crore for works NH(O) for the entire plan period, which is unsustainable
considering the length of roads taken-up for improvement under this scheme. This reduced
allocation for the NH(O) will affect the NH(O) works adversely and ultimately result in
diversion of cess towards development of non-NHDP projects from NHDP projects. The
Committee is concerned to note that such diversion of cess meant for NHDP projects has
been taking place for the last three years which is alarming. The Committee feels that the
Ministry should take-up the enhancement of GBS during mid-term plan review so that new
projects/schemes apart from the on-going ones do not suffer. (Para 13)
Annual Plan 2013-14
The Committee finds that the GBS component for the Ministry has seen only a
marginal increase of Rs.500 crore, from Rs.23,000 crore in 2012-13(BE) to Rs.23,500 crore
for 2013-14 (BE). As against the projected requirement of Rs.26,450 crores, the Ministry has
been allocated Rs.23,500 crore only as GBS for 2013-14 which is about 88% of the projected
requirement. The Committee feels that static allocation forces the Ministry not to take up
new projects/schemes and it also affects the progress of the sanctioned projects adversely.
The Committee finds that Rs.3,024 crores meant for financing of NHDP will be diverted to
NH(O) works resulting in various schemes under NHDP being delayed. The Committee feels
that the Ministry should find ways to check such diversions in consultation with the
Planning Commission so that major national highway projects are not delayed for want of
funds. Implementation of our road transport Schemes/programmes are not visible/felt at the
ground level at all and, therefore, these may be supported in a big way. (Para 18)

National Highways Authority of India
The Committee, in view of the complicated and long procedure involving multiple
agencies at different levels coupled with strict norms, legal, administrative, financial etc.
that are required to be followed at different stages, appreciates the constraints under which
highways projects have to be executed. Then there are problems with land acquisition and
environmental clearances that are time taking. But, the Committee feels that such
infrastructure projects as highways have to be taken up and completed in this setting only;
there in no escape from the constraints as these are built into the system. Real challenge,
therefore, before the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is to expedite the projects
in minimum time and to maximum possible extent, by having a robust monitoring and
coordination mechanism at different level/stages. Every important agency involved in the
process like financial institutions such as Banks and RBI, State Governments, other
Ministries and particularly Planning Commission will have to realize the urgency and
importance of faster development of Highways and will have to make it a joint effort.
(Para 34)
The Committee hopes that certain measures taken recently in respect of making
loans from Banks as secured loans; separating forest clearance from the environmental and
efforts made to revise Concession Agreement will certainly go a long way in expediting our
highways development programmes. (Para 35)
In this regard, a disturbing aspect about Planning Commission’s approach towards
highways construction came to notice of the Committee. To Committee’s utter dismay, the
Planning Commission instead of promoting and facilitating highways construction is alleged
to have been exceeding its brief. The Committee feels that the Commission’s overbearing
attitude was causing unease amongst the line Ministries/Departments. It is the considered
opinion of the Committee that documents/papers prepared by the line Ministries for
implementing a project may be seen by the Commission just as is done by the Department
of Law and Department of Expenditure; it should refrain from commenting upon
engineering & technical aspects and should confine itself to planning and allocation/
management of resources only. Moreover, the ultimate accountability for anything done or
not done rests with Ministry and not with the Planning Commission. For example, in the
case of Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, the Committee learnt that even
Concessionaire Agreements are being finalized in the Commission on the basis of models
being followed in Western Countries. If it is true, the Committee feels that Planning
Commission by doing so was going beyond its original mandate visualized by Pandit
Jawaharlal Nehru, whose idea was to have such a body which could facilitate faster growth
and development in planned manner. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Concessionaire Agreement devised by the Planning Commission should be reformulated in
concurrence with the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways to address the local conditions
and changing circumstances. The Committee feels the Western models will not work in our
country because we have our own peculiarities and constraints. (Para 36)
The Committee has strong reasons to conclude that PPP as a model for the
development of road projects needs to be reviewed seriously in the light of our experience
so far in this regard. That the private capital is mainly for profit is borne out by the fact
that the profitable projects are being bid out fast. A vast country which needs faster

32
connectivity to its length and breadth has to adopt an alternative model of project
development which is development driven and not profit driven. Since profitable projects are
sold out first, the Government is facing problem in getting bidders for such non-profitable
road projects as those in LWE and North-East Areas. India needs faster construction of
roads and the Committee, therefore, recommends that all such projects without bidders
should be developed with the budgetary support from the Government and Planning
Commission needs to be more practical and careful about its role and function. (Para 37)
National Highways Development Project (NHDP)
The Committee observes that performance of NHDP from 2006 onwards has not been
very good. For instance out of the 1000 km. envisaged in NHDP-VI in 2006, the Government
could not award even a single contract till March, 2013. Likewise, out of the 19,800 km.
envisaged in the NHDP IV , just 187 km. could be completed till March 2013. Apart from the
stated reasons such as poor market conditions, economic slow down, strict stipulations of
RBI for funding projects, poor response of bidders for projects, the Committee feels that
poor performance of NHDP projects may be due to reasons narrated in previous paragraphs.
The Committee, therefore, feels that the Government should look into such aspects and try
to curb such practice to prevent breakdown of implementation of projects. (Para 50)
The Committee feels that environment and forest clearance are necessary
components of any development. However, there is no point in delaying the clearance or
keeping it in abeyance for unduly long, affecting the viability of the projects. The
Committee recommends that the Government should fix a specific time-frame within which
the concerned authorities have to take a decision on such clearances. There should be an
effective coordinating mechanism with different Government agencies to give clearances in
a time bound manner. (Para 56)
The Committee after examining all the information provided by the Ministry as well
as the National Highways Authority of India expresses its concern over the status and
progress of development of national highways in the country. The NHAI’s ability to spend
only Rs.7780 crores as against the allocated amount of Rs.20780 crores (RE) during the year
2012-13 certainly does not inspire confidence with regard to development of national
highways. When the concessionaires who have awarded projects under BOT, are withdrawing
from various projects due to funding issues, the public sector agency is not able to spend
even half of the allocated money. The replies submitted by the Ministry are not convincing.
(Para 61)
The Committee was informed about the poor condition of Rewa-Katni-Jabalpur
section of NH7 and also the non-availability of approach roads to NH-60 of GQ in
Medinipur(WB). Poor condition of NH 25 between Kanpur and Jhansi was also brought
before the Committee. The Committee hopes that NHAI will take-up with State Government
of Tamil Nadu on the pending issues relating to completion of Chennai port-Maduravoyal
connectivity project. The Committee was also informed about the delay in acquisition of land
for national highways projects and release of share of central funds to Kerala. The
Committee also hopes that the development of Dehradun-Gangotri national highway via
Harsil will get the due importance from the implementing agencies and the project will be
completed within the stipulated time. (Para 62)
The Committee feels that the model Concession Agreement prepared by the Planning
Commission is acting as an impediment in awarding projects as there is no flexibility to
accommodate the local Conditions. The Committee is of the opinion that the NHAI may be
allowed to have its own Concession Agreement depending on the local conditions for the

33
roads to facilitate greater amount of private participation. The Committee also feels that
NHAI should not keep on adding projects in its kitty without completing the existing
projects. The Committee hopes that NHAI should fix its target levels based on ground
realities involved in road projects. (Para 63)
National Highways (Original) Works: NH(O)
The Committee feels that development of non-NHDP highways is not getting much
attention from the Government. Reduced allocation of Rs.5421.83 crores - 2013-14 against

Government this sector. The Committee notes with concern that to meet this shortfall of

of cess increased to Rs.5,719 crore in 2012-13 from Rs.l,162 crore diverted in 2011-12. The
Ministry has the planning to divert Rs.3,024 crore during the current fiscal. The Committee
finds that achievement of targets fixed under the development of non-NHDP is also not
encouraging. The Committee feels that dedicated allocation of funds for this head is a
necessity for strengthening the core non-NHDP national highways. (Para 68)
Road Projects in Left Wing Extremism affected areas
The Committee is of the view that developing road network in LWE areas forms a
strategic and economic significance to connect them with the rest of the country. The

only 2267 km. could be completed till February, 2013. The Government machinery need to

projects in the regions. The Committee observes that the relaxation of eligibility conditions

(Para 71)
Maintenance and Repairs of National Highways
The Committee notes that over the past six decades, the volume of traffic on Indian

The Committee feels that allocation of funds for maintenance and repair of national

1I3
rd of the requirement of funds for maintenance of National Highways. The Committee
