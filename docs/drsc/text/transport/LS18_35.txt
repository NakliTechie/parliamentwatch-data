12.       The Managing Director Air India while deposing before the Committee has stated that the basic cause for
 declining in profitability is the Company’s own policy for introduction of wetleased Aircraft, without adequate landing
 facilities for purchasing these aircraft heavy burden fell on Air India because of loan repayment and interest payment. 
 Its size rather than quality of services emerged as the critical success factor for the airline. Thus due to three main
 factors i.e. introduction of wet leased aircraft, expansion of aircraft fleet and repayment of loan plus interest cost the Air
 India running into heavy losses. The Committee was informed that the Air India is incurring losses to the tune of rupees
 one crore daily.
 
13.       Another reason for the decline in profitability has been stated to be gulf crisis.  The continuing  recession in the
 world’s major traffic generating economies and over capacity (caused primarily by indiscriminate fleet expansion by
 the major international carriers in an effort to garner market share) have also  diluted revenues and yields and placed
 heavy interest and depreciation burdens on Airlines. The Committee was also informed that  upto the year 1992-93 Air
 India was consistently making profits but with Indian Airlines entry into the gulf sector the position has been changed. 
 There was a sizeable  impact on the profit and from 1992-93 over the years the generation of profits from the gulf as a
 proportion of their total revenue has been falling considerably  though the passenger load factor has remained more or
 less static.  In face it has improved from 1996-97.  The fall in net profit from 1992-93 to the present time is over
 hundred and twenty five crores.
 
 14.      The Committee was further informed  that although Air India has been consistently generating profits since 1985
 (it generated net profit in each year with the exception of 1987/88) its net profits have been negative in 1995-96.  This

 Air India has shrunk in size during the last 5 years.  Its marketing standing – measured both in terms of its share of the

 Similarly, it has experienced deterioration in the health of its assets – its policy of replacing aircraft after 20/25 years of

15.       In reply to question regarding reasons behind the delay in purchasing new aircrafts to replace the ageing fleet,
 the Committee was informed   that it is because of  the long procedure involved in materialising the proposal for the
 purpose. In order to reduce delay in getting government’s approval for acquisition of aircraft a proposal is under
 consideration wherein it is proposed that instead of PIB clearance such projects may be approved by a Committee under
 the Chairmanship of Secretary (Civil Aviation) with members from concerned organisations viz. Ministry of Finance
 and Planning Commission.
 
16.       Regarding status of the aircraft projects for three A 310-200 the Committee has been informed that earlier the
 project report for acquisition of these aircraft was submitted by Air India in December 1993, which was kept in
 abeyance by the Ministry of Civil Aviation because of the surplus A 320 aircraft with Indian Airlines.  In October 1996
 the Ministry requested Air India to recast this Proposal and the same was submitted to the Ministry of Civil Aviation in
 December, 1996 and the Board was apprised of this action.  However, the Ministry directed Air India to seek specific
 approval of the Board for the revised project.  Since then the matter has been under consideration of the Board level and
 a techno-economic evaluation of the A 310-300 vis-à-vis its competitor has been initiated. 
 
17.     Regarding B747-400 aircraft the Committee has been informed that the project report for acquisition of this
 aircraft was originally submitted to the Ministry in August, 1995.  In April, 1997 the Ministry requested Air India to
 formally withdraw this proposal.  In September, 1997 Air India informed the Ministry that the matter was thoroughly
 reviewed in the light of its  changed operating plans.  On July, 24, 1998 the Ministry has indicated  that the project is
 being treated as deferred due to the resource constraints  and difficult financial position of  Air India.  Similarly
 induction of 3 medium capacity long range aircraft (MCLR) in the near future is uncertain in view of the present
 financial position of Air India.
 
18.       During deposition before the Committee the Secretary, Ministry  of Civil Aviation informed  that  some 4-5
 years back whatever profit was earned by Air India were kept in the banks instead of spending it on purchasing new
 aircrafts.  This policy of air India in not spending profits on purchasing new aircrafts has affected the expansion of its
 fleet, where as other airlines like Malaysia, Singapore expanded but Air India’s position remained the same.  However,
 at present their policy is towards purchasing of new aircrafts.
 
19.             Increase in fuel cost is also one of the main reasons for decline in profitability of Air India.  Air India is
 getting air turbine fuel at a very higher cost as compared to international airlines.

OBSERVATION/RECOMMENDATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
22.       The Committee has been informed that all reserves of Air India have been depleted and they have no money to
 buy planes and also today equity base of Air India is totally unrealistic compared to assets they have. The Committee
 feels that equity of Air India should be restructured so that they can have a much wider equity on which they can build
 up debt. The Committee understand that the matter of restructuring of Air India has been under the consideration of
 Kelkar Committee. The Ministry of Civil Aviation in their action taken replies to 32
nd report on demand for grants
 (1998-99) had informed that the report of Kelkar Committee is expected shortly. The Committee again emphasise that
 Kelkar Committee report should be finalised immediately and Government should take appropriate action at the earliest
 because every single day of delay in restructuring of Air India is costing it Rs. 1 crore per day.
 
23.       It has come to the notice of the Committee that on certain Sectors like Gulf and South East Asia Air India and
 Indian Airlines are competing with each other. The Committee observed that both the national carriers instead of
 competing with each other and this has resulted into an unproductive competition between the two airlines.  The
 unproductive competition has effected the efficiency and profitability of both Air India and Indian Airlines.  The
 Committee, therefore expressed serious concern on this issue and strongly recommends that both the airlines should be
 amalgamated into a single company for optimum utilization of the resources available with the two national carriers. 
 The Committee further recommends that till the merger of the Air India and Indian Airlines takes place, there should be
 joint operation of the fleet/manpower so as to avoid duplicacy in the operation of flights on the same route as is
 presently being done by both the airlines.  The joint operation of fleet of both the airlines may be carried out is such a
 manner that Air India should handle the international traffic and Indian Airlines domestic traffic only so as to achieve
 synergy in the utilization of already scarce resources of the two national carriers.
 
24.       During deliberations the Committee was informed that Air India has more manpower as compared to other
 Airlines in the world. It has around 20 thousand employees and around 700 employees per plane whereas in other
 Airlines it is 350 people per plane. With the present strength of employees Air India can not extricate from its present
 crises. The Committee during discussion was also informed that Air India has taken some steps to reduce its manpower
 such as abolition of a number of posts, recall of certain Indian based officers to base station, half  day work with half
 pay, voluntary retirement scheme, to encourage people to go on long leave provided they belong to non-license
 category. The Committee appreciates the steps taken by Air India and strongly recommend that a complete ban should
 be imposed on fresh recruitment in Air India and all the surplus staff/officers posted in its  offices abroad should be
 recalled immediately so as to reduce the expenditure incurring on wage bill etc. and the services of the surplus staff in
 Air India may be used in engineering/catering departments so as to have better utilization of present staff strength.

25.       The Committee has observed that in the past  Air India used to keep the profits earned in banks instead of
 purchasing new Aircrafts or to replace old ones. With the result that Air India at present its facing funds  shortage to
 purchase the new aircrafts. The Committee is of the view that Air India as a national carrier should grow and in future
 they should not put funds aside to show profit rather  the profits must be ploughed back to create  productive assets for
 the service of the nation and  Govt. should draft a policy in this regard to avoid occurrence of  such situation in future.
 
26.       The Committee was informed that the Air India is operating on some uneconomical routs where passenger load
 factor is not sufficient The Committee is therefore of the view that Air India should explore the possibility of
 encouraging export and cargo transportation on these routs where they feel that the passenger load factor is low.  Thus 
 they can compensate their declining  revenue due to the low  passengers load factor. The Committee understand that the
 Thai Airlines is bringing in more than 80% of cargo from Hong Kong to Delhi. The Committee feels that Air India can
 also lift this cargo at lower rates which, would be better than flying at half capacity.
 
APPOINTMENT OF GSA
 27.         The Committee raised the question of appointment of GSA by Air India. The Committee was informed that
 very rigid procedure is followed in respect of appointment of GSA. GSA represents the Air India in those territories
 where they do not have means to represent by and large. The recommendation for appointment of GSA has to come
 from the Sales Manager of that particular territory.  The Committee was also informed that there are three stages
 involved in this regard. (I) A local Committee is appointed to invite applications (II) The Committee scrutinizes the
 applications carefully and  (III) It short lists a few of them and send the list to the Standing Committee on GSA.  The
 Committee on GSA goes to the particular territory and after going through the entire process select the GSA.  The
 Committee, however, recommends that more transparency may be observed in this regard and the appointment of
 GSA’s  may be through auction or tender may be called.  The Committee further recommends that in order to check the
 indulgence in malpractices by these GSA’s a strict supervision may be kept on their functioning.
 
 USE OF AIRCRAFT VVIP’S
28.       In reply to a question whether Air India charges any amount from the Government for the visit of VVIPs abroad
 and how it affects the regular schedule of flights, the Committee was informed that whenever Air India is required to
 operate flights for the travel of the VVIPs , the Government is billed for expenditure towards cost of fuel,
 landing/handling fees,allowances for operating/cabin crew and deployment of staff at stations abroad etc. Additional
 flight may have to be cancelled to arrange for stand by aircraft, in which case the estimated loss of revenue on account
 of such cancellation is also billed to the Government. The amount outstanding from the Government for operation of
 VVIPs charter flight is approximately Rs. 876 lacs.
 
29.       The Committee has noted that there is no separate aircraft for VVIPs movement to abroad and the entire
 schedule of flight is changed as a result of which the reputation of Air India goes down. Hence the Committee
 recommends that Government should buy a separate Aircraft from Air India, which would be kept under the custody of
 Air India and made available to VVIPs within 24 hours notice whenever required.
 
30.       The Committee was informed that earlier there was a Government policy that any Government/PSU officials
 going abroad on Govt. duty must go by Air India flight but now the Govt. delegations going abroad are not traveling by
 Air India flights and are availing the services of other Airlines. Managing Director Air India informed the Committee
 that the earlier policy has diluted over a period of time.   The Committee recommends very strongly that the earlier
 policy of Government should be made mandatory for all the officers going on official tours to travel by Air India or
 Indian Airlines only and not by private airlines.  the Committee further recommends that Government/PSU officials
 going on official tour should book their tickets through Air India/Indian Airlines Offices so as to give them the
 maximum benefit and private parties should not be allowed to do the job of ticketing.
The Committee was also informed that Air India is also taking some measures with a view to reduce expenditure by
 shifting its offices from prime locations to suburbs or vicinity of Airport and subletting of excess office space and
 reduction in size of accommodation for its officers. The Committee while appreciating the steps being taken by Air
 India in its direction recommended that all these measures may be implemented without any delay and with full vigour
 so as to achieve maximum results which would go a long way in minimizing its losses being incurred presently.

SUMMARY OF THE RECOMMENDATIONS
Kelkar Committee Report on restructuring of Air India should be finalized immediately and appropriate action should
 be taken at the earliest.
Air India and Indian Airlines should be merged.  Till merger takes place there should be joint operation of their fleets to
 avoid duplicacy.
Reduction in man power to control expenditure on wage bill etc.
Proper utilization of funds for expansion of fleets.
Uneconomical routes may be reduced and transportation of cargo may be encouraged to compensate low passenger load
 factor on such routes.
Appointment of GSA’s through auction or by inviting tenders.
Separate aircraft for VVIP movement so that the regular flight schedule should not be disturbed.
Travelling of Government/PSU officials abroad by Air India only and private parties should not be allowed to do
 ticketing.
Expenditure on Air India’s offices may be reduced by shifting its offices from prime locations to suburb vicinity of
 Airports and by subletting of excess office space.
Decision should be taken immediately on the proposal of acquisition of aircraft, which is lying pending with the
 Ministry of Civil Aviation/Ministry of Finance since long.
