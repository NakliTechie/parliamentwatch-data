Port Sector  
RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION  OF THE COMMITTEE 
40. The Committee takes serious note of the fact th at many of the Major Ports have been 
neglected during the budget allocations. Some of th e cash strapped Ports like Kolkata Port, 
Haldia Dock Complex and New Mangalore Port, have no  allocation during 2015-16. The 
allocations made to Cochin Port, Chennai Port and M armugao Port are also negligible. The 
Committee feels that this neglect would aggravate t he already worsened financial situation of 
these Ports. Since capital dredging and maintenance  dredging are very essential for Port 
operation, especially for the riverine Ports like K olkata and Haldia, the Committee 
recommends that Government should extend adequate f inancial support to these major Ports 
meeting their dredging and other requirements. 
(Para No. 30 of ibid ) 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT  
41. Government Budgetary Support (GBS) is provided to various major Ports for 
different types of projects undertaken by them espe cially relating to Capital Dredging, 
Connectivity,etc. GBS is allocated based on the req uests from the Ports and keeping in view 
the financial status of the Ports and relative prio rity of the projects and limited availability of 
financial resources. Ports with sound financial pos ition like NMPT normally do not avail 
Budgetary Support from Government for their Project s and they meet their Plan Expenditure 
from out of their internal resources. In so far as KoPT is concerned, due to riverine nature of 
the Port they are being provided Government subsidy  for maintenance dredging under Non 
Plan. This Scheme is continuing since 1969 and the Cabinet has extended it upto 2016-17. In 
the BE 2015-16, an amount of Rs. 279.94 crore has b een allocated under Non Plan as subsidy 
to KoPT towards maintenance and dredging in Haldia Channel (Rs. 192.14 crore) and River 
Dredging and Maintenance of river Hoogly and Haldia  Channel (Rs. 87.80 crore). Further an 
amount of Rs. 1.65 crore has been allocated under BE 2015-16 (Plan) to KoPT as GBS toward 
River Training Works. GBS allocations have also bee n made under Plan (BE 2015-16) for a 
number of other Ports as under: 
(i) Capital dredging projects of VOCPT. – Rs. 25 crore 
(ii) Development of Outer harbour at VOCPT.  – Rs. 2 crore (Token Provision) 
(iii) Chennai Port Trust Coastal Road protection Work. –  Rs. 10 crore 
 
 
(iv) Construction of Four Lane Connectivity Road –MOPT. – Rs. 5 crore (Rs.75 crore    
was released for the same Project in 2014-15) 
(v) Projects of Cochin Port Trust- Rail Connectivity to ICTT, Vallarpadam. – Rs. 3 crore  
  (vi)  R&D Project in the Port Sector namely ‘Amel iorative Measures for Siltation and 
Optimal Utility at Cochin Port’ – Rs. 2 crore was r eleased to CoPT during the current 
year (2015-16)

effectively brought down the tax liability of the s hipping companies to a level 
comparable to the international level from the prev ious levels of the corporate tax 
regime.

Indian flag vessels for transportation of EXIM and empty containers between two 
or more ports in India.

rather than road or rail, the Government has, in th e Union Budget 2015-2016, has 
brought the abatement of service tax at par with road and rail i.e. 70%.

Indian shipping industry in a competitive framework , the Indian flag vessels have 
been allowed to exercise Right of First Refusal (Ro FR) provided they match the

202. Regarding Campus Selection in IMU, the Shippin g Industry is cyclical and goes

been a prolonged ‘bust’ from 2009 onwards due to th e global economic recession/ slowdown.

witnessed a prolonged recession since 2009 which is  yet to bottom out. To give an indication 
of the magnitude of the crisis faced by the Shippin g Industry, the Baltic Dry Index (which is a

later. As a result, there has been drastic reductio n in job placements in the Shipping Industry 
which in turn led to nearly 50% drop in admissions between 2008 and 2015. It is just 
unfortunate that the setting up of IMU coincided wi th onset of the prolonged recession in 
Global Shipping. These are global developments that are beyond IMU’s control.   The data on

REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT 
205. Indian shipping industry is going through a di fficult period since the global meltdown 
of 2008.  Even though some signs of growth have bee n witnessed, the shipping and the ship-
building industry in particular face a tax regime i n India which renders them non-competitive 
in domestic as well as in international markets.  T herefore, despite 100% FDI being permitted 
in these sectors, no foreign shipping company has s et up a base in India which also points 
towards the lack of a level playing field.  To tide  over the situation this Ministry in its pre-
budget memorandum has sought undernoted tax incenti ves for the Indian shipping and 
shipbuilding industry so as to provide them a level  playing field in the intensely competitive 
global environment. 
206.  DIRECT TAX: 
• Grant of NRI status to Indian seafarers serving on Indian flag ships to bring them at 
par with their counterpart serving on foreign flag ships. 
• Income from sale of qualifying vessels to be leviable to Tonnage Tax. 
• Income resulting from deployment of tonnage tax res erves to be leviable to Tonnage 
Tax. 
• Relaxation of criteria for entry, continuation, dis qualification and exclusion from 
Tonnage Tax Scheme. 
• Amendment in Tonnage Tax Scheme to include all types of dredgers. 
• Amendments in Section 80-IA(1) of Income Tax Act, 1 961 for inclusion of dredging 
as an Infrastructure facility.  
207.  INDIRECT TAX: 
Although the tonnage tax scheme introduced in 2004-05 provided relief from corporate tax, 
some changes in the direct and indirect taxation su bsequently have diluted the benefits 
provided. The fiscal policy and lack of level playi ng field for Indian shipping companies has 
adversely affected the growth of the domestic shipp ing industry and the share of Indian 
shipping in carrying the EXIM trade from the country. These taxes include:- 
• Tonnage Tax is paid for relevant shipping income i. e. income arising from core and 
incidental shipping activities. 
• Corporate Tax or MAT is paid on income obtained fro m sale and transfer of ships 
which is an integral part of shipping activity. 
• Corporate Tax or MAT is paid on income arising from  deployment of tonnage tax 
reserve, which is meant for acquiring new tonnage. 
• Service Tax is not paid on voyage chartering of ves sels as well as on inland waterway 
transportation as the services are presently under negative list of service tax. This, 
however, blocks CENVAT credit for the tax already p aid on the inputs and prevents 
passing the tax credit to the service user thereby increasing the cost of transportation 
for the service user. 
• Service Tax is paid on time chartering of vessels, over a period of one month for 
transportation of goods. 
• An abatement of 70% on the taxable value is present ly available for payment of 
Service Tax for coastal transportation of cargo. 
 
 

208. Steps taken by the Government till date  
To encourage the Indian shipping industry the Gover nment has taken the following 
measures, namely, though more needs to be done:- 
• Acquisition of all type of ships, through import ha s been brought under the Open 
General Licence (OGL). 
• To attract investment for the growth of this Sector , the Government has allowed 100% 
Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in the Shipping Sector. 
• Indian Shipping industry has been provided cargo su pport in the form of right of first 
refusal and policy of Free On Board (FOB) import is  being followed for Government 
owned/controlled cargoes. 
• In the Union Budget 2015-16, Government has announc ed an increased abatement from 
taxable value for levy of service tax on coastal tr ansportation services from 60% to 70% 
which has resulted in decrease of service tax incidence on end users. 
• To keep pace with the growth in EXIM trade and to e ncourage Indian controlled 
tonnage owned by Indian entities, Government has al lowed flagging of ships owned by 
Indian entities outside the country, subject to con ditions. This measure is expected to 
facilitate Indian shipowners to get access to finan ce and business abroad. Indian 
controlled ships shall be eligible for a priority h igher than non-Indian ships i.e. they 
would be given the right of first refusal next in the hierarchy after Indian flag vessels. 
209. The Ministry of Shipping had proposed relaxati on of residency criteria of Indian 
seafarers employed on Indian foreign going vessels while in Indian coastal waters.  The 
Hon’ble Finance Minister in his Budget speech for 2 015-16 has proposed to provide an 
enabling power to CBDT to  prescribe in the rule a uniform method of computation of period 
of stay in India for the purposes of determination of “Resident” status in the case of a Indian 
seafarer, whether working on a Indian-ship or foreign ships.
