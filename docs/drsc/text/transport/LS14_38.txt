REPORT
 
The Ministry of Civil Aviation formulates national policies and programmes for the development and regulation of Civil
 Aviation and is responsible for devising and implementing schemes for orderly growth and expansion of civil air
 transport.  Its functions also extend to overseeing the provisions of airport facilities, air traffic services and carriage of
 passengers and goods by air.  The Ministry is also administratively responsible for the Commission of Railway Safety,
 a statutory body set up under the Indian Railways Act.
2. The Civil Aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organisations under the Department of Civil
 Aviation i.e. Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation Security.
3. The following public sector undertakings/companies/autonomous bodies also function under the administrative
 control of the Ministry of Civil Aviation:
(a) Air India Limited and its wholly owned subsidiaries viz., Hotel Corporation of India Ltd. and Air India Charters Ltd.
(b) Indian Airlines Limited and its wholly owned subsidiary viz. Airlines Allied Services Ltd.
(C)  Airports Authority of India.
(D)  Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited.
4.   Under Demand No.7 a budget allocation of Rs. 218.77 crores has been provided for the Ministry of Civil Aviation
 for the year 1999-2000 (Rs. 55 crores on plan side and Rs. 163.77 crores on non-plan side.)
5.  Provision in this demand mainly include allocations for the Secretariat of the Ministry, the establishment 
 expenditure and procurement of equipment and accessories for DGCA, Bureau of Civil Aviation Security, release of
 budgetary support to Airport Authority of India for improvement of operational facilities in the North Eastern region
 and other crucial areas and upgradation of Amritsar Airport, for making payment of share of FTT to AAI, subsidy for
 operation of Haj Charters, Grant-in-aid to Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Acadamy and token amount of Rs. 1 lakh for
 each Indian Airlines and Air India. The annual plan of Hotel Corporation of  India is financed by Air India Ltd. and that
 of  IGRUA equally by Air India and Indian Airlines.

AIRPORT AUTHORITY OF INDIA
7.         The approved annual plan outlay of AAI for the year 1999-2000 is Rs. 697.93 crores (Plan Outlay for IAD is Rs.
 300.32 crores and Rs. 397.61 crores for NAD)  The provision of Rs. 41 crores as budgetary support during the year is
 for improvement of operational facilities in the North-Eastern region and other crucial areas and upgradation of
 Amritsar airport..
 
8.         The approved plan outlay for the year 1998-99 was Rupees 68.17 crores and Rs. 732.26 crores on plan and non-
plan side respectively which was reduced to Rs. 25 crores and 513.12 crores respectively.
9.         In reply to a question regarding not utilising the allocated amount i.e. Rs. 68.17 crores during the year 1998-99,
 the Committee was informed that the reasons for  reduction in outlay of AAI is mainly due to:
(i)         Slow progress of  works in the North Eastern Region resulting in lesser expenditure by AAI  on such projects
 due to difficult local conditions including contractual problems.
(ii)        Land was provided free of cost by Govt. of Punjab for development of Amritsar Airport resulting in a saving of
 Rs. 20 crores.
(iii)            Provision of Rs. 12 crores towards civil work for development of Amritsar Airport could not be utilised as
 construction  works could not commence during the year due to delay in completion of certain pre-construction
 formalities.
The Committee  has also observed that out of an allocated amount of Rs. 103.91 crores to AAI during 1997-98 & 1998-
99 for improvement of operational facilities in the North-Eastern Region and for development of Aurangabad and
 Amritsar project a sum of Rs. 35 crores could be spent during the first two years of the 9
th plan. The Committee is
 surprised to note that Rs. 283.37 crores have been allocated as budgetary support to AAI for the whole 9th plan,
 whereas only Rs. 35 crores could be spent in first two years of the plan period.
 
10.       The Committee expresses its strong concern with respect to the non utilisation of funds by AAI and was not
 satisfied with the reasons furnished by the AAI and Ministry of Civil Aviation in this regard. The Committee is of the
 view that routine matters like contractual and construction problems should have been taken care of at the planning
 stage itself. The Committee, therefore, directed that all the funds allocated in a particular year for specific projects
 should be utilised in full not only by AAI but by other organisations as also.  The Committee also recommends that
 there should be proper monitoring system to look after the utilisation of funds and also the progress of various projects
 undertaken by the AAI and appraisal report should be submitted to the Committee. 
TASK FORCE FOR NORTH EASTERN REGION
11.       The Committee has been informed that a Task Force under the Chairmanship of Secretary, Civil Aviation, has
 been constituted to examine and make recommendations to facilitate improvements in airport infrastructure and air-
links in the North Eastern Region.
12.       The Task Force, in its first phase of inspection of the airports in North East, has visited Silchar, Imphal, Aizwal,
 and Agartala during January, 1999 and in its second phase of inspection, the Task Force had  visited Guwahati, Tura,
 Shillong, Tezpur, Dimapur, Jorhat, Itanagar, Lilabari, Passighat and Dibrugarh.

CORPORATISATION OF AIRPORTS
The Committee in a written material has been informed  that it has been decided to corporatise the existing airports at
 Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai and Calcutta and the proposed new airport at Bangalore. These airports have been selected in
 view of the fact that they  handle a major chunk of the traffic to/from India and massive investments are required to
 restructure the airports to make them world. class. This corporatisation will bring about effective changes in the
 management and contribute  to the overall increases in the productivity and will be able to attract investments to
 improve  the standards of service/facilities.  The Committee was also informed that the Airports Authority of India has
 invited ‘Expressions of Interests’ from legal and financial consultants for advising on the various aspects of
 corporatisation as well as restructuring of AAI. The consultant will also advise on the financial administrative and
 human resource aspects of corporatisation of airports. 
15.       The Committee is of the view that an in-depth study with regard to corporatisation of these 5 airports be carried
 out immediately and the Committee should be informed about the total policy,  time frame and financial implications
 for its implementation before taking any final decision. 
 
ATS FACILITIES AT AIRPORTS
16.       The Committee has been informed that during the 9
th plan period the Govt. has given emphasis on upgradation
 and expansion of airports infrastructure. In reply to a question how AAI would extend communication and ATS
 facilities at airports so as to avoid delay in flights specially in fog condition in Delhi and other hilly areas in the
 country,  it was also explain to the Committee that AAI has approved establishment of dedicated Satellite
 Communication Network (SATCOM) with Very Small Aperture Terminal (VSAT) at 80 airports at an estimated cost
 of Rs. 57.93 crores. When implemented, this would vastly improve the communication for the airports particularly
 those in the hilly areas and the North Eastern region. At present, AAI has leased a large number of speech and the data
 circuits from the Department of Telecommunication  (DOT) for meeting the communication needs between airports.
 These leased circuits are falling short of the standard performance specified by International  Civil Aviation
 Organisation (ICAO) for these communication needs primarily because of unreliable performance of the  existing DOT
 Network. Since the VSAT in the proposed  SATCOM network would be placed at the airport  itself, it will improve
 reliability of the Communication Channels to fully meet the ICAO requirements and performance standard for inter-
airport communication.
17.  AAI Board has approved the proposal for establishing a dedicated Satellite Communication Network at an
 estimated cost of Rs. 57.93 crores which when implemented would vastly improve communication between airports,
 particularly those in hilly areas and the North East. Better communication facilities would result in improved ATS
 facilities.
18. Category II precision approach and landing system has already been installed and commissioned at Indira Gandhi
 International Airport, Delhi in February, 1999. Further, it has been decided to upgrade this system for Category IIIA
 level by November, 1999 at an estimated cost of Rs. 43.16 crores. It has also been decided to upgrade the Instrument
 Landing system at Lucknow, Jammu and Amritsar Airport to Category-II at an estimated cost of Rs. 1.5 crores each to
 enable aircraft to land under comparatively poorer visibility conditions.
 19.  The Ministry has informed that AAI has provided Rs. 4 crores in 1998-99 and Rs. 53.67 crores during 1999-2000
 for this purpose.  Further funds will also be provided during 1999-2000 depending upon the progress of the projects. 
20.       The Committee expresses its unhappiness at delays which occurred  in recent past when the flight schedules of
 Indian Airlines/Air India as well as that of other airlines were disturbed due to presence of heavy fog at Delhi and other

 INDIAN AIRLINES:
23.       The approved plan outlay for Indian Airlines for the year 1999-2000 is Rs. 540.01 consisting of Rs. 491.91
 crores for aircraft projects and Rs. 48.10 crores for other supporting facilities under non aircraft projects.  The major
 portion of funds i.e. Rs. 446.90 crores is for repayment of loans towards airbus A-320 project. An amount of Rs. 45
 crores has been projected towards purchase of 50 seater aircraft. A token provision of Rs. 1 lakh has been made for
 Indian Airlines as budgetary support for strengthening their capital base by injection of additional equity based on the
 recommendations of Kelkar Committee. 
24.       The approved plan outlay for the year 1998-99 was Rs. 630 crores including budgetary support of Rs. 125 crores
 for providing additional equity as a part of the revival package of the company. The Committee has been informed that
 the revival package is still under consideration of the Govt., budgetary support provided in the budget was not released
 to Indian Airlines as Ministry of Finance withdrew the allocation as Ministry of Finance felt that the provisions should
 be allowed only after specific approval of the CCEA had been obtained for the revival packages in respect of the
 organisation and assured that requisite funds would be made available after approval of the CCEA on the subject has
 been obtained. 
25.       The Committee is not satisfied with the explanation furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation and is of the
 view that both the Ministry of Civil Aviation and Finance are not taking the matter seriously and  there is undue long
 delay  in arriving at decision  with regard to the   revival package for Indian Airlines which have not been performing
 upto its potential for the last two to three years.  The Committee further observed that especially when provisions were
 made in the budget for the year 1998-99 for implementing revival package for Indian Airlines,  the Ministry of Finance
 should have taken a decision in the matter instead of reducing the provision at R.E. stage.  The Committee, therefore,
 strongly recommends that decision should be taken with regard to the implementation of revival package for Indian
 Airlines by the Ministry of Civil Aviation/Finance without any further delay and the matter should be handled on top
 priority basis.   
26.       The Committee noted with concern that the prime timings are being given to the private airlines and Indian
 Airlines operates their flights at odd hours which results in inconvenience to the passengers and due to this most of
 passenger load is diverted towards private airlines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends for rescheduling of Indian
 Airlines flight timings for better utilisation of their seat capacity.  The Committee further observed that inspite of  
 guidelines issued by the DGCA, private airlines are not operating their flights on category II and III routes.  The
 Committee therefore recommends that the private airlines should also bear the social obligation by operating on such
 routes and DGCA should take strong action in case of non compliance of this requirements. 
ACQUISITION OF 50 SEATER AIRCRAFT 
27.       During the year 1999-2000 Indian Airlines propose to acquire six 50-seater aircraft to meet the requirements on
 the short haul routes. The estimated cost of the project is around Rs. 439.61 crores. Indian Airlines has since completed
 techno-economic evaluation of the 50-seater aircraft to be inducted by it and is considering procurement of Turbo prop
 50 seater category aircraft. Indian Airlines has requested that the Government as budgetary support may provide the
 entire cost of these aircrafts and a final view in the matter is yet to be taken. The Committee had been informed during
 evidence that after acquiring of 50 seater aircrafts the North- Eastern states can be well connected and it will be viable
 to use these aircrafts in this sector. 
27-A.   The Committee in the past has been recommending that the Govt. should take an early  action for acquisition of
 50 seater aircrafts as planned by them.  The Committee find to its surprise  that so far no decision have been taken in
 the matter.  The Committee, therefore, again  recommends that an early decision should be taken in the matter so that
 unutilised small airports could be made usable which would certainly increase the revenue of the Government and
 would go a long way in solving  the problems of growth of aviation sector  in the  North Eastern region and other
 remote areas and explore the possibility to acquire of different capacity aircrafts on wet lease.

COMMISSION  OF RAILWAY SAFETY 
32.       The  actual expenditure towards the Commission in 1997-98 was Rs. 1.72 crores which was enhanced to Rs.
 2.06 crores in 1998-99. An amount of Rs. 2.13 crores has been allocated on the Budget Estimate during 1999-2000 to
 meet the establishment expenditure of the Commission of Railway Safety which deals with matters pertaining to safety
 in Rail Travel and operation and perform certain railway function specified in the Indian Railway Act. It is also
 responsible for conducting statutory inquiries into rail accidents and other allied functions.
33.       The Committee in its 9
th and 15th reports had recommended that since the Commission discharges its functions
 entirely for rail travel, the expenditure being incurred on it should be reimbursed by the Ministry of Railways. The
 Committee in the action taken replies furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation was informed that the Ministry of
 Railways did not agree to the proposal on the ground that in order to retain its independence in investigations, the
 Commission should not be dependent on Railways finances.
34.       The Ministry of Civil Aviation had not accepted the views of the Ministry of Railways and the matter was being
 further pursued .
35.       In response to the Committee’s recommendation on the matter made in its 24
th report the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation informed that the Ministry of Railways was being requested again to reconsider the matter.
36.       The Committee is dissatisfied in the manner in which the whole matter is being taken for the last so many years
 and no final decision is coming out. The Committee strongly recommends that the expenditure booked under the head
 other expenses on  Commission of Railway Safety should not be a part of the Civil Aviation Budget and Ministry of
 Railways should make their own independent body to look into the cases of accidents and other related functions and
 the budget provisions for the purpose should be made by the Ministry of Railways in their own budget.
 
BUREAU OF CIVIL AVIATION SECURITY
37.       The total budgetary support in the 9
th plan period for Bureau of Civil Aviation Security is Rs. 25 crores.
38.       The Bureau of Civil Aviation Security looks after Civil Aviation Security and Airport Security in all aspects.
 The bureau issues from time to time instructions and guidelines to State/Union Territory Police, airport authorities and
 air carriers about measures to be enforced to prevent hijacking and other terrorist activities and for ensuring security at
 airports. 
39.       During deliberation the Committee was informed that there is duplicity between BCAS and AAI at the airports
 such as procuring of baggage X-ray machines and other security related equipments and their operationalisation,
 maintenance and over all control. The Committee feels that there is an overlapping of the functions between AAI and
 BCAS.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the role and functions of BCAS may be reviewed and only one
 agency, may be entrusted with the work relating to the management of airports in all respects. 
 
AVIATION TURBINE FUEL
40.       The Committee in a written material has been informed that the domestic air transport industry in India has been
 facing serious problems due to high cost of operations  for which one of the major reasons is the heavy cost of Aviation
 Turbine Fuel (ATF).  ATF constitutes the largest cost component, being about 20-30% of the cost of operation on
 domestic network.  The Committee has been informed that the price of ATF supplied to the domestic operators is

REPORT
file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/38threport.htm[28-08-2018 12:45:01]
 nearly  two to three times the price it is available in the international market (Rs. 5600/- per KL.) the price of ATF paid
 by domestic airlines and by international airlines Air India and Indian Airlines for international operations is Rs.
 15,000/- per KL and  Rs. 9600/- per KL respectively.
 
41.       The reason for the high price of ATF for domestic operations is due to additional imports on ATF to bear the
 cost of subsidy on kerosene and diesel.  The cost of ATF further goes up due to high rates of sales tax levied by state
 governments/ union territory administration ranging between 0% and 3.055% .  The high price of ATF affects the
 viability of domestic airlines operations as the fuel cost accounts for more than 1/4
th of the total cost of operation in
 India whereas in other countries it accounts for less than 20% of the cost of operations.
42.       To make the airline industry viable by reduction in ATF price a meeting held on 13.10.1998 under the
 Chairmanship of Finance Secretary was held and it was suggested that the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas should
 immediately dismantle Administered Price Mechanism (APM) at one stroke or alternatively, a phased dismantling be
 considered by reduction of prices in the first year by 25% (of the excess of international rates), in the second year by
 50% and in the third year by 75%, bringing the price at par with the international price of ATF in the fourth year. 
 Keeping in view the deficit in Oil Pool Account, it was felt difficult to consider dismantling of APM at this stage and it
 was agreed that in the current year 20% reduction (of the excess of international rates) should take place which can be
 followed in subsequent years thereby bringing the price at part with international price of ATF in 4 to 5 years.  The
 Secretary (P&NG) has been requested on 28.10.1998 to take appropriate action for reduction of APM by 20% in the
 first phase.  It has been reported by the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas that the deregulation of import and price
 of ATF would take place from the year 2000-01 onwards. 
43.       For rationalisation of sales tax being charged on ATF by various state governments/ U.T. Administration a
 Committee of  sales tax  rationalisation headed by the Chief Minister, West Bengal has been constituted and a reference
 has been made to the Secretary of the Committee for placing the issues of the matter before this Committee. 
44.       The Committee was  informed during evidence by  the Ministry of Tourism also that due to the high price of
 ATF, the fare of domestic airlines is very high because of which the tourism is  affected badly in the country.  The
 Ministry of Tourism requested that in view of the promotion of tourism in the country relief may be given to domestic
 airlines in their operational cost by reducing the high price of ATF.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends
 that the decision for taking to the reduction in price of ATF may be taken without any delay as this has been one of the
 major factors effecting the financial viability of the Indian Airlines  and other private airlines which in turn is
 responsible for slow development   and growth of tourism in the country.
