The Ministry of Surface Transport is responsible for development, formulation and implementation of policies
 relating to Shipping, Shipbuilding, Major Ports, National Highways, Road Transport, Inland Water Transport etc.  Its
 main functions relate to Planning and Co-ordination of Road Development including Border Roads, Road Transport,
 Development of Inland Water Transport, Maritime Shipping and Navigation, Development of Major Ports,
 Development of Lighthouses and Lightships, Shipbuilding and Chartering of Ships. 
 2.  Administratively, the Ministry has been divided into two Departments, viz, Department of Road Transport and
 Highways and Department of shipping w.e.f. 15 th October 99.  Under the Department of Road Transport are Roads, 
 Road Transport Wings and National Highways. Ports, Shipping, Chartering, Development and Inland Water Transport
 Wings are under the Department of Shipping.  Each of these Departments is headed by a Secretary.   Finance Wing is
 common to both the Departments.

 is met by the Government of India.

 construction, development and maintenance of National Highways, Strategic and Border roads, Inter-state Roads,
 permanent bridges and roads of economic importance, schemes financed under the Central Road Fund, bridges fee fund
 etc.

NATIONAL HIGHWAY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (NHDP) 
10.     The Committee was informed about National Highway Development Programme under which, for developing the
 entire National highway System to a desired level of service, it has been decided to prioritise the work of development
 by corridor development approach.

11.     In the Ninth Plan, the objective of National Highway development is to lay stress on increased mobility of road
 transport system.  It is proposed to follow a methodology by which the traffic would be provided with “high level of
 service” corridors along selected routes running across the country and then distributed to spurs of lower level of
 service.  This would be achieved by developing National Highways falling in the alignment of NHDP (spine and grid
 system) comprising of the Golden Quadrilateral connecting the four metropolitan cities of Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and
 Calcutta, North-South (Kashmir to Kanyakumari) and East-West (Silchar to Saurashtra) corridors and NH-47
 connecting Salem to Cochin.  The total length that will be upgraded under NHDP is 12000 kms. (Approx.)  This project
 has been entrusted to National Highways Authority of India (NHAI).  The total fund requirement for this project has
 been worked out at Rs.54,000 crores and this project is targeted to be completed by March 2009.  Four laning of
 Golden Quadrilateral is targeted for completion by March 2004.  Also expressways would be considered along the
 existing alignments based on the projected traffic.  With this spine and grid system, it would be possible to optimise the
 utilisation of resources and, at the same time, ensure increased mobility.  This will also provide a basis for achieving
 uniform economic development over all the regions of the country.
 12.     The Committee appreciates this ambitious National Highway Development Programme which comprises 4/6
 laning of Highways from Srinagar to Kanyakumari and Silchar to Saurashtra of North-South and East-West Corridor
 respectively and Golden Quadrilateral connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Calcutta. 
13.     The Committee, however, is disappointed to find that the coastal area from Mumbai towards south upto
 Kanyakumari that includes major ports viz Mumbai, JNPT, Mangalore, Goa, Cochin and other  important centres like
 Karvar have been ignored.  This coastal area has been completely left out which is of great importance from the point
 of view of commercial and industrial production.
14.     The Committee, therefore, recommends that road connectivity of the Golden Quadrilateral and Corridors with the
 Major Ports of the country should also be taken into consideration while implementing the National Highway
 Development Programme. 
15.     It has also come to the notice of the Committee that several State Governments are interested in participating in
 this ambitious National Highway Development Project Programme.  State Governments have developed and spent huge
 money on stretches of roads, which are situated within their States, but those stretches have been neglected by the
 National Highway Authority of India and have not been included in the Golden Quadrilateral and Corridors. 
16.     The Committee is of the view that the initiative taken by the State Governments should be taken into account
 while implementing the NHD Programme and stretches of roads developed by State Governments should also be
 included in the NHD Programme, as State Governments have spent money on them.  This will definitely ensure faster
 execution of NHD Programme and avoid duplication of work. The Committee also feels that it would reduce the
 financial burden of the Govt. of India to some extent.
 
ALLOCATION/EXECUTION OF WORKS UNDER NHAI PROJECTS
17.     The Committee also went into the issue of allocation of National Highway Projects to NHAI and PWDs of State
 Governments. The Committee was informed that as per the National Highway Act, 1956 the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways have been working on agency system.  The works on National Highways are executed by the
 agencies viz.  State PWD and Border Road Organisation (BRO). Since February 1995, the task of the development,
 maintenance and operation of the National Highways and associated facilities has been entrusted to the National
 Highways Authority of India.  Because the Authority is yet to make adequate institutional arrangements therefor, it has
 not been possible to bring the entire length of National Highways under National Highways Authority of India.  The
 Authority has been entrusted with the ambitious
 National Highway Development Programme comprising of Golden
 Quadrilateral and North-South and East-West Corridors along with other projects.  The total length that will be
 upgraded under National Highway Development Programme is 12000 kms. (approx.) at an estimated cost of Rs. 54000
 crores. In reply to a specific question, the representative of the Ministry informed the committee that NHAI’s  main
 mandate is to four/six laning of Golden Quadrilateral and North South, East West corridors popularly known as
 National Highway Development Project.  However, there are no guidelines or criteria to decide as to which work is to
 be done by the NHAI and which is to be done through the agencies of various PWDs of States. The Committee is
 surprised to note that no guidelines or norms have been fixed by the Ministry of Surface Transport for allocation of the
 works amongst various executing agencies viz. State PWDs, National Highway Authority of India, Border Road
 Organisation and private contractors and the Ministry of Surface Transport is allocating project works arbitrarily  to 
 executing  agencies  without  following  any  specific
 guidelines/norms. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 Ministry should prescribe the guidelines and norms for deciding the allocation of various project works to the executing
 agencies so that the work is carried out by these agencies as per their specified norms.

ROAD DEVELOPMENT
19.     The Committee was informed that the focus of the road development programme in the Ninth Plan would be on
 strengthening and improving the crucial sections of the highway network through phased removal of deficiencies and
 multi-laning of high-density corridors; improving the road communications in remote areas such as the North-East;
 providing all  weather connectivity to remaining villages and promoting energy conservation, safety and environment
 protection.  In its Ninth Plan document, Planning Commission has indicated the targets to be achieved during the Ninth
 Plan keeping in view the availability of resources.   
20.     The Committee was also informed that the Government has issued instructions that at least 10% of the roads
 proposed to be constructed should be rubberised.  The Committee understands that the rubberisation will make the
 roads more durable and the method of construction of roads in this manner is also very cost effective.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that keeping in view the availability of rubber in abundance in the country, there should be more
 emphasis on rubberisation of roads

SHORTFALL IN TARGETS 
24.     The Committee also examined some cases of shortfalls in targets under maintenance/repairs of National
 Highways.   In case of widening to two lanes, the target was 1194 km for the entire Ninth Five Year Plan but till date,
 the achievement is hardly 218 kms.  Similarly, as regards the strengthening of the two lanes, the target was 2908 kms,
 for the entire Ninth Five Year Plan but achievement was 47.97% only in the pasts three years.  The Ministry have stated
 that shortfalls are due to land acquisition, shifting of utilities, contractual labour, foundation/construction problems etc.
 The Ministry has taken some steps to remove the obstacles, which are causing frequent delays in implementation of
 targets.  By amendment of NH Act, process of land acquisition has been simplified and the Ministry has been able to
 meet the procedural delays, which occur in land acquisition.  Under the new procedure, the land can be taken over for
 construction of NH immediately after notification and the affected party can contest only the quantum of the
 compensation payable.
 
25.  Project preparation and the details included in preparatory stage now include identification of land to be acquired,
 the services to be shifted and the costs involved in doing so.  The project preparation is done by external consultants. 
 The consultants are required to carry out detailed investigation.
 
26.     The Committee expressed its concern at the low level of achievement in physical targets in respect of projects of
 widening of Highways to two-lane and strengthening of weak two-lane Highways.  The Committee feels that this
 shortfall in achievement may result in cost overruns also, thereby increasing the burden on the exchequer which is
 already short of funds.  The Committee is also dissatisfied to see that the reasons and the problems of land acquisition,
 shifting of utilities, contractual labour, foundation/construction etc. are being repeated for years together.  It appears
 that no thought has been given to overcome these recurring problems.
 
27.     The Committee notes the steps taken by the Ministry to overcome the obstacles causing frequent delays in
 achieving the targets in development of National Highways projects and hopes that the amendment of National
 Highway Act and improved project preparation would definitely minimize the problems while implementing the
 projects.

CLOSURE OF INDIAN ROAD CONSTRUCTION CORPORATION LTD. (IRCC)         
38. The Indian Road Construction Corporation (IRCC) was set up in 1976 for undertaking construction/development of
 roads and bridges as also other connected civil engineering works including airfields both within and outside India. 
39. The Committee was informed that in view of the accumulating huge losses, the Government of India has decided in
 February 2000 to wind up the Indian Road Construction Corporation Ltd. (IRCC).  The existing employees of the
 Corporation numbering 151 will be forced to opt for voluntary retirement as per the present scheme, failing which they
 will be entitled to compensation payable on closure as per the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
40.  The Committee also came to know that the reasons for the accumulation of huge losses was due to the ban imposed
 by the Central Government prohibiting the corporation from undertaking any further work.  The said ban was imposed
 in February, 1986 and inspite of repeated requests of the Corporation, the Central Government did not allow the
 Corporation to undertake any new work.  The Corporation thus was made non-functional by the Central
 Government.
         
41.     The Committee fails to understand as to why the Government have imposed the ban on IRCC prohibiting it from
 undertaking further work especially so when it had been a profit making undertaking and for not taking any steps for its
 revival.  The Committee is aware of the fact that in the past, as and when any undertaking/establishment was closed, the
 Central Government consistently followed the policy of absorbing the employees in its other Public Sector
 Undertakings/establishments rather than terminating their services viz Vayudoot Airlines, Akbar Hotel, Lakshmi
 Commercial Bank and New Bank etc.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that consistent with the national
 approach and in view of the increasing manpower requirements of NHAI, the possibility of accommodating the
 employees of IRCC in PSUs of the Ministry may be explored.
       
 ROAD TRANSPORT SECTOR
42.       The Committee was apprised that to keep pace with the economic development in the country and consequent
 surge in demand for transport services, passenger and freight, various modes of transport experienced substantial
 growth over the years.  Road transport, however, is the most dominant mode of transport both for movement of
 passengers and goods and is likely to continue to enjoy this status increasingly in the coming years.  The users
 preference towards road transport is on account of its inherent advantages viz. easy availability, adaptability to
 individual needs, door-to-door service, and reliability.  At present 80% of passengers and over 60% of freight is
 estimated to move by roads.  It also acts as a feeder service to Railways, Ports and Air Traffic.
