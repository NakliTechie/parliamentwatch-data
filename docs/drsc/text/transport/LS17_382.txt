Recommendation/Observation  
1. The Committee further notes that although overall budget utilisation remains high, 
expenditure patterns across various cultural schemes have been inconsistent. Programmes 
such as museum development, cultural mapping, and global cultural engagement have 
experienced fluctuating levels of fund disbursement, with some initiatives facing under-
utilisation in certain years and excessive allocations in other. The Committee strongly 
recommends the development of a Cultural Calendar-Based Budgeting system that: 
a. Maintains a comprehensive five-year cultural events calendar that identifies 
recurring festivals, commemorations, and international engagements with historical 
budget requirements. 
b. Integrates cyclical cultural events (such as Kumbh Mela, which occurs every 12 
years) into long-term financial planning through a dedicated cultural events fund. 
c. Implements quarterly budget review mechanisms to assess expenditure against 
planned activities and make necessary adjustments before the Revised Estimates 
stage. 
d. Develops an AI-assisted forecasting model that analyzes historical expenditure 
patterns and predicts resource requirements for upcoming cultural activities based 
on past event costs, inflation factors, and attendance projections. 
This systematic approach would ensure that cultural events of national importance receive 
appropriate financial provisions at the Budget Estimate stage rather than relying on mid-
year adjustments 
 (Para 11)   
 
 
Reply of the Ministry   
1.1 For comprehensive Budget planning, a calendar of events for the entire Ministry is being 
prepared. Expenditure on special events which have not been envisaged at the time of 
submission stage of BE/RE is taken care of by seeking extra budgetary support. Review of 

3 
 
budget/expenditure is a continuous exercise in the Ministry. Efforts shall be made to link budget 
with cultural calendar and integration of cyclical events in long term planning.  
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2. The Committee recommends addressing delays in ongoing Museum Projects like the 
Dholavira Museum and the Leh Museum as they have faced significant delays due to issues 
such as land allotments, extending timelines by 8– 12 months. The committee further 
recommends prioritizing the resolution of these delays, ensuring that land allotments are 
expedited and necessary clearances are streamlined. A dedicated task force or coordination 
mechanism should be set up to oversee and speed up the approval processes for critical 
infrastructure projects. 
 (Para 15)   
 
Reply of the Ministry   
 
2.1 Dholavira Museum: The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) has made significant 
advanced efforts towards the development of the Dholavira Archaeological Museum. In this 
regard 10.5 Acres of land has been acquired by ASI and the same has been secured through a 
boundary wall. ASI is exploring the engagement of implementation partner for execution. 
Further detailed site visits have been conducted by subject/domain experts to assess and develop 
the mechanism for construction and development of the site museum. These measures are aimed 
at expediting the project while maintaining the highest standards of quality and coordination. 
Leh Museum: At present, there is no proposal for the development of a specific museum facility 
at Leh. However, to ensure meaningful engagement with the region’s rich history and culture, an 
interpretative display along with an audio-visual room with seating capacity of approximately 
30-40 pax has already been established at the historic Leh Palace as a museum showcasing the 
history and cultural heritage of the Ladakh and the Indus river valley. 
To showcase India’s archaeological excellence to the world, the Ministry is proposing to launch 
a new scheme to develop archaeological sites—including Dholavira, and Leh Palace —into 
vibrant, experiential cultural destinations. This scheme proposes to boost cultural tourism, 
generate local livelihoods, and reaffirm India’s global leadership in heritage management. 
Recommendation/Observation  
3. The Committee recommends comprehensive digitalization of heritage assets to 
enhance both preservation efforts and public engagement: 

4 
 
a. Develop a phased implementation of "Digital Twin" technology for all World 
Heritage Sites and at least 100 nationally significant monuments by 2028. This 
would involve creating precise digital replicas to monitor structural changes, 
simulate conservation interventions, and enhance visitor experience through 
augmented reality applications. 
b. Establish a centralized Heritage Monitoring System using remote sensing and IoT 
devices to provide real-time data on environmental conditions, structural stability, 
and visitor impact at critical monuments. This would enable predictive maintenance 
and early intervention, potentially reducing long-term conservation costs by 30-
35%. 
c. Launch a Metaverse Heritage Initiative that creates immersive digital experiences of 
inaccessible or vulnerable monument areas, allowing for virtual tourism while 
reducing physical impact on fragile structures. This initiative should prioritize 
monuments facing access restrictions due to conservation concerns. 
d. Implement block chain technology for monument documentation and conservation 
history, creating an immutable record of interventions and condition assessments 
that ensures accountability and preservation of institutional knowledge across 
administrative transitions. 
 (Para 16)   
 
Reply of the Ministry   
 The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) is currently engaged in various IT-related 
initiatives. In addition, we are using the latest digital technologies to enhance the digital 
experience at various monuments and museums. Internally, ASI has introduced several digital 
advancements to support the above-mentioned initiatives and archaeological research. 
(i)   Website Revamp: ASI revamped its official website in February 2024. 
(ii)  Monuments and Sites: ASI has launched new applications and websites such as the Adopt 
a Heritage portal and the Heritage Atlas application. These initiatives aim to provide visitors 
with up-to-date information about centrally protected monuments and enable corporate bodies to 
contribute towards development of these monuments under the Adopt a Heritage program. ASI 
has also created 360-degree views and drone footage for various monuments. 
(iii)  Museums: Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR) experiences have been 
developed at various museums to enhance visitor engagement. The virtual experiential museum 
(VEM) was inaugurated at Sarnath in 2019. Digital upgradation of the Hampi museum and the 
DPR for the Nalanda has also been prepared. 

5 
 
(iv)  Antiquities: Under the National Mission for Monuments and Antiquities, 17.2 lakh approx. 
antiquities and 11 thousand “Biodiversity Heritage Sites” (BHS) (both protected and 
unprotected, located across the country) have been digitised. The website is proposed to be 
enhanced with new features soon. 
(v) Archives & Archival Data Digitisation: Around 22,000 glass negatives digitised and 
30,000 photographs have been digitised and are being prepared for public access, so that revenue 
can be generated from this archive. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) is actively 
engaged in the digitisation of archival materials, which have been systematically sorted, 
identified, arranged, and documented. These materials include photographs, glass negatives, 
drawings, plans, elevation maps, and old files from various ASI circles, with the aim of 
preserving and enhancing access to historical records. 
(vi)  Library: The Central Archaeological Library in the ASI is one of the largest libraries on the 
subject in the country. Currently, a part of its collection has been digitised and is in the process 
of going online through the library management system KOHA. 
(vii) Permission Portal: ASI is developing an online portal to facilitate easy access for citizens 
seeking permissions related to filming, videography, cultural events, and archival materials. This 
portal will be live soon. Internally, ASI is also revamping its e-Governance portal for better 
usability. 
(viii)  Digital technology is evolving rapidly, and ASI is committed to adopt the latest 
technologies across its activities. We remain dedicated to support all future-oriented IT 
developments in the field of heritage conservation. 
  
 
Recommendation/Observation  
4. Institutional Reforms for Museum Project Implementation: The Committee 
recommends fundamental reforms to the project implementation mechanisms: 
a. Implement a modular development approach for museum projects, enabling phased 
completion and partial operationalization while resolving issues with remaining 
components. This would allow for incremental public access and revenue generation 
rather than complete closure during extended development periods. 
b. Develop a standardized pre-implementation framework for museum projects that 
mandates resolution of land acquisition, environmental clearances, and conceptual 
design before budget allocation and project announcement, minimizing mid-project 
disruptions. 

6 
 
c. Create specialized project management units for each major museum initiative with 
representation from relevant stakeholders, including state governments, ASI, 
content specialists, and community representatives to ensure coordinated action and 
accountability. 
 (Para 16.1)   
Reply of the Ministry:-  
4.1 The Committee’s recommendations on institutional reforms for museum project 
implementation broadly align with the evolving practices being adopted by major national 
museums. The Ministry has already initiated measures that reflect these principles, as evidenced 
by ongoing modernization and governance reforms in Museums under the Ministry of Culture. 
 a) Modular and Phased Development of Museum Projects 
 The Indian Museum, Kolkata, has undertaken a major modernization initiative involving the 
phased re-curation of nine galleries, with four key galleries targeted for completion initially.  An 
Implementation Committee has been constituted to facilitate the effective planning and seamless 
execution of the ongoing re-curation project at the Indian Museum.  
 The National Council of Science Museum (NCSM) also employs modular development 
strategies to facilitate phased implementation in Science Cities/Centres projects. 
 b) Strengthening Pre-Implementation Planning Frameworks 
The Committee’s recommendation for a standardized pre-implementation framework is 
acknowledged as critical for minimizing mid-project disruptions. The Indian Museum 
modernization project reflects this approach, with clearly defined galleries, content themes, and 
implementation timelines established before execution. 
 Land acquisition and environmental clearances are prerequisite conditions that must be fulfilled 
by the State Government prior to signing of the MoU under SPoCS (Scheme for Promotion of 
Culture of Science). 
 c) Dedicated Project Management and Governance Structures 
 NCSM deploys dedicated project management units for its projects and engages proactively 
with State Governments to facilitate seamless project completion. 
 A Task Force has been constituted to develop a comprehensive roadmap for transforming 
Victoria Memorial Hall (VMH) into a vibrant national hub of cultural activities. 

7 
 
The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) has adopted a modular and scalable approach for the 
development of both new and ongoing museum projects, enabling phased execution with greater 
flexibility and efficiency. In collaboration with concerned state authorities, ASI now ensures that 
critical statutory and logistical clearances-such as land acquisition and environmental approvals-
are secured prior to budgetary allocations, thereby minimizing delays and facilitating seamless 
project implementation. Effective coordination, timely decision-making, and alignment among 
all stakeholders throughout the project lifecycle shall be ensured through proper project 
management. 
The Ministry of Culture is also proposing a new scheme for creation of cultural infrastructure, 
including Museums with better project implementation provisions. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
5. To address content finalization delays as observed in the Digital Museum project at 
Kanakakunnu Palace, the Committee recommends: 
a. Establish a permanent Museum Content Advisory Board comprising historians, 
cultural anthropologists, museologists, and digital content specialists to expedite 
content development processes across all museum projects. 
b. Implement concurrent content development alongside infrastructure creation 
rather than sequential processes, with rolling approvals for thematic sections to 
prevent complete project stagnation during content refinement. 
c. Develop a shared digital content repository for Ministry museums that enables 
efficient resource sharing and adaptation of existing content for new museums, 
reducing redundant research and development costs. 
d. Integrate emerging technologies such as AI-assisted curation, interactive 
installations, and personalized visitor journeys through mobile applications to 
enhance the museum experience and attract younger audiences. 
e. Implement a "Living Museum" approach that encourages regular content updates 
and community contributions, moving away from static permanent exhibitions 
toward more dynamic and participatory models of heritage presentation. 
 
(Para 16.2)   
 

8 
 
Reply of the Ministry:-  
5.1 Museums being narrative-based institutions, established mechanisms already exist for content 
conceptualization, vetting, and finalization. Museum projects are supported through Content Advisory 
Committees, comprising subject experts such as historians, museologists, domain specialists, and other 
scholars as required. These committees guide thematic development, review narratives, and finalize 
exhibition content at various stages of project implementation. In Museums, exhibit design and content 
development are already undertaken in parallel with building construction, in rolling and phased manner 
for thematic sections. This ensures optimal time utilization and prevents project delays or stagnation 
during content finalization. 
 Regarding the emerging technologies NCSM has been actively leveraging advanced technologies such as 
augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR) and artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance visitor engagement 
and create immersive interactive museum experiences. NCSM is also developing digital content 
repositories to optimize costs and ensure sustainability.  
 Indian Museum, Kolkata has also taken initiatives to digitally reconstruct significant portions of damaged 
archeological artifacts especially those from Gandhara period, utilizing advanced deep learning based AI 
models for 3D point cloud processing.  
 Museums under the control of Central Government are actively undertaking the digitization of their 
artefacts and collections, making digital content accessible through national and institutional online 
platforms, e.g. ‘Museums of India’ portal. This collective effort supports the creation of shared digital 
repositories, improves preservation, and significantly enhances public outreach, research access, and 
cultural engagement.

6. Heritage-Led Regional Development Strategy: The Committee recommends 
leveraging archaeological sites for sustainable local development: 
a. Implement a Heritage-Led Regeneration approach, integrating archaeological sites 
into broader regional development plans. This would involve creating 
archaeological tourism circuits with appropriate infrastructure, interpretation 
facilities, and community engagement. 
b. Establish Archaeological Heritage Zones around significant excavation sites with 
special planning regulations, economic incentives for heritage-sensitive 
development, and community stewardship programs.

c. Develop skills training centres in proximity to major archaeological sites to train 
local communities in conservation techniques, heritage interpretation, and

d. Create Archaeological Research Stations with permanent facilities at key excavation

rather than intermittent excavation campaigns. 
(Para 16.3)   
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
6.1 Recommendations of the Committee are noted. ASI is running Institute of Archaeology 
to provide extensive training in Archaeological field work, conservation and chemical 
preservation. Short term training programmes, workshops and exhibitions are organized to 
extend the subject knowledge for the interested persons and citizens. A new scheme is also being 
proposed to showcase India’s archaeological excellence to the world and to boost cultural 
tourism, generate local livelihoods, and reaffirm India’s global leadership in heritage 
management.

6. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry not only focus on the

 (Para 19)   
 
Reply of the Ministry  
6.1 The Ministry has incorporated a legacy-oriented approach within the proposed revamped

Recommendation/Observation  
7. The committee further recommends that the ministry actively pursue international 
collaborations and public-private partnerships to reduce reliance on domestic financial 
allocations. By securing expertise and additional funding through these collaborations, the 
ministry can ensure the successful implementation and sustainability of large-scale cultural 
preservation projects, thereby enhancing their impact and reach. 
 (Para 26)   
Reply of the Ministry     
7.1 In furtherance of the Committee’s recommendation to actively pursue international 
collaborations and public–private partnerships (PPPs), the Ministry has demonstrated a 
successful model through the recent repatriation and exhibition of the sacred Piprahwa relics. 
These revered Buddhist relics- comprising reliquaries, gemstone offerings and associated 
remains linked to the life of Lord Buddha were secured and brought back to India through a 
strategic partnership with the Godrej Industries Group, which facilitated their recovery and 
return. The Ministry subsequently curated and presented the international exhibition “The Light 
and the Lotus: Relics of the Awakened One”, which combined the repatriated artefacts with 
national collections to enable wide public access, deepen civilizational understanding, and 
advance India’s cultural diplomacy. This initiative illustrates how PPPs can mobilize private 
expertise and resources to achieve high-value heritage outcomes that might otherwise be difficult 
to realize through public funding alone. 
 The Ministry is also strengthening PPP frameworks to expand India’s global cultural footprint 
by leveraging the expertise, resources and managerial capacities of leading private institutions 
and patrons. A key illustration is the National Pavilion of India at the 61st International Art 
Exhibition of La Biennale di Venezia (2026) only the third Indian national pavilion in the 
Biennale’s 125-year history presented by the Ministry of Culture and commissioned by the 
National Gallery of Modern Art, in partnership with the Nita Mukesh Ambani Cultural Centre 
and the Serendipity Arts Foundation,  and supported by a distinguished list of patrons. Through 
this PPP model, the Government provides curatorial oversight and national representation, while 
private partners contribute sponsorship, institutional capacity, and international outreach, 
ensuring efficient execution and enhanced global visibility for India’s contemporary art and 
cultural diversity at the Venice Art Biennale 2026.  Together, these initiatives demonstrate a 
coherent, scalable approach to PPP-led cultural engagement that directly responds to the 
Committee’s guidance on reducing fiscal reliance while amplifying impact and reach.

Recommendation/Observation  
8. The Committee recommends establishing a dedicated research program for 
developing advanced conservation technologies. This should include creating five 
specialized Conservation Research Laboratories focused on different manuscript materials 
(palm leaf, birch bark, paper, cloth, and metals) to develop non-invasive preservation 
techniques, research environmentally sustainable conservation materials, and establish 
evidence-based protocols for treatment of deteriorating manuscripts. The implementation 
of a "Preventive Conservation Network" with environmental monitoring systems at all 
major manuscript repositories would enable real-time assessment of conservation risks and 
preventive interventions, significantly reducing deterioration rates in vulnerable 
collections.                     
8.1    Developing specialized training modules in conservation science for Manuscript 
Conservation Centres (MCCs) would incorporate latest international practices with 
traditional knowledge systems, creating a uniquely Indian approach to conservation that 
respects both scientific principles and cultural heritage. Establishing international research 
partnerships with leading conservation institutes worldwide would facilitate knowledge 
exchange, joint research projects, and capacity building for Indian conservation specialists. 
The Committee notes that while the NMM employs various methods of preservation such 
as lamination, restoration, and de-acidification, there is a need for more systematic 
research into conservation technologies specific to Indian manuscript materials and 
environmental conditions, especially considering the diverse climatic zones across the 
country and their distinct conservation challenges. 
8.2    Building on the existing digitization guidelines, the Committee recommends 
developing an advanced knowledge extraction framework. This should include creating an 
AI-assisted transcription and translation platform that automates the conversion of 
manuscript images to searchable text, provides provisional translations across major 
Indian languages, and allows scholarly review and refinement of automated outputs. The 
establishment of a "Manuscript Knowledge Portal" would contextualize manuscript 
content with related historical information, create thematic collections across manuscripts 
from different repositories, and develop educational modules for various age groups and 
knowledge levels, democratizing access to manuscript knowledge beyond specialized 
academic circles. 
8.3    The implementation of a "Digital Humanities Incubator" program would provide 
grants and technical support for innovative projects utilizing digitized manuscript 
collections for interdisciplinary research, stimulating new scholarly approaches to 
manuscript studies.  Developing mobile and immersive applications would bring 
manuscript content to wider audiences through virtual reality experiences, interactive 
storytelling, and gamified learning environments, particularly engaging younger 

12 
 
generations with India's manuscript heritage. The Committee believes that mere 
digitization without knowledge extraction and meaningful access would significantly limit 
the cultural and scholarly impact of the NMM's work. This recommendation addresses 
that concern by proposing a more comprehensive approach to manuscript content 
utilization that transforms static documents into dynamic knowledge resources. 
 (Para 32.2, 32.3, 32.4 & 32.5)   
 
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
8.4 The recommendations have been noted and will be explored.

Recommendation/Observation  
1. The Committee observes that there is a significant reduction in the Ministry's total 
budget from ₹ 3,406.90 crore (actual) in 2023-24 to ₹ 3,260.93 crore (RE) in 2024-25 and an 
increase to ₹ 3,360.96 crore for 2025-26. The Committee further observes the reduction in 
the Ministry’s total budget from ₹ 3,406.90 crore (actual) in 2023-24 to ₹ 3,260.93 crore 
(RE) in 2024-25 can be attributed primarily to the one-time expenditures related to the 
Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav (AKAM). The AKAM celebrations saw a substantial increase in 
expenditure, with a revision of the Budget Estimate (BE) from ₹ 185 crore to ₹ 335 crore 
during 2023-24, resulting in an expenditure of ₹ 334.39 crore, which was more than 80% 
over and above the original BE allocation. 
 (Para 8)   
Reply of the Ministry     
1.1 The Ministry respectfully submits that the observed budget fluctuations reflect prudent 
fiscal management rather than any shortfall in planning or commitment. The reduction from 
₹3,406.90 crore (actual) in 2023-24 to ₹3,260.93 crore (RE) in 2024-25 was a deliberate 
adjustment following the successful completion of one-time expenditures for the Azadi Ka Amrit 
Mahotsav (AKAM), ensuring no wasteful carryover of unspent funds. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2. The Committee acknowledges that the reduction in budget during the 2024-25 fiscal 
year is a reflection of the conclusion of AKAM-related activities, which were exceptional in 
nature and not a recurring part of the Ministry's regular expenditure plans. However, the 
reduction in the budget allocation for 2024-25 compared to 2023-24 highlights the need to 
ensure that the Ministry's cultural preservation and development initiatives are adequately 
funded, especially in light of long-term goals for heritage conservation. 
 (Para 9)

6. The Archaeological Survey of India, operating under the Ministry of Culture, is spearheading this 
significant cultural recovery effort, which includes the Kohinoor and beyond it to encompass thousands of 
artifacts removed during British colonial rule. 
6.2. To strengthen India’s campaign for cultural repatriation, the government should consider 
establishing a specialized “Heritage Recovery Task Force” comprised of diplomats, legal experts, 
archaeologists, and art historians. This multidisciplinary team would be dedicated exclusively to 
identifying, documenting, and pursuing claims for stolen Indian artifacts worldwide. The task force could 
develop comprehensive dossiers on priority items, including detailed provenance research, legal 
arguments for restitution, and cultural significance documentation to bolster India’s claims. 

34 
 
6.3.     India should leverage its growing economic and diplomatic influence to negotiate cultural 
property agreements with more countries, similar to the recent pact with the United States. These 
agreements should establish clear mechanisms for identifying looted artifacts, resolving disputes, and 
facilitating returns. Special attention should be paid to former colonial powers like the United Kingdom, 
where many significant Indian treasures remain. These agreements could be tied to broader diplomatic 
and trade relationships, creating incentives for cooperation on repatriation issues. 
6.4. To strengthen legal arguments for repatriation, India should invest in cutting-edge technological 
tools for provenance research and authentication. Advanced imaging techniques, DNA analysis of 
organic materials, and AI- powered databases could help establish the Indian origin of disputed artifacts 
and track the chain of ownership. This scientific evidence could prove invaluable in making compelling 
cases for return, particularly for items with unclear provenance records. 
6.5. Additionally, India should explore innovative public-private partnerships to support repatriation 
efforts. The government could establish a “Heritage Repatriation Fund” that accepts contributions from 
corporations, wealthy individuals, and the Indian diaspora to support legal challenges, negotiate 
purchases when necessary, and fund transportation and conservation of returned artifacts. This approach 
would expand the resources available for recovery efforts while engaging broader segments of society in 
the mission. 
(Para 29.1, 29.2, 29.3, 29.4, 29.5) 
Reply of the Ministry     
6.6 ASI undertakes the retrieval of the antiquarian objects in close coordination with the MEA. The

for investment in technological tools like AI have been noted for compliance. The retrieval of the objects 
are managed from the regular budget allocation of the ASI. Since this is not a regular expenditure hence 
there is no need of creation of a separate fund. 
National Museum has also made significant efforts for the repatriation of artifacts from other 
countries. Indian Museum, Kolkata has joined forces with the Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, 
for the ‘AI-based Digital Restoration of Indian Archaeological Artefacts’ project. 
Further Recommendations/Observations
