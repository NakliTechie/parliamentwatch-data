REPORT 
 
Shipping is the life  blood of the global economy. Shipping‟s ability to transport goods 
cheaply, in large volumes and with the minimum environmental footprint is unmatched by any 
other mode of transportation. Around 80% of the international trade in goods is carried by sea. 
The importance of maritime trade has been underscored in recent times by the Covid Pandemic 
which disrupted suppl y chains and shipping networks.  The trade loss due to delay s in cargo 
shipments, higher shipping costs and lower m aritime connectivity lead to higher inflation and 
shortage of food and adversely impact the  world economy. Maritime Transport is a critical 
infrastructure for the economic development of a country. It influences the pace, structure and 
pattern of development.  
2. The Maritime sector in India comprises Ports, Shipping, Ship Building, Ship Repair and 
Inland Water Systems. India has a long coastline of about 7,500 kilometers spread on the western 
and eastern shelves of the mainland and also along  the islands with 12 government-owned major 
ports and approximately 200 minor and intermediate ports dotting the coastline.  Around 95% of 
the country‟s trade by volume and 68% by value is moved through Maritime Transport.  India is 
also endowed with 14500 kilometers of in land waterways in the form of rivers, canals, 
backwaters and creeks. The geographical location of India is at the centre of the Indian Ocean 
which is the epicenter of economic activity due to key international trade routes  which give 
strategic advantage to  India. Shipping is therefore a vital part of the Indian economy. As Prime 
Minister Narendra Modi summed it up succinctly, the blue chakra or wheel in India's national 
flag represents the potential of Blue Revolution or the Ocean Economy and that is how ce ntral 
the Ocean Economy is to India. 
3. The Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways (MoPS&W) has been entrusted with the 
responsibility to formulate policies and programs on the subjects of ports, shipping, ship 
building, ship repair and Inland Water Systems and their implementation. The following 
subordinate/attached offices, autonomous organizations, societies/ associations and public sector 
undertakings are functioning under the administrative control of the MoPS&W:

ABSORPTIVE CAPACITY OF B.E. 2023-24 ALLOCATIONS 
17. Upon query regarding the absorptive capacity of B.E. 2023 -24 allocations, the Ministry 
has submitted that this may pertain to two Budgetary Heads – Assistance to Ship Building and 
Inland Waterways.  In the case of Budgetary Head – Assistance to Ship Building, the projections 
are based on the demand of Indian shipyards in light of delivery of their vessels.  The entire fund 
can be utilized if shipyards deliver their vessels on schedu led time.   The Ministry has added that 
increased allocation under the Revenue and Capital Heads is of Rs. 10.07 crores and Rs. 5 crores 
respectively, hence the increase is marginal. 
18. Regarding Inland Waterways, the Ministry has stated that the enhanced exp enditure 
would be used for the completion of newly sanctioned and ongoing projects at NW -1 and NW -
2 and Phase-I development of NW-3,4, 5 and 13 new NWs sanctioned last year.  
19. The Ministry has also stated that the enhanced BE 2023 -24 of Rs. 250.10 crore a llocated

Coastal Shipping & IWT 
129. The MoPS&W, under its Sagarmala  Programme, has embarked on developing an 
ecosystem for promotion of Coastal Shipping and RORO/Ro-Pax Ferry waterway transportation 
in the country. This mode of transportation  has proved to have multiple benefits over other 
modes of transportation such as improved  cargo delivery and passenger travel time, reduced risk 
of accidents, improved operational speed, low-cost transportation, lower fuel consumption, lower 
congestion on roads and rail,  lowered air, noise and land pollution, along with providing 
seamless travel to passengers, and vehicles. 
130. To promote coastal shipping, Ministry  of Ports Shipping & Waterways has  undertaken 
following initiatives.  
 To encourage a modal shift, Coastal vessels are being provided  a discount of 40% in port 
charges over foreign going vessels.  
 reduction of GST on bunker fuel from l8% to 5%,  
 Cabotage relaxation for cargo vessels, integration of inland and coastal cargo. 
 Subsidy support to Indian shipping companies  
 Green channel clearance for coastal cargoes and priority berthing for coastal vessels at 
major ports  
131. Under the Sagarmala Programme, currentl y two Ro-Pax ferry services are operational  in 
the country on the Gogha- Hazira route in the state of Gujarat and Mumbai- Mandwa route in the 
state of Maharashtra bringing the travel time to 3 hours and 45 minutes with an  estimated carbon 
(CO2 emissions) reduction of 24MT per day and l3MT per day  respectively. These two services 
itself have benefited  more than 24.15 lakh  passengers and around 4.58 lakh passenger vehicles 
and 0.36 lakhs trucks till December 2022  and is continuously getting overwhelming pub lic 
response. 
132. Further, 9 projects including the above two have already been completed and  are 
expected to commence the services soon, and 35 more projects are under various stages of  
development and implementation. Moreover, dedicated berths for the movement of goods and  
passenger are being developed under the Coastal Berth Scheme. Five dedicated Coastal  Berths 
have already been constructed and another six are under development. 
133. Coastal Shipping is a competing mode for road and rail transport however with o nly less 
than 7% modal share of water transport.  India's transport modal share is relatively more skewed 
towards road and rail. Both road and rail transport are s ubsidised by various Government 
policies, however currently, no such benefit is available for the passenger and freight transport 
by Coastal Shipping i n order to reduce the burden on the operations of the Coastal/RORO/ Ro-
Pax ferry urban waterway transportation which are currently on the verge of unavailability due to 
above stated challenges/ issues and for augmenting this newly emerging ecosystem towards  
sustainable operations to compete with the copiously subsidised modes of transportation in  the 
country. Therefore in the Bu dget 2023 -24 to promote coastal shipping VGF guidelines  which 
shall be issued by Ministry of Finance will be followed to promote coastal shipping.

142. The efficiency of a port directly affects the economy of the country since the major share 
of the global trade is carried by sea . Ports handle around 90% of EXIM Cargo by volume and 
70% by value. During the pandemic, freight rates reached record highs. Surges in freight rates 
can affect the prices of goods and  impact global food security.  Ports are  therefore vital to the 
economy. 
143. Port performance indicators are measures of various aspects of a port‟s performance. 
Total cargo traffic handled and Turnaround time are good measures of a port‟s financial and 
operational productivity respectively. Turnaro und time is the total time spent by a vessel at the 
port from the time of its arrival to its departure. The Average Ship Berth Day Output (OSBD) is 
the total cargo handled by vessels/total stay at working berth.  
144. The Ministry has submitted that in order to meet the ever increasing trade requirements, 
expansion of Port Capacity has been accorded the highest priority through implementation of 
well-conceived infrastructure development projects. The government has given a strong push to 
port modernisation and d igitalisation, with a number of ease of doing business initiatives. 
National Logistics Portal - Marine has been developed to facilitate "Ease of Doing Business" by 
bringing all stakeholders (viz.,  Terminal operators, Shipping line agents, Banking, Exporters, 
Importers, Customs/Cargo clearing authorities, PGAs, logistics service providers etc.) onboard to 
enable seamless exchange of information/ documents in digital format through this portal. This 
Portal has been formally inaugurated by Hon‟ble Minister of P orts, Shipping & Waterways on 
27.01.2023. Direct Port Delivery and Direct Port Entry have been implemented in all Major 
Ports which has significantly reduced cost and dwell time.

CYBER SECURITY  
183. The Committee observes that cyber attacks are increasingly becoming an issue of concern 
in the maritime industry. Last year, a container terminal at Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust (JNPT) 
experienced a suspected ransomware attack that affected its management i nformation system 
(MIS). The Committee asked the Ministry to inform about the steps taken to ensure cyber 
security in the maritime sector. 
184. The Ministry submitted that two Cyber Attacks have been reported in  the last five years. 
 MoPS&W, Government of India  has taken various measures like appointing  Chief Information 
Security Officer (CISO) at each port, Various technology trainings on Cyber -attacks and its 
threat analysis, Security Audit & Drills, Cyber Security Advisories from NCIIPC, Analysis of 
Log files and Traffic flow.   Professional Manpower requirement such as System 
Administrator, Network Administrator and dedicated manpower for deploying / complying with 

Page 46 of 93 
 
advisories received from NCIIPC, CERT -in, IB. There is no dedicated fund allocated for cyber 
security. The expenditure is incurred from the Ports‟ budget on a need basis. 
185. The Committee observes that in the increasingly digital world, cyber security has a 
huge potential to affect the safety of the crew, vessel, cargo and even ports. Ships are 
increasingly integrated with onshore operations as digital communications are used to 
conduct business. In addition, critical vessel systems required for the safety of navigation, 
power supply and cargo management are increasingly digitized and connected to the 
Internet.  The Committee observes that digitalization is only going to increase manifold in 
the future in all sectors and the maritime sector is no exception.  The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry may take initiative to allocate a separate fund for cy ber 
security.

233. The Committee had queries the Ministry regarding the number of containers   locally

234. The Ministry has stated that the number of shipping containers manufactured in India is 
not readily available with this Ministry and may be available with the Department of Revenue 
(Customs) or the Department of Commerce. The Ministry has enumerated the following steps 
taken to boost container manufacturing in India -  
235. BIS has issued amendment no. 1 Aug 2022 to the corresponding 
Indian Standard IS 11587:1986 Structural Weather Resistant Steels, incorporating CORTEN A, J
IS G 3125:2021 equivalent Steel grades in IS 11587:1986 and the same has also been established 
through gazette on 18th August, 2022.

'Design and Manufacturing of Sea borne 20 ft containers in India'. The project is under

the Indian Standard  at the earliest  to enable them to produce the said steel. M/o Steel 
is following up with the steel producers on the progress of obtaining BIS license for  the 
aforementioned BIS Standard. 
238. All approv ed Ship Classification Societies in India have been authorised by DG 
(Shipping) to carry out testing and certification of Containers. 
239. The Ministry also informed that a  PLI scheme for manufacturing shipping containers is 
currently under consideration in MoP S&W. This Ministry has circulated the EFC note for inter 
ministerial consultations with respect to the draft PLI Scheme. Accordingly, the comments 
received from various stakeholders have been sent to DG Shipping with a request to examine the 
above comments  and accordingly conduct consultations with the stakeholders and submit the 
revised comprehensive PLI Scheme after due deliberations to this Ministry. 
240. The Ministry also informed that the key challenges in augmenting container 
manufacturing capacity were as follows :- 
 Low volume of domestic demand, primarily due to the huge cost difference between 
domestically manufactured containers and the imported ones. 
 Poor availability of CORTEN steel plates, extruded sections and corner pieces used for 
manufacturing shipping containers. 
 Steel manufacturers require a minimum order quantity of CORTEN steel in each pour, 
for the production cost to be viable; whereas one TEU container unit requires less than 3 

Page 58 of 93 
 
tons of steel materials only.  Ordering a lesser quantity of steel will considerably increase 
the price of CORTEN steel. Hence the container manufacturing industry will be viable 
only if bulk orders are received. 
 High investment in automated machinery for steel works (eg. Sheet roll forming machine 
for Corrugation of l arge steel plates, automatic welding sets etc). Automation will 
considerably reduce the production time of containers. 
 The Ministry also stated that the shipping container manufacturing industry is at a 
nascent stage and would require initial support from policymakers.  
241. The Committee observes that India requires about 3.5 lakh containers every year. 
There is no container production in India and the country has to depend mainly on C hina 
which is a global producer.  
242. The Committee notes that „Aatmanirbhar Bhar at‟ is the motto of the Government 
and „Make in India‟ is the major principal governing the manufacturing sector. The 
Committee recommends that private participation may be encouraged to achieve India ‟s 
goals in Container production, by giving various incentives.

2. At the outs et, the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee and the senior 
officers of the Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways to the meeting. The agenda of the 
meeting was deliberations on the Demands for Grants (2023 –24) of the Ministry. The Chairman 
noted that the B.E. allocations in 2023 -24 were significantly higher as compared to the 
allocations in the preceding three years and the increase was mainly in the Capital outlay. He 
sought to know the plans of the Ministry to utilise the enhanced Capital Outl ay. He   desired to 
know the performance of various schemes of the Ministry in both financial & physical terms. He 
also desired to know the reasons for slow progress under Port Modernization and the initiatives 
taken by the Ministry for promotion of coastal shipping mode of transportation.  
3. The Secretary informed the Committee that the Ministry has already attained an 
expenditure level of 75% in the allocations under R.E. 2022 -23. The Secretary then expounded 
on the major initiatives of the Ministry: 
 Promotion of Landlocked model of port development through PPP partnerships  at Major 
Ports 
 Development of Vadhavan Port in Maharashtra under the PPP model which is at the stage 
of environmental clearance 
 Development of International transhipment hub at Great Nico bar Island for which 
  Expression of Interest has been invited  
 Promotion of coastal shipping as an energy -efficient and low -cost mode of transport and 
increased cargo movement of 30 % on National Waterways in 2022. 
 Initiatives for promotion of Ease of doi ng business (EoDB)  including launching of a 
 single-window platform - National Logistics Portal -Maritime -   to enable end -to-end 
logistic solutions for maritime stakeholders for EoDB at ports, the promulgation of Major 
Ports Authorities Act, 2021, Marine  Navigation Act, 2021, Inland Vessels Act, 2021. 
 Other legislations like the Merchant Shipping Bill, the Indian Ports Bill and the Coastal 
Shipping Bill are expected to be introduced in Parliament shortly.   
 
4. Through his presentation, the Secretary briefed the Committee about the Gross Budgetary 
Support (GBS) and Internal & External Budgetary Resources (IEBR) Outlay of the Ministry. 
While the GBS for 2023 -24 was Rs. 2218.74 crores, the targeted Internal and Extra -Budgetary 
resources (IEBR) was Rs. 3633.21 crores.  The Secretary informed that the unique feature of the 
Ministry was that the IEBR was nearly 65% higher than the GBS. The ports were not being 
given any GBS but the Inland Waterways, still in a nascent stage of development, was dependent
