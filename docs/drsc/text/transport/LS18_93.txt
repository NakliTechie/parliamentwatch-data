THE APPROACH IN THE 10
TH PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2005-06
 (Recommendation at Para 9)
 
5.         The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Culture had proposed an outlay of Rs.551.12 crore and also
 got an approved outlay of the same amount with a capital component of Rs.61.80 crore.   The Committee hopes that the
 Ministry of Culture will be able to utilize the funds for implementation of new Schemes/Projects optimally. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
6.         Ministry of Culture has taken necessary steps to ensure timely utilization of the funds allocated to the various
 Schemes/Institutions during the current year.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
7.         The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry.
 
(Recommendation at Para 10)
 
8.         The Committee, however, is not convinced about the reasons cited by the Ministry that budget was passed late
 and accordingly, there was shorter time available to spend the money allocated to them and observes that if the annual
 plan starts after the beginning of the financial year itself, then it considerably reduces the capacity of the Ministry to
 plan out expenditure.   Therefore, the planning for financial progress has to start even before as the broad heads are
 already known to the Ministry under the contours of the five-year plan.  The Committee also fails to understand as to
 how this intra-year financial development should deter the Ministry in planning it in such a manner that none of the
 financial allocations made can be successfully used by the end of the year.  Therefore, the Committee strongly
 recommends that the Finance Ministry recalling the money which has been already allocated is a serious problem and
 the Ministry should take adequate care and evolve a sound method of financial planning so that such kind of
 circumstance may never occur in future.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
9.         In order to avoid reduction in our allocations, this Ministry has planned quarterly targets of funds utilization,
 which will ensure that funds are not carried to the last quarter.

 unachieved.

PROBLEM OF ENCROACHMENT AND SECURITY OF MONUMENTS/SITES UNDER THE
 PROTECTION OF ASI
(Recommendation at Para 31)
 
28.       The Committee expresses its anxiety over the incidents of encroachment upon the land of Centrally Protected
 Monuments.  The Committee is more concerned about the inability of Archeological Survey of India to get back the
 land from encroachers.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that a complete assessment should be made
 regarding encroachment of monuments/sites under the protection of Archaeological Survey of India all over the
 country.  Prompt action ought be taken against the encroachers with the help of State Governments/Local
 Administrations and the matter should be pursued at the highest level.  The Committee also recommends that adequate
 budgetary provisions should be made for security arrangements and protection of monuments, as this is the most crucial
 aspect of preserving our cultural heritage and wherever possible modern gadgets like, electronic surveillance cameras
 should be installed in the monuments.  The Committee feels that security is a sovereign function.  Hence, it is the
 responsibility of Central Government to get the monuments and other important assets protected.  In view of this, the
 Committee is of the opinion that the Union Home Ministry should pay the bills towards the expenses incurred for
 paying the Central Security Agencies under this head.  Accordingly, the Ministry of Culture should pursue the matter at
 the highest level. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
29.       The Archaeological Survey of India has undertaken an exercise to make an assessment of the encroached area of
 the Monuments/Sites which are under Central protection.  Out of 3759 protected monuments and sites, 263 monuments
 and sites are under encroachment.
 
30.       For effective action against the encroachers, all the Circle Offices of Archaeological Survey of India have been
 instructed to be vigilant and prompt in taking action against any such encroachment right from the beginning.  For
 effective monitoring, periodical report on this is being sought from each Circle.  Besides, Chief Secretaries of all States
 as well as District authorities have been requested to help ASI in the removal of encroachments.
 
31.       Instructions have been issued to all  Circle Offices that 25% of the budget under Plan should be ear-marked for
 providing fencing to the protected monuments.
 
32.       As observed by the Parliamentary Standing Committee, the Archaeological Survey of India has written to the
 Ministry of Home Affairs that the expenditure on CISF in the protected monuments may be borne by the Ministry of
 Home Affairs.  The matter will pursued with the Ministry of Home Affairs.

NATIONAL MESUEM AND MESUEM SECURITY
(Recommendation at Para 38)
 
38.       The Committee expresses its concern towards the prevailing scenario of museum security all over the country
 especially, the National Museum.  It is a matter of urgent need to have full proof security to guard the invaluable
 antiquities preserved at museums.  The Committee, therefore, recommends to acquire modern security gadgets to
 strengthen the museum security besides training of security personnel to operate on these security gadgets, since it is
 felt that modernization and security are closely inter-related.  The Committee further directs the Ministry to provide
 museums with adequate funds to strengthen their security and to appoint a consultant to solicit global expertise as this
 is a very specialized subject.  
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
39.       Security of National Museum is primarily taken care of by the C.I.S.F. headed by a Deputy Commandant. 
 Training of personnel is also done by the CISF.  The Museum has installed modern security gadgets such as X-Ray
 Machines at the gates, high-resolution sets of Walkie Takie, Door Frame Metal Detectors (DFMD), Hand Held Metal
 Detectors (HHMD) etc. in its galleries.  The Departmental Security Committee has assessed the security requirement of
 National Museum.  Adequate provision has been made in the budget for enhancement of security of National Museum
 and procurement of modern security gadgets.  An Amount of Rs.8.00 crores has been earmarked during the current
 financial year 2005-06 for upgradation of security and modernization of Museums.  The security requirements have
 been reviewed by the Ministry of Culture from time to time in consultation with the CISF, IB and Ministry of Home
 Affairs.  Recommendations of the Security Committee have been circulated to the concerned museums and they have
 been requested to implement the recommendation.

LIBRARY SECTOR

REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
63.       With regard to setting up of the National Mission for Preservation of India’s Intangible Cultural Heritage, it has
 been decided,  with the approval of Minister I&B/Culture,  to start a pilot project for preservation of India’s Intangible
