Recommendation/Observation
3. The Committee desire to know the comparison of the targets fixed by the erstwhile Planning
Commission during 12th Plan periodfor the Ministry of Shipping and the achievements of the targets
made by the Ministry at the end of the 12th Plan period. The year wise details and the percentage of
achievement etc. of each of the targets set in the Port and Shipping Sectors may be provided at the time
of submission of Action Taken Replies to this Report. The Committee notes that the trend of not utilizing
the allocated fund for various programmers in all the five years of the 12th Plan period is a bad
management and inefficiency on the part of the Ministry of Shipping. The BE got reduced sustainably
at RE levels during the Plan period and this definitely shows the Ministry in poor light. The Committee
may be informed about the steps taken by the Ministry to utilize the allocated fund effectively in future.
(Para 9)
Reply of the Government
3.1. The position with reference to the targets envisaged in the 12th Plan for the Ports Wing is as under:
(i) Traffic and Capacity Augmentation: The 12t Plan projected total capacity of the port sector
to be 2,289.04 million tonnes (1,229.24 MT - Major Ports and 1,059.80 MT.- non-Major
2

3
Ports) and over all traffic of the ports to be 1,758.26 MT (943.06 MT. - Major Ports and
815.20 MT. - Non Major Ports) By 2016-17. The total installed capacity of the Indian Ports
at the end of the 12th Plan was 2161.85 MT (1359 MTMajor Ports, 802.85 MT - Non Major
Ports) as on 31 March 2017 compared to 1245.30 MT (696.53 MT Major Ports, 548.77 MT
Non Major Ports) at the end of 11th plan. The traffic handled by the Ports at the end of the
12th Plan has been 1133.61 MT (648.40 MT Major Ports, 485.21 MT Non Major Ports) as
on 31st March 2017 compared to 913.32 MT (560.14 MT Major Ports, 353.18 MT Non
Major Ports) at the end of the 11th plan.
(ii) Dredging: The 12th Plan highlighted that drafts at Indian ports both in the channel and at
berths need to be improved. Objective should be to increase the drafts to at least 14 meters
in all ports by the end of 12th plan period and achieve 17 meters in Hub-Ports. 10 of the Major
Ports have already achieved a draft of 14 meters or more. Some of the ports like Mormugao,
Kamarajar (Ennore), have place to achieve much higher drafts.
(iii) Productivity and Dwell Time: As emphasized in the 12thPlan, successful attempts have already
been initiated for closely working with Customs Department to reduce the dwell time for
containers in Ports and CFSs. Also Direct Port Delivery facility, under the accredited client
programme has been extended by JNPT.
(iv) Containerization and Hinterland Connectivity.: The share of Containerized traffic during 2016-
17 was 19.23%. The container handling capacity of Major Ports increased from 138.88 Million
Tonnes during 2011-12 to 183.81 Million Tonnes during 2016-17 with a CAGR of 5.92% Due
focus was given in Twelfth Plan on increasing the share of containerized cargo in ports with
a view to capturing a higher share of international trade. A number of projects for rail/road
connectivity were taken up and monitored closely both for Major and non-Major Ports.
(v) Private Sector Participation: In the 12th Plan, the need for working out PPP model for
infrastructure Port development Project was stressed. Active steps have been taken to fully
utilize the potential, resources and professionalism of the private sector. These include
standardization of bidding documents, transparent bidding process for selection of PPP operators
etc. Further 100% FDI is permitted under automatic route in the port sector. These steps have
resulted in several port PPP projects coming up in the 12 Major Ports. At present, a total of
38 PPP projects are operational and 20 more PPP projects are under implementation/construction.
(vi) Non Major Ports: The share of capacity creation at the Indian Ports is constantly increasing.
The increase is due to number of private ports coming up particularly in the states of Gujarat,
Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra etc.
3.2. In order to give fillip to the infrastructure development and improve efficiency and productivity of
the ports, a number of key initiatives and reforms have been undertaken in the recent past. These include
Sagarmala Programme launched by the Government which aims at revolutionizing logistics in India
through port led development. More than 173 projects at an estimated cost of ` 4 lakh crore have been
identified in the areas of port modernization, connectivity, port led industrialization and coastal community

4
development. A benchmarking study to international standard of major ports was carried out. The study
has recommended 116 initiatives for various major ports. 87 of these initiatives have already been
implemented and remaining will be implemented by end of 2019. The implementation of these initiatives
has resulted in improvement in the efficiency and productivity of the ports. The average turn around time
of the major ports has improved considerably. It has come down from 101.76 hrs. in 2012-13 to 82.28
hrs in 2016-17.
3.3. For the IWT Sector, the 12thPlan had stipulated increase in modal share from 0.5% to 1-1.5% and
increase in BTKM from 5 BTKM to 15 BTKM. During 2012-13 to 2016-17, there has been a continuous
increase in the volume of traffic carried through the IWT mode. It has increased from 23.68 Millions
Tones in 2012-13 to 55.20 Million Tones in 2016-17. Similarly, in terms of BTKM also, IWT has
registered an upward trend from 2012 onwards. It has doubled in the five year period to reach 5.063
BTKM in 2016-17, compared to 2.488 BTKM in 2012-13.
3.4. IWAI has undertaken various projects/initiatives, which will substantially increase the volume of
cargo movement through IWT and its modal share in the coming years.
3.5. At the commencement of the 12th Plan in 2012, the indicative GBS of Ministry of Shipping was
` 6960 crore for the 12th Plan period (2012-17). The actual GBS allocation during the 12th Plan based
on annual budgetary allocations was only Rs 3258.96 crore. The expenditure under GBS upto 31/03/2017
was 3185.40 crore, which is 97.74 % of the total GBS allocations of the Ministry during the 12th plan
period. Thus, the Ministry had utilized almost the complete plan funds provided to it as per the budgetary
allocations. It may be appreciated that Ministry of Finance undertakes the budgetary allocations cross
Ministries/Departments/States depending on the overall priorities of the Government.

SHIPPING CORPORATION OF INDIA
Recommendation/Observation
23. The Committee notes the financial performance of SCI. The Committee further notes that the SCI
has now recovered from its losses and has been continuously making profit for the last three years.
However, the Committee fails to understand the reasons for incurring PBT and P AT in negative during
2017-18 (till September 2017). (Para 103)
Reply of the Government
23.1. SCI recovered from its losses due to sustained financial efforts which were, however, not supported
by any revival in the shipping markets. The period of recession in the shipping markets, intertwined with
depressed world economic activity, has lasted over nine years. However, SCI has been performing
reasonably well despite the downturn in the industry. The dynamics of the shipping industry is cyclical
in nature and is subject to vagaries of the market, combined with volatility of the rates. It may be
pertinent to highlight here that the current trough (depression) which the shipping industry has been
facing since 2008 has not yet evened out.
23.2.In 2017-18, PBT/PAT is negative till September 2017 due to steep fall in freight and charter hire
rates under the tanker segment, which is a major contributor to revenue and profit of SCI. The freight
rates are depressed due to overcapacity in the market, resulting into a severe recession in the tanker
market. The charter hire rates of Dry Bulk segment have also not fully revived. Thus, the Company has
been affected by the freight markets, added to which the T&OS division incurred a minor loss due to
loss of operating days on account of off hiring of vessels by ONGC. The benefit of the lower operating
cost in the previous years due to abysmally low crude prices has been wiped out with the steep rise in
bunker cost, which comprises almost 60% of Direct Operating expense for operating the vessels.
23.3.It may be noted that PBT is positive for nine months ending 31st December 2017 and the PAT for
nine months ending 31st December 2017 is marginally negative due to increase in tax liability.

IWT
Recommendation/Observation
33. The Committee notes that the discharge of ballast water also causes pollution. The Committee
recommends that Ministry of Shipping may take up the matter under the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan
seriously and strict protocol and control measures may be put in place to prevent pollution. The Ministry
should monitor that the protocol in this regard is strictly adhered to. (Para 154)
Reply of the Government (IWT)
33.1. The Inland Vessels (Prevention and Control of Pollution of Inland Water) Rules, 2016 have been
notified in July, 2016. These rules provide for prohibition to discharge of oil, oily mixture, providing
reception facilities at Inland Port cargo or passenger terminals and prescribes for fitment of oil mixture
treatment equipment on shore and on board vessels in certain cases. These rules are to be implemented
by the State Governments.

19
Reply of the Government
33.2. The Ministry of Shipping is mindful of the fact that the ballast water, carried in ships for maintaining
buoyancy, causes pollution in the sea/ocean. The International Maritime Organisation (IMO), in 2004,
adopted the Ballast Water Management (BWM) Convention with a view to prevent ballast water pollution.
The convention establishes standards and procedures for the management and control of ships' ballast
water and sediments. All ships will also have to carry a ballast water record book and an international
ballast water management certificate. This Convention entered into force internationally on 8 September
2017.
The Ministry of Shipping has already initiated the legislative proposal incorporating the provisions of the
BWM Convention into the Indian statute. These provisions put in place the protocol/standard and control
measures to prevent such pollution and strict monitoring thereof. The provisions include penalty for
violation of statutory provisions, setting up of reception facilities in ports, shipyards etc for ballast water
management. India intends to accede to the BWM convention. The proposal of India's accession to BWM
Convention along with the Merchant Shipping Bill 2016 was approved by the Union Cabinet in its meeting
held on 23rd November 2016.
33.3. The Merchant Shipping Bill 2016, in which the provisions of BWM Convention are incorporated,
was introduced in the Parliament on 16 December 2016 and was referred to the Parliamentary Standing
Committee. The Parliamentary Standing Committee has already presented its report which is processed
in the Ministry in the form of a draft Note for Cabinet containing proposal for official amendments to
the Bill to give effect to the Recommendation/ Observations of the Standing Committee.
33.4. However, the Ministry is not in a position to deal with ballast water pollution under the Swachh
Bharat Abhiyan (SBA) as SBA aims to clean up the streets, roads and infrastructure of India's cities,
smaller towns, and rural areas.

proposed to introduce more reforms for welfare of seafarers which are as follows: Clause 5 of the Bill
provides for Constitution of Seafarer Welfare Board.
(a) In Part V of the Bill the provisions relating to the Maritime Labour Convention (MLC) 2006
of the International Labour Organization (ILO) covers aspects like seafarers employment,
minimum age, requirement for medical fitness, employment agreements between seafarers and
employers, regulation of working hours, providing proper accommodation to seafarers, payment
of wages, responsibility towards repatriation of seafarer in case of untoward incident of
stranding/death/disability/injury/sickness of seafarer, death and disability compensation,
establishment of social security measures for seafarers etc. India has ratified the said convention
and incorporated the provisions of the convention in the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958. The
above provisions of Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 are retained in Part V of the Bill.

26
Clause 68 of the Bill empowers central government to draft rules for the employment and
welfare of seafarers in accordance with the Maritime Labour Convention.
(b) In clause 53 of the Bill the training institutes imparting maritime training to seafarers shall be
monitored w.r.t content of the course and method of training, keeping in view the standards
set in STCW convention of 1978 as amended.
(c) Recruitment of Indian Seafarers on foreign flag ships shall be mandatorily done through
authorized agencies to prevent the exploitation of seafarers by unscrupulous elements and
ensure their well being and repatriation in distress situations (sub-clause (2) of Clause 66).
(d) Seafarers held in captivity (including piracy situations) shall continue to receive wages during
the period of captivity, even after expiry of the contract (sub-clause (4) of Clause 69).
(e) Bill provides for Insurance for seafarers engaged on fishing & sailing vessels as well as small
vessels with net tonnage of less than 15 tons.
(f) With the concept of Indian controlled tonnage/BBCD/Substantial ownership, training cum
employment opportunity for seafarer is expected to rise.
39.2. The above amendments are expected to attract the seafarers towards the maritime field by ensuring
quality education and providing a safe working environment as per international standards. It would be
difficult to estimate the expected rise in number of seafarers though.
