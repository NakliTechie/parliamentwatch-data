            Demand No. 75 pertains to the Ministry of Shipping.  The Ministry of Surface Transport has been bifurcated into
 the two Ministries i.e. Ministry of Shipping and Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.  The Department of
 Shipping, which was part of the erstwhile Ministry of Surface Transport, has been notified as a separate Ministry of
 Shipping w.e.f. 7.11.2000. 
2.         The Ministry of Shipping is responsible for development, formulation and implementation of policies relating to
 Shipping, Ship-Building, Major Ports, Inland Water Transport etc.  Its main function relates to Development of Inland
 Water Transport, Maritime Shipping and Navigation, Development of Major Ports, Development of Lighthouses and
 Lightships, Shipbuilding and Chartering of Ships. 
3.         The Ministry is administratively divided into different wings, namely Shipping, Ports and Chartering of Ships. 
 The Ministry is responsible for formulation of policy regarding Inland Water Transport, Development and
 Modernisation of Major Ports, Lighthouses and Lightships, Shipbuilding Industries.

DEVELOPMENT OF MINOR PORTS
 
18.       The Committee was informed that the port traffic is on the rise steadily.  Hitherto, the sea borne trade was
 primarily served by the Major Ports.  The Major Ports for most of the time were operating beyond their capacity.  An
 urgent need was felt to enhance the capacity of the Major Ports to adequately meet the growing demand of the trade. 
 Simultaneously, minor Ports supplemented the efforts to the Major Ports by creating capacity. 
19.       As per the statistics made available by the State Governments, the cargo traffic handled at minor/intermediate
 Ports during 1999-2000 was 62.52 MT recording a growth of 72% over the previous year.  Minor Ports come under the
 purview of respective Maritime State Governments and their traffic trends are monitored by the Maritime State
 themselves. 
20.       The Secretary, in his deposition before the Committee, told that growth in traffic of minor Ports from 36 million
 tonnes in 1998-99 to about 80 million tonnes this year and 100 million tonnes approximately next year is an important
 and remarkable development in the Port Sector.  
21.       The Committee was further informed that it is expected that during the terminal year of 9
th Five Year Plan i.e.
 2001-2002, the Major Ports will be handling about 325 million tonnes of cargo, an increase of about 100 million tonnes
 over the cargo handled in the terminal year of the 8th Five Year Plan i.e. 1996-97.  On the other hand the minor/captive
 Ports will also be handling about 100 million tonnes cargo during the 2001-2002.  If the minor/captive Ports were not
 supplement the cargo handling capacity, it would not have been possible for Major Ports to handle the entire cargo.  In
 this way the major and minor Ports have to jointly service the trade.  Ministry of Shipping is supportive of the
 development of the minor/captive Ports as they offer a viable alternative to users. 
22.       The Committee appreciates the performance shown by these minor/captive Ports.  Minor Ports are serving as
 alternative of the Major Ports in some cases and minimising the extra burden on the Major Ports.  The Committee is
 happy to note that the Ministry of Shipping is giving attention to it and supporting the development of these Minor
 Ports.  The Committee is of the view that Major Ports of the country are in the process of Corporatisation and
 modernisation which will definitely enhance their Cargo handling efficiency.  There is a need to look after these minor
 Ports also.  Their modernisation is also much needed along with the modernisation of Major Ports. Minor Ports can
 play catalytic and proactive role in the development of Inland Water Transport System in the country.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that Ministry of Shipping should frame such schemes for technical/financial support to the
 respective maritime States for modernisation and development of these minor Ports, inspite of the fact that these are
 monitored and maintained by the States. 
 
CONSTRUCTION OF SINGLE BUOY MOORING (SBM)
 
23.       The Department-Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport and Tourism under took the study
 visit to Cochin and came to know the peculiar problem being faced by Cochin Port Trust by construction of Single
 Buoy Mooring (SBM).  In this connection the Committee held discussion with representatives of Cochin Port Trust and
 Kochi Refineries Ltd. 
24.       In his deposition before the Committee during the discussion, the representative of Kochi Refineries Ltd.
 informed that the present crude oil processing capacity of KRL is 7.5 million tonnes per annum.  The crude oil is
 received in tankers at specially constructed jetties in Cochin Port Trust and pumped to the Refineries’ Storage tanks
 through pipelines.  Since March 1998, Administered Pricing Mechanism (APM) was in force and the cost incurred by
 the Refineries in receiving crude oil and processing it into products was reimbursed completely by the Oil Co-
ordination Committee (OCC).  Since April 1998, the APM has been dismantled and the entire cost has to be borne by

 individual Refineries.  In this de-regulated scenario, KRL has to face competition from Private Sector Refineries in
 India as well as for imports, it was proposed to expand the crude oil processing capacity to 13.5 million tonnes per
 annum, along with measures to achieve value addition and cost reduction.  The present transportation cost of crude oil
 which contributes more than 50% of the total operating cost excluding the cost of crude oil, was identified as the most
 important area for cost reduction.  The only way to reduce cost was to receive crude oil in large ships.  However,
 Cochin Port, being an inland Port, has limitations for receiving large oil tankers.  It was in this background KRL
 thought of setting up an off-shore (i.e. 12 kms. away from the sea-shore and 6 kms. away from the Port limits) Single
 Buoy Mooring (SBM) and connected facilities which would be the cheapest option for operating the Refineries even at
 the existing level of 7.5 million tonnes.  
25.       The Committee also heard the views of the representative of the Cochin Port Trust after the presentation of the
 Representatives of Kochi Refinery Ltd. on the crucial problem faced by Cochin Port Trust.  The Committee was
 informed that in the event of setting up of the SBM by the KRL in the open sea, there would be non-utilisation of the
 existing infrastructure created by the port exclusively for the Kochi Refineries Ltd. (KRL) for handling POL products
 for their Refineries. 
26.       Cochin Port Trust developed two tanker berths way back in the 1950’s.  The Port Trust also developed an
 exclusive Oil Terminal in 1984 for handling POL products for KRL.  Other allied infrastructure such as flotilla, fire
 float, fire fighting facilities and also especially skilled manpower was created by the Port Trust exclusively for the
 Refinery.  The channel, which was 9.75 meters depth, was also dredged to 12.9 meters for handling bigger size vessels
 with a draft of 11.7 meters at Cochin Oil Terminal, by expending around Rs. 50 crores.  The total investment on the
 entire infrastructure created for the KRL would come to Rs. 100 crores.  The present move of KRL will make the entire
 Port infrastructure created exclusively for the KRL infructuous.   Once the POL handling operations are shifted to the
 proposed SBM, it will jeopardise the very survival of the Port.  The Port has already suggested to KRL that the Port
 Trust is prepared to create additional infrastructure for handling crude oil products. 
27.       The Committee wanted to know as to why the KRL did not take the Cochin Port Trust into confidence before
 going ahead with setting up of the SBM Project, especially, when the Port Trust was serving the KRL for the last so
 many years for handling POL products for the Refineries.  The Committee wanted to know the action taken by the
 Cochin Port Trust on their part after knowing about setting up of the SBM Project by KRL. 
28.       In reply, the Committee was informed by the KRL representative that the service being provided by the Cochin
 Port Trust was not considered economical in the de-regulated scenario.  Moreover, once the SBM was set up, there
 would not be any recurring loss to KRL, and the Consultant appointed to make feasibility study for the purpose opined
 that the project should be taken up at the earliest for implementation.  Any delay in its implementation would entail cost
 escalation.  So they decided to go ahead with the project independently.  It was also felt that consulting another
 organisation might delay the process.  It was mentioned that the mechanical completion of the project would take 36
 months and after that another three months for commissioning.  The Environmental Clearance for KRL’s Capacity
 Expansion Project including the crude oil receipt facilities had been obtained from the Ministry of Environment &
 Forests.  KRL is against any alternative proposal which would attract recurring expenditure on account of dredging etc. 
 Any delay in timely completion of the Project will entail cost escalation by about 25% of the total estimate.  The
 Planning Commission is also vehemently against any delay in commissioning the Project. 
29.       While clarifying the points raised by the KRL, the representative of Cochin Port Trust Stated that despite
 repeated requests by Cochin Port Trust,  KRL was extremely reluctant to share any information about their plans of
 expansion including the setting up of the SBM.  However, based on information having obtained from informal sources
 about the SBM, Cochin Port Trust immediately made representation to the Ministry of Surface Transport and the
 MOST in turn referred the matter to the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas requesting them to advise KRL to
 review the proposal in the light of Cochin Port Trust’s concern and consider alternative options available.  The
 representative also drew attention of the Committee to the minutes of proceedings of meeting, wherein representative of
 KRL stated that the management of the KRL was ready to entrust the construction and operation of the proposed SBM
 Project to Cochin Port Trust on BOT basis.  In spite of this, the KRL had gone ahead with setting up the SBM.

Construction of one berth for receiving fully loaded Suez Max vessels and light loaded VLCC.

CUSTOMS RELATED DIFFICULTIES FACED BY USER AGENCIES WORKING AT THE PORTS 
 
33.       The Committee also came to know about the Custom related problems being faced by various using agencies
 working at the Ports which in turn affecting the functioning of the Ports.   In this connection Committee during its visits
 to Cochin had the opportunity to acquaint itself to situation particularly at the Cochin Port Trust and held discussion
 with the concerned agencies.  The details of the some of the problems, which were brought to notice of the Committee,
 have been given in succeeding paragraphs.   
(i)         Issue of passes to main line operators. 
34.       The representative of the Cochin Steamer Agent’s Association Stated that the Customs do not recognize the
 Main Line Operators (MLOs) as Steamer Agents as far as containerized cargo is concerned and insist for
 filling/amendment of manifests by feeder line operators only.  If one MLO delays the submission of the Imports
 manifest of one container to the feeder line operator, the filling of the entire vessel’s containers manifest would be
 delayed resulting in delay for onward movement of containers like ICD, House De-stuffing, CFS movements etc.  In
 fact, the custodian of the cargo is the MLO.  It was stated that mammoth carriers, viz. Evergreen, Mearsk, German
 Express etc are not in a position to call directly at the Port and they can only operate through feeder lines.  In the
 circumstance, it may be considered to recognise the MLOs as Steamer Agents to amend/file manifest separately for
 each of their container, which would save a lot of time in clearance of Cargo/containers and consequential financial loss
 to the trade. 
35.       The representative of Customs Cochin clarified that as per the Customs Act, 1962, the transaction i.e.
 entry/departure/clearance/loading and unloading of the cargo in the Customs area has to be performed by the Master of
 the vessel or his authorised Agents.  As per the Act, the responsibility to account for the cargo carried by the vessel
 rests on the Master of the vessel.  If the cargo is not satisfactorily accounted for, the Master of the vessel is liable for
 penal action.  The Act, hence does not recognise Main Line Operators or feeder line operators, and expects the Master
 of the vessel to be responsible for the cargo carried by him.  Hence manifest filed by the Master of the vessel cannot be
 amended by any person other than the authorised Steamer Agents without his concurrence.  As such, it would not be
 possible to permit either the MLOs or feeder line operators other than the authorised Steamer Agents from
 filing/amending the manifest as per the provisions of the Customs Act.  The Customs do not have any control or
 jurisdiction over the Main Line Operator who operates in a far off place.  The Customs has control only over the person
 who actually brings the cargo to the Port.   The Customs Act warrants proper accountability of the cargo being brought
 to India.  At present, the feeder line operator has to depend on the MLOs for all information’s relating to the cargo
 coming through containers, i.e. the feeder line operator need not always be aware of the contents of the container.  In
 the absence of proper accountability no one could be held responsible particularly so in case contraband goods,
 narcotics or explosives are carried in containers, even though Section 116 of he Customs Act envisages taking penal
 action against the person who brings unaccounted cargo to India.  In the circumstances, without amending the relevant
 provisions in the existed Customs Act, the Customs is not in position to issue passes to the MLOs.

36.       The Committee is of the view that world over the cargo handling activities have undergone a lot of
 transformation.  If the Main Line Operators are allowed to file/amend the manifest treating them as Steamer Agents for
 their specific boxes, it will reduce the difficulties of Custom House Agents and also the duration of stay of imports
 loaded boxes at the Port.  The Committee feels that with the advancement of the containerization, there are multiple
 Steamer Agents for containers carried by a particular vessel and therefore recommends that the Main Line Operators
 should also be recognised as Steamer Agents. 
37.       The Committee is also of the view that of late the definition of cargo has changed from a physical unit to one
 which assures responsibility.  World over container traffic has been growing in a fast pace.  In the changed scenario of
 containerization the responsibility of the cargo actually rests with the main line operators.  The Customs Act was
 enacted in 1962 at a time when the concept of containerization was not there.  Amendment to the existing custom is
 inevitable to cater the needs of the Custom House Agents.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry of
 Shipping should look into the matter and approach concerned Ministry of the Government of India for suitable
 amendment in the Custom Act 1962.
 
(ii)             Customs Overtime Bookings
 
38.       The Committee was informed about the difficulty being experienced is that the Cochin Customs insist that every
 Agent/Shipper who requires the Customs clearance on Sundays/holidays and after office hours should engage the
 Customs staff on payment of overtime individually and separately though the same set of officers attend the Customs
 clearing works of many Agents.  This result in avoidable over-heads on the trade which would add up to the handling
 cost at Cochin Port.  It is reported that at JNPT, Customs staff are being booked on over time by the Port Trust and
 various parties are allowed to avail the services, and the parties themselves are not required to book the Customs
 officers separately and individually.  
39.       The representative of the Customs Cochin clarified that Customs overtime charges are to be remitted by the
 persons who avail of the services of the Custom Staff.  Port Trust being the custodian of the goods cannot avail the
 services of the Customs staff and pay for the same on behalf of other Port Users. It was ascertained from the JNPT that,
 on a trial basis, the Port Trust was permitted to make the application for booking Custom staff on overtime.  As the
 Ministry of Finance observed that this procedure was not correct, the same was discontinued.  However, Customs
 Cochin agreed to reconsider the matter and sort out the problem in consultation with the concerned parties. 
40.       The Committee is surprised to see the present system of engaging the custom officials on payment of overtime
 individually and separately for custom clearance on Sundays/holidays and after office hours which perhaps may be
 against the existing financial principles.  The Committee is unable to understand that why every Agent/Shipper is
 insisted to pay overtime to the custom officials.  The Committee is of the opinion that in the era of containerization of
 Cargoes, it is not an appreciable practice and recommends that Port Trusts should be allowed to pay overtime payments
 to the custom officials in case they handle the work on Sundays/holiday and after office hour.  The Committee
 recommends that the Ministry of Shipping should take initiative in the matter and should approach the Ministry of
 Finance to settle the matter. 
(iii)            Amendments on Orders Relating to Customs Regulations/Notifications 
41.       The Committee was informed that time to time the Ministry of Finance, issues clarifications on the orders
 relating to Customs Regulations/Notifications.  In majority of Major Ports, such clarifications are treated as
 amendments and implemented with immediate effect by the Customs except in Cochin Port.  The Cochin Customs wait
 for receipt of the Notification issued in this respect.  Which result in delay in processing of the claims in accordance
 with the amended Regulations.  While clarifying the said point, the Cochin Custom put forth their view that the
 Ministry of Finance issues notification/clarification regarding EXIM Policy, which are being implemented by Customs.
 The Ministry of Finance issues customs Notifications and clarifications.  The Notifications are issued with prospective
 effect only.  As such, Customs was not in a position to implement any amendment being made in the Customs

UNIFORMITY IN PORTS TARIFF
 
43.       The Committee was informed that the regulation is considered essential wherever the provide of services
 factions in a monopoly or near monopoly situation.  The regulatory authority is considered necessary even when the
 monopoly service provides in the State. The Major Ports Trusts Act, 1963 was amended by Port Laws (Amendment)
 Act 1997 (15 of 1997) to provide for an independent Tariff Authority for Major Ports for fixing and revising the Port
 Tariff.  An independent authority i.e. tariff authority for Major Ports (TAMP) for the above purpose was set up on 10
th
 April, 1997.  TAMP has jurisdiction only on major port trusts which are administered under the Major Port Trusts Act
 1963. 
44.       Till very recently, Indian Ports faced no competition and in most cases lay down the law in their own without
 any fear of loosing customers and facing down turn in business.   Their main concern was how to reduce the time that
 ships spent outside the berths waiting for a chance to avail of their services.  However the developments of the last few
 years have changed all this drastically.  Where formerly ships had to wait for a chance to enter on over crowded Indian
 Port, they have new choices of using developing and fast coming new Ports i.e. Minor/State Ports. In the Mumbai itself,
 Mumbai Port, Jawaharlal Nehru Port, the privately owned and managed Pipavau Port or even the newly developed
 Mundra Port further north on the west coast.  Each of these Ports is competing with each other to woo customers by
 competitive tariffs and superior service. The traffic of minor Ports from 36 million tonnes in 1998-99 to about 80
 million tonnes this year and 100 MT approximately during 2001-02 is an important remarkable development in the port
 sector.
 
45.       The Committee is of the view that in the present age of liberlisation and decentarlisation of power, monopoly of
 the State is itself going out.  To boost the Indian business and make friendly competitive environment, there is need to
 eradicate red tapism, rules & regulations to the extent as to which it is possible.  In the present scenario, minor and
 State Ports are developing with the fast pace and challenging the status of the Major Ports.  Tariff authority for Major
 Ports which regularise only the tariff for the Major Ports does not have any authority /control over the tariff at minor
 and State Ports. This situation gives special benefit of lower tariff to Minor/State Ports as to attract comparatively more
 cargo. 
46.       It is not appreciable that while facing the new challenges, the Major Ports are to be regulated in case of port
 tariff by the extra statutory/constitutional authority.  Due to the regulation of port tariff of Major Ports, the level playing
 field between Major Ports and minor/State Ports does not exist since only Major Ports come under the jurisdiction of
 regulating authority while other Ports do not.  It is a clearly discrimination towards the Major Ports of the country.  The
 Committee therefore recommends that uniformity of tariff at Ports in the country is highly required among the major,
 minor and State Ports.  In the present scenario of modernisation and Corporatisation of Ports, it is very essential that our
 Major Ports should have liberty to fix port tariff at their own.  In such case, there is no need of regulatory authority over
 Ports i.e. Tariff authority for Major Ports.  The Ministry of Shipping should seriously analyse the situation and bring
 necessary legislation in this regard to ensure uniformity in the Tariff of all Ports in the country.

HEAVY TAXATION ON INDIAN SHIPPING SECTOR
 
65.       The Committee was apprised that now a day Indian Shipping Sector is facing heavy taxation, which is
 obstructing its natural growth.  The Mudaliar Committee, constituted after independence to go into the question of
 recommendations on how to increase India’s Shipping strongly recommended that Independent India should have a
 strong merchant fleet.  Till 1975-76, Indian Shipping achieved the target of 5 MT.  After that it has not been able to
 achieve much increase in the rate of growth.  The Ninth Plan target is 9 MTs.  The Ministry is of the view that this
 target is hardly to achieve due to several reasons, which obstructs the growth of shipping sector. 
66.       The Committee further apprised that Indian Shipping Sector is yet to be provided a friendly and facilitator
 policy.  It is evident that the shipping industry does not grow with itself, as the automobile industry or any other
 industry.  Where there are a number of indigenous companies, which compete with each other.  Where as in India’s
 Shipping Sector all major concerns i.e. Shipping Corporation of India, Great Eastern or Chougule do not compete each
 other in the Indian market.  They compete with international market.  This type of situation is not favourable to India’s
 Shipping Sector. 
67.       The Committee also came to know that shipping fleets of the most of the countries are enjoying benefits of zero-
tax regimes i.e. Singapore, U.K. etc.  All of them introduced zero tax-regimes.  The rate of taxation is 1% at the most. 
 Whereas, in India, shipping sector have regular taxation, which costs from 35% to 38%.  Besides this, the five percent
 (5%) customs duty has also imposed on the import of ships, which has never been in the past.  There is no country in
 the international arena, which imposes tax on ships.  High rate of tax i.e. 25% on the interest payment of external

REPORT
file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/52ndreport.htm[28-08-2018 12:52:11]
 commercial borrowings and payment of 10% income tax of its foreign trade towards freight are other taxation
 problems, which are being faced by the shipping sector. 
68.       The Committee does not appreciate the heavy taxation imposed on Indian Shipping Sector which is
 underdeveloped and yet to have to compete with the International Shipping.  The Committee is of the view that without
 having a policy that not only facilitates but also helps in boosting the development and upliftment of the Shipping
 Industry, it is impossible to expect achievements.  In comparison to International fleet, our Indian Shipping Sector is
 facing more challenges and has not been provided any benefit of tax relaxation.  The Committee is disappointed to note
 that 5% customs duty has also imposed on the import of ships first time.  This move will definitely have negative
 impact on the Shipping sector.  The Committee therefore recommends that the matter may be taken with the Ministry of
 Finance (Department of Revenue) to review the decision of imposing custom duty of 5% on the import of ships and
 should be abolished immediately.  The Committee also recommends that the Government should give attention to the
 prevailing tax situation in the shipping sector and try to remove the obstacles which is hindering the development of
 shipping industry in India.

 enacted in 1962 at a time when the concept of containerization was not there.  Amendment to the existing custom is
 inevitable to cater the needs of the Custom House Agents.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that Ministry of
 Shipping should look into the matter and approach concerned Ministry of the Government of India for suitable
 amendment in the Custom Act 1962.

 Shipping should ensure the frequent inspections of these training institutes so as to prohibit these institutions from
 indulging in unfair means and malpractices.  The Ministry should, if necessary, amend and make more stringent
 relevant rules regulating the functioning of   these institutes. The Ministry of Shipping should also take steps to keep a
 watch on the functioning of Seamen’s Employment Offices.

HEAVY TAXATION ON INDIAN SHIPPING SECTOR
 
The Committee does not appreciate the heavy taxation imposed on Indian Shipping Sector which is underdeveloped and
 yet to have to compete with the International Shipping.  The Committee is of the view that without having a policy that
 not only facilitates but also helps in boosting the development and upliftment of the Shipping Industry, it is impossible
 to expect achievements.  In comparison to International fleet, our Indian Shipping Sector is facing more challenges and
 has not been provided any benefit of tax relaxation.  The Committee is disappointed to note that 5% customs duty has
 also imposed on the import of ships first time.  This move will definitely have negative impact on the Shipping sector. 
 The Committee therefore recommends that the matter may be taken with the Ministry of Finance (Department of
 Revenue) to review the decision of imposing custom duty of 5% on the import of ships and should be abolished
 immediately.  The Committee also recommends that the Government should give attention to the prevailing tax
 situation in the shipping sector and try to remove the obstacles which is hindering the development of shipping industry
 in India.
