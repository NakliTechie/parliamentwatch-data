REPORT
The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation of national policies and
programmes for the development and regulation of civil aviation in the country and for devising
and implementing schemes for orderly growth and expansion of civil air transport. Its functions
also extend to overseeing the provision of airport facilities, air traffic services, carriage of
passengers and goods by air, safeguarding civil aviation operations, regulation of air transport
services, licensing of aerodromes, air carriers, pilots and aircraft maintenance engineers. In
addition to framing policies, the Ministry provides guidance to the organisations in the
implementation of policy guidelines. It supervises implementation by the organisations of special
programmes of the Government, particularly those intended for weaker sections.
2. The Civil Aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organizations under
the Ministry of Civil Aviation i.e., Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil
Aviation Security. The Commission of Railway Safety deals with matters pertaining to safety in
rail travel and operations and performs certain statutory functions specified in the Indian Railway
Act and the rules framed thereunder. Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following Public Sector
Undertakings/Companies/Autonomous Bodies under its administrative control:
(i) National Aviation Company of India Limited and its subsidiaries viz.  Hotel
Corporation of India Ltd., Air India Charters Limited, Air India Air Transport
Services Limited, Air India Engineering Services Limited, Airline Allied Services
Limited, V ayudoot Limited and IAL Airport Services Limited.
(ii) Airports Authority of India
(iii) Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited
(iv) Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi

6. The Committee notes that Ministry of Civil Aviation has failed to fully utilize the
outlay approved during 2007-08. This looks something strange at time when the civil
aviation sector is witnessing an unprecedented growth for past few years. The Ministry
anticipates to utilize an amount of Rs.11211.61 crore only out of the approved allocation
of Rs.12347.09 crore during the year 2007-08, which exemplifies the poor financial
management in the Ministry of Civil Aviation. The Committee feels that there is a lack
of proper planning in the various organisations of Ministry of Civil Aviation, as a result
of which the Ministry was unable to utilize optimally the approved outlays at its disposal.
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress
upon the concerned agencies to make a realistic assessment of their financial
requirements so that the allocations made do not remain unutilized.
BUDGET 2008-09 AND ANNUAL PLAN FOR 2008-09
7. The Annual Plan outlay of the Ministry for the year 2008-09 is Rs.10031.00 crore.
8. To a specific query on the impact of Union Budget 2008-09 on the Civil Aviation sector
in the country and also about the incentives and concessions which have been announced in the
Union Budget 2008-09 for civil aviation sector, the Ministry replied that up to the financial year
2006-07, Section 10 (I5-A) of the Income Tax Act provided tax exemption on lease rentals for
lease of imported aircraft and aero engines This provision has not been continued in the Union
Budget 2008-09 and accordingly, lease of imported aircrafts and aero engines will be taxed at
rates ranging between 10 per cent and 40 per cent + surcharge etc. This makes leasing of
imported aircraft and aero engines more expensive and would adversely affect the financial health
of the aviation sector as leasing is an option exercised by many airlines in India. In terms of
concessions/initiatives, import of simulators for helicopters along with spares has been exempted
from Customs Duty in the Union Budget 2008-09.
9. The Ministry was further asked about the projected demand of the Ministry for the year
2008-09 and whether there has been any cut in its demand, if so, what was the reason extended
and which are the projects/schemes that might be affected by this cut. The Ministry replied
that for the Annual Plan 2008-09 projections in respect of Ministry of Civil Aviation was
Rs.10170.27 crores, including budgetary support of Rs.328.94 crores. Against this projection,
Planning Commission approved an outlay of Rs.10031.00 crores including budgetary support of
Rs.190.00 crores for 2008-09. Planning Commission, however, has not indicated any reason for
this cut. The scheme for setting up of Greenfield Airport at Pakyong, Sikkim will be affected
by this cut as the approved budgetary support component of Rs.190.00 crores does not include
the outlay of Rs.45.00 crores proposed to be given as grant for the scheme at Sikkim.

3
10. When the Committee pointed out the reduction in annual outlay for 2008-09 as
compared to the previous year, the Ministry replied that the Tenth Five Year Plan outlay was
Rs.12,2928 crores with a budgetary support component of Rs.400 crores. As against this, the
Eleventh Plan proposes Rs.55,302 crores with a budgetary support of Rs.1360.13 crores. This
quantum jump of more than four times is a clear indicator of the high growth potential and the
significance of the sector in the overall scheme of economic development. As for the financial
year 2008-09, the Plan outlay is Rs.10,031 crores which is almost one-fifth of the total five year
outlay.
11. The Committee feels that reduction in Budgetary support of the Ministry will
definitely have a negative impact on the expansion of aviation sector in North-Eastern
region as the approved budgetary support component does not include the outlay of
Rs.45.00 cores proposed to be given as a grant for the setting up of Greenfield Airport
at Pakyong, Sikkim. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may take this issue
with the Finance Ministry at RE stage and prevail upon them to grant more money to
ensure that works for project start well in time. The Committee expects that the
Government will ensure that the targets set for internal and extra budgetary resources
as well as external commercial borrowings from foreign banks/financial institutions for
bridging the gap in budgetary support will materialize. The Committee recommends that
the Ministry should take adequate steps during 2008-09 also to ensure optimum utilisation
of the enhanced allocations made to various organisations.

18. The Committee was informed by the Ministry that the erstwhile Air India had suffered
a provisional net loss (after tax) of Rs.447.93 crore during the year 2006-07 as against a net
profit of Rs.14.94 crore for the year 2005-06. The Ministry in its reply to the query on the
reasons for the loss has stated that this is due to settlement of wage arrears to the tune of
Rs.425 crores, decrease in load factor and fall in yield due to competition. The Ministry has also
informed that the passenger load factor has declined from 66.2 per cent to 63.8 per cent for
the year 2006-07.
19. The Committee was further informed by the Ministry that the erstwhile Indian
Airlines Limited had suffered a provisional net loss (after tax) of Rs.240.29 crore during the year
2006-07 as against a net profit of Rs.49.50 crore for the year 2005-06. The Ministry in its reply
to the query on the reasons for the loss has stated that the varied price of ATF is one of the
main causes for the loss. The Ministry also replied that on the revenue front also airlines
continued to be hit by rapidly declining passenger revenue yields on account of intense
competition and deployment of excess capacity in the market. Further, the bottom line of the
company was affected due to the writing off of the entire amount due from its subsidiary
company Airline Allied Services Limited (AASL) However, the passenger load factor has increased
from 71.6 per cent to 73.1 per cent for the year 2006-07.
20. The Committee desired to know the details of the steps taken to increase the profit/
passenger load factor of the Air India (new brand name) as the passenger load factor has a
direct impact on the profit or loss of the newly created NACIL. The Ministry in its reply stated
that the phased induction of new aircraft in the ageing fleet of Air India will have a very positive
impact in its operations. The Ministry has further stated that Purchase of 111 state-of-the-art
new aircraft with in-flight entertainment system, Refurbishment of existing aircraft with new
seats, Membership of Star Alliance, Rationalisation of schedules to eliminate overlapping of
flights, Streamlining of operations, implementation of free flow code share, Joint participation in

6
all schemes/promotions on domestic sectors, through check-in and seamless transfer at Mumbai,
Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Bangalore and Hyderabad where by passengers can connect domestic
flight to an international flight in a single check-in process, Introduction of non-stop flights from
Mumbai and Delhi to New York, Mumbai to Hong Kong and Mumbai to Singapore, Introduced
e-ticketing on select routes, Introduced city check-in, advance airport check-in, Establishment of
call centres, Introduced SMS alerts in case of cancellations/delays will yield a positive result in
its profits and an increase in the passenger load factor in the ensuing years.
21. The Committee notes that profit of both the erstwhile Air India and Indian Airlines
has gone down substantially due to varied price of ATF, rapidly declining passenger
revenue yields on account of intense competition and deployment of excess capacity in the
market, etc. The Committee hopes that merger process of both these carriers will be
completed in time and this process will help the national carrier in long run in
consolidating its position in domestic and international market. The Committee hopes that
the synergisation process would help to improve the operational efficiency of both the
carriers. However, the Committee is of the view that NACIL should adopt an aggressive
marketing strategy to face the stiff competition at global level as well as domestic level
in order to have a level playing field with that of competitors. The Committee appreciates
the steps taken by NACIL to increase the market share of Air India with the fleet
strength of around 110. The Committee desires that the number of bilateral agreements
which were signed during the last year should be materialised to the maximum possible
extent. The Committee hopes that with the induction of new aircrafts in its fleet, Air
India can regain its glory and Maharaja status. The Committee desires that the NACIL
will soar to new heights by optimally utilizing its man power and available resources which
is not available with any of its competitor. The Committee hopes to see that NACIL will
come out of the blue and make profits in near future. The Committee recommends to the
Government that they may commission a study to know how the private airlines are
functioning including the financial freedom they are enjoying, their efficiency and
customer service. The findings of the study may be used for the improvement of NACIL.
The Committee may be apprised about the findings of such a study and the steps taken
to improve the performance of NACIL.

the integrated development, expansion and modernization of the air traffic services, passenger

‘‘...A noteworthy part of the infrastructure upgradation is the work on hand at almost 50

29. When the Committee enquired about the status of international cargo hub at Nagpur, the
Ministry replied that a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) was signed in December, 2006
between Ministry of Civil Aviation, Airports Authority of India (AAI) and Government of
Maharashtra/Maharashtra Airport Development Company Limited (MADC) for transfer of Nagpur
International Airport to a Joint Venture Company for development of the airport to a Multi-modal
International Cargo Hub. After the signing of MOU, the matter was further examined and a
proposal to hand over Nagpur airport to the JVC, on the terms and conditions indicated in the
MOU, for development of Multi Modal International Cargo Hub as also permitting AAI to invest
an amount of Rs. 4.90 crore in the equity of JVC was considered. Government have since given
in-principle approval for this proposal with the direction that:
(i) The Indian Air Force (IAF) will continue to use its existing facilities in Nagpur till
appropriate alternative arrangements are put in place and that any transfer of land of
the IAF to the Joint Venture will take place only after concurrence by the Ministry
of Defence; and
(ii) A Committee comprising of Cabinet Secretary, Finance Secretary, Secretary, Ministry
of Civil Aviation and Chief Secretary, Government of Maharashtra will examine the
financial structure of the proposed JV and related issues. The deliberations of the
committee are in process.
30. The Committee expresses its apprehension over the handing over of Nagpur airport
to JVC for a token sum of Re.1/-. The Committee is not convinced by the reasons given
by the Ministry for this whole episode. The Committee feels that this will set a wrong
precedent. After investing a large amount of public money for development and
modernization of this airport, the Committee sees no logic behind the decision of the
Government in handing over the airport to a JVC virtually free of cost. The Committee
further recommends a proper valuation of the assets of the Nagpur airport including the
land may be made and brought into the Books of the JVC so that the information is
available in the public domain. The Committee may be apprised of the further
developments in this front and a copy of MOU signed with the JVC may be sent to the
Committee for perusal.

UNIFORM TAXATION OF ATF
84. The Committee finds that one of the major reasons for the poor financial performance
of the airline operators in the country is high cost of Aviation Turbine Fuel. Even though the
pricing of ATF is now on import parity basis, the rates applicable for domestic operations
continue to be significantly higher than that of international operations. The cost of A TF goes
up even further due to high rates of sales tax levied by the State Governments/Union Territory
Administration varying from 25 per cent to 30 per cent which affects the viability of domestic
airline operations.
85. The Committee while appreciating the steps taken by the State Governments
of Kerala and Andhra Pradesh in reducing the Sales Tax on ATF from 32 per cent to
4 per cent recommends that the Ministry pursue this long standing issue with other State
Governments so that uniformity of tax on ATF can be maintained. Since the Cost of fuel
is an important component of cost structure, reduction in taxes on ATF will definitely
have a positive impact on the airfares. The Committee once again recommends that
Ministry of Finance should sympathetically consider and give approval to the proposal of
Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding notifying ATF as ‘Declared Good’ under Section 14 of
Central Sales Tax Act on priority basis to all the aircrafts so that sales tax on ATF could
be contained within the maximum rate of 4 per cent. The Committee further recommends
that the Ministry of Finance may also discuss the issue with the State Governments and
try to persuade them to reduce the sales tax on ATF. The Committee is of the opinion
that the success of the concept of low cost carrier mainly depends on the taxation of ATF
which is very much on the higher side. The Committee feels that reduction in airfares
will bring more new passengers to the airports.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
ANNUAL PLAN 2007-08
The Committee notes that Ministry of Civil Aviation has failed to fully utilize the
outlay approved during 2007-08. This looks something strange at time when the civil
aviation sector is witnessing an unprecedented growth for past few years. The Ministry
anticipates to utilize an amount of Rs.11211.61 crore only out of the approved allocation
of Rs.12347.09 crore during the year 2007-08, which exemplifies the poor financial
management in the Ministry of Civil Aviation. The Committee feels that there is a lack
of proper planning in the various organisations of Ministry of Civil Aviation, as a result
of which the Ministry was unable to utilize optimally the approved outlays at its disposal.
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress
upon the concerned agencies to make a realistic assessment of their financial
requirements so that the allocations made do not remain unutilized. (Para 6)
BUDGET 2008-09 AND ANNUAL PLAN FOR 2008-09
The Committee feels that reduction in Budgetary support of the Ministry will
definitely have a negative impact on the expansion of aviation sector in North-Eastern
region as the approved budgetary support component does not include the outlay of
Rs.45.00 crores proposed to be given as a grant for the setting up of Greenfield Airport
at Pakyong, Sikkim. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may take this issue
with the Finance Ministry at RE stage and prevail upon them to grant more money to
ensure that works for project start well in time. The Committee expects that the
Government will ensure that the targets set for internal and extra budgetary resources
as well as external commercial borrowings from foreign banks/financial institutions for
bridging the gap in budgetary support will materialize. The Committee recommends that
the Ministry should take adequate steps during 2008-09 also to ensure optimum utilisation
of the enhanced allocations made to various organisations. (Para 11)
NATIONAL A VIATION COMPANY OF INDIA LIMITED
The Committee notes that profit of both the erstwhile Air India and Indian Airlines
has gone down substantially due to varied price of ATF, rapidly declining passenger
revenue yields on account of intense competition and deployment of excess capacity in the
market, etc. The Committee hopes that merger process of both these carriers will be
completed in time and this process will help the national carrier in long run in
consolidating its position in domestic and international market. The Committee hopes that
the synergisation process would help to improve the operational efficiency of both the
carriers. However, the Committee is of the view that NACIL should adopt an aggressive
marketing strategy to face the stiff competition at global level as well as domestic level
in order to have a level playing field with that of competitors. The Committee appreciates
the steps taken by NACIL to increase the market share of Air India with the fleet
strength of around 110. The Committee desires that the number of bilateral agreements
which were signed during the last year should be materialised to the maximum possible
extent. The Committee hopes that with the induction of new aircrafts in its fleet, Air
20

21
India can regain its glory and Maharaja status. The Committee desires that the NACIL
will soar to new heights by optimally utilizing its man power and available resources which
is not available with any of its competitor. The Committee hopes to see that NACIL will
come out of the red and make profits in near future. The Committee recommends to the
Government that they may commission a study to know how the private airlines are
functioning including the financial freedom they are enjoying, their efficiency and
customer service. The findings of the study may be used for the improvement of NACIL.
The Committee may be apprised about the findings of such a study and the steps taken
to improve the performance of NACIL. (Para 21)
AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA (AAI)
The Committee expresses its apprehension over the handing over of Nagpur airport
to JVC for a token sum of Re.1/-. The Committee is not convinced by the reasons given
by the Ministry for this whole episode. The Committee feels that this will set a wrong
precedent. After investing a large amount of public money for development and
modernization of this airport, the Committee sees no logic behind the decision of the
Government in handing over the airport to a JVC virtually free of cost. The Committee
further recommends a proper valuation of the assets of the Nagpur airport including the
land may be made and brought into the Books of the JVC so that the information is
available in the public domain. The Committee may be apprised of the further
developments in this front and a copy of MOU signed with the JVC may be sent to the
Committee for perusal. (Para 30)
The Committee feels that the enhanced annual outlay for Airports Authority of
India during this year will help the Authority to complete its ongoing projects in time and
match the aviation infrastructure at par with international standards and also with that
of boom in aviation sector. The Committee recommends that Airports Authority of India
should be given necessary financial freedom in selecting and executing the aviation
infrastructure projects with its potential and expertise in this field. The Committee hopes
that development of non-metro projects will be completed within the scheduled period as
these airports will help in easing the traffic congestion at metro airports. The Committee
feels that while the Airports Authority of India will develop, maintain and manage the air-
side upgradation and terminal modernization, the issue of city side development of these
airports should not become a hurdle in completing these airports. The Committee hopes
that in addition to these metro and non-metro airports, small cities having adequate
tourism potential should also be brought in the aviation map of the country and this could
be only possible by the active role of Airports Authority of India in developing unutilized
airstrips in such cities. The Committee desires that ongoing development works in the
North-Eastern sector should be given priority by the Airports Authority of India so that
necessary aviation infrastructure is developed in this region to give air connectivity to the
North-Eastern States. (Para 41)
PA WAN HANS HELICOPTERS LIMITED
The Committee is unhappy to note that the Board of Directors of Pawan Hans
Limited decided to stop the purchase of seven helicopters in 2007-08. The Committee
observes that this should not happen on the plea that in the coming year a large number
of helicopters have to be purchased and all of it can be purchased at one go. The
Committee recommends that the Pawan Hans should implement its activities in the
relevant year itself instead of postponing it year after year. (Para 48)

22
The Committee observes that the services of Pawan Hans are still underutilized.
The Committee feels that there is tremendous scope of utilizing the helicopter facility to
connect the remote tourist places. The Committee recommends that Pawan Hans
Helicopters Limited should prepare specific plans to diversify/connect to important tourist
places as a part of diversification of its operations. The Committee expects that the
approved outlay for new fleet acquisition will be fully utilized in the current financial
year. The Committee notes that entry of Pawan Hans in smaller aircraft operations will
definitely provide air connectivity in North-Eastern region and also to small cities of
tourist and commercial importance. The Committee desires to see that Pawan Hans uses
its resources to the maximum possible extent to make the Y ear of Helicopters a
successful one by expanding its horizon. (Para 52)
HOTEL CORPORATION OF INDIA LIMITED
The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction over the performance of Centaur Hotel,
Delhi which is not noteworthy. The Committee feels that the allocation of meager amount
of 6 crores will not improve its ambience which is very pathetic now. The Committee
recommends that measures should be taken by the Government to improve the
performance of the hotel with that of the other luxurious hotels in Delhi. (Para 55)
INDIRA GANDHI RASHTRIYA URAN AKADEMI
The Committee finds no merit in handing over the management of IGRUA to a
private organisation. The Committee feels that an institute receiving grants from the
Government and also having a good infrastructure which has been developed on public
money has been handed over to a private organisation with a simple excuse of
professionalisation of its management. This year also an amount of Rs. l.12 crores is
proposed to be provided by Government to IGRUA in the form grants-in-aid. The
Committee is of the opinion that simply professionalizing the management of an institute
will not give any positive output immediately. The Committee feels that the existing
management could have been given extra powers to improve the performance of this
akademi. The Committee expresses its displeasure on this issue and recommends that
remedial measures should be taken to ensure that the undertaking being managed by the
public money is not handed over to a private management on a platter. The Committee
also notes that after upgradation of infrastructure of IGRUA, the training period for pilots
will be reduced from two years to one year. The Committee hopes that this decision will
not affect the proficiency of pilots. (Para 62)
DIRECTORATE GENERAL OF CIVIL A VIATION
The Committee hopes that DGCA will utilize its annual outlay to the fullest level
possible as it plays a vital role in regulating air traffic and air safety. The Committee is
constrained to note that though civil aviation sector is undergoing a boom in the country,
the Government is still in process of identifying the requirement of manpower of DGCA.
The Committee recommends that DGCA should address the issue of shortage of manpower
with all seriousness and recruitment of manpower should be started well in time so that
its core activities do not suffer further. There are media reports that Ministry is finding
difficulty to fill up the post of Director-General in DGCA. The Committee would like that
the post is filled up with the person having technical experience in the field of Civil
Aviation sector. (Para 66)

BUREAU OF CIVIL AVIATION SECURITY
The setting up of Headquarters building has been under consideration for quite a
long time. The Committee is constrained to note that the matter could not see the light
of the day till date for the one or other reason. Delay in construction of BCAS
Headquarters building has in turn affected the setting up of Training Academy of the
BCAS and restructuring and strengthening of the BCAS. Now the land issue has been
settled in respect of BCAS headquarters building at Delhi, the Committee hopes that the
proposal for restructuring and strengthening of BCAS and setting of Training Academy
will be finalized without delay. The Committee recommends to the Ministry that all out
efforts may be made to utilize the entire money for the purposes to which it is allocated.
(Para 69)
AERO CLUB OF INDIA
The country is in need of a large number of pilots. The Committee recommends
that the Government should adequately strengthen the Aero Club of India to produce and
train more pilots. (Para 70)
NATIONAL CIVIL A VIATION POLICY
The Committee is surprised to note that the much talked National Civil Aviation
Policy is not able to see light of the day even after considered discussions by committees
after committees. This shows a poor and lackadaisical approach of the Government in
announcing an important policy which is so much relevant at the time when aviation
sector is witnessing an unimaginable growth. The Committee feels that issues relating
level playing field for stakeholders, FDI in aviation sector and other air safety issues can
be well addressed by the New Civil Aviation Policy only. The Committee recommends that
the Government should come up with the National Civil Aviation Policy at the earliest as
a large number of private players entering the aviation sector in infrastructure side as
well as in air service side. (Para 72)
CHENNAI AND KOLKATA AIRPORTS
The Committee is constrained to note the matter of modernisation of Chennai and
Kolkata has been engaging the attention of the Government for considerably long time
but the matter is still under process. The Committee reiterates its earlier
recommendation that the modernisation of Chennai and Kolkata airports should be
completed on priority so as to ease the traffic over Delhi and Mumbai airports. The
Committee also desires that both these airports may be developed as international hubs
to cater to the needs of the people in the Eastern and Southern part of the country. The
Committee recommends that Airports Authority of IndIa should be given all the financial
and operational freedoms enjoyed by any private player in the field. The procedural
wrangles and red tapism should never be allowed to crop up in the functioning of Airports
Authority of India. The Committee opines that the modernization and development of
Chennai and Kolkata airports are a golden opportunity to prove the efficiency and mettle
of the Airports Authority of India and its employees. The Committee hopes that they will
stand up to the expectations reposed on them. The Committee hopes that the Airports
Authority of India will complete all the modernisation and developmental works of
Kolkata and Chennai airports within a specified time-frame. The Committee may be

24
apprised about the progress made by Airpor ts Authority of India in modernizing the
airports at Chennai and Kolkata. (Para 76)
SHORTAGE OF PILOTS AND ATCs
The Committee anxiously notes that the vacancy of 576 ATC is overburdening the
existing staff monitoring the herculeous task. Moreover, the Government has opened
Green Field Airports at Hyderabad and Bangalore. The Committee is surprised to note
that the Government has not taken any action in advance to start the recruitment
process to meet the operational requirement of these airports.
The Committee has been drawing the attention of the Government towards the
likely shortage of manpower for growing civil aviation sector. The Committee, however, is
constrained to observe that the Government has not been serious at all on this issue.
There is now shortage of trained manpower in every area of civil aviation sector. There
is a shortage of pilots and 576 posts of ATC out of sanctioned strength of 2051 are lying
vacant. The matter of setting up of flying training academy at Gondia, establishment of
training academy for BCAS, construction of BCAS headquarters building are still under
process. In the name of bringing in professionalisation in IGRUA, the training period for
pilots have been reduced from two years to one year. Such situation could have been
avoided had the Government taken note of the recommendations of the Committee and
adopted the proactive approach in the matter. The Committee hopes that in future
Government will give necessary consideration to complete the projects in time in a time
bound manner.
The Committee hopes that provision of modern equipments at IGRUA and
establishment of new pilot training institute in Gondia will help to meet the shortage of
pilots. The Committee recommends that necessary aid/grant should be given for starting
flying institutions so that the demand of pilots can be adequately taken care of. However,
availability of proper infrastructure for training of pilots and setting up of standards for
pilots training should also get proper consideration before giving licenses to new flying
institutes. The Committee hopes that initiatives taken by the Government will match the
demand and supply of pilots and ATCs in due course.
The Committee notes that the strength of aerodrome inspectors is much less than
the sanctioned strength. The Committee recommends that Government should take
necessary steps on priority to fill the vacancies as any shortage of trained man power to
inspect the aircraft before its flying will definitely compromise air safety playing with the
lives of passengers. (Para 80, 81, 82, 83)
UNIFORM TAXATION OF ATF
The Committee while appreciating the steps taken by the State Governments
of Kerala and Andhra Pradesh in reducing the Sales Tax on ATF from 32 per cent to
4 per cent recommends that the Ministry pursue this long standing issue with other State
Governments so that uniformity of tax on ATF can be maintained. Since the cost of fuel
is an important component of cost structure, reduction in taxes on ATF will definitely
have a positive impact on the airfares. The Committee once again recommends that
Ministry of Finance should sympathetically consider and give approval to the proposal of
Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding notifying ATF as ‘Declared Good’ under Section 14 of
Central Sales Tax Act on priority basis to all the aircrafts so that sales tax on ATF could

25
be contained within the maximum rate of 4 per cent. The Committee further recommends
that the Ministry of Finance may also discuss the issue with the State Governments and
try to persuade them to reduce the sales tax on ATF. The Committee is of the opinion
that the success of the concept of low cost carrier mainly depends on the taxation of
ATF which is very much on the higher side. The Committee feels that reduction in
airfares will bring more new passengers to the airports. (Para 85)
