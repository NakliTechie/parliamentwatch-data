Report 
 India has a long coastline of about 7517 km, spread on the western and eastern shelves of 
the mainland and also along the islands. Hence, Shipping plays a pivotal role in the economic 
development of the country. India has 13 Major Ports and about 200 Minor Ports. Approximately 
95% of the country‟s trade by volume and 68% by value is moved through maritime transport. 
Therefore, development of Major & Minor Ports and harbours, human resource and finance, 
maritime safety issues, training and skill development need to be strengthened. The Ministry of 
Shipping has been entrusted with the responsibility to formulate various policies and 
programmes on these issues and implement them in an effective and efficient manner.

6. The Committee notes that as against the projected demand of Rs 3328.70 crores for 
the Ministry for FY 2020 -21, an allocation of Rs 1800.00 crores has been made, leaving a 
shortfall of Rs 1528.70 crores. In comparison to the RE 2019 -20 stage allocation of Rs 
1523.40 crores, the increase in BE 2020-21 is of Rs 276.60 crores only, which, in the opinion 
of the Committee, is inadequate and will be eaten up by inf lation. The Committee gathers 
from the information made available to it that Maritime Transport is a critical 
infrastructure for economic development of the country and approximately 95% of the 
country‟s trade by volume and 68% by value is moved through Ma ritime transport and 
Shipping continues to remain unchallenged as the world‟s most efficient means of 
transportation. 
 

 
 
5 
 
7. The Ministry of Shipping which has drawn ambitious program for Port 
infrastructure development and capacity augmentation will become h andicapped because 
of the budgetary cuts which will jeopardize the targeted outcomes. The Committee takes 
note of the fiscal constraints faced by Ministry of Finance due to moderation in economic 
growth and the impact of global slowdown. Still , while seeking a balance between public 
good and need for containing fiscal deficit, the Committee would advocate higher 
budgetary provisions in favour of  Sagarmala Scheme and Kolkata Port Trust at the RE 
2020-21 stage. The Committee recommends that when the growth mo mentum is revived 
and the additional resources become available as a result of revenue buoyancy, the 
allocation for the Ministry should be increased further.   
8.       The Committee observes that there is a huge gap between the projected demand and 
the sa nctioned budget for the Ministry for the FY 2020 -21. As against the projected 
demand of Rs. 3328.70 crores, the Ministry has been provided with Rs. 1800.00 crores only. 
Obviously, such huge variation is suggestive of the shortcomings in the budgetary planning. 
The Committee would urge upon the Ministry to exercise due diligence in the budget 
formulation with proper planning and assessment , taking into account its needs and the 
capacity to deliver the functions assigned to it. 
9. The Committee notes that against the projected demand of Rs 350.00 crores for 
development of Major Ports, the allocation in BE 2020 -21 has been reduced to nil which is 
far below Rs. 107.00 crores in BE 2019 -20. The Committee would like to be apprised of the 
reasons behind the same. Similarly, the Committee fails to comprehend that no allocation 
has been made for Sagarmala Development Company Limited (SDCL), against projected 
demand of Rs. 250.00 crores despit e the fact that  SDCL was formed to provide equity 
support for the project Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) set up by the Ports / State / Central 
Ministries and formulate plans and policies for projects under Sagarmala Programme. The 
Committee apprehends tha t the prime objective of SDCL would get diluted with no funds 
allocated therein. The Committee would like a detailed note with justifications for nil 
allocation under the head “Sagarmala Development Company”.

because a resource constrained country like Ind ia cannot afford to keep a vast chunk of its 
financial resources locked up and surrendered towards the end of financial year. The 
Committee desires to be kept apprised of the quantum of savings under the above scheme for 
2019-20.

19.  The Ministry has stated in its reply that the installed capacity of Major Ports in 2017 -18 
was 1451.19 MTPA which has increased to 1524.91MTPA in 2019 -20 (upto December). 
Further, the creation of additional capacity of 73.72 MTPA has been possibl e on account of 
completion of various infrastructure Port projects relating to mechanization, development of 
berths etc during the 2018 -19 and current year 2019 -20 (upto Dec, 2019). A list of such projects 
is at Annexure -II. 
20. The Committee observes that in Major Ports productivity in terms of ship turn -
around time, waiting time and average output ship berthday continues to be modest when 
compared with global standards and performance of Ports in developed maritime 
countries. Since globalization, rapid adoption of technology and competitiveness are critical 
objectives to the exporters and importers in India, modernization of Major Ports is critical 
to the success of their supply chain objectives. It is in this context that, the c apacity

67. Some of the main problems before the shipbuilding industry are as follows: 
(i)   Lack of economies of scale due to small order -book and due to lack of     dedicated 
domestic shipbuilding orders. 
(ii)  Lack of cheaper and long term flexible financing support from the domestic banking 
system for infrastructure investment and modernization. 
(iii)  Lack of access to cheaper working capital (including that available globally) due to 
restrictive External Commercial Borrowing norms. 
(iv)  Lack of competitive and domestic ancillary industry in the domestic industry due to 
small size and scale of the domestic shipbuilding industry thereby depending more 
on imports of shipbuilding components and equipment as well as reducing 
bargaining power on procurement of inputs.

68.    The following steps have been taken by Ministry of Shipping to promote shipbuilding 
industry:- 
(i)  Financial Assis tance Policy on Shipbuilding: To promote shipbuilding in Indian 
shipyards and to provide them a level playing field vis-a-vis foreign shipyards, the 
Union Cabinet had on December 9, 2015 approved New Shipbuilding Financial 
Assistance policy for Indian ship yards for contracts signed during a ten year period, 
viz. 2016 -2026 with budgetary projections of Rs. 4,000 crores for this period. 
Financial assistance is being granted @ 20% of contract price, fair price and actual 
receipts by the shipyard for the vessel  which is being delivered, whichever is least. 
Financial assistance is released after delivery of vessels. The rate of financial 
assistance will be reduced by 3% after every 3 years. 
(ii)    Right of Refusal to Indian Shipyards: The Union Cabinet has on 09 .12.2015 
approved that all government departments or agencies including CPSUs have to 
provide Right of First Refusal to Indian shipyards while procuring or repairing 
vessels meant for governmental or own use till 2025 after which only Indian 
shipyards would build and repair such vessels for above mentioned organizations.  
(iii)  Grant of Infrastructure Status: The Department of Economic Affairs has notified the 
inclusion of standalone „Shipyards‟ in the Harmonized Master List of 
Infrastructure Sub -sectors o n 13.04.2016. With this inclusion, shipyards will be 
able to avail flexible structuring of long term project loans, long term funding from 
Infrastructure Funds at lower rates of interest and for a longer tenure equivalent to 
the economic life of their asse ts, relaxed ECB norms, issuance of infrastructure 
bonds for meeting working capital requirements.

“We have a scheme for shipbuilding t hrough the shipyards where the private shipyards 
are also eligible to build ships and where the Government of India provides some 
percentage as subsidy. The amount of subsidy is fixed as per the time-limit.”

115. To a query whether the Kolkata Port Trust is being granted maintenance dredging 
subsidy over the years in terms of the decision of the Government, the Ministry has clarified that 
Major Ports are meeting their dredging expenditure through their Internal & Extra Budgetary 
Resources (IEBR). The internal resources are generated through port services such as pilotage, 
berth hire, storage, lease rentals etc. As regards Kolkata Port Trust, subsidy for dredging and 
river maintenance in the river Hooghly should not  be considered in isolation but viewed in the 
overall context of the national interest. 
 
116. Kolkata Port Trust established Dredger and Despatch Service and Department of Chief 
Hydraulic Engineer for undertaking dredging works and maintenance of the river  channel to 
counter the menace of siltation.  Based on the reports of several expert committees the Union 
Cabinet approved 80% river dredging subsidy from 01.04.1976.  This system of providing 
dredging subsidy is being continued and Government of India app roved dredging subsidy of 
Rs.998.08 cr. to Kolkata Port Trust for the period from 2016-17 to 2019-20. 
 
117. Further, cargo traffic has been showing a consistent growth for the last five years and the 
operating surplus of Kolkata Port Trust has improved fro m Rs.384.70 cr. in 2014 -15 to 
Rs.897.51 cr. in 2018 -19.  However, holding the Kolkata Port Trust entirely responsible for 
funding the dredging and river conservation requirements for maintenance of the 232 km channel 
may not be in the public interest.  The  river channel is vital for the existence of the port and only 
with the availability of adequate draft can the Kolkata Port justify its sustainable existence. 
 
118. The Committee notes that due to extensive use of Ganga water and bank and bed 
erosion in the upstream states, the Hooghly estuary underwent substantial changes and the 
silt that used to be flushed out into the Bay of Bengal through this river channel started to 
be deposited due to reduction in the volume of river water.  It is, therefore, essent ial that 
the existing system of subsidy for dredging and river conservancy works of the Kolkata

of time.
