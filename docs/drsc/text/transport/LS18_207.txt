RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
4. The Committee appreciates 108% increase in the Twelfth Plan allocation but takes a serious
note of the fact that during the Eleventh Plan period, of the total Gross Budgetary Support of
Rs.3337.58 crore, the Ministry could incur actual expenditure of Rs. 2151.53 crore only. With such
a poor spending during Eleventh Plan, 108% enhancement in the allocations for the Twelfth Plan,
throws a big challenge before the Ministry to optimally utilize the enhanced amount during the
Twelfth Plan. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may take necessary steps right from
the beginning itself to ensure that the on-going as well as proposed projects go as per the schedule.
No expenditure should be incurred hurriedly at the fag end of the year and it should be as per the
guidelines in this regard. Moreover, delay should also be avoided as it ultimately results into cost
and time overruns. There should not be any compromise on the quality of works.
(Para No. 6 of 194
th Report)
Reply of the Government
5. Noted for compliance.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
6. The Committee notes the reply.

3
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
7. The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry was not able to utilize its funds mainly
due to ship-building and dredging subsidy schemes. It could have been avoided with proper
assessment and foresight as these schemes are not new. Underutilization, in fact, defeats the
purpose of having a planned budget and allocations itself. The Ministry has claimed that except
IWAI, the Shipping and Port sectors have expended the allocations satisfactorily. Whereas,
compared to the BE, the expenditure incurred in the Port Sector is only 54.77% and 33.56% by
the other organization like DCI, ALHW, SCI, etc., altogether in the Shipping Sector the expenditure
is about 71.77%.
(Para No. 10)
Reply of the Government
8. Prior to formulation of budget estimates, generally Ministry ascertains from Shipyard
Association of India (SAI) as to how many ships are likely to be delivered by different yards during
the year so that adequate outlay is kept for honouring the subsidy claims of the shipyards in
respect of the vessels to be delivered. However, in the last couple of years it has generally been
observed that Shipyards have not adhered to the delivery schedule with the result that a major part
of the outlay are left unutilized with no fault of the Ministry. Although, these outlays have
substantially been reduced at RE stage but still a good part thereof remained unutilized. For the year
2012-13, Shipyard Association of India projected the requirement of Rs. 817 crores but seeing to
the past trend of expenditure, Ministry pegged the Budget Estimates at Rs. 400.00 and same was
reduced to Rs. 220.00 crores at RE stage. As the utilization of funds during the year was closely
monitored on monthly basis therefore, resulting we succeeded in utilization of 100% of the outlay
kept at the RE stage.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
9. The Committee notes the reply. The Committee however is not able to assess and
make any observation in this regard without knowing the reason as to why the Shipyards
are not being able to adhere to the delivery schedule and the efforts made to overcome
them. These reasons may not be strictly under Ministry’s control yet, it cannot remain a
mute spectator in this regard. Ministry should ensure that improved methodology be
adopted in assessing the actual requirement of funds for subsidy schemes as the current
procedure proved to be faulty and shipyards should strictly adhere to the delivery time.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
10. The Committee further observes that the B.E. allocations had been reduced in many sectors
and there are under-utilization as well. Major reductions have taken place in Ports, Ship Building
and Inland Water Transport. Only exception was Light Houses, allocation for which was marginally
increased. The Committee once again recommends that, the Ministry should project a realistic
demand, prepare right estimate and also execute its plans properly.
(Para No. 11)
Reply of the Government
11. Noted for compliance.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
12. The Committee notes the reply.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
13. The Committee observes that the GBS of 2012-13 at the BE stage was Rs. 817 crore which
was reduced to Rs.502 crore in the RE. The actual spending was Rs.494.83 crore. The Committee
feels that due to reduction/under-utilization of the allocations, the Planning Commission had to peg
the GBS during 2013-14 to Rs.852 crore even though the projected demand by the Ministry was
Rs.1329.42 crore. It can be seen that about 30% of the projected demand has been cut by the
Planning Commission. The Committee is of the view that such a cut in allocation to the Ministry
of Shipping could have been avoided if the funds were expended optimally during 2012-13 as the
Plan allocations have been decided on the basis of actual expenditure made in the previous year.
The Committee recommends that the Ministry may make it sure that its proposed plan expenditure
during 2013-14 may be kept as per schedule so that the present allocation of Rs.852 crore may
not be reduced at the RE stage as done in the previous years, leading to more cuts in the
succeeding years.
(Para No. 16)
Reply of the Government
14. Planning Commission fixes the Budget Estimate and Revised Estimate based on expenditure
trends as well as overall resource availability. In order to ensure that Plan expenditure keeps pace
with the schedule of expenditure of the projects, periodic review meetings are taken by Ports,
Shipping and IWT wings of the Ministry. Secretary (Shipping), also on a quarterly basis reviews
the Plan expenditure. The Committee’s recommendation to the Ministry that Plan expenditure during
2013-14 be kept as per schedule has been noted for compliance.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
15. The Committee notes the efforts made by the Ministry through periodic review
meetings and hopes suitable action will be taken in the light thereof so that the BE for
2013-14 is utilized optimally.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
16. The Committee feels concerned about the planning and execution of the Plan projects of
the Ministry of Shipping. Out of the Rs. 6960 crore approved allocation for the Twelfth Plan; so
far the allocation made to the Ministry is only Rs. 1354 crore in the first two years (RE of 2012-
13 Rs. 502 crore + BE of 2013-14 Rs. 852 crore). The Committee fails to understand as to how
far the Ministry will be able to achieve the targets. The Committee recommends that Ministry of
Shipping should review the pace of the on-going works and necessary corrective measures should
be taken at the earliest.
(Para No. 17)
Reply of the Government
17. The Annual Plan allocation is made by Planning Commission after making a holistic review
of the previous year’s expenditure trends and resource availability. It is expected that Annual Plan
allocation for the last three years of the Twelfth Plan would be enhanced and the Ministry would
be able to achieve the targets set for the Twelfth Plan period.
18. The Ministry, on a periodic basis reviews the progress of the Plan and Non-Plan schemes
and takes necessary mid course correction to ensure that projects are completed within time with
no cost over runs.

FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
19. The Committee appreciates the practical constraint in implementing various
schemes-plan and non-plan; Ministry has little control over certain aspects beyond the
point. However, the Committee has seen in the past also that despite mid term review and
mid-course corrections undertaken, some projects/programmes get delayed. This should not

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
20. The Committee notes that during 2013-14 the ministry of Shipping has an ambitious target
of about 32 projects involving a capacity addition of 288 MTPA in the Major Ports. However,
during the year 2012-13, against a target of 245 MTPA, the achievement was only 135 MTPA. In
this context, the committee does not know on which grounds the Maritime Agenda 2010-20 has
been fixed with a target of 3130 Million Tonne port capacity addition by the year 2020. The present
day capacity of the Major Ports is only 696.53 MT and the annual capacity addition ratio is mere
6.65%. The Committee is of the view that even the Twelfth Five Year Plan target of 1229.24 MT
by the year 2017 appears to be a distant dream with the current pace of 6.65% capacity addition
annually.
(Para No. 30)
Reply of the Government
21. During the current year 2013-14, a challenging target of 30 projects with capacity addition
of around 287 MTPA has been set for the Ministry. One project with capacity of 23.7 MTPA has
been awarded in the month of May. The Ministry is confident that the target set would be largely
achieved. Moreover 2 new Major Ports one in West Bengal and the other in Andhra Pradesh, which
will entail capacity addition of around 100 MTPA are proposed to be established during the Twelfth
Plan period.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
22. The Committee reiterates its apprehension about the ability of the Ministry to
achieve the target of capacity addition during 2013-14. The present day capacity of the
Major Port is very low vis-à-vis the Maritime Agenda 2010-20 and annual capacity addition
is merely 6.65 per cent. The Committee is surprised to note that Ministry has not
mentioned anything about the capacity addition to the existing Major Ports, instead the
capacity addition is being calculated in terms of two new ports yet to be established. It
appears that the targets in this regard are being fixed routinely without any rational basis
and no connection with past performance in this regard. The Committee is surprised at the
confidence of the Ministry to achieve the target fixed for the year 2013-14.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
23. The Committee feels that major capacity addition has been done by the private ports and
capacity addition by our Major Ports is not as much. Therefore, the Committee recommends that
Major Ports may be equipped with modern facilities so that the targets of Maritime Agenda and
that of the Twelfth Five Year Plan may be achieved in letter and spirit.
(Para No. 31)
Reply of the Government

FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
25. Equipping our Major Ports with modern facilities is the utmost necessity for

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
27. The Committee feels concerned over the negative growth of traffic during 2011-12 in the
major ports such as Mormugao (-40.46%), Visakhapatnam (-17.72%), Kolkata (-15.23%), Chennai
(-6.00%), Paradip (-2.48%) and JNPT (-1.76%). This is not a healthy trend especially at a stage
where ports are heading for capacity additions. As the Major Ports are not able to find the required
cargo as a result of ban on the mining activities, the Committee recommends that the Ports may
review the capacity addition targets keeping in view the availability of cargo they may handle in
future. The Committee recommends to explore the possibility of alternate cargos for such ports,
including cruise tourism for Mormugao Port so that negative growth in many of these Ports may
be addressed.
(Para No. 32)
Reply of the Government
28. Chennai Port Trust : The reasons for negative growth in ChPT is due to stoppage of
handling of coal from 11.5.2011 in pursuance of order of High Court of Madras and due to the
ban on export of iron-ore. Though ChPT is trying to compensate the loss with alternate cargo, the
quantity is much less than coal and iron ore which are bulk cargoes.
29. Mormugao Port Trust: Due to ban on iron ore mining and export iron-ore, traffic at the
port has declined drastically. Considering future trends port is developing Coal Terminal at Berth
No.7 on DBFOT basis and it is expected to be operational in December,2013. Capacity of berth
No. 6 is being augmented by installing Rapid in motion wagon loading facility for handling coal.
The port has constructed 450 mtrs. long new Cruise Terminal exclusively for cruise vessels. The
port is striving hard to transform the port from mono-commodity to multi-commodities. During the
year 2012-13 new commodities like wheat, maize were exported through this port. Export of
Granite has also started in 2011-12.
30. Paradip Port Trust: It is intimated that Paradip Port Trust handled 56.55 million tones of
cargo during 2012-13 against previous year tariff of 54.25 million tones exhibiting growth of
4.24%. During the year 2012-13, various cargo like Oliflux, Direct Reduced Iron and Cokebreeze
were handled in the port first time. In order to diversify the cargo handling capability for emerging
new cargo, M/s RITES has been asked to prepare a detailed study and submit a Techno-economic
report on feasibility of development of infrastructure for handling of container, liquid cargo,
LNG etc.
31. Jawahar Lal Nehru Port Trust: The negative growth at JNPT is mainly due to slow down
by PPP partners on account of drastic reduction in tariff by TAMP. So far as JNP owned and
operated terminal is concerned there is an increase in traffic of about 17.53% in terms of TEUs.

46. The Committee welcomes the new initiatives taken for establishing two new Major Ports
in the country. The Committee also observes that the Kolkata Port has been running in losses and
the Haldia Port is earning much more and the Ministry is collecting money from the Haldia Port
and spending it on Kolkata Port. However, the Haldia Port is not being developed, as is required.
The Committee recommends the Haldia Port may be developed to an independent port.

Reply of the Government
47. Kolkata Port Trust (KoPT) is a composite entity comprising Kolkata Dock System (KDS)
and Haldia Dock Complex (HDC) under one umbrella and common Board of Trustees. Consolidated
Annual Accounts of KoPT are prepared as a corporate entity and sent to the Ministry of Shipping
for laying before both the Houses of Parliament after approval of the Board of Trustees and
certification by the Govt. Audit. However, separate books of accounts are maintained in HDC and
in KDS and separate Balance Sheets are drawn up for Management Information System (MIS).
Management and control of both the docks of Kolkata Port Trust are administered by a common
Board of Trustees and the Chairman is the executive head of both the docks and also of the
organization as a whole.
48. It is to be appreciated that productivity level of the two dock systems are not comparable
because of the difference in nature of cargo. Cost of rendering services is also different for
historical and geographical reasons. KDS has to bear the expenses of about 30,000 pensioners and
therefore, it may not be proper to view the two dock systems in isolation.

A. Two-Man Committee
In 1975, Government of India in the Ministry of Shipping and Transport constituted a
Committee to examine the Working and Financial Position of the Kolkata Port and other allied
matters which included likely economic effects of Kolkata and Haldia Dock Systems operating
together as one port or separately as two ports. The Committee comprised Shri A.K. Mukherjee,
Dy. Comptroller and Auditor General of India and Shri P.R. Subramaniam, Former General Manager,
Bombay Port Trust. The findings and recommendations of the Committee submitted in June, 1976
on the issue of effect of commissioning of Haldia Dock Complex on Kolkata Dock System were
as follows :–
● Both Kolkata and Haldia Dock Systems are situated on the same river. The RT works
taken up by the Kolkata Port will have to be executed by one authority.
● In the interest of efficiency and economy, entire dredging work in the river should
remain under one authority.
● The river Bhagirathi-Hooghly is an extremely tricky waterway and therefore, hydraulic
study, river survey and river maintenance should remain under one authority to achieve
optimum result.
● With regard to the complex pilotage problem over 126 miles of fairway and shortage
of pilots, it is necessary that one authority should control the pilotage service. This will
ensure coordination of pilotage service to the best advantage of the two systems.
● As one port complex, Kolkata – Haldia will economically share the common workshop,
drydock, etc. Development of parallel facilities at Haldia will be wastage of scarce
financial resources.
● The two dock systems working as separate ports are likely to incur heavier
expenditure by way of overheads.
● As per paper prepared by IIPM, Kolkata Dock System would break even in 1987,
irrespective of operating alone or with Haldia as one unit. Haldia would break even in
1992 if it operates alone. Benefit/cost ratio from 1995 onwards would be higher for
the two dock systems operating together than if they operated as two ports.
● Port users (Chambers of Commerce, Shipping and Trade Interests, Labour Trustees,
State Government) had given memoranda or evidence showing that they were
overwhelmingly in favour of the two dock system being administered by one authority.
● If the two dock systems operated separately, overheads would be heavy, cost and
charges in Kolkata would increase, procedural difficulties would arise for exporters and
importers and competition may develop between Kolkata and Haldia for general cargo
traffic.

40
● Without any central assistance, the deficits of KDS were likely to be much less than
HDC. With dredging subsidy, KDS was likely to show surplus much earlier than Haldia.
There was very little likelihood of Haldia being saddled with the deficits of KDS if the
two systems operated as one port complex. The combined results of the two dock
systems would be better financially than are likely to be if they operate as separate
ports.
● Problem of surplus labour in Kolkata would not affect Haldia. Care should be taken
from the beginning that no restrictive practices among the labour developed at Haldia
as in Kolkata (restriction on inter-sectional movements).
● For day to day operation at Haldia, autonomy can be ensured by giving adequate
powers, both executive and financial to Haldia Management.
● In the matter of administrative arrangement for the ports or the dock systems situated
in the same river or estuary or in close proximity to another, the international trend
is to have one administrative authority. [ Eg. Port of London Authority for London and
Tilbury on Thames; Port of Rotterdam with Euro Port near Sea entrance; Bremen with
Bremerhaven; Port of Hamburg and Copshaven near the sea; Five dock systems on the
Garonne-Gironde River System in France operate under one authority; Port of
Baltimore and its new terminal at Dundalh Bay; Port of Philadelphia with facility at
Delaware Bay for deeper drafted vessels].
● It is the general practice that where the existing port authority on the river creates new
facilities down the river for obtaining deeper draft, the new dock system thus created
operates as a part of the older port. Similarly, when a number of port authorities exist
in close proximity, the tendency is to amalgamate them under one authority. This trend
is rational and allows the separate dock system to operate more economically and
optimally utilize the facilities by avoiding wasteful competition.
● In view of the above, the Committee recommended that the two dock systems should
be placed under the same Trust Body. Haldia should have an organization invested with
adequate power. It should have a General Manager ( subsequently designated as Dy.
Chairman) corresponding in status to the Dy. Chairman of Kolkata Port Trust and he
should be a member of the Board of Trustees and work directly under the Chairman
and the Board. The personnel in HDC will have separate cadre. The accounting of
Haldia and Kolkata should be separate. At the end of the month, a combined account
should be prepared treating the accounts of the two dock systems as subsidiary
accounts. Common services should be apportioned suitably. HDC should operate as
complementary to KDS as one Trust Body.
In June, 1977, Government of India in the Ministry of Shipping & Transport accepted the
recommendations of the Committee in respect of the two dock systems being placed under one
Trust Body. To ensure development of Haldia on healthy lines, Govt. issued further instructions on
the accounting system, personnel matters (separate cadres with separate promotional opportunities),
cost apportionment, major repairs to mechanical plants and machinery at KDS, not developing
restrictive labour practices at Haldia, etc.).

14.1.2.2 Procurement
As per the Board approved Purchase Procedure of DCI vide Chapter VI, Exceptions and
Deviations, Sl. No. 6.1, it is stated that tender procedure need not be followed in the
cases where the prices are controlled by Government or Government bodies, such as
POL. As such, view of the Audit that Purchase Procedure is not followed by not inviting
tender for supply of fuel and lubricants is not correct.
Based on the need for supplying fuel at dredging sites located at various ports on both
coasts, and the sourcing from IOC based on the prices controlled by Government, the
system followed by the company for procurement of fuel and lubricants is as follows:
The indent/requisition is raised by dredgers and forwarded to the concerned project
office with copy to Head Office. The indents are made taking into consideration the
available fuel on board and the requirement for the dredging operations. On receipt of
the indent, the project office intimates the bunker requirement, place and time of supply
to IOC and closely coordinates the timely fuel supplies. The payment for the supplies
is made at Head Office basing on the scheduled price lists. The fuel oil consumption is
monitored from Head Office. Since a formal system of indenting/ordering/receiving land
bill payment/monitoring is in existence, the contention of Audit that the Company did not
follow a regular internal control procedure is not correct.
The Company tried to identify suitable alternate source of supply for bunkers from other
companies like HPCL, BPCL etc., which proved unsuccessful due to non response/
insufficient infrastructure by these companies. The companies like HPCL/BPCL expect
assurance from DCI for the off take of minimum assured quantity for developing
infrastructure in ports whereas in case of IOC, the company is well established and
having fully developed facilities in all ports in India.
IOC declined to extend the credit facilities for supply of fuel and lubricants due to their
present financial crunch. All the PSU oil companies are facing similar crunch. Hence to
ensure uninterrupted supplies of fuel the request of IOC to make weekly progressive
payment of Rs. 4 crore to be adjusted against invoices for supply was accepted.
However basing on the adjustments carried out the excess paid amount if any, is being
reduced regularly from the advance payments.

14.1.2.3 Issue and consumption of fuel lubricants
The present bunkering system being followed in the Company is stated by the Audit.
However the statement that there is no laid down procedure for recording consumption
of fuel on board the dredgers is not correct. Quantities of bunkers received, consumed
and balances are recorded in the logs maintained on board dredgers. The data from these
logs is furnished to HO in the Daily Utilisation Reports (OUR). The system of reconciliation
of fuel consumption is based on the OUR and Engine Log Book data. The OUR data
received from the dredgers comprise of reserve on board along with fuel consumption
on day to day basis and is being monitored.
Supply of fuel at Sethusamudram Canal Project
Dredgers Sagar Hansa and Pacifique were hired on daily rate basis and there was no
specific clause in the Charter Party (CP) Agreements that the rate payable was subject
to any specific production guarantee. The fuel oil to be provided by charterer as per the
daily consumption given in the CPs was based on the technical specifications of the
dredgers and not related to or subject to specific production. The estimated
production(material dredged as measured in the hopper) arrived and indicated in the CPs
was only for the purpose of regularisation of cost of spares and repairs of soil touching
parts. In any case, the said estimated production cannot be taken as actual production.
Considering this estimated production as guaranteed production and evaluating excess
issue of fuel is technically not appropriate and realistic. Further technically no direct
relation can be established between the fuel consumption and production as there are
other related factors like dredging cycle, dumping distance, soil characteristics, area of
deployment and condition of equipment used - have bearing on both consumption and
production.
The designed theoretical consumption of fuel oil varies from dredger to dredger depending
on factors like design of Main Engine, dredging and other equipment, their capacities,
usage and operating performances, age of the vessel and condition, as also the type of
soil dredged. It also varies depending on the grade of fuel oil used. Variable parameters
like dredging cycle, dumping distance, area of deployment cause a major impact on fuel
consumption. In view of these conditions, direct comparison of fuel oil consumption
between two dredgers is not appropriate even though the installed power/ capacities are
similar.

14.1.2.4 Claims for reimbursement of fuel escalation
The claims for fuel escalation are raised at project offices where all claims on the
customer are made. For settling the claims, customer requires copies of bills with pass
orders as proof of payment. The delays in raising fuel escalation claims are partly due
to delay in receiving bills form IOC for adjustment after making the weekly advance.
In some instances the bills were received two to three months after supplies. This matter
is being continuously pursued with lOC. After receipt of the bills from IOC the same
are being verified for the quantity supplied, passed for payment/adjustment against
advance and forwarded to project offices for raising the claims. This process involves
time and sometimes delays are occurring in the process.
The long outstanding escalation claims are related to the long outstanding dredging
claims which are in turn due to disputes. Customers did not make payments of these
escalation claims as the related dredging claims were not accepted by them. However,
all efforts are being made to obtain payment in respect of long outstanding claims
As regards comment of Audit that due to non existence of any contractual provision
relating to levy of interest on delayed payment of fuel escalation claim, the Company
suffered interest loss, the following is submitted.
Except, Kolkata Port, all other Ports award dredging contracts basing on Tenders.
Tender clauses are specified by these Ports, which vary from port to port. The
Company, upon receiving tender documents analyzes the tender conditions and seek
necessary amendments/modifications to the tender conditions keeping in view, the
technical feasibility, commercial viability and Company’s interests. However, the tenders
being open tenders and in case the ports are not willing to modify the tender conditions
to include interest payment for delayed payment of escalation bills. The Company is left
with no option except to go by the tender conditions so that it is not disqualified due
to non-compliance of tender conditions. In fact, the Company has taken deviations and
sought payment of interest on delayed payments over the agreed time schedules in
almost all the tenders which, however, the respective ports have not agreed to.
In respect of contracts awarded on nomination basis, as most of these contracts are
awarded on long term basis, it is not possible to insist on uniform payment conditions
including escalation payments keeping in view the longstanding relationships with these ports.

14.1.2.5 Oversight Role
Governance by the Board of Directors.
The fuel consumption norms as reflected in the MOU parameter are fixed not only
basing on the OEM recommended SFOC but also the actual consumption trends
observed with the experience gained over the years. The actual performance against
MOU targets is submitted to Board of Directors on quarterly basis and the performance
is analyzed and discussed in the Board meetings.
The conclusion of the audit that targets of fuel consumption are not fixed scientifically
since MOU norms fixed are above the SFOC of the OEM, is not correct since as stated
above the targets are fixed based on OEM norms as adjusted to the actual dredging
conditions.
Review of performance by the Administrative Ministry
The parameters under various categories in MOU like, Financial, Dynamic and Sector /
Enterprise specific, and their respective weightages are determined by OPE which are
reflected in the MOU Guidelines issued by OPE each year. The tri-partite, MOU
negotiation meeting discusses various parameters selected and their weightages with due
consideration as to the “vitality” of the parameter.
Conclusion
The norms for fuel oil consumption are fixed basing on the designed theoretical
consumption norms indicated by the equipment manufacturers (OEM), as adjusted to the
actual working conditions. The consumption of fuel by a dredger varies from port to
port and from site to site within a port depending on the dredging cycle and the soil
characteristics at the site of dredging. As such arriving at a standard rate of consumption
for a dredger is difficult. However, the norms are fixed most scientifically taking various
operational parameters into account.
The procurement of the fuel oil is as per Board approved purchase procedure for
supplies whose prices are controlled by the Government. The procurement from IOC is

the dredgers at all the dredging locations in the country and at the time of requirement
by the dredgers for continuous dredging operations.

Reports sent regularly by the dredgers. Claims for fuel escalation are being made at
earliest possible time after obtaining ‘invoices’ from IOC.
Considering the above factors, the conclusion of Audit that the Company lost opportunities
to save Rs. 164.63 crore over period of four years, is hypothetical.

2. Under the Kolkata Port Trust, various agencies and firms undertake different types of cargo
handling activities at KDS and HDC for which different types of licences and/or permits
are issued by port.
3. The provisions regarding issuance of different types of Licenses/Permits were first laid
down in 1949 in the By-laws of the Commissioners for the Port of Kolkata. The licences
covered under this include the following:

(v) Hawker’s License Sec 76
4. Subsequently, as the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963 came up, which empowered the Board
of Port Trusts to pass regulations to control various activities concerning the ports. In line
with the same, the Calcutta Port Trust (Licensing of Stevedores) Regulations came in force
in 1987.
5. Although Haldia Dock came into being in the year 1977, the on board handling of cargo
have been undertaken by port through deployment of its own workforce. Shore handling of
cargo at HDC is done by different agents (commonly called as Handling Agents) appointed
by the concerned importers/ exporters. Normally, the agencies who were working at KDS
and are having Stevedoring or Clearing or Shipping Agency License were allowed to
undertake activities as Handling Agent in Haldia. No separate license was thus enforced for
undertaking such activities at HDC since beginning. It may be on the same logic that HDC
being a part and parcel of KoPT, the licenses issued from KDS are also valid as the same
are valid at Budge Budge, Diamond Harbour or Saugor which are far away places from
KDS.
6. With the introduction of private equipments in the Container Operation at KDS, the need for
license to Handling Agents was felt necessary and accordingly based on the rate for
54

55
Handling Agent’s license was first time included in the Scale of Rates on 10.03.88.
Procedure for issuance of Handling Agency License by Traffic Manager at Kolkata was
framed. The present Scale of Rates for KoPT is also having a provision of issuance of
Clearing-Forwarding/Handling Agency license for a period of two years.
7. Relevantly, till 2007, for all bulk and break bulk cargo at Haldia (excluding at mechanised
berths), transfer of cargo on shore to the hook point to stack yard and vice versa  is
arranged by the concerned importer/exporter at HDC for which they engage their service
providers (called as Handling Agents). KoPT started issuing license to them from 1988 as
stated in para 7 above. Prior to that, no license for such shore operation was in vogue;
but based on the declaration of the importer or the exporter concerned, permits were being
issued for bringing in equipments and manpower for such operation.
8. The exclusive provision of shore handling (including transportation) of bulk and break bulk
cargo at the port along with the charges thereof was first created in 2007 under Section
6 of KoPT’s SoR, which was approved by TAMP. However, beside this the said SoR, under
Note (i) of Section 6, also contained a provision as follow:
“Port reserves the right to supply or not supply labour/equipment for shore handling
operation other than tippling of wagon.”
Based on this provision, KoPT for the first time, considered engaging a contractor for
integrated ship-shore cargo handling operation at Berth No. 2 & 8 only through an open
tender floated in 2007 and ABG Haldia Bulk Terminal Ltd. was inducted as the contractor
for providing such services on behalf of KoPT, for which KoPT could levy tariff approved
by TAMP. At other berths, however, the importers/exporters continued to arrange shore
handling of cargo through their appointed agents.
9. However, consequent to the unilateral decision of M/s ABG Haldia Bulk Terminal Ltd. to
withdraw themselves from Haldia Dock, the cargo handling operation vis-à-vis  revenue
earning of KoPT faced a set back resulting in drop in revenue of KoPT. In this context,
various issues including revenue loss of KoPT as well as non-generation on income from
shore activities at other berths was raised by the both print and electronic media.
10. In course of exploring alternative avenues for additional revenue generation, discussions
were held at appropriate levels. Accordingly the issue of levy of royalty on service providers
was discussed with KoPT’s counsel Sri Jayanta Mitra, Bar-at-Law who opined in favour of
levy of royalty on the service providers under Sub-section 3A of Section 42 of the MPT
Act, 1963. The said counsel opined for levy of such royalty by appointing service providers
through open auctioning. Upon placement of proposal, levy of such royalty was approved
by the Board on 12.10.12, which, however, excluded commodities like container & liquid
cargo.
11. In Board’s meeting held on 13.12.12, it was resolved to send the Board’s aforesaid
approved proposal to the Ministry of Shipping for their necessary approval towards levy of
such royalty and auctioning of Handling Agent licenses on the basis of performance
indicators & TAMP approved tariff.
12. Relevantly, so far the Ministry has notified policy and levy only in respect of Stevedoring
Activities, which is different from shore handling operations. As gathered from the other
Indian Major Ports, shore handling operations at these ports are mostly done by the service
providers appointed by the concerned importers/exporters and no levy is imposed there on
such service providers.
