REPORT
The Ministry of Shipping has been mandated with the responsibility of formulating policy
framework concerning the shipping industry as a whole, devise programmes in keeping with the
policy and to implement the programmes through appropriate delivery mechanism. The functions
of the Ministry are broadly divided into two wings — ports and shipping. Since India has a long
coastline of about 7517 km., functioning of the Ministry assumes critical importance from
economic as well as strategic points of view. Indian Shipping Industry has, over the years played
a crucial role in the transport sector of India’s economy. Approximately 90% of the country’s trade
by volume and 70% by value are moved through Maritime Transport. In the global context,
shipping continues to remain unchallenged as the world’s most efficient means of transportation
and there is no doubt that our Country has to strive hard in order to compete with global shipping
giants.

TWELFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN (2012-17)
4. The Ministry of Shipping proposes to adopt a strategy for the Twelfth Five Year Plan to
ensure adequate investment in the Port Sector; to meet the growing capacity needs and improve
the efficiency of ports by reducing ‘dwell time’ and ‘turn around time’. One of the major
objectives is to increase the draft of all the ports to at least 14 meters by the end of the 12th Five
Year Plan . The Twelfth Plan is also focusing on increasing the share of containerized cargo in ports
with a view to capture higher share of international trade. Competitive mode of tariff fixation,

2
efficient environment clearance and land acquisition, integrating the port community system,
capacity augmentation through PPP mode, institutional reforms, etc. are the other strategies
proposed during the Plan period.
5. Planning Commission has approved a Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) of Rs.6960 crore for
the Ministry for the Twelfth Five Year Plan period. This is a 108.5% increase in over the GBS for
the Eleventh Plan period, which was Rs.3337.58 crore. Out of the total GBS approved for Twelfth
Plan, the share of Ports, Shipping and Inland Water Transport (IWT) sectors are Rs.3057.47 crore,
Rs.2402.53 crore and Rs.1500.00 crore respectively. The Committee was also informed that the
total actual expenditure incurred during the entire Eleventh Plan Period stood at Rs. 2151.53 crore only.
6. The Committee appreciates 108% increase in Twelfth Plan allocation but takes a
serious note of the fact that during the Eleventh Plan period, of the total Gross Budgetary
Support of Rs.3337.58 crore, the Ministry could incur actual expenditure of Rs.2151.53 crore
only. With such a poor spending during Eleventh Plan, 108% enhancement in the
allocations for the Twelfth Plan, throws a big challenge before the Ministry to optimally
utilize the enhanced amount during Twelfth Plan. The Committee recommends that the
Ministry may take necessary steps right from the beginning itself to ensure that the on-
going as well as proposed projects go as per the schedule. No expenditure should be incurred
hurriedly at the fag end of the year and it should be as per the guidelines in this regard.
Moreover, delay should also be avoided as it ultimately results into cost and time overruns.
There should not be any compromise on the quality of works.
ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2012-13
7. The Budget Estimate for the Ministry for Financial Year 2012-13 was Rs.817.00 crore
which was reduced to Rs.502.00 crore at RE stage. IEBR for 2012-13 of Rs.4858.47 crore,
however, was enhanced to Rs.5051.30 crore at the RE stage.
8. Ministry of Shipping has informed that against the RE allocation of Rs.502.00 crore, actual
expenditure as on 31.12.2013 is Rs.377.63 crore. While the Ports and Shipping sectors have
utilized their RE amount, Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) lagged a little bit as its
expenditure is seasonal in nature, which generally would pick up from January onwards.
9. The Secretary, Shipping informed the Committee that the reduction in allocations during the
RE stage was mainly on two accounts. Firstly, the ship-building subsidy had a provision of Rs.400
crore out of Rs.817 crore. This was an old scheme which, actually, expired in 2007. As per the
assessment made at that time, the requirements were not more than Rs.200 to Rs.220 crore.
Therefore, this had to be reduced from Rs.400 crore to Rs.200 crore at the R.E. stage. Thus, there
was a straight reduction of Rs.200 crore on account of this only. Secondly, Kolkata Port Dredging
Subsidy backlog was still being cleared for 2010-11 and 2011-12, which was about Rs.332 crore.
It was reduced to about Rs.71 crore at the R.E. stage.
10. The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry was not able to utilize its funds
mainly due to ship-building and dredging subsidy schemes. It could have been avoided with
proper assessment and foresight as these schemes are not new. Underutilization, in fact,
defeats the purpose of having a planned budget and allocations itself. The Ministry has
claimed that except IWAI, the Shipping and Port sectors have expended the allocations
satisfactorily. Whereas, compared to the BE, the expenditure incurred in the Port Sector is
only 54.77% and 33.56% by the other organizations like DCI, ALHW, SCL, etc., altogether
in the Shipping sector the expenditure is about 71.77%.
11. The Committee further observes that the B.E. allocations had been reduced in
many sectors and there are under-utilizations as well. Major reductions have taken place

3
in Ports, Ship Building and Inland Water Transport. Only exception was Light Houses,
allocation for which was marginally increased. The Committee once again recommends
that, the Ministry should project a realistic demand, prepare right estimate and also
execute its plans properly.

recommends that Ministry of Shipping should review the pace of the on-going works and
necessary corrective measures should be taken at the earliest.

19. The Eleventh Five Year Plan envisaged an increase in capacity of major ports to 1,016.55
Million Tonne (MT) by the end of 2011-12 from the pre-plan base level of 504.75 MT. Average
annual growth in capacity addition was envisaged at 15 per cent. Actual capacity addition during
the plan (2006-07 to 2011-12) was, however, moderate and showed a growth of 6.65 per cent per
annum. The cargo handling capacity at the end of March, 2012, was 696.53 MT .
20. As per Twelfth Plan projection, Ministry of Shipping has planned to increase the capacity
of Major Ports in the country during 2012-13 to 745.86 million tonnes from 670.13 million tonnes
and for Non-Major ports it is expected to increase to 447.15 million tonnes from 391.66 million
tonnes. Major Ports have identified various projects/schemes or implementation during 2012-13 to
create the targeted capacity. The Ministry of Shipping has submitted that by the end of Twelfth
Plan ( i.e. March, 2017), it is proposed to enhance the capacity of Major Ports to 1229.21 million
tonnes and Non-Major Ports to 1457.342 million tonnes. In the Maritime Agenda 2010-20 prepared
by the Ministry of Shipping, a target of 3,130 MT port capacities has been set for the year 2020.
More than 50 per cent of this capacity is to be created in the non-major ports as the traffic
handling by these ports is expected to increase to 1,280 MT.
21. The Secretary Shipping during the evidence informed that the total capacity at major ports
stood at 750 million tons on 31
st March, 2013 and the non-major ports as on 31.3.2013 was 630
million tons, the total becoming 1380 million tons at the end of the last financial year in terms of
capacity.
22. The Secretary Shipping also stated that during the year 2012-13 a record number of 32 port
projects were awarded resulting in a capacity addition of 136.75 MMTPA involving an estimated
investment of Rs. 6765.63 crore. Out of these 32 projects, 13 are on the basis of PPP and an equal
number of 13 are non-PPP and six are captive projects, with the PSUs. The PSUs will spend the
money and create those berths. The ports do not have to spend the money.

7
23. On being asked about the reasons for negative growth in the certain ports and the
requirement of starting two new major ports, the Secretary stated that in the last two years, some
of the Major Ports like Mormugao, Chennai, Kolkata and Vishakhapatnam have registered poor
growth because of ban on iron ore mining by the Supreme Court. Moreover, the slow down and
recession in the global market has also adversely impacted the traffic growth of the Major Ports
in recent times. However, the emerging global economy has opened up new avenues for the
maritime sector. Given India’s high GDP growth in the last decade, it is necessary to build adequate
infrastructure in the port sector to cope with this growth. The Maritime Agenda for the period
2010-20 prepared by the Ministry of Shipping has indicated that the traffic at Indian Ports is likely
to grow at CAGR of about 11% from 849.89 million tonnes (during 2009-10) to about 2485 million
tonnes by 2019-20. The traffic at Major Ports is likely to grow at a CAGR of about 8% from
561.09 million tonnes in 2009-10 to about 1215 million tonnes by 2019-20. Therefore, there is a
need to augment the capacity in the port sector through all means – through non-Major Ports,
additional berths/terminals in the Major Ports and setting up new Major Ports.
24. The Secretary, Ministry of Shipping confirmed that Government has taken the decision to
establish two new major ports, one at Sagar Island in West Bengal and the other in Andhra
Pradesh. These will create an additional capacity of around 100 million MMTPA. Sagar Port is at
a more advanced stage because the location has also been identified already. The State Government
of West Bengal have agreed to take 26 per cent equity in an Special Purpose Vehicle(SPV) and
remaining will be with the Kolkata Port and this SPV will then find the partner for development
of the port. There is no land acquisition involved in the development of this major port because
the land has to be reclaimed. There will be small land acquisition of about 70 hectares, which
would be required for providing road and rail connectivity from the main land to the Sagar Island.
In the case of Andhra Pradesh, there are two locations being considered right now. The proposal
that has been moved to the Cabinet is to commission the feasibility report for both these locations
and the model is again the same. There will be an SPV jointly by the concerned State Government.
They have given their consent and for the Vishakhapatnam port.
25. In addition, a new outer harbour will be developed in the Tuticorin Port at Thoothukkudi,
Tamil Nadu through PPP at an estimated cost of Rs.7,500 crore. When completed, this will add
42 million tones of capacity.

28. As regards procedural delays, the Secretary, Shipping clarified that with their initiative now,
up to Rs. 500 crore of projects, there is no need of a cabinet approval. This is in line with the
delegation of financial powers exercised by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for
highway project.
29. The major bottlenecks or delays were being caused by delays in security clearances. Now,
as per the Cabinet approved guidelines, the security inputs have to be provided by all the agencies
within three months and once those inputs are given, they will be valid for three years. This is
a major change and major de-bottlenecking in executing projects.
30. The Committee notes that during 2013-14 the Ministry of Shipping has an ambitious
target of about 32 projects involving a capacity addition of 288 MMTPA in the Major Ports.
However, during the year 2012-13, against a target of 245 MMPTA, the achievement was
only 135 MMTPA. In this context, the Committee does not know on which grounds the
Maritime Agenda 2010-20 has been fixed with a target of 3130 Million Tonne port capacity
addition by the year 2020. The present day capacity of the Major Ports is only 696.53 MT
and the annual capacity addition ratio is mere 6.65%. The Committee is of the view that
even the Twelfth Five Year Plan target of 1229.24 MT by the year 2017 appears to be a
distant dream with the current pace of 6.65% capacity addition annually.

10
31. The Committee feels that major capacity addition has been done by the private ports
and the capacity addition by our Major Ports is not as much. Therefore, the Committee
recommends that Major Ports may be equipped with modern facilities so that the targets of
Maritime Agenda and that of the Twelfth Five Year Plan may be achieved in letter and
spirit.
32. The Committee feels concerned over the negative growth of traffic during 2011-12
in the major ports such as Mormugao (-40.46%), Visakhapatnam (-17.72%), Kolkata
(-15.23%), Chennai (-6.00%), Paradip (-2.48%) and JNPT (-1.76%). This is not a healthy
trend especially at a stage where ports are heading for capacity additions. As the Major
Ports are not able to find the required cargo as a result of ban on the mining activities,
the Committee recommends that the Ports may review the capacity addition targets
keeping in view the availability of cargo they may handle in future. The Committee
recommends to explore the possibility of alternate cargos for such ports, including cruise

Kolkata and Haldia Port System
35. The Secretary Shipping during the evidence explained that Kolkata Port, is a combination
of Haldia Dock System and Kolkata Dock System. The Haldia Dock System is served by one
channel, called Haldia channel. The main problem of dredging is really in the Haldia channel. 98%
of total subsidy which the Government of India gives is spent on the Haldia channel, which has
a draft of usually 6-7 metres. Even to maintain that draft that much money has to be spent. The
Kolkata Dock System is served by the Rangfala channel which has a better draft and less
siltation.
36. In the Haldia Dock System, the capacity of available lock through which the ships pass is
already exhausted. Thus, an alternative has to be developed through a new project which is outside
the lock system. At Haldia, two major projects have already been approved. One is called the Haldia
Dock South and North, Haldia Dock-II. Like the existing dock, port is trying to develop Haldia
Dock-II which is upstream of the existing Haldia Dock, about 15 kms. off shore. It is outside
the existing dock system. Here, there will be trans-shipment. Smaller vessels will go to Haldia
Dock-II because they can’t go to Haldia Dock-I. The capacity of Haldia Dock-I is already
exhausted.
37. On the Kolkata Dock, there is not much dredging required, as the Rangfala channel is better
on this account. In 2013-14, the major project proposed is Diamond Harbour Container Terminal.
This will be on the Rangfala channel on the Kolkata Dock System. The project will be awarded
this year. This is for the development of the Kolkata Dock System.
38. Even after these developments at Kolkata and Haldia, still there will be a gap of about 52
MT because Kolkata Port has a large hinterland. Once upon a time the entire State of West Bengal,

11
Bihar, Jharkhand, and land-locked countries like Nepal, Bhutan, all used to depend on Kolkata Port.
But, now, they are getting their cargo from Paradip Port, from Visakhapatnam Port because the
Kolkata Port is not working due to lack of adequate draft. The eastern freight corridor will be
connected to the proposed Sagar Port. Once that connectivity is available, the difficulties faced by
Bihar and Jharkhand will also be solved.
39. The Ministry informed that there is a scope for development of port at Rasulpur. The
Rasulpur Port can also supplement whatever cargo we are handling at Haldia and Kolkata. The
Kolkata Port Trust was approached by the State Government in 2008 for carrying out
oceanographic survey and data. Since the Chairman of the Kolkata Port Trust is also the
conservator of the entire navigation channel for Hooghley River, they had to take permission
from the Ministry and it was immediately granted. Kolkata Port Trust will be very willing to
extend any kind of help if the State Government wants to develop it in the minor ports sector.
Whatever help was sought for by the State Government has been given to them by the
Ministry.
40. The Committee welcomes the new initiatives taken for establishing two new Major
Ports in the country. The Committee also observes that the Kolkata Port has been running
in losses and the Haldia Port is earning much more and the Ministry is collecting money
from the Haldia Port and spending it on Kolkata Port. However, the Haldia Port is not being
developed, as is required. The Committee recommends that Haldia Port may be developed
to an independent port.
41. The Committee feels that like so many Ports abroad including the Shanghai Port,
which is the number one port in the world, the Kolkata Port has the potential to grow. But,
for this the port needs to be extended to deep sea. This will enable the port to pick up the
cargo from deep sea from mother ships and then to bring in through the barges into the
mainland. For this, the facilities/manpower with the CIWTC can also be meaningfully
utilized.

The Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP)
48. The Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP) – an autonomous body – regulates the tariff
structure for all the activities of the Major Ports including private terminals, etc., located within
them. There has been a major bottleneck, both for our major ports as well as for the investors
because TAMP has to fix tariff on cost plus basis only for our major ports, whereas for non-major
ports they are not governed by TAMP. The private ports and the non-major ports are not governed
by TAMP due to which there is no level playing field and the Major ports are adversely affected
because of being governed by TAMP.
49. The Committee recommends that the review of the TAMP should be done on priority
so that the Major Ports may be able to get a level playing field as compared to its counter
parts in the non major ports.

DIRECTORATE GENERAL OF LIGHTHOUSES AND LIGHTSHIPS
81. Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships (DGLL), located in Noida, is headed by
the Director General of Lighthouses and Lightships, who advises the Government of India in the
matters relating to Lighthouses. DGLL provides service to enable the mariner to know his position
with respect to a fixed point on the land with the help of Visual Aids to Navigation such as
lighthouses, light vessels, buoys beacons and Radio Aids to Navigation like Differential Global
Positioning System (DGPS, Radar Beacons (Racons), Automatic Identification System (AIS) etc.

INLAND WATERWAY AUTHORITY OF INDIA (IWAI)
90. The IWAI was set up on 27 th October, 1986 vide Inland Waterways Authority of India
Act, 1985, for regulation and development of inland waterways for the purposes of shipping and
navigation. IWAI is primarily responsible for development, maintenance and regulation of National
Waterways (NWs). The waterways which are not declared as NWs remain under the domain of
the respective State Governments.
91. As of now, there are five National Waterways. IWAI undertakes development and
maintenance of IWT related infrastructure facilities on first three NWs. Various projects for
developing and maintaining following infrastructure on these NWs have been completed and some
are in progress.
92. At the initiative of IWAI, the concerned Ministries of Home Affairs, Department of Revenue,
Environment and Forests, Govt. of West Bengal and Bangladesh Authorities have approved the
proposal to use an alternative route bypassing Core Area of Sunderbans Tiger Reserve. This route
has been made operational w.e.f. 1
st June, 2011, within a short span of 5 months. Besides helping
in the preservation of the flora and fauna of the unique biosphere reserve of the Sundarbans, it
will prevent poaching; illegal infiltration and facilitate enhanced vigil on Indo-Bangladesh border by
Border Security Force.
93. Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport project was conceptualized by the Ministry of
External Affairs to provide an alternative connectivity of Mizoram with Haldia/Kolkata ports through
Kaladan river in Myanmar. The project envisages road transport from Mizoram to Paletwa
(Myanmar), thereafter from Paletwa to Sittwe (Myanmar) by IWT and from Sittwe to Haldia by
Maritime Shipping. The project is piloted and funded by the MEA which appointed IWAI as their
Project Development Consultant (PDC). Based on IWAIs recommendations, MEA awarded the work
to the successful bidder [M/s ESSAR projects (I) Pvt Ltd.] for construction of port and IWT
components at a cost Rs. 342 cr. The construction work of Sittwe IWT terminal is in progress
and the entire work targeted to be completed by June, 2013.

PIRACY, SAFETY AND SECURITY
108. The menace of piracy continues unabated in spite of increased naval presence in the Gulf
of Aden region and merchant ships being asked to comply with best management practices which
includes establishment of “Citadel”.
109. Shipping Ministry has stated that there are reports of more and more shipowners deploying
security guards on their ships while transiting piracy infested waters of Gulf of Aden and Arabian
sea. It has been estimated that about 35% of the total number of ships transiting these waters,
deploy armed security guards. A merchant ship arriving with weapons on board, in a commercial
port of a coastal state, would also invoke concern for customs, police and other security agencies
tasked with law enforcement and coastal security. Therefore, International Maritime Organization
(IMO) has left the decision to the concerned flag States.
110. The subject has been deliberated at length in the Ministry of Shipping and it is noted that
decision to employ armed security guards is complex one and whilst not endorsing the use of
armed guards on merchant ships, given the present serious danger to shipping from the pirates,
the Indian ship owners may deploy armed security guards, subject to their risk assessment
particularly for ships which bear slow speed and have low freeboard, and in pursuance of section
86 “Transit and transshipment of stores” 77- “Declaration by owner of baggage” and
80-Temporary Detention of Baggage of Customs Act, 1962 and section 45 A of the Indian Arms
Act, 1959.
111. The Ministry of Shipping has informed that the incidence of piracy has been of great
concern to the Government. The Government has deployed naval vessels for assistance to
merchant vessels in the piracy-affected areas. The government has also been raising the issue of
piracy and the need for more concerted international action at the meetings of the United Nations
and the International Maritime Organization (IMO). (In 2011, 237 vessels were attacked, 28 vessels
were hijacked and 1,118 crew members were taken hostage (of which 24 seafarers were killed).
As on date, there are a total 37 Indian seafarers held hostage by the Somalia pirates.
112. With regard to accidents, the Secretary, Shipping has deposed before the Committee that
New Port Entry Rules were notified, basically, to prevent old ships to call on our ports. Under
these rules, ships above 25 years are not allowed to come in unless they have a valid PNI
insurance and valid classification. This is working very well. Very old ships which are 30-35 years
old belonging to different companies were coming and causing accidents in the previous years. Last
year we had seen accident at Mumbai and every year major accidents are happening. As a result
of this initiative, there were not many major accidents this year. The Committee was also informed
that a very important Maritime Labour Convention which ensures appropriate labour conditions on
ships are made available to the workers. It is in an advanced stage of processing and we hope to
take it before the Cabinet very soon.
113. The Committee is concerned about the increasing cases of piracy and notes the
various initiatives taken for protecting the merchant ships from pirate attacks. The
Committee also shares the concerns of the Ministry of Shipping as regards deploying armed
guards in the vessels to deter the pirates on the high seas. The Committee feels that joint
efforts should be made with other coastal countries in strengthening security of our ships
passing through area of potential threat.

utilized. (Para 41)
Road Connectivity to Ports
The Committee recommends that complete port connectivity is the urgent need of
the hour and remaining work should be completed without further delay. The Ministry of
Shipping may proactively engage itself with the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways
and the State Governments to finish the remaining work. The Committee also recommends
that wherever possible, inland water transport, railways and further sea connectivity may be
explored as a part of a compact and comprehensive modal mix and the Ports should not
depend exclusively on roads. The Committee desires that status report with regard to each
major port road connectivity project may be furnished. (Para 44)
Cabotage Law
The Committee feels that change in Cabotage Law is a welcome step and hopes that
Cabotage regulations at the ICTT would attract more vessels thereby increase the revenue
earning of the Cochin Port. (Para 47)
The Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP)
The Committee recommends that the review of the TAMP should be done on priority
so that the Major Ports may be able to get a level playing field as compared to its counter
parts in the non major ports. (Para 49)
DREDGING CORPORATION OF INDIA (DCI)
The Committee observes that the DCI is presently having a total capacity of 914
lakhs cu.m. per annum. However, as per the data provided by the DCI, the dredging order
with them currently is 514 lakhs cu.m. only. The surplus capacity with them is calculated
to be 400 lakhs cu.m. That means 44% of the total dredging capacity with DCI will remain
unutilized during 2013-14. The Committee feels that the DCI can take up more work and
it, therefore, should try to get more orders. The Committee also notes the fleet strength
of the DCI is going to increase with the addition of new dredgers in the coming months.
(Para 56)
The Committee also notes that the projected dredging capacity during the terminal
year of Twelfth five year plan period for the Major ports would be 1229.24 MT which is
nearly 1.76 times of the existing capacity of the ports. The projected maintenance dredging
requirement of major and minor ports during Twelfth Plan is worked out to be 947.86 MCM.
The expected demand by the end of the Twelfth year plan in terms of cargo handling at
major Ports is 943.06 MT with an estimated annual growth of 10.98%. However, the
dredging orders of major ports awarded to DCI seem to be disappointing. The DCI is
incurring net loss of Rs. 57.15 crore during 2012-13 while the anticipated loss for 2013-14

32
is Rs. 41.45 crore. The Committee recommends that Major Ports should, apart from going
for open tenders for dredging, spare a certain portion of the dredging works to DCI so that
DCI could optimally use its capacity in the coming years. (Para 57)
As regards loss making trends of DCI in the recent years, the Committee also would
like to share its concerns over the observations given in the Audit Para No. 8 of 2012-13
regarding Dredging Corporation of India Limited which commented that:
“Management of Fuel - The Company did not focus on optimizations of expenditure on fuel
and lubricants. The company did not fix the norms for fuel consumptions rate on a
scientific assessment, followed inappropriate purchase procedure for sourcing the
requirement, did not determining competitive prices for procurement of fuel and lubricants
and had inadequate internal control procedures for procurement of fuel and lubricants.
There was no proper system and procedure to ensure regular reconciliation of fuel consumed
with the issues made, prompt raising of claims for fuel escalation as well as realizing the
amounts, linking the guaranteed performance and fuel supply rate agreed as per the
agreements for chartering dredgers. These deficiencies indicate a lack of professional
handling of fuel consumption issues.
As a result of the above deficiencies, the Company lost an opportunity to save Rs. 164.63
crore on fuel consumption during 2007-11. This amount is equal to 49 per cent of to profit
Before Tax (Rs. 335.19) reported by the Company during these years. This shows that a
professional approach in fuel management can bring in tremendous benefits to the Company
and improve its bottom line substantially.” (Para 58)
The Committee understands that audit objection is being separately addressed. It
would like to see its replies about it. The Committee, taking note of the situation,
recommends that the DCI may formulate a revised method for procurement and judicious/
economical use of fuels so that wastages may be minimized to make the company sustain
on its own. (Para 59)
SHIPPING SECTOR
The Committee notes that only 10 per cent of India’s EXIM cargo is carried by the
Indian Flag vessels. Similarly, on the shipbuilding side, our share in the global shipping
market is less than one per cent. The Committee recommends that Ministry of Shipping
needs to attend the sector for improving the prevailing situation. (Para 63)
SHIPPING CORPORATION OF INDIA LTD. (SCI)
The Committee observes that SCI is handling 32.62% of the total Indian tonnage.
However, the Committee also notes that during the year 2011-12, the SCI incurred a net
loss of Rs.428.21 crore. The total earnings and the profit both are on a downward path all
these years. In view of this, the Committee desires that clear vision, appropriate strategy
and professionalism can only help this Navratna PSU to get back its financial health.
(Para 69)
The Committee also observes that the loan liability of SCI has increased from
Rs. 6322.93 crore in 2011-12 to Rs.7289.44 crore. The total liability of SCI has increased
from Rs.7297.18 crore in 2011-12 to Rs.8725.95 crore in the year 2012-13 - an increase of
Rs.1428.77 crore. Compared to the previous year, this increase is about 20%. The
Committee observes that taking the compound interest factor also into consideration; the
total liabilities may overtake the assets of the company within a short span of time. The

33
Committee fears that SCI may go Air India way in the case of financial (mis)management.
The Committee feels that SCI may need to have a re-look at its fleet acquisition orders
already placed with a view to save it from falling into deep troubles. (Para 70)
The Committee notes the submission of SCI that “future acquisitions will be
considered based on need to acquire vessels and subject to availability of resources”. The
Committee feels that the decision on 62 vessel acquisition was taken not according to the
need to acquire vessels but the resources available for such a huge procurement. The
Committee therefore recommends that the remaining orders may not be further processed
unless and until the SCI becomes capable of bearing the liability on its own. (Para 71)
As the freight rates across all segments in the dry bulk sector were stifled by a
subdued commodity demand in the wake of frail global sentiments, the Committee feels
that the fleet acquisition plan of the SCI should be reviewed at appropriate levels and
remedial measures should be taken at the earliest. (Para 72)

PIRACY, SAFETY AND SECURITY
The Committee is concerned about the increasing cases of piracy and notes the
various initiatives taken for protecting the merchant ships from pirate attacks. The
Committee also shares the concerns of the Ministry of Shipping as regards deploying armed
guards in the vessels to deter the pirates on the high seas. The Committee feels that joint
efforts should be made with other coastal countries in strengthening security of our ships
passing through area of potential threat. (Para 113)
The Committee notes the steps taken to prevent abandoning of ships in the Indian
waters. The Committee also draws the attention towards an abandoned ship causing damage
to the Pamban Bridge of Rameshwaram. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of
Shipping may take the immediate steps to move away the ship from the site to a safe area
as a first step. The Committee also recommends for taking stringent action against the ship
owner and the Committee may be informed about the follow up action in this matter.

NORTH EAST REGION
The Committee recommends that Ministry of Shipping should work out the viable

equipment to the North-East. The only way, through which we can save this, is water

into the North-East. The Committee observes that if Dhubri, the lifeline for that entire

OTHER ISSUES
The Committee fails to understand the motive behind handing over of a profitable
entity to the private hands. The Committee also recalls the recent incidents that happened
at the Haldia Dock Complex where the two terminals were abruptly closed by the private
operator causing irreparable loss to the Government. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that Government should review any such decision, if taken, as privatization of
profitable work at the Visakhapatnam Port. (Para 121)
The Committee observes that public money goes into the building, development and
upkeep of our Public sector ports. When the matters of generating revenue out of it come,
these should not be flowing into private hands. Therefore, the Committee recommends that
this reported pilferage at Haldia Port should be checked at once and a status report should
be furnished to the Committee in this regard. The Committee also recommends that the
Ministry of Shipping should move a bill for making the necessary amendments to the Major
Port Trust Act. (Para 124)
GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The Committee observes that Fifty-six per cent of the GBS goes to the shipping
sector but there is consistent decline in the share of Indian ships in the overseas trade
from 31.5 per cent in 2000 to 13.7 per cent in 2005 and further to 7.95 per cent in 2011.
Moreover, Indian vessels are older than the international average. These are ageing very
fast. About 42 per cent of the fleet is over 20 years of age and another 11 per cent in the
age group of 16 to 20 years. In terms of container ship operation, India’s world share is
1.74 per cent. Our country’s share is only 0.7 per cent in terms of compensated gross
tonnage in the world. India is ranked 22
nd in 2011 according to UNCTAD liner shipping
industry index, one step down from 21 st position in 2004. (Para 125)
During 2012-13, major and non-major ports in India registered an increase of only
1.8 per cent in cargo throughput over 2011-12. This is mainly attributable to a decline of
3.3 per cent in the cargo handled at major ports. That is, major ports have been in the
negative and minor are growing very fast. In contrast, non-major ports’ growth increased
to 10.3 per cent in the first half of 2012-13 compared to 8.2 per cent in the corresponding
period of 2011-12. (Para 126)
The Committee observes that earlier, the SCI had decided to invest in the Vizhinjam
Port Project and no development took place in this regard later on. The natural advantages
for a 16M draft without any major dredging works are available at Vizhinjam. However, the
Ministry of Shipping has not taken any interest in materializing the project to a reality. The
proposed port is able to handle the modern mother ships and that it may be able to grab
even the business of the nearest international ports. The Committee recommends that the
Ministry of Shipping may take necessary step with the State Government to develop this
port without delay. (Para 127)
The Committee observes that major ports in the country were functioning more like
landlords than mercantile business hubs so far as use of port assets are concerned. Some
of the major ports are also acting like moneylenders because they have a lot of money
parked in their kitty. The Committee feels bad that the ports cannot decide as to how to

37
spend the amount on their modernization and expansion. The Committee recommends that
these are serious issues which need to be addressed immediately. (Para 128)
Similarly, the Committee is highly concerned about the present condition of
Murmugao Port and recommends, going into handling diversified cargo other than mineral
ores to bring it back to sustenance level. The Committee also directs to consider using port
facilities for cruise tourism activities in Goa which has lot of potential to generate revenue.
(Para 129)
