19.       The Committee notes that the outlay for non-aircraft projects is an important component of the budget provision
 for Air India Limited as the programmes/projects undertaken thereunder viz. purchase of Ground Handling Equipments,
 Engineering Workshop Equipments, Security Equipments, Aircraft Refurbishment Programme etc. are aimed at
 enhancing the operational efficiency.   The Committee expects that the allocated funds would be fully utilised during
 2006-07 and recommends that the Ministry should take suitable steps to ensure that the operational efficiency of Air
 India Limited is not compromised for want of funds.
20.       The Committee notes that Air India has been reporting net profits consistently since 2001-02 and expects that
 this healthy situation will continue. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should endeavour to focus on all the
 individual parameters that enhance the overall operational performance with the available aircraft capacity till new and
 modern aircrafts join its fleet.
21.       The Ministry has informed that the Air India’s Passenger Load Factor (PLF) during the year 2004 was around
 70% as compared to 75% of other major airlines.  As per the Air India’s estimates the desirable level of PLF should be
 in the region of 75%.  Further, the PLF of Air India has declined marginally to 69.5% in 2004-05 as compared to 70.5%
 in 2003-04 despite the increase in capacity of Air India and major capacity induction by other carriers into India.  The
 Ministry has cited the reason of ageing fleet of Air India as compared to the state-of-the-art aircrafts available with
 major airlines all over the world for the lower level of PLF.  The Ministry expects that with the induction of new
 aircrafts w.e.f. 2007, Air India would be in a position to attain the desired level of PLF.  The Ministry has also outlined
 a number of other measures initiated by Air India to substantially increase the PLF.
22.       The Ministry has also negotiated and signed new bilateral air services agreements with many countries during
 the last two years thereby, increasing the entitlements of traffic rights for operation of international air services. As per
 the data furnished to the Committee, although there has been consistent rate of growth in the number of passengers in
 international sector, the market share of the national carriers have remained stagnant at around 30% from 2002-03 to
 2004-05.
23.       The level of Passenger Load Factor has a direct impact on the profit/loss balance sheet. The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that Air India should adopt aggressive marketing strategies to improve its Passenger Load
 Factor to the desired level.  The Committee also recommends that the bilateral air services agreements should be
 leveraged to diversify the international air services and to avail the entitlements of new traffic rights to the full extent.
24.       The response of the Ministry was also sought by the Committee on the estimated benefits in monetary terms
 likely to accrue during 2006-07 on account of the new announcements made in the Annual Supplement, 2006 under the
 Foreign Trade Policy 2004-09 which, inter-alia will enable India to offer competitive fuel prices and attract mid route
 stops for international flights.  The Committee also desired to know the steps proposed by the Ministry to optimize the
 benefits of the said announcements.  The Ministry has replied that the exact scope and extent of the
 concessions/exemptions would be known only after the issue of the notifications related to the announcements made in
 the Annual Supplement, 2006 under the Foreign Trade Policy 2004-09.  However, at the current level of operations, Air
 India is anticipated to save about Rs.96.00 crore.
25.       The Committee notes that important announcements have been made in the Annual Supplement, 2006 under the
 Foreign Trade Policy 2004-09, which inter-alia, will enable the country to offer competitive fuel prices and attract mid
 route stops for international flights and they are likely to play a catalytic role in the growth of civil aviation sector. The
 Committee recommends that the Ministry should pursue the matter regarding the issuance of notifications specifying
 the scope and extent of concessions/exemptions with the Commerce Ministry/Finance Ministry for deriving the
 anticipated benefits from the announcements made in the Annual Supplement, 2006 under the Foreign Trade Policy
 2004-09.   The Committee further recommends that the Ministry should initiate suitable steps in advance to facilitate
 the National Carriers to avail the full benefits of the announcements.

 therefore lacks transparency.  The Committee feels that the impending shortage could have been anticipated early had
 there been a constant assessment of the available aircraft capacity. The Committee further recommends that adequate
 contingency plans should be prepared including standing arrangements for lease of aircrafts during exigencies by
 following the proper tendering process.

BENCHMARKING OF AIRPORTS
 
130..    With regard to the query of the Committee regarding the standard norms and definitions for benchmarking of
 airports and categorizing them as international airports, airports of world class standards etc., the Ministry has
 responded that declaration of an airport as international depends upon traffic potential and demand from airlines for
 operation of international flights. The following facilities at domestic airport are considered sufficient, at present, for
 declaring a domestic airport as ‘International airport’.
 
Runway length should be 9000 feet to cater to medium capacity long-range aircraft or equivalent type of aircraft, as
 these aircraft constitute majority of aircraft used in international operations.
 
Availability of Ground Lighting Facilities, Instrument Landing System, for operation of aircrafts at night.
 
Requirement/demand from Scheduled National /International AirlineOperators.
 
Availability of Customs, Immigration, Health, animal and plant quarantine services.
 
Bilateral agreement between the States (Nations) under which it may be offered as a point-of-call to foreign carriers for
 operations.
 
International Cargo Complex.
 
Adequate size of terminal building to handle international and transit passengers.
 
131.     Taking in account the above requirements and demand from general and other agencies, declaration of domestic
 airports as international airports are considered from time to time. Airport of World Class standards implies
 development of an airport with facilities comparable to major airports in the region.  While planning facilities at the
 airports, it is ensured that adequate facilities are provided comparable those available at major airports in the region.

The policy of opening up of international skies to Indian scheduled airlines was adopted to ensure full realization of the
 objective to enhance utilisation of traffic rights available to India.  The Committee, however notes that despite the
 permission granted to private carriers to operate services to USA and Belgium, the services have not commenced and it
 is surprising that the Ministry has left the matter to the commercial judgement of the concerned airlines.  The
 Committee is of the view that there should be proper monitoring of the permissions granted by the Ministry and
 recommends that it must be ensured that actual operations are started by the operators so that the stated objective of
 optimum utilisation of traffic rights is achieved.  The Committee also reiterates its recommendations contained in its
 96
th Report, which is reproduced below: -
 
“The Committee notes from the reply of the Ministry that there are only two minimum eligibility criteria prescribed for
 selection of scheduled domestic airlines to be allowed to operate on international routes, namely, a minimum fleet

SHORTAGE OF TRAINED MANPOWER IN CIVIL AVIATION SECTOR
 
The Committee notes that the civil aviation sector in the country is growing at a fast rate and more and more private
 carriers are joining the field.  There is also a proposal for large-scale aircrafts acquisition by Indian Airlines and Air
 India.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the various efforts at augmenting the number of trained Pilots, Air
 Traffic Control (ATC) Personnel and Service and Maintenance Engineers should receive focussed attention, so that
 there are no slippages in the targets sought to be achieved.   The Committee also recommends that   the capacity of
 upgradation of Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Academy (IGRUA) and the construction of the new Flying Training
 Academy at Gondia should be completed within a stipulated time frame.(Para 118)
 
MERGER OF AIRLINES
 
The Committee observes that of late there have been changes in the strategy of private airlines operators by way of
 consolidation of their operations.  The Committee feels that these conditions can lead to monopolistic situation in civil
 aviation sector and there is a need to check this trend.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that while formulating
 the new Civil Aviation Policy, all the consequences of merger of airlines including the matter regarding reallocation of
 infrastructural facilities at airports should be addressed properly.(Para 121)
 
ROUTE DISPERSAL GUIDELINES
 
123.     The Committee is surprised to note that no final decision has been taken by the Ministry to review the existing
 Routes Dispersal Guidelines although the internal Committee set up under the Chairmanship of Director General of
 Civil Aviation had submitted its report on 18
th March 2005. The Committee also notes that although no decision has
 been taken on the proposal to impose a surcharge to fund the minimum subsidy to be given to the successful bidder
 who offers to operate in North Eastern region, island territories and the States of Jammu & Kashmir, the proposal was
 initiated on the demands made by airlines operators for amending/scrapping of the Route Dispersal Guidelines as they
 find it uneconomical to operate to the said regions on purely commercial considerations.  The Committee is of the view
 that the demands of the private operators are unjustified as the national carriers are also operating to the said regions on
 purely social considerations. The Committee is of the opinion that the private carriers should also share the social
 obligations.  In view of the boom in the domestic passenger traffic, the Committee feels that the hitherto non-
commercial or unviable routes have been transformed into profit making routes.  The Committee recommends that the
 Ministry should make an analysis of the profit/loss on the so-called non-commercial routes before taking a final
 decisions in the matter.   The Committee is also of the opinion that the profits generated by the private carriers are used
 by them to operate flights in the non-commercial routes, if any.  In this scenario, the Committee recommends that the
 Ministry should also make a study as to whether the grant of minimum subsidy to airline operators will result in
 corresponding reduction in airfares on the main routes.(Para 123)

BENCHMARKING OF AIRPORTS
 
The Committee notes that the although there are certain pre-requisites for declaration of an airport as international
 airport, the term world class airport is an objective term depending on the availability of facilities comparable to major
 airports in the region.  The Committee is, therefore, of the opinion that the airports declared as international airports in
 the country will not certainly be of world class standards unless passenger friendly facilities are developed.  In this
 context, the Committee notes that the customer satisfaction survey has revealed that overall satisfaction level with
 Mumbai International Airport, Delhi International Airport and Delhi Domestic Airport are well below the desired
 standard of 3.5 on a five point scale and also the average obtaining in airports in the Middle East/Asia Pacific region. 
 Only the figures in respect of Mumbai Domestic Airport are near to the desired standard of 3.5 and the average
 obtaining in airports in the Middle East/Asia Pacific region.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that there is a
 need to develop suitable institutional mechanism to assess the performance standards on continuous basis and take steps
 to upgrade the same to world-class standards.(Para 133)
 
ALLOTMENT OF PARKING BASES
 
The Committee notes that the Airports Authority of India is in the process of developing a policy on the allotment of
 night parking bays to various airlines and additional parking bases are being constructed at various airports in the
 country to meet the demand.  The Committee recommends that the policy should be formulated at the earliest and the
 work relating to construction of additional parking bases at various airports should be completed within a stipulated
 time frame.  The Committee also expects that the airline operators would avail the concessions offered by the Ministry
 to decongest the bigger airports.(Para 135)
 
PACKAGES FOR OFFICIAL TRAVEL AND LEAVE TRAVEL CONCESSION (LTC)
 
The Committee notes that Air India has initiated discussions with the various Ministries in the Government of India and
 is formulating a very attractive package for Government of India personnel travelling abroad.  The Committee
 recommends that the signing of the Memoranda of Understanding with various Ministries to ensure that a large part of
 Government of India personnel travel on Air India should be completed at the earliest so that the hitherto assured
 revenue will remain with the organisation.   The Committee notes that the special package of Leave Travel Concession
 to Government of India personnel to visit Jammu and Kashmir expires in June 2006 and therefore recommends that the
 Ministry should provide additional flights till such period to the extent possible in view of the non-availability of
 accommodation in the present flight schedule.(Para 139)

NATIONAL CIVIL AVIATION POLICY
 
The Committee notes that the draft National Civil Aviation Policy has been prepared prior to March 2005 and the same
 has not been finalised so far.  The Committee feels that there is an urgent need to regulate the growth of civil aviation
 sector particularly after the entry of private sector in this field.  The Committee is of the opinion that the boom in
 passenger and cargo traffic in the domestic sector emphasizes the need to provide protection to the users of the services
 provided by the airlines operators.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the National Civil Aviation Policy
 should be formulated and announcedat the earliest to address the complex issues prevalent in the civil aviation sector of
 the country.(Para 141)
