REPORT
The civil aviation sector in the country is the world's ninth largest civil aviation market and
ranks fourth in domestic passenger volume. It is one of the growing sectors of the Indian economy.
India's scheduled airlines carried 67.73 million passengers in 2014 compared to 61.42 million
passengers in 2013. Air traffic in India grew between 20 and 40 per cent for six years starting from
2003, when the Low Cost Carrier module was launched. The civil aviation market in India is all set
to become the world's third largest by 2020. During the next five years, aircraft movements,
passengers and freight at all Indian airports are expected to grow at a rate of 4.2%, 5.3% and 5%
respectively.
2. The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation and implementation of national
policies and programmes in the civil aviation sector. The Ministry oversees the development and
regulation of civil aviation in the country including establishment of new aerodromes, maintenance
and upgradation of existing aerodromes, regulation of carriage of traffic by air and ensuring civil
aviation safety and security.
3. The civil aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organizations under the
Ministry of Civil Aviation i.e., Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation
Security. The Commission of Railway Safety, which deals with matters pertaining to safety in rail
travel and operations and performs certain statutory functions specified in the Indian Railway Act and
the Rules framed thereunder, is also under the Administrative control of Ministry of Civil Aviation.
Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following Public Sector Undertakings/Companies/Autonomous
Bodies under its administrative control:
(i) Air India Limited and its subsidiaries viz. Hotel Corporation of India Limited, Air India
Charters Limited, Air India Air Transport Services Limited, Air India Engineering
Services Limited and Airline Allied Services Limited
(ii) Airports Authority of India
(iii) Pawan Hans Limited
(iv) Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi
(v) Airports Economic Regulatory Authority of India

formulation of aviation legislation and undertake Research and Development activities in the field of

AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA (AAI)
36. Airports Authority of India (AAI) is developing airport infrastructure along the length and
breadth of the country including remote and far flung areas. AAI came into existence on 01.04.1995
with the merger of National Airports Authority and International Airports Authority of India. The
merger brought into existence a single organization entrusted with the responsibility of creating,
upgrading, maintaining and managing civil aviation infrastructure both on the ground and air space
in the country; with a view to accelerate the integrated development, expansion and modernization
of the air traffic services, passenger terminals, operational areas and cargo facilities at the airports
in the country. It manages 125 airports consisting of 68 operational airports (11 International airports,
8 Customs and 49 Domestic airports), 26 Civil Enclaves (3 International, 4 Customs and 19
Domestic) and 31 Non-Operational Domestic Airports. In addition, AAI provides Air Navigation
Services (ANS) at all civil airports including 9 other airports that are not managed by AAI. AAI also
provides Air Traffic Management Services (ATMS) over entire Indian Air Space and adjoining oceanic
areas with ground installations at all airports and 25 other locations to ensure safety of aircraft
operations. AAI is a Mini Ratna Category-1 Public Sector Enterprise.

REGULATION OF AIRFARES
122. The Committee finds that currently there is no transparent mechanism to regulate the
airfare in the country. As a result, airlines are free to charge as much as they can and the
travellers also are caught unawares about it. The Committee does not agree with the
argument of the Ministry that some Rules/Regulations under Aircraft Act, 1934 regulate
airfare in the country.
123. The Committee in view of the hardship faced by common travellers and the air travel
becoming a necessity, cannot agree with this lame excuse, which is purely technical. The said
Act and the Rules framed is more than 80 years old and the civil aviation sector during this

28
period has undergone a sea change. The Committee, therefore, strongly feels that this Act/
Rule also needs to be overhauled now. Secondly, the idea of providing a competition between
airlines in this sector has not worked. Therefore, we are facing the problem of predatory
airfare today. It may also be due to some kind of cartelization amongst the airlines. Even the
benefit of reduction in the price of ATF is also not passed on to the passengers as reduction
in air fares. Therefore, the Committee strongly feels that it is high time now that the
Ministry must step in by putting a regulatory mechanism in place to see that airfares remain
within a reasonable range. The existing system has not worked.

The Committee feels that adequate seats should be reserved for last minute booking
with affordable fares, for persons travelling in medical emergency and persons accompanying
a corpse or for the next of kin in case of bereavement in a family. The Committee also feels
that large number of students from not so well-to-do background are going for higher studies
to far off areas of the country and when they have to visit their family, they lose at least 3-
4 days in travel to and fro; they cannot afford the air travel. The Committee is of the view
that in such cases, some kind of concessional fare may be devised appropriately under social
obligation of the airlines. (Para 102)
HOTEL CORPORATION OF INDIA
The Committee would like to be informed about the logic behind non-allocation of funds
for HCIL during the current fiscal. The Committee feels that the Government needs to revisit
the necessity of the HCIL. The Committee hopes that allocation of funds to Centaur Hotel
Delhi, Centaur Hotel Srinagar and Chefair Flight Catering Delhi will be made accordingly.
The Committee expects that the ongoing renovation works in all the units of HCIL are
completed on time. (Para 108)
BUREAU OF CIVIL AVIATION SECURITY (BCAS)
The Committee notes that the BCAS was able to spend Rs. 15.14 crore only out of
Rs. 32.00 crore allocated at RE stage under Plan Head during 2014-15. The Committee would
like to know the reasons for under-utilization of funds under Plan Head. The Committee
hopes that the funds allocated for current fiscal under various Heads are utilized by BCAS
judiciously. The Committee feels that the construction of new building for BCAS should be

33
expedited and the pending issues on the project may be sorted out with the concerned
agencies. The Committee desires that filling up of vacant posts at various levels in BCAS
should get due consideration during the current fiscal also, to strengthen its security
apparatus. (Para 113)
INDIRA GANDHI RASHTRIYA URAN AKADEMI (IGRUA)
The Committee finds that IGRUA has not been allocated any grant-in-aid for the
current fiscal and Rs.13.60 crores allocated for 2014-15 could not be utilized fully. The
Committee would like to know the sources through which IGRUA will mobilize its funds so
that it can take up its pending projects, for development/expansion. (Para 115)
AERO CLUB OF INDIA
The Committee finds that Rs. 10 lakhs was provided as a Grants-in-aid to Aero Club of
India for flying training and aero sports development during 2014-15 but the Club was able
to spend Rs.3 lakhs only out of the allocation without any valid reason. The Committee also
feels that the Aero Club of India which is also working with the Ministry of Sports and Youth
Affairs and State Governments to give training to people with gliders and other equipment,
may be provided with necessary budgetary allocation at RE stage. (Para 118)
CIVIL AVIATION POLICY
The Committee has been consistently emphasizing the need to have a civil aviation
policy for such a growing sector for which India has humongous potential. Now, it is happy that
the formulation of Civil Aviation Policy is already in advance stage. The Committee feels that
the views, comments and suggestions given by the stakeholders from the aviation industry be
given due consideration in finalizing the National Civil Aviation Policy. (Para 120)
As regards creating international hubs, Kochi International Airport which is handling a
large number of domestic and international passengers and has already become a hub for
international operations especially to Gulf countries, the Committee feels, may be included.
This airport has substantial international traffic and has immense growth potential.
(Para 121)
REGULATION OF AIRFARES
The Committee finds that currently there is no transparent mechanism to regulate the
air fare in the country. As a result, airlines are free to charge as much as they can and the
travellers also are caught unawares about it. The Committee does not agree with the
argument of the Ministry that some Rules/Regulations under Aircraft Act, 1934 regulate air
fare in the country. (Para 122)
The Committee in view of the hardship faced by common travellers and the air travel
becoming a necessity, cannot agree with this lame excuse, which is purely technical. The said
Act and the Rules framed is more than 80 years old and the civil aviation sector during this
period has undergone a sea change. The Committee, therefore, strongly feels that this Act/
Rule also needs to be overhauled now. Secondly, the idea of providing a competition between
airlines in this sector has not worked. Therefore, we are facing the problem of predatory
airfare today. It may also be due to some kind of cartelization amongst the airlines. Even the
benefit of reduction in the price of ATF is also not passed on to the passengers as reduced in
air fares. Therefore, the Committee strongly feels that it is high time now that the Ministry

34
must step in by putting a regulatory mechanism in place to see that airfares remain within
a reasonable range. The existing system has not worked. (Para 123)
GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
The Committee, on scrutinizing of budget papers of Ministry of Civil Aviation finds that
the overall performance of the Ministry during the last year has been satisfactory, but
utilization of funds needs further improvement. The Committee also finds that the Union
Budget 2015-16 shows a huge gap between projected demand and actual allocation. For
example, civil aviation sector was able to get merely 71% of the projected annual plan and
49% of the previous year's allocation at RE stage. The Committee hopes that allocation of
additional funds for Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), Bureau of Civil Aviation
Security (BCAS), Air India and Airports Authority of India will help these Organizations to
complete various ongoing aviation projects under their jurisdiction. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation may take-up the requirement of budgetary
support with the Ministry of Finance at RE stage vigorously. (Para 124)
The Committee desires the creation of Civil Aviation Authority, absence of such an
agency in India was pointed out by the Federal Aviation Agency during its last audit. The
Committee also had recommended about this. Subsequently, a Bill was brought before
Parliament which was examined and reported by the Committee. The Committee hopes that
the Bill including the recommendations of this Committee in its 203rd Report (on the Civil
Aviation Authority of India Bill, 2013) presented to the Parliament in February, 2014 will be
introduced in the Parliament at the earliest. (Para 125)
The Committee also hopes that the Government will finalize the Civil Aviation Policy
on priority so that all the issues concerning aviation sector in the country are duly addressed
and the ever growing aviation sector gets much needed impetus from the Government.
(Para 126)
