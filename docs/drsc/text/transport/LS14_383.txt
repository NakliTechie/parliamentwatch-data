CHAPTER-I 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE 
MINISTRY 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
1. The Committee recommends immediate attention to the challenges facing maritime 
container movement schedules due to global disruptions and trade volatility. The 
Committee strongly urges the Ministry to implement comprehensive digital tracking 
platforms for real-time monitoring of container movements across all major ports. 
Furthermore, the Committee recommends establishing a dedicated Maritime Response 
Unit to address contingencies arising from geopolitical disruptions along key shipping 
routes. The Committee believes these measures are essential to mitigate extended lead 
times currently affecting Asia-Europe shipments and to reduce congestion at strategic port 
hubs.  
(Para 8.1)   
Reply of the Ministry   
1.1 In alignment with the Committee’s recommendations, the Ministry is actively assessing 
the implementation of comprehensive digital tracking platforms to enhance real-time monitoring 
of container movements across all major ports. These efforts align with our broader objective of 
leveraging technology to improve supply chain efficiency and transparency.  
Furthermore, the Ministry acknowledges the strategic significance of establishing a 
dedicated Maritime Response Unit to ensure timely and coordinated responses to geopolitical 
disruptions along key shipping routes. As part of this initiative, the Ministry is evaluating 
potential frameworks for enhancing resilience in maritime logistics and optimizing trade routes 
to safeguard India’s shipping interests. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2. The Committee notes the ambitious targets under Maritime India Vision 2030 and 
recommends accelerated implementation of mechanization initiatives at all major ports to 
achieve the sub-20-hour turnaround time goal. The Committee strongly advises prioritizing 
budget allocation for modernizing port infrastructure, particularly automated cargo 
handling systems and digital documentation processes. The Committee further 

3 
 
recommends quarterly performance reviews of port efficiency metrics with standardized 
benchmarks to ensure measurable progress toward capacity enhancement targets. 
(Para 8.2)   
 
Reply of the Ministry   
 
2.1 The Ministry acknowledges the Committee’s recommendation for the accelerated 
implementation of mechanisation initiatives across all major ports. In FY 2024–25, the median 
container turnaround time at major ports was recorded at 26.2 hours. Over the past five years, 
major ports in India have undertaken capital expenditure amounting to ₹14,080 crore through 
Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) and Gross Budgetary Support (GBS), along with 
an additional ₹8,898 crore through Public-Private Partnership (PPP) projects. These investments 
have been directed towards comprehensive port modernisation, capacity enhancement for 
handling large volumes of cargo, and the development of port-led industrialisation initiatives 
aimed at streamlining operations and improving overall efficiency. The Ministry also conducts 
regular monthly reviews of key port efficiency parameters to ensure continuous performance 
monitoring and improvement. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
3. The Committee emphasizes that inadequate multi-modal connectivity remains a 
significant bottleneck affecting port efficiency. The Committee recommends establishing a 
dedicated Inter-Ministerial Coordination Cell to expedite port connectivity projects under 
PM Gati Shakti. With reference to 10 Paradip Port, the Committee recommends 
immediate completion of pending railway linkage projects and upgradation of NH-53A to 
facilitate smoother cargo evacuation. The Committee strongly urges synchronized planning 
between the Ministries of Railways, Road Transport and Highways, and Ports, Shipping 
and Waterways to ensure infrastructure development aligns with projected cargo volumes. 
The Committee recommends that the Paradip Port development model be replicated across 
other major ports to enhance seamless connectivity and reduce logistics costs. 
(Para 8.3)   
 
 

4 
 
Reply of the Ministry   
3.1 The Ministry acknowledges the Committee’s observations regarding multi-modal 
connectivity challenges affecting port efficiency. Strengthening infrastructure linkages remains a 
key priority, particularly under the PM Gati Shakti initiative, which is designed to facilitate 
synchronized planning across multiple sectors, ensuring seamless cargo movement and reduced 
logistics costs. 
In alignment with the Committee’s recommendations, the Department for Promotion of Industry 
and Internal Trade (DPIIT) is leading inter-ministerial coordination efforts to expedite 
connectivity projects and ensure alignment with projected cargo volumes. Through DPIIT’s 
leadership, coordination among the Ministries of Railways, Road Transport and Highways, and 
Ports, Shipping and Waterways has been streamlined to accelerate infrastructure development 
and optimize multimodal logistics solutions. 
Regarding Paradip Port, the Ministry remains committed to the timely completion of pending 
railway linkage projects and the upgradation of NH-53A, recognizing their strategic importance 
in facilitating efficient cargo evacuation. These initiatives are being fast-tracked to enhance 
operational capacity, improve trade facilitation, and bolster India’s maritime connectivity 
framework. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
4. The Committee appreciates the Government’s continued emphasis on portled

Sagarmala Programme, inclusive of ₹30 crores for Community Development and ₹24.10 
crore for administrative expenditure. At the same time, however, it recommends that a

investments are directly correlated with economic growth, employment generation, and 
infrastructural improvements. Future funding for Sagarmala Programme should be tied to 
performance-based outcomes, with a clear roadmap for project execution timelines and 
transparent reporting mechanisms.  
(Para 9)   
Reply of the Ministry:-  
4.1 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee have been duly noted.

Recommendation/Observation  
5. The Committee observes that the allocation of ₹ 15 crore for the Inland Water 
Transport - North Eastern Region (IWT-NER) appears disproportionately low given the 
vast potential of inland waterways as a cost-effective and environmentally sustainable 
mode of transport. It did note the investment of ₹ 1,752.31 crore in inland water transport 
infrastructure which would go a long way in strengthening the waterway connectivity. The 
Committee, therefore recommends that a dedicated Inland Waterway Corridor 
Development Plan be instituted, which will ensure that investments extend beyond mere 
infrastructural augmentation to include operational efficiency, multimodal logistics 
integration, and cargo handling capabilities. A periodic review mechanism must be 
established to assess the effectiveness of these investments in enhancing inland navigability 
and economic viability. 
 (Para 10)   
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
5.1 The allocation of ₹ 15 crore is for the Central Sector Schemes for Inland Water Transport 
- North Eastern Region (IWT-NER) under which works are being executed by the concerned 
States on their inland waterways. For the development of cost-effective and environmentally 
sustainable mode of transport on National Waterways (NWs) of NER through Inland Waterways 
Authority of India (IWAI), an investment of Rs. 421 cr. is proposed under BE 2025-26 under 
Capital outlay in North Eastern Areas- Inland Waterways Authority of India. 
In line with the recommendations of the Committee, for the development of a regional 
waterways network integrated with road and rail, the Regional Waterway Grid (RWG) project is 
proposed. The broad objective of the RWG project is to enhance economic activity in the region 
by facilitating trade and commerce via efficient movement of goods and passengers by 
augmenting waterways, putting in place connectivity in a manner that is socially, 
environmentally, and economically sustainable. 
A robust weekly/fortnightly/monthly review mechanism has been established at regional/ 
organizational /ministerial level for effective implementation of works being executed by IWAI 
for close monitoring and targeted progress.

Recommendation/Observation  
6. The Committee is happy to note the allocation of ₹100.62 crore to the Indian 
Maritime University (IMU) which it believes reflects a commendable commitment on the 
part of the Government to maritime education and workforce development. However, the 
Committee notes with concerns the relatively modest provision of crore for the Tariff 
Authority for Major Ports (TAMP), as it may affect its ability to effectively regulate port 
tariffs in an increasingly complex maritime economy. The Committee therefore 
recommends strengthening regulatory capabilities within TAMP, so that it is equipped 
with the necessary financial and technological resources to maintain an effective regulatory 
framework.  
(Para 11)   
 
 
Reply of the Ministry  
6.1 The Ministry appreciates the Committee’s recognition of the Government’s commitment 
to maritime education and workforce development through the allocation of ₹100.62 crore to the 
Indian Maritime University (IMU). This investment underscores our dedication to fostering high-
quality training, research, and capacity-building to strengthen India’s maritime sector. 
Regarding the observations on the Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP), the Ministry would 
like to clarify that with the enforcement of the Major Port Authorities (MPA) Act, 2021, on 
November 3, 2021, TAMP's tariff-setting functions have been withdrawn. In accordance with the 
new legislative framework, the Central Government is in the process of constituting an 
Adjudicatory Board, which will assume adjudicatory functions as outlined in the Act. This 
transition aims to enhance transparency, efficiency, and responsiveness in port tariff regulation, 
ensuring a robust framework aligned with evolving industry dynamics.   
 
Recommendation/Observation  
7. The Committee, upon examination of the financial allocations and expenditure 
trends under the Sagarmala Programme, notes that while the Budget Estimate (BE) for 
2025-26 has been set at ₹ 810 crore which is a 26.6% increase from the previous year’s BE 
of ₹ 640 crore, there are concerns regarding fund utilisation efficiency. In 2024-25, the 
Revised Estimate (RE) was marginally increased to ₹ 649 crore, but actual expenditure, as 
of date, stands at ₹ 566 crore, reflecting an 87.2% utilisation rate. In the opinion of the 
Committee, there seems to be delays in project execution, or administrative inefficiencies, 

7 
 
which, if left unaddressed, may hinder the full realisation of the ambitious programme’s 
intended objectives. The Committee, therefore, urges the Ministry to ensure greater 
financial discipline and proactive project monitoring to facilitate timely and effective 
utilisation of allocated resources. It is recommended that a comprehensive review be 
undertaken to identify and rectify procedural impediments that have contributed to 
underutilisation in 2024-25, ensuring that the ₹ 810 crore allocated for 2025-26 is expended 
in a structured and time-bound manner. Furthermore, the Committee stresses the 
importance of accurate budget forecasting to minimise the need for midyear revisions and 
recommends that the Ministry strengthen its project implementation framework, including 
regular financial performance reviews and enhanced inter-agency coordination, to uphold 
fiscal prudence and programme efficacy. 
(Para 16)   
Reply of the Ministry     
7.1 The Ministry acknowledges the Committee’s recommendations and is committed to 
ensuring greater financial discipline and proactive project monitoring for effective utilisation of 
the allocated budget for 2025–26. The Ministry is strengthening its project implementation 
framework through regular financial performance reviews and enhanced inter-agency 
coordination. These measures aim to uphold fiscal prudence and ensure timely execution of 
projects in alignment with the Committee’s valuable guidance. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
8. The Committee observes imbalance between capital investments (₹994.33 crore for 
JMVP) and maintenance expenditure (₹267 crore) which in its opinion reflects a critical 
concern in infrastructure development strategy. The Jal Marg Vikas Project, currently 
extended to 2026 with World Bank support, represents a substantial commitment to 
expanding the inland waterway infrastructure along National Waterway-1. While the 
project has demonstrated progress in creating multi-modal terminals at Varanasi, 
Sahibganj, and Haldia, evidence suggests implementation challenges persist, with certain 
tenders being cancelled due to significantly higher bids than estimated values. The 
Committee notes that maintenance expenditure forms the backbone of any sustainable 
infrastructure network, particularly for waterways where regular dredging and channel 
maintenance are essential for ensuring navigability. The current allocation of ₹267 crore 
for maintaining existing waterways appears insufficient, especially when considered against 
the extensive network of 111 National Waterways. The experience from European 
waterway management indicates that sustained maintenance funding is crucial for 

8 
 
ensuring the reliability and performance of inland navigation infrastructure. The 
Committee therefore recommends a more 19 strategic approach involving implementation 
of a Life-Cycle Cost Assessment (LCCA) framework for waterway infrastructure that 
balances initial capital expenditure with long-term maintenance requirements. This 
framework should incorporate detailed assessment of annual maintenance needs for each 
operational waterway segment, ensuring sufficient allocation for critical activities like 
dredging campaigns, navigation aids maintenance, and terminal upkeep. The Ministry 
should establish a minimum maintenance-to-capital expenditure ratio of 1:2 for mature 
waterways, gradually transitioning to 1:1 as the network stabilizes, ensuring sustainable 
operation while continuing necessary expansion  
(Para 19)   
 
Reply of the Ministry of the Ministry     
8.1 As recommended by the Committee, a more strategic approach involving implementation 
of a Life-Cycle Cost Assessment (LCCA) framework for waterway infrastructure that balances 
initial capital expenditure with long-term maintenance requirement, is noted for compliance, at 
planning stage. 
Further, when the waterway regime gets stability, a minimum maintenance-to-capital 
expenditure ratio of 1:2 for mature waterways will be maintained as recommended by the 
Committee.

As part of the Ministry’s long-term strategy to modernize port operations, we remain committed

the current R&D allocation has been set based on existing priorities, the Ministry is actively

strengthen India’s maritime competitiveness. 
Efforts are already underway to collaborate with research institutions, technology experts, and

reduce emissions, and improve overall sustainability. The Ministry remains dedicated to ensuring 
that India’s ports continue to evolve in alignment with global best practices and emerging

Recommendation/Observation  
3. The Committee observes that the Budget Estimates (BE) for 2025-26 at  ₹ 3,470.58 
crore marks a substantial 45.9% increase over the previous year’s BE of ₹ 2,377.49 crore 
which is the largest annual increase in the past four fiscal years. However, it also notes a 
persistent trend of mid-year budgetary revisions as evident, with Revised Estimates (RE) 
consistently exceeding BE- notably, in 2024-25, RE surged by ₹ 481.05 crore above BE. 
Furthermore, the RE has been declining, with expenditure as a percentage of RE dropping 
from 94.11% in 2022-23 to 86.15% in 2024-25 (as of February 2025) which can either be 

11 
 
due to project execution delays, and possible inefficiencies in financial governance. In the 
opinion of the Committee, given the unprecedented budgetary increase for 2025-26, it 
becomes all the imperative that fiscal discipline is reinforced, fund utilisation is optimised, 
and hurdles in project executions are identified and systematically addressed to prevent a 
further decline in expenditure efficiency.   
 (Para 14)   
Reply of the Ministry     
3.1 The Ministry constantly reviews the project execution and fund utilization. Expenditure 
reported earlier was upto 27.02.2025. However, actual expenditure in FY 2024-25 was Rs 
2837.60 crores which was 99.26 % of RE 2024-25. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
4. The Committee notes the allocation of ₹76.50 crore towards interest payments on 
IWAI bonds and raises concerns about the sustainability of debt-based financing for inland 
waterway projects. While debt instruments provide immediate access to capital necessary 
for infrastructure development, they create rigid fiscal obligations that constrain future 
budgetary flexibility. The current financing structure of the JMVP, with its reliance on 
both international loans (₹2,512 crore from IBRD) and government bonds, exemplifies this 
mixed approach. 
Debt financing offers certain advantages, including preservation of operational autonomy 
and prevention of ownership dilution. However, it also introduces financial covenants that 
may restrict operational flexibility and increases overall financial risk exposure. The 
experience from the financing survey of the inland waterway transport sector indicates that 
while loans on favorable terms remain a preferred financial instrument, there is growing 
interest in alternative financing approaches, particularly among larger operators.  
4.1 The Committee therefore recommends a more balanced financing strategy that  
incorporates diversified funding sources beyond traditional debt instruments. The Ministry 
should develop a Waterway Infrastructure Finance Framework that includes:  
(1) Project-specific revenue bonds tied to direct income streams from waterway operations, 
reducing dependency on general budgetary allocations;  

12 
 
(2) Dedicated Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) specifically for waterway projects 
with stable revenue potential, allowing broader market participation; and  
(3) Blended finance models combining concessional loans, grants, and commercial 
financing to optimize the capital structure for different project components. 
(Para 20 & 20.1)   
Reply of the Ministry     
4.2 The Ministry recognizes the importance of evolving a sustainable and diversified 
financial framework for the Inland Water Transport (IWT) sector. However, it is important to 
contextualize these within the current developmental stage of IWT in India. IWT remains a 
nascent sector with limited commercial maturity and revenue-generating capacity. Unlike sectors 
such as roads and highways, which have seen decades of development, the inland waterways 
sector is still building its foundational infrastructure and operational ecosystem.  
Further, the Ministry is cognizant of the financing challenges across the maritime domain and is 
actively working towards the establishment of Maritime Development Fund (MDF). This fund 
will provide both equity and debt financing tailored to the unique needs of the sector, thereby 
reducing reliance on traditional financial sources. Additionally, the Sagarmala Development 
Company Limited (SDCL) has recently been granted Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) 
status. This strategic development will enable SDCL (Now Sagarmala Finance Corporation 
Limited - SMFCL) to extend debt financing to maritime projects, further strengthening the 
sector’s financial ecosystem.  While the suggestions on revenue bonds, InvITs, and blended 
finance models are Noted, their implementation must be calibrated to the sector’s current 
maturity level. The Ministry remains committed to exploring these instruments progressively, in 
alignment with sectoral growth, investor appetite, and regulatory readiness. 
 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
5. The Committee notes the allocation of ₹421 crore for North-Eastern Region 
infrastructure development targeting NW-2 (Brahmaputra), NW-16 (Barak), and the Indo-
Bangladesh Protocol route which represents a significant opportunity to enhance regional 
connectivity. However, the unique geographical and logistical challenges of the region 
necessitate more nuanced implementation strategies to ensure these investments yield 
meaningful economic returns. In its opinion, stakeholder consultations in the North-
Eastern Region reveal limited awareness about environmental safeguards and coordination 

13 
 
challenges among different agencies involved in riverine infrastructure. Local communities 
prioritize rapid development but lack understanding of the quality standards necessary for 
sustainable waterway operations. Field observations indicate that structural interventions 
like piers, bridges, and river training works can pose navigational hazards when not 
properly coordinated among stakeholders. In order to maximize the effectiveness of the 
₹1,010 crore five-year NER project (2020-2025), the Committee therefore recommends that 
the Ministry implements a Regional Waterway Development Consortium model that brings 
together state governments, local bodies, technical institutions, and private stakeholders in 
a structured governance framework. This consortium should establish a unified project 
monitoring system with standardized reporting protocols and performance 21 indicators 
for all NER waterway projects, enabling real-time tracking of implementation progress 
and rapid intervention where needed. 
(Para 21)   
Reply of the Ministry  
5.1 In the light of recommendation of the Parliament Standing Committee, a National level 
committee viz. Inland Waterway Development Council (IWDC) has been set up under the 
Chairmanship of Hon’ble Minister of Ports, Shipping and Waterways on 12.10.2023 for better 
cooperation and on-boarding of States / UTs in order to accelerate holistic development of Inland 
Water Transport in the country for enhancement of cargo, passenger movement and for river 
cruise tourism. 
As of now, 2 meetings of IWDC have been held at Kolkata and at Kaziranga in which 
announcements regarding development of more waterways including NER states have been 
made.  
 
In the first IWDC meeting with the States / UTs held at Kolkata on 8th January 2024 with 
participation of various States and Stakeholders, two major initiatives were launched in the 
inland waterways sector by Hon'ble Minister of Ports Shipping & Waterways viz. "HaritNauka" 
Guidelines i.e. Green Transition Guidelines for Inland Vessels, and “River Cruise Tourism

(I) The reasons for lower disbursement till date are as under: 

18 
 
(a) At the time of drafting the Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Policy, several major shipyards 
such as Reliance Naval and Engineering Limited, ABG Shipyard, Bharati Shipyard, and Tebma 
Shipyard Limited were operational, with numerous vessels under construction. These shipyards 
represented the bulk of India’s commercial shipbuilding capacity. However, due to multiple 
challenges—including the absence of a shipbuilding subsidy between September 2007 and 
March 2016 —many of these shipyards entered insolvency proceedings under the National 
Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). This led to a substantial decline in India’s shipbuilding 
capacity, reducing it by more than half, and significantly impacting the country’s overall 
shipbuilding capability. 
(b) In addition to the above, the COVID-19 pandemic and its aftermath further affected the 
sector, causing high lead times and extremely low production volumes during the period 2020–
2022. This severely constrained shipbuilding operations nationwide. 
(c) It may also be noted that ship construction is a long-gestation process. Hence, though the 
current policy was introduced in 2016, the first application for financial assistance was received 
only in January 2018. As a result, no disbursements occurred during the initial 1.5 years of 
policy implementation. 
(d) Under the present Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Policy, approvals are granted in two 
stages. First, an in-principle approval is accorded at the time of signing of the shipbuilding 
contract. Second, the actual disbursement of financial assistance takes place upon the successful 
delivery of the vessel. Accordingly, the applicability and timeline of the policy must be 
interpreted in this framework. While in-principle approvals will be granted until March 31, 2026, 
fund disbursement under these approvals will necessarily extend beyond this date. For instance: 
For regular vessels, which are allowed three year construction period, disbursement may extend 
until March 2029, if the contract is signed in Mar 2026. For specialized vessels, which are 
allowed a six-year construction period, disbursement may extend until Mar 2032, if the contract 
is signed in Mar 2026. 
(e) In this context, it is important to highlight that a significant number of shipbuilding contracts 
were signed in the post-COVID period, reflecting a revival of market confidence and renewed 
interest in domestic shipbuilding. As a result, while the actual disbursement under the policy 
currently stands at approximately 
Rs. 464.15 crore, the committed liability under approved contracts is already estimated at around 
Rs. 2,700 crore. These further underscores the necessity for continued and predictable policy 
support to ensure the long-term sustainability and growth of the shipbuilding sector in India. 
(f) Considering the capital intensive and long gestation nature of shipbuilding, initiatives to boost 
shipbuilding need a stable, long-term perspective to alter the existing market dynamics. 
Accordingly, the impact of the shipbuilding financial assistance policy has taken some time to 
reflect in the increased shipbuilding contracts being signed.

11. The Committee observes the current SBFAP framework includes provisions for 
enhanced incentives—30% for vessels powered by green fuels (methanol, ammonia, 
hydrogen) and 20% for those with electric or hybrid propulsion systems. However, without 
a dedicated allocation specifically earmarked for these sustainable technologies within the 
₹138 crore budget for FY 2025-26, there remains a serious risk that traditional 
shipbuilding might continue to dominate fund utilization. As global shipping increasingly 
focuses on decarbonization, India risks falling behind in developing indigenous capabilities 
for green vessel construction. The implications extend beyond environmental 
considerations to encompass future market competitiveness, as regulatory pressures and 
market demands increasingly favor low-emission vessels. The shipping ministry's 
recognition of green vessels in its policy framework indicates awareness of this shift, but 
implementation requires more targeted financial commitment.  
11.1 The Committee further notes the upcoming SBFAP 2.0, with its proposed allocation 
of ₹18,000 crore and enhanced subsidies of 25% for specialized vessels and 30% for green 
vessels, represents a significant step forward. However, this policy requires immediate 
operationalisation with a clear timeline for implementation and specific allocations for 
different vessel categories. The Ministry should establish immediate ring-fencing of at least 
40% of the current year's allocation (approximately ₹55 crore) exclusively for green vessel 
construction, creating a dedicated funding stream that ensures India builds capacity in this 
emerging technology area. 
11.2 The Committee notes that the current practice of projecting SBFAP funds based on 
tentative ship delivery schedules introduces significant inefficiencies in fund utilization. 
Shipbuilding projects, particularly those involving complex vessels, are vulnerable to 
production delays, supply chain disruptions, and technical challenges that can extend 
timelines considerably. This creates a mismatch between projected fund requirements and 

20 
 
actual disbursement schedules, contributing to the observed pattern of underutilization. 
The construction of specialized and green vessels, which involve more complex technologies 
and often require sourcing components from global supply chains, are particularly 
susceptible to such delays. This problem is likely to be exacerbated as Indian shipyards 
expand their capabilities to build more sophisticated vessels with lower emission profiles. 
The Committee feels implementing a milestone-based fund release mechanism would 
fundamentally transform how financial assistance is distributed. Under this approach, 
shipyards would receive predetermined percentages of the total assistance amount upon 
completion of defined construction milestones, such as steel cutting, keel laying, launching, 
and final delivery. This approach would provide shipyards with steady cash flow 
throughout the construction process, rather than concentrating financial support at project 
completion. 
11.3 The Committee therefore urges the Ministry develops a standardized set of 
verifiable milestones for different vessel categories, with appropriate weightage assigned to 
each stage. For instance, the steel cutting stage might trigger release of 15% of the total 
assistance, keel laying another 25%, launching 30%, and final delivery the remaining 30%. 
This structured approach would maintain financial pressure on shipyards to progress 
systematically while providing them with the liquidity needed to finance ongoing 
construction.
