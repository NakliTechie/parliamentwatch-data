INTRODUCTION
 
            The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is responsible for development, formulation and implementation
 of policies relating to Road Transport and National Highways.  Its main function relate to road development and
 maintenance including Border Roads, Planning and co-ordination and Road Transport.
 
2.         The Ministry is administratively divided into two wings namely Transport Wing and Roads Wing.
 
3.         Transport Wing is concerned with the formulation of broad policies relating to Road Transport, Motor Vehicles
 Act, legislation relating to Road Transport Corporations, besides making arrangements for movement of motor
 vehicular traffic with the neighbouring countries.
 
4.         Roads Wing is mainly concerned with the matters relating to:-
 
Advising Government on all general policy matters relating to Highways;
 
Development & Maintenance of roads declared as National Highways;
 
Roads other than National Highways in Union Territories;
 
Administration of Central Road Fund (CRF) pertaining to State Roads other than rural roads;
 
Evaluation of standards for roads and bridges and formulation of specifications;
 
Performance of road research;
 
National Highways Authority of India and National Institute for Training of Highway Engineers.
 
5.         The following autonomous bodies function under the administrative control of the Ministry of Road Transport &
 Highways :-
 
National Highways Authority of India (NHAI); and
National Institute of Training of Highway Engineers (NITHE).

NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
 
9.         The Ministry is primarily responsible for construction and maintenance of National Highways in the country.  As
 on 31
st March 2002, the length of National Highways was 58,112 kms. constituting about 2% of the total road network
 in the country and carrying about 45% of the road based traffic.
 
10.       Strengthening and improvement of National Highways (excluding the stretches entrusted to NHAI) are done
 through various State Government and Union Territories.  They act as an agency of the Central Government and
 execute Road and Bridge Works.  Budgetary provision for such National Highway Works for the year 2003-04 is
 Rs.1399 crores for the States and Union Territories other than for National Highway Works in Sikkim and North
 Eastern States.   A sum of Rs.150 crores has been earmarked for 2003-04 for Sikkim and North Eastern States. 
 Accordingly, a sum total of Rs.1549 cores is proposed to be allocated for National Highway works.

NATIONAL HIGHWAY DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (NHDP)
 
14.       Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has informed that the present emphasis of the Government is on
 improvement of Riding Quality of National Highways and for providing comforts to the road users.  Apart from
 National Highways Development Programme (NHDP), there is about 44,000 kms. of National Highways maintenance
 of which is being carried out from the funds available from Budget.  Since 1999, Riding Quality of about 29,000 kms.
 length have been improved by utilising funds allocated under both Plan and Non-Plan heads.  Riding Quality of the
 balance length is targeted to be completed within the next two years.
 
15.       The Government has embarked upon a massive National Highways Development Project (NHDP) in the
 country.  The NHDP is largest highways project ever undertaken in the country.  Under this project about 13,146 kms.
 of National Highways are proposed to be 4 to 6 laned at a total estimated cost of Rs.54,000 crores (at 1999 prices). 
 This is being implemented by NHAI.  The NHDP consists of following two components:-
 
Golden Quadrilateral (GQ) comprising of National Highways connecting four metro cities viz., Delhi, Mumbai,
 Chennai and Kolkata.  Total length of GQ is 5846 kms.  Four laning of 1159 kms. has already been completed and
 work is going on the remaining length except of 84 kms. of Allahabad bypass, which would also be awarded shortly.  
 The Golden Quadrilateral is targeted for substantial completion by December 2003.
 
North-South and East-West corridors comprising the National Highways connecting Srinagar to Kanyakumari including
 Kochi-Salem spur and Silchar to Porbandar.  The total length of the corridors is about 7300 kms.  Four/six laning of
 817 kms. has already been completed and work on 671 kms. is under implementation.  Work on 2500 kms. will start
 next year.  The target for the completion of the corridors is December 2007. 
 
16.       The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways regarding the physical achievements
 made in the Golden Quadrilateral Project so far and whether the Government expected the project to be completed
 within the targeted period.
 
17.       The Committee was informed by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways that the four-laning of the Golden
 Quadrilateral (GQ) component of the National Highway Development Project (NHDP) covers a total length of 5846
 kms. and was targeted for completion by December 2004.  It is expected that GQ will be completed within the targeted
 period, except for some contracts which may spill over to early part of 2005.  The projects which will spill over to 2005
 are mostly funded by World Bank.  The World Bank has its own procedures which had to be complied with and most
 of the contracts could be awarded only in March 2002 with a direction for completion of these contracts within a period
 of 36 months i.e. upto March 2005.  Contracts for 136 kms. are yet to be awarded.  Out of these, one package of 52
 kms. was held up due to a court case.  Of the total GQ length of 5846 kms., 1327 kms. have already been 4-laned and
 another 1320 kms. will be 4-laned by December, 2003 (i.e. a total length of 2647 kms.).

24.       Commenting upon the economic benefits of the Golden Quadrilateral, the Ministry of Road Transport &
 Highways have stated that the Golden Quadrilateral project is likely to generate requirement of 2.5 to 4 million Metric
 Tonne of cement, and 2.5 to 3 lakh Metric Tonne of steel.  The project will also generate employment of about 189
 million man-days.  Right now, about 2.5 lakhs people are employed per day on this project.  Once completed, the GQ
 will result in a saving of Rs.8,000 crores per year as per a World Bank Report.  With the implementation of NHDP,
 cement industry has registered a growth of 8.5%.  Similarly steel industry has registered a growth of 6.8%. 
 Commercial vehicles have also registered an increase of 53% during last year.   Further, commenting upon the thrust
 being laid on improving the riding quality, the Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways stated as under:
 
“With the improvement of the riding quality, there has been great economic benefit for people who live along the length
 of the National Highways.  Karnataka is one such example, where we find that now there is better market accessibility,
 and so, the produce of farmers is sold very easily.  A larger number of animals are being brought to the market or
 veterinary dispensaries.  Proper connectivity by roads has also improved the level of education.  Earlier, the number of
 absentees in schools was more because of poor connectivity of roads.  Now, because of improved roads and
 connectivity, more and more children can travel conveniently to the nearby schools, thereby improving the level of
 education.  According to a rough estimate, there has been about 26 per cent improvement in the economy of the areas
 where the riding quality has been improved.”
 
25.       The Committee is happy to note that the improvement of the riding quality has led to great economic benefits for
 people living along the length of the roads. Roads are the key to the development of an economy. The Committee hopes
 that the revolution in the road sector will go a long way in promoting the economic development of the country and
 integrating remote regions with the mainstream economic activity by opening them to trade and investment.
 
 
CENTRAL ROAD FUND (CRF)
 
26.       The Committee was informed that in recognition of the increasing requirement of funds for road infrastructure
 sector, Union Budget for 1998-99 had provided for levy of additional excise duty and additional custom duty of Re.1
 per litre of petrol.  Subsequently, in the Union Budget for 1999-2000, an additional duty of Re.1 per litre on imported
 and domestic high-speed diesel was levied.  The revenue from the cess would be used to finance all categories of
 roads.  This fund has been given the statutory status by Central Road Fund Act in December 2000.  Large amount of
 allocation is coming into the road sector under Central Road Fund.  This is a three-tier development programme.
 
27.       Allocation from the Central Road Fund are being made in the following manner:-
50% of the cess on high-speed diesel oil for development of rural roads.
 
50% of cess of High Speed Diesel (HSD) and the entire cess collected on petrol are being allocated as per the
 following:-
 
An amount equal to 57.5% of such sum for the development and maintenance of National Highways;
The amount equal to 12.5% for the construction of road under or over bridges and safety works at unmanned railway
 crossings; and
An amount equal to 30% on development and maintenance of State roads.  Out of this amount, 10% i.e., 3% of the total
 share of the State Roads shall be kept as reserved by the Central Government for allocation to States for
 implementation of State Road Schemes of Inter-State and economic importance to be approved by the Central
 Government.

 Road Fund is now based on the consumption of petrol and diesel and geographical area of the State concerned.  Release
 of funds is project specific and further instalments are released subject to the receipt of the utilisation certificates. 
 During the course of the evidence, the Committee was informed that as against the total provision of Rs.980 crores in
 2002-03, funds to the extent of Rs.940 crores had been released to States and UTs upto 25.3.2003 as against Rs.300
 crores or Rs.400 crores in the earlier years.  The Committee was also informed that the entire fund allocated to the
 various States/UTs was expected to be fully utilised by them during 2002-2003.  As on 31.1.2003, projects of the value
 of about   Rs.3117 crores had already been approved.  A sum of Rs.910.76 crores has been earmarked for States and
 UTs from CRF for 2003-2004.
 
30.       The Committee in its 59
th Report on Demands for Grants 2002-03 of the Ministry of Road Transport and
 Highways had commented upon the savings under Central Road Fund during 2001-02 and recommended that proper
 monitoring for utilisation of funds be ensured.  The Committee appreciates that there has been a major improvement in
 the rate of utilisation of Central Road Fund by the States/UTs as compared to the earlier years.  The Committee notes
 with satisfaction that the funds allocated for maintenance and development of National Highways to the States/UTs was
 expected to be fully utilised by them during 2002-03.  The Committee recommends that there should be no let up in the
 measures adopted for the complete utilisation of the funds by the States/UTs and the said trend should continue in the
 future also.
 
31.       During the evidence, the Committee also enquired from the representatives of the Ministry of Road Transport
 and Highways as to whether any monitoring was being done on the allocations made to the State/UT Governments out
 of the Central Road Fund for the State Highways.  In response, the Secretary informed the Committee that they released
 the allocations to the State Governments for the development of State Highways and it was their responsibility to spend
 it.  The Ministry plays a limited role in this matter and sees to it that the projects should not be limited to any particular
 area and should be spread all over the State.  He further elaborated that monitoring and investment of the fund allocated
 are the responsibility of the concerned State Government.
 
32.       The Committee is of the view that since the allocation out of the CRF for the State Highways is done by the
 Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, it becomes the responsibility of the Ministry to ensure proper utilisation of
 the funds.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should develop
 some mechanism to ensure that the funds released out of the CRF for State Highways are utilised for that purpose only.

 five by the Central Government over the rates notified.  All fee levied under rules shall be collected by the executing
 agencies concerned, departmentally or through private contractors on the basis of competitive bidding on behalf of
 Central Government.  It further states that the fee shall be collected in perpetuity by the executive agency.  The fee
 collected for the bridges are allocated to the respective States for development of National Highways.
           
41.       The Committee took note of the fact that the toll for bridges and roads was to be collected in perpetuity and
 brought to the notice of the Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways that the Government was already
 levying a cess on petrol and diesel for the purpose.  In response, the Secretary clarified that the cess on petrol and diesel
 is for development whereas the toll is for maintenance of roads.
 
42.       The Committee is not convinced with the reasons advanced by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for
 collecting toll on roads and bridges in perpetuity.  The Committee feels that the Government is not justified in
 burdening the common people with the development as well as the maintenance of the roads in the country.  The
 Committee had also commented upon this matter in its 43
rd Report on Demands for Grants 2000-2001 of the then
 Ministry of Surface Transport.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that once the cost of construction of a
 road/bridge plus interest is recovered, the collection of toll should be stopped.   The Committee also feels that there
 exists a need for the rationalisation of the toll tax being charged on the National Highways.  The Committee
 recommends that persons travelling on smaller stretches of the National Highways where toll is being collected should
 be charged a lower amount than the persons using the entire length of that National Highway.

PROBLEM OF OVERLOADING
 
59.       The share of Road Transport in Cargo Movement has gone up from only 12% in the year 1950-51 to now about
 65%.  The number of goods vehicles has gone up from 80,000 in the year 1950-51 to 26.8 lakhs by the year 2000 and is
 now estimated to be around 30 lakhs.
 
60.       The pavement design of road is based on the cumulative number of standard axle loads and therefore, higher
 axle load cause premature distress on the road pavements which in turn increases the maintenance cost of Indian roads. 
 An overloaded vehicle causes higher pollution besides inflicting high vehicle operating costs.  Moreover overloading is
 also a potential hazard not only to the driver of such vehicles but also to other road users.
 
61.       During the course of the evidence, the Committee drew the attention of the representatives of the Ministry of
 Road Transport & Highways to the ever-growing problem of overloading of vehicles, particularly trucks.  The
 Committee had also commented upon the matter in its 59
th Report on Demands for Grants 2002-03 of the Ministry of
 Road Transport & Highways.  The Committee also referred to the recommendations made in this context in its 59th
 Report and was of the view that the reply furnished by the Ministry had not been satisfactory.
 
62.       The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways as to whether the problem of
 overloading of vehicles was discussed during the meeting of Transport Development Council (TDC) and what remedial
 steps had been undertaken by the Ministry to tackle the problem of overloading of vehicles.  In their reply, the Ministry
 of Road Transport & Highways stated that the issue of overloading was discussed in the 30
th Meeting of Transport
 Development Council (TDC) held on 16.1.2003 and the following decisions were arrived at:
 
(a)        Strict enforcement of the provisions relating to overloading under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and Central
 Motor Vehicle Rules, 1989.
 
(b)        The State Governments are not to issue special cards/passes which legalise overloading.
 
(c)        The decision of the 28
th meeting of TDC held on 10.10.1998 for States to give a 25% tax rebate to popularise
 use of multi axle vehicles, which has been implemented so far by a few States like Karnataka, West Bengal,
 Meghalaya, Uttar Pradesh, Tripura and Gujarat may be carried out by other States also.

(d)        Keeping in view a number of court cases, the registering authority shall ensure that the vehicles are registered
 only as per the load ceilings notified and in case erroneously a higher load rating has been incorporated then the same
 shall be got rectified.
 
(e)        Non-renewal of registration and denial of permit to habitual offenders of overloading.
 
63.       The Ministry further informed that implementation of the above is the responsibility of the Governments of
 respective States/UTs who have been reminded in the matter on 19.1.2003 and again on 18.2.2003.  Moreover, the

 consider holding back the release of Central Assistance under provisions of Central Road Fund (CRF) Act, 2000 to that
 State/UT.
 
64.       Further elaborating on the problem of overloading, the Secretary stated during the evidence as under: -
 
“Sir, as far as problem of over-loading is concerned, I find that several States – I am not taking the names– are
 regularising over-loading by issuing some cards like green card, etc.  That means that if you buy a card at a particular
 price, you are free to overload the truck.  What we have done is under the provisions of the Central Road Fund Act,
 there is a provision that if the Central Government feels that the States are not taking adequate steps to maintain the
 roads, we can cut the grant.  I have held out the threat by saying that you are allowing over-loading which is totally
 against the rules and which is also damaging the roads much faster.  Therefore, I will have to invoke the provisions of
 this Act and stop the Central grant to you.  Letters have also been written in this regard.  I have had no response of the
 States but we intend to pursue it very seriously.  We have also proposed an amendment to the Motor Vehicles Act. 
 Today, as per the laws overloaded trucks are to be offloaded with the excess portion of load.  The truck owner has to
 pay penalty for the excess load and then he can go.  Today the offence is compounded.  Most States are only
 compounding the offence; they are not offloading the excess portion of the load.  We are trying to eliminate this
 practice.  That amendment will also be brought up in the near future.  These are some of the steps that we are taking.”
 
65.       The Committee is of the view that the problem of overloading of vehicles has been growing steadily inspite of
 the measures adopted by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.  The Committee feels that overloading of
 vehicles has not only been causing early destruction of several road stretches but also resulting in accidents leading to
 loss of life and property.  The Committee is constrained to note that certain state governments have been regularising
 overloading for financial gains.  The Committee appreciates the States of Karnataka, West Bengal, Meghalaya, Uttar
 Pradesh, Tripura and Gujarat for offering tax rebate to popularise the use of multi axle vehicles.  The Committee
 recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should further take up the matter with the concerned
 state governments so as to check the problem of overloading. Also the legislation proposed for this purpose should be
 introduced at the earliest.  The Committee also reiterates the recommendations made in its 59
th Report that the Ministry
 of Road Transport & Highways should come up with publicity efforts, on large scale, addressing to the truck drivers
 and the agencies engaged in transportation business, including general public.  Themes dealing with dangers of
 overloading should form an important part of the publicity campaigns undertaken in this regard.  Also awareness
 campaigns should be launched including advertisements in print/electronic media. 
 
COMPUTERISATION OF ROAD TRANSPORT SECTOR
 
66.       During the course of the evidence the Committee enquired about the progress achieved with regard to the
 computerization of the road transport sector.  The Committee also drew the attention of the representatives of the
 Ministry of Road Transport & Highways towards the lack of uniformity in the issuance of driving licenses in the
 various parts of the country and enquired about the procedure for issue of driving licenses.
 
67.       In their reply the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways stated that standardized software for both front-end
 and back-end computerization of the RTO Offices in the country have been made available to all the States/UTs.  The
 back-end computerization envisages computerization of the existing records of Driving License (DL) and Registration
 Certificate (RC) of the motor vehicles.  The front-end computerization envisages issuance of Driving License and

D R A F T R E P O R T

 Registration Certificate on a Smart Card medium by the States/UTs.  The Ministry has been constantly urging the
 States and UTs to expeditiously undertake both front-end and back-end computerization. As per information available,

68.       As regards the procedure for issue of driving licenses, the Committee was informed that under the Motor
 Vehicles Act, 1988 the procedure is for grant of a learner’s license initially.  Thereafter, a normal driving license is
 issued.  These provisions are uniformly applicable throughout the country. The applicant is required to pass the
 prescribed test to the satisfaction of the Regional Transport Authority Officers (RTO) before such a license is issued. 
 The procedure and format of the license have been spelt out under Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989.
 
69.       The Licensing Authority has to satisfy itself about the competence of the person undertaking driving test to
 negotiate the various tests prescribed.  A driving license can be issued (i) on paper, (ii) on laminated plastic card or (iii)
 on a smart card.  On many occasions licenses are tampered with.  In some other cases persons have acquired more than
 one driving license.  In order to introduce a foolproof system for driving licenses standardized software specifications
 for the Smart Card have been prepared in consultation with NIC and made available free of cost to all the States.  Use
 of standardized software would ensure their readability and inter-operability throughout the country.  The Ministry has
 been requesting the State Governments to introduce Smart Card based documents at an early date and some States like
 Maharashtra, Sikkim, Gujarat, etc. have already taken initiative in this regard.  The pace of computerization and issue
 of Smart Card based driving licenses would ultimately depend upon the Government of respective States/UTs.
 
70.       The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways in respect of
 computerization of Road Transport Sector.  However, the success achieved in the computerization programme leaves
 much to the desired.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should
 impress upon the States/UTs for expediting both front-end and back-end computerization programme of the road
 transport sectors.  The Ministry should also explore the possibility of providing financial assistance/incentives to the
 States for completing the computerization programme at the earliest.  This would not only help in putting a curb on the
 tampering of licenses by unscrupulous elements but would also ensure the existence of uniform and foolproof system
 for driving licenses by the various states.

(Para 32)
 
MAINTENANCE AND REPAIRS OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
           
The Committee is of the view that if the roads are not well maintained, it would certainly have an adverse impact on the
 aspect of road safety and also the investments made in the construction of the roads go waste.  The Committee is
 constrained to note that the financial resources made available to the Ministry under Maintenance & Repairs head
 (Non-Plan) have been only 40% of the requirement projected by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.  The
 Committee understands that there is available capacity in the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways to undertake
 more works for maintenance and repair of roads.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport &
 Highways should utilize funds available for the purpose in the initial few months of the financial year so that the
 Ministry can emphatically impress upon the Government for higher allocation of funds for maintenance and repair of
 roads at the RE stage.  The Committee also recommends that higher allocations be made in the coming years to
 Ministry of Road Transport & Highways for maintenance and repair of roads.
(Para 37)

The Committee is not convinced with the reasons advanced by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for
 collecting toll on roads and bridges in perpetuity.  The Committee feels that the Government is not justified in
 burdening the common people with the development as well as the maintenance of the roads in the country.  The
 Committee had also commented upon this matter in its 43

 Ministry of Surface Transport.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that once the cost of construction of a

 exists a need for the rationalisation of the toll tax being charged on the National Highways.  The Committee
 recommends that persons travelling on smaller stretches of the National Highways where toll is being collected should
 be charged a lower amount than the persons using the entire length of that National Highway.
(Para 42)
PORT CONNECTIVITY PROJECTS
 
The Committee feels that connectivity of the ports to the National Highways is of great importance from the point of
 view of commercial and industrial production.  The Committee is surprised to note that Mumbai Port Trust has not been
 included in the port connectivity projects.  The Committee has been informed that there is no proper road link to
 Mumbai Port and the only available road route to the port is in a poor condition.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should also include the Mumbai Port in their port
 connectivity project.   For this purpose, they can also approach the Mumbai Port Trust for their assistance in the project.
(Para 46)
 
INTERACTION OF THE MINISTRY OF ROAD TRANSPORT AND HIGHWAYS WITH THE MEMBERS OF THE
 COMMITTEE
 
The Committee is of the view that Members of the Committee, as representatives of the people, can assist and
 coordinate with the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways in the implementation of the projects in their respective
 states.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Members of the Committee should be informed, every three
 months, about the status of the ongoing schemes/projects in all states/UTs as well as about the hindrances faced in the
 implementation of the projects.  It will help the Members of Parliament in taking up and sorting out matters with
 various agencies of the State Government and evolving solutions to the problems faced by the Ministry in achieving
 their objectives.  This will also ensure the smooth and speedy implementation of the projects.  Also it will help the
 Ministry of Road Transport and Highways in doing away with some of the reasons mentioned in para 19 of the Report
 for delay in the execution of the projects which the Ministry is facing every year.
(Para 50)
 
ROAD TRANSPORT
 
The Committee is of the view that road safety is an area of serious concern and the sheer magnitude and severity of road
 accidents in the country requires immediate attention.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the study on
 National Road Safety Policy should be completed at the earliest.  The government should also take measures involving
 technology, education and also enforcement of legal provisions in order to bring down the number of accidents within a
 fixed time frame. 
(Para 56)
 
The Committee feels that with the development and improvement in riding quality of the roads, danger to the lives of
 the villagers and cattle has also increased on those roads.  In spite of the construction of underpasses at the traditional
 crossing points, the incidents of accidents on such roads has been rising.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 while undertaking the development of the roads, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should make some extra
 efforts and, if necessary, construct underpasses and over-bridges, taking into account the population and the
 requirement of the users of that area so that accidents can be avoided and there is no loss of lives.  Systematic survey
 for assessment of users convenience should also be conducted in this regard. 
(Para 58)
 
PROBLEM OF OVERLOADING
 
The Committee is of the view that the problem of overloading of vehicles has been growing steadily in spite of the
 measures adopted by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.  The Committee feels that overloading of vehicles
 has not only been causing early destruction of several road stretches but also resulting in accidents leading to loss of life
 and property.  The Committee is constrained to note that certain state governments have been regularising overloading
 for financial gains.  The Committee appreciates the States of Karnataka, West Bengal, Meghalaya, Uttar Pradesh,

 that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should further take up the matter with the concerned state
 governments so as to check the problem of overloading. Also the legislation proposed for this purpose should be
 introduced at the earliest.  The Committee also reiterates the recommendations made in its 59th Report that the Ministry
 of Road Transport & Highways should come up with publicity efforts, on large scale, addressing to the truck drivers
 and the agencies engaged in transportation business, including general public.  Themes dealing with dangers of
 overloading should form an important part of the publicity campaigns undertaken in this regard.  Also awareness
 campaigns should be launched including advertisements in print/electronic media. 
(Para 65)
 
COMPUTERISATION OF ROAD TRANSPORT SECTOR
 
The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways in respect of
 computerization of Road Transport Sector.  However, the success achieved in the computerization programme leaves
 much to the desired.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should
 impress upon the States/UTs for expediting both front-end and back-end computerization programme of the road
 transport sectors.  The Ministry should also explore the possibility of providing financial assistance/incentives to the
 States for completing the computerization programme at the earliest.  This would not only help in putting a curb on the
 tampering of licenses by unscrupulous elements but would also ensure the existence of uniform and foolproof system
 for driving licenses by the various states. 
(Para 70)
 
PERFORMANCE BUDGET
 
A perusal of the Performance Budget 2003-04 of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways indicates that the
 Performance Budget lacks the information regarding targets/achievements about the specific schemes undertaken by
 the Ministry during the previous year.  In the absence of the above details, the Committee finds it difficult to assess the
 progress of the projects.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Performance Budget should comprise of
 information indicating the targets fixed with specific parameters, actual physical achievements and slippages alongwith
 the reasons for the same.  The physical/financial achievements should be clearly indicated together with the reasons for
 shortfall, if any, in respect of the projects so that the Committee can make proper assessment of the projects executed
 by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.
(Para 71)
