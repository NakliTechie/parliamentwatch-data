REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Ministry of Civil Aviation
on the observations/recommendations as contained in its Two Hundred and Third Report on
“Privatization of Services at Airports”, which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on
17
th December, 2013.
2. The Committee desires that final replies in respect of these recommendations/observations,
which have been categorized as interim replies by the Committee should be furnished to it at the
earliest.
3. On scrutiny of the Action Taken Replies, the Committee has made observations on each
item of Action taken/proposed to be taken by the Ministry. The recommendations of the Committee
contained in the Two Hundred and Third Report, action taken thereon by the Ministry and
recommendations/observations of the Committee on each item of action taken has been given in the
succeeding paragraphs.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
4. Initially, when Delhi and Mumbai airports were restructured under PPP mode in 2005, it
was decided at the level of the Prime Minister that Chennai and Kolkata airports will not be
privatized. A tripartite agreement was also signed between AAI, the Employees Union and the
Government to that effect. It was also decided by COI headed by the Prime Minister himself way
back in 2006 that the services on the air side and in the terminal building would be assigned to
AAI only; it would not be given to private people. The Ministry could not give a convincing
explanation as to (i) how can these two airports be given under PPP now; and (ii) how can the
services on air side and in terminal building be given to the private people. Therefore, the Model
Concession Agreement, the Committee feels, cannot be amended to assign the services on airside
and in terminal building to the private people as contended by the Ministry/Planning Commission.
It is strange that within six years the perception of the Task Force has changed and it has come
to an opposite conclusion and the decision of the COI is also given a go by. No reasons have been
for taking contradictory stand on this issue by the Planning Commission/Ministry.
(Recommendation at Para 24 of 203rd Report)
Reply of the Government
5. The Task Force in its Report on “Financing Plan For Airports” published by Planning
Commission in July, 2012 states the following as a reason for its recommendation for undertaking
the operation and management of AAI airports (both city side and airside) through Public Private
Partnership basis:-
“…The next challenge for AAI would be to ensure their management and upkeep at a level
commensurate with the high quality of these terminals. Moreover, the potential for non-
aeronautical revenues from car parking, cargo facilities, hotels, passenger amenities,
shopping etc. cannot be fully harnessed by AAI due to the inherent constraints of a public
sector entity. The anticipated shortfall in nonaeronautical revenues is bound to lead to higher
passenger and aeronautical charges. Problems related to monitoring and supervision of the
large number of sub-optimal service contracts being awarded by AAI could also be
eliminated if a single PPP concession for operation and maintenance of the entire airport is
granted to an experienced and competent authority”

2
6. Subsequently, the Prime Minister took a meeting on 28.06.2013 to approve the targets for
infrastructure sector for the financial year 2013-14 for the Ministries of Railways, Road Transport
and Highways, Civil Aviation, Shipping, Power and Coal. In this meeting it was decided that Airport
Operations and Maintenance in AAI airports would be awarded on PPP basis. Accordingly, Ministry
of Civil Aviation (MoCA) has taken up 20 select airports under PPP on the basis of Operation
Management and Transfer (Copy enclosed).
7. Report of the Task Force does not have any reference of the tripartite agreement signed
between AAI, the Employees Union and the Government and the decision of Committee on
Infrastructure that the services on the air side and in the terminal building would be assigned to
AAI only; it would not be given to private people.
8. It is further mentioned that a Tripartite Committee was constituted comprising AAI, AAI
Employees Joint Forum and Government of India, in 2006. The Committee submitted its
recommendation in June, 2009 in which it was inter-alia  recommended that Chennai and Kolkata
Airports would be developed/modernized by AAI besides other non-metro airports. The private
players’ involvement for non-metro airports shall be r estricted for maintenance/commercial
operations of Terminal Building and City Side development including cargo operations. In a related
development in the year 2009, Planning Commission had also prepared a Model Concession
Agreement for Non-Metro Airports which covers only city side development including maintenance
and commercial utilization of the Terminal Building and Cargo Operations. On the line of above,
Bids for Amritsar and Udaipur airports were also called for but could not materialise due to absence
of finalisation of scope of work.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
9. The Committee does not agree with the Task Force/Ministry that potential for ‘non
aeronautical revenues’ from car parking, cargo facilities, hotels, passenger amenities, shopping etc.
“cannot be fully harnessed by AAI due to its inherent constraints”. But the Committee was neither
informed specifically about those constraints, nor about the efforts, if any, made by the
Government to enable AAI to overcome them. The Ministry of Civil Aviation did not have
convincing answers for these queries raised by the Committee. Even the Task Force report itself
has acknowledged the development of 35 non metro airports by AAI during the Eleventh Plan, of
which, development of 27 airports has been completed with the investment of 
R4565 crores (upto
March, 2012) and the development work of remaining airports is in progress. Thus, Airport
Authority of India has just shown its ability to modernize and operate 35 airports, of which several
airports including Kolkata and Chennai Airports are the profit making ones. The Government has
invested thousands of crores for modernizing these airports and unfortunately, now it is proposed
to hand them over to private people. The Committee feels that instead of privatizing these airports,
their management can be retained by AAI and a subsidiary of AAI or a Special Purpose Vehicle can
be formed to acquire the necessary expertise in managing those airports. (Para 25 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
10. Post implementation of PPP at DIAL, MIAL, BIAL and HIAL, non-aero revenue for these
airports increased substantially i.e. about 50% to 60% of total revenue. Whereas, in AAI airports,
non-aeronautical revenue even today is restricted to 16%. Therefore, it is evident that AAI has not
been able to maximize their non-aeronautical revenue due to various restrictions including delay in
tendering of F&B counters, availability of city side etc.
11. Many of the AAI land in City Side is under encroachment and being a Government
organization, AAI finds it difficult to get the encroachment vacated. AAI has not been able to
exploit the potential of non-aeronautical revenue.

3
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
12. The Committee notes the reply giving the reasons for Airports Authority of India not
being able to maximise their non-aeronautical revenue. The Committee is of the view that
the Ministry should endeavour to empower the AAI also to maximize its non aeronautical
revenue.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
13. During the deliberations the Committee was informed that privatization of Delhi and Mumbai
Airports helped the Airports Authority of India to garner a lot of resources which was not possible
otherwise. The Committee does not accept this argument. While arguing repeatedly in favour of
privatization having been beneficial to AAI, the Government conveniently tries to overlook the fact
that if the AAI had been allowed to run Delhi and Mumbai airports by itself, the magnitude of their
earnings would have been much higher. The Committee feels that Model Concession Agreement
signed at the time of privatization of airports at Delhi and Mumbai allowed the private airport
operators to generate huge revenue with minimum investment. (Para 26 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
14. It is a fact that the non-aeronautical services at IGI, CSI and AAI airports, are not run by
the airport operators. Almost all of non-aeronautical activities are concessioned out. However, private
airport operators are able to exploit the potential of non-aeronautical services more than AAI.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
15. The Committee notes the reply and strongly feels that issues relating to generating
more non-aeronautical revenue at Indira Gandhi International Airport, Chatrapathi Shivaji
International Airport and Airports Authority of India need to be examined by it in detail
in future. The Committee will be in a better position to make an informed comment on this
issue only after going into Model Concessionaire Agreement, Operation, Maintenance and
Development Agreement, etc. of these airports. Cost of services and social obligations are
also issues to be taken into account.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
16. The Committee, therefore, recommends that at the first instance AAI be allowed to operate
the airports at Chennai and Kolkata and other non-metro airports for a few years. When asked
whether AAI is incapable of managing the newly modernized airports, the Chairman, AAI stated that
there were certain issues and these are being resolved but nowhere did he say or accept that AAI
is incapable of carrying out this responsibility. The Secretary of the Civil Aviation, however, tried
to argue that the AAI will not be able to do the job, almost toeing the line taken by the Task Force
of the Planning Commission in this regard. (Para 27 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
17. As explained in reply to para 24 above, as per the recommendations of the Task Force and
directions of PMO, MoCA has decided to take up these airports under PPP in order to maximize
all the revenue streams and introduce greater efficiency in the running of these airports.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
18. The Committee notes the reply and reiterates its comments made above.

4
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
19. The Committee is of the view that efficiency of AAI and constraints, if any, can be
assessed precisely only after they are allowed to manage and run/operate these airports themselves.
An agency which has been managing our airports till now may not become inefficient just one fine
morning. Such a decision is based on presumptive conclusions that AAI does not have the
expertise, that it will not be able to mobilize required resources, that it will not be able to manage
non-aeronautical services and that it will not be able to manage so many agencies by itself. It
appears that the decision to give the airports under PPP has been taken merely on fallacious
conclusions based merely on assumptions, with no concrete bases. (Para 28 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
20. AAI has the expertise to run the airports. However, expertise of private sector in marketing
and exploitation of all sources of income results in the success of PPP model.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
21. The Committee notes the reply and feels that the Ministry should explore
possibilities of taking positive initiatives to empower AAI, which will have to run those
airports which will not find private people to run them on commercial reasons.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
22. The Committee is dismayed that instead of strengthening AAI by giving it much needed
financial and administrative autonomy to enable it to take its own decisions without being
influenced/advised by either the Ministry or the Planning Commission, a decision to give our
airports on platter to private parties was taken. The Committee fails to understand the logic behind
privatizing all these airports after spending tax payers’ money. The public utilities created by public
fund cannot be given to private parties for commercial considerations. (Para 29 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
23. Airports Authority of India has mini-ratna status and as per the AAI Act, 1994, the
authority already has financial and administrative autonomy. During the PPP process it will be
ensured that the financial interests of AAI are fully protected and AAI gets a higher return on
their investment.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
24. The Committee notes the reply.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
25. The Committee was informed that commercial space in the terminal buildings of both
Chennai and Kolkata airports could not be utilized to generate revenue from the available huge
space in terminal buildings. Entire blame for this has been laid on the Airports Authority of
India and the Ministry had nothing to show about the efforts made by itself to help Airports
Authority of India. It appears that AAI is being made a scapegoat in this regard. The
Committee feels that it was due to negligence more on the part of the Ministry of Civil Aviation
than the AAI. (Para 30 of ibid)

5
Reply of the Government
26. As per ICAO standards, the percentage of total floor area that can be used for commercial
activities in an airport has been fixed and space for commercial utilisation at Chennai and Kolkata
airports, have been earmarked accordingly. Any additional usage of floor area for commercial
activity may fetch additional non-aeronautical revenue but would compromise the floor area
available for users of the airport. In addition, the additional floor area usage for commercial
activities within the terminal building may affect the aesthetic look of the terminal building.
Therefore, considering all these aspects, AAI has struck a balance in fixing the commercial floor
area in its airports.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
27. The Committee notes the reply of the Government and would like to know the
details in respect of percentage use of floor area for commercial activities to the total floor
areas at Indira Gandhi International Airport, Chatrapati Shivaji International Airport,
Bangalore International Airport and Hyderabad International Airport.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
28. The Committee notes the argument that large number of sub-optimal service contracts being
awarded by AAI could also be eliminated if the operation and maintenance of the entire airport is
granted to a single PPP concessionaire. The Committee fails to understand why AAI itself cannot
be allowed to adopt a ‘single agency model’ which the Ministry/Planning Commission is visualizing
under the PPP mode. Even in the airports at Delhi, Mumbai, Hyderabad and Bangalore, services
including commercial operations in the terminal buildings have been given to several agencies. The
Committee, therefore, does not agree with the contention of the Planning Commission/Ministry in
this regard. (Para 31 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
29. In case of Chennai and Kolkata airports, AAI has already adopted ‘single agency model’ for
Food and Beverages. Contracts have already been awarded by AAI under this model to optimise
the non-aeronautical services and also increase the revenue from this source while taking into
account the interest of users of these services.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
30. The Committee notes the reply and reiterates its comment made above.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
31. The private players in airport infrastructure sector are using entirely the money borrowed
from Indian Financial Institutions. The Committee is at a loss to see that the Government generally
does not allow the AAI to issue tax free bonds for mobilizing project finance and sanction of grants
for funding of non-viable small airports. AAI’s finances are highly constrained due to huge pending
dues from various airlines, particularly Air India. The Committee would like to know the steps
taken by Government to recover such dues from private players so far. (Para 32 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
32. AAI has been allowed to raise tax free bond as announced in 2013-14 budget. AAI is
expected to raise R500 crore in the Year 2013-14.

6
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
33. The Committee notes the reply. The Committee may be furnished a status report on
the steps taken by Government to recover outstanding dues from private as well as
Government air service providers.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
34. The Committee finds it strange that AAI is now being forced to build, operate and manage
smaller and non-metro airports which are loss-making. Thus, with the metro and other profit-
making airports going to private people, AAI will be left with economically unviable ones. Earlier,
Airports Authority of India was in a position to off-set the losses of non-metro airports by revenue
earned from metro airports through cross-subsidy. The Committee fails to understand how the AAI
will be able to carry out its mandate with the depleted resources. Other lucrative activities of AAI
are being taken out and being given to a separate corporation. (Para 33 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
35. Presently, AAI is getting a revenue share of about R2000 crore from JVC airports viz DIAL
and MIAL. This may increase further in the coming years. Also some of the major airports of AAI
are profit making airports and thus profits are used to cross subsidize the low cost / loss making
airports. AAI is also ensuing construction/upgradation of non-operational airports through PPP/State
participation.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
36. The Committee notes the reply of the Government.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
37. The Committee is of the view that construction, operation and maintenance of airports
should remain with Airports Authority of India. It should be empowered to take decisions to
generate resources rather than handing over the core activities of the airports newly constructed
with public money to the private players. (Para 34 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
38. As explained in reply to para 24 above, as per the recommendations of the Task Force and
directions of PMO, MoCA has decided to take up these airports under PPP.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
39. The Committee notes emphasis laid on “direction from PMO/MoCA” in
Government’s reply. The Committee reiterates that Airports Authority of India should be
empowered with more financially and administratively as it has to eventually operate and
maintain non-lucrative airports in the country.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
40. The Committee is surprised to see the unusual haste being shown in the process of
privatization right from the constitution of Task Force, constitution of three Inter-Ministerial
Groups (IMGs) one after another, placing the matter before Cabinet and obtaining its clearance,

7
process of pre-bidding, etc. The Committee also was not given a final view regarding whether the
existing Section 12A of Airports Authority Act and the relevant Rules/Regulations do adequately and
clearly provide for allowing all the services – airside, terminal building and city side to private
parties. The Committee would like to have clear opinion of the Law Ministry in this regard.
(Para 35 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
41. Both CSI and IGI (both city side and airside) airports are given to private companies for
operation, management and development under Section 12A of AAI Act, 1994. Hence, it is
permissible as per AAI Act to go in for PPP.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
42. The Committee notes the reply and feels that these aspects of IGI and CSI need
deeper study.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
43. The Committee expresses its concern about the fate of employees of both Chennai (1050)
and Kolkata (1200) airports, after they are put under PPP. The Committee would like to remind
the Ministry and the Planning Commission the poor handling of employee related issues after the
airports in Delhi and Mumbai were privatized. Employees in these airports had to undergo tough
times and ultimately, most of them either had to take voluntary retirement or were transferred to
remote locations where they did not have any work and the AAI had to pay them full salaries and
allowances. The private parties generally try to stall the absorption of existing employees with them
even if they are required to do so under the agreement. The Committee feels that same thing is
going to be repeated this time as well. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the
fate of the existing employees needs to be tackled first at both the airports before taking any step
in this regard. (Para 36 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
44. While finalising the Concession Agreement, interest of the employees will be fully protected
by making adequate provisions to this effect in the Concession Agreement.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
45. The Committee notes the reply of the Government and reiterate its earlier
recommendation of protecting the interest of AAI employees in consultation with them.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
46. By giving away more and more airports to the private sector on long term transfer (it has
been proposed to give these airports on 60 year long-term lease) for management and operations,
the role of private sector will increase and the airports will be controlled by only private companies
leading to a situation where there will be private sector monopoly and a strong public sector will
be sacrificed. AAI will end up in managing only the loss making airports and other non-operational
airports and also airports in remote areas which are being considered for development at present
for improving air connectivity to remote areas and tourist destinations, medical tourism, etc.
(Para 37 of ibid)

8
Reply of the Government
47. Even after PPP of AAI airports, ATC/ANS services will be fully managed by AAI and 100%
revenue from that activity will accrue to AAI. Further, AAI will also get revenue share from JVC.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
48. The Committee notes the reply of the Government.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
49. The Committee would like to remind the Ministry that India is a vast country and its civil
aviation sector is growing fast. Large parts of the country need to be connected by air for which
we need modern airports, and AAI is the agency which has to undertake this mammoth task. The
Committee feels that by cutting AAI into size or shrinking its area of operation, the Government
of India is not facilitating the national task of air connectivity to the length and breadth of the
country. (Para 38 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
50. Government of India is in the process of establishment of 50 low cost airports for
providing air connectivity to tier-II and tier-III cities with a view to provide regional connectivity.
The task of establishment of these airports has been assigned to AAI. The resources after
developing these airports will be raised from the profit making AAI airports and PPP airports of
AAI.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
51. The Government should address these issues before embarking on the process of operation
and management of these airports through PPP mode. (Para 39 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
52. While finalising the Concession Agreement observations of the Committee will be taken into
account to protect the interest of AAI and its employees.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
53. The Committee notes the reply of the Government. The Committee would like to
know if the resources earned by AAI from its profit-making airports will be adequate
enough to meet expenditure of the airports in tier-II and III cities, which may not be profit
making.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
54. Even if it is a question of bringing in external management expertise to augment the internal
expertise of AAI in managing these airports, this could be achieved even without leasing out these
newly-built airports to the private sector. A better way to achieve efficiencies in operations can be
through awarding a ‘management contract’ to an entity with expertise in this domain by the AAI
itself. (Para 40 of ibid)

9
Reply of the Government
55. While finalising the model of PPP, the observations of the Committee will be taken into
account in the best interest of AAI.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
56. The Committee notes the reply of the Government and hopes that interest of the
AAI would be well protected while taking such a major policy decision.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
57. The Committee feels that the PPP model has its own pitfalls. We have seen in development
of national highways that due to poor margins in road projects, concessionaires are abandoning the
projects in the recent years as toll collection did not turn out as per their expectations. The private
airports developers, we have seen, no doubt gave world class facilities but at a prohibitive cost
to the passengers. These are not being run as for public service. The Committee feels that they
tend to increase user fees, as they have done in the past, on the pretext that operations in the
terminals and city side areas are not profitable. The Committee is of the considered opinion that
our people need good facilities at airports but not which are unaffordable. It could be done by a
public owned body and not by private agencies. In fact, motto of AAI is to develop and provide
affordable facilities to the people. (Para 41 of ibid)
Reply of the Government
58. As against the observations of the Committee, the PPP model is very successful in the
airport sector. Further, Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) has been put in place as
a regulator. Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) conducts extensive stakeholder
consultation before fixing any airport charge.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
59. The Committee notes the reply of the Government. However, the Committee feels
that even the role of Airports Economic Regulatory Authority of India as a regulator also
needs to be reviewed. It is said that AERA has not been able to fulfil its desired mandate.
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
60. In view of what has been mentioned above, the Committee recommends that AAI may be
permitted to manage and operate all its airports, including the lossmaking ones, with a rider that
there should be time bound delivery of world class passenger services in a more efficient and
transparent manner, matching with those being rendered by private airport operators.
(Para 42 ibid)
Reply of the Government
