The Shipbuilding and shiprepair sectors O<Xupy a very important place in the economy 
of a maritime country like India which bas a vast coastline of over 5000 kms., dotted with 11 
major ports and 139 intennediate and minor port.1. The growth of these sectors is of crucial 
importance for the augmentation, service and maintenance of the country's shipping fleet, 
international trade and commerce, employment generation, and other strategic reasons . 
2. At the time of independence, India had ubout a dozen shipyards, centered mainly 
around Calcutta and Bombay. This number has now grown to 42. Seven of these shipyards 
are in the public sector, and 35 small ones in the private sector. Of the seven public sector 
shipyards, four are under the administrative control of Ministry of Surface Transport 
(MOST), and the rest under the Ministry of Defence. The nodal responsibility for the entire 
Shipbuilding and Shiprepair Industry, including the F"IShing Vessels Industry and Floating 
Craft Industry, vests with Ministry of Surface Transport. The Shipbuilding ~d Shiprepair 
(SBR) Wing, headed by the Development Adviser is the apex technical organisation 
connected with the development of Shipbuilding and Sbiprepair industry, ancillary 
development and related R&D activities. The Wing renders advice on matters re.lated to the 
developmental facilities for Shipbuilding, Sbiprcpair Fishing Trawlers, Ship Ancillary and 
R & D activities. The Development Adviser also renders advice to other Ministries and 
Government departments .

4. After Independence, Indian shipping tonnage has steadily increased Crom 0.19 million 
GRT with 59 vessels, to 7.10 million GRT with 481 vessels as on 1.9.96. The fundamental 
objectives of Indian shipping since independence have been: 
(i) To reduce the dependence of external sea borne trade on foreign shipping 
services. 
(ii) To safeguard the imports of essentiul supplies especially POL, for the national 
economy.

5. The Indian Shipbuilding industry is important for the following main reasons: 
(i) Given the importance of shipping for the Indian economy, specially foreign 
trade , ships have to be acquired, either from indigenous or external sources. 
Hence, a domestic shipbuilding industry can save valuable foreign excha nge for

(ii) In spite of many advances in shipbuilding techniques, it is still a labour intensive 
process. Hence, the sector can provide considerable indigenous employment 
opportunities. 
(iii) As value addition is high in shipbuilding activity, this sector is commercially 
beneficial. 
(iv) The development of shipbuilding has :1 multiplier effect on ancillary industries. 
(v) Though shiprepair is a more profiwble business as compared to shipbuilding, for 
the overall progress of economy and due to strategic reasons, shipbuilding 
remains important. 
COMPETITIVENESS OF THE INDIAN SUlPBUILDING INDUSTRY 
6. The Comminee was told that the Indian shipbuilding industry can potentially be a 
compe titive one internationally for the following reasons: 
Labour Cost: Shipbuilding is a labour intensive process. As labour cost in India is much 
lower than in other nations, India has a competitive edge in labour costs. The physical shift 
of shipbuilding capacity over a period of time across the world suggests this. During the 60's, 
Europe used to be the main shipbuilding centre. In the 70's, shipbuilding in Japan started 
picking up due to increases in labour costs in Europe. Later, during the SO's, due io the very 
same reasons, shipbuilding shifted to South Korea and started declining in Europe and 
Japan. The 90's have seen the emergence of China, Brazil and Taiwan as prominent 
shipbuilding nations. For these same reasons , the future years can belong to the Indian 
shipbuilding industry, if productivity levels which are low in Indian shipyards, can be 
improved . 
Technics/ Expertise: India has a large reservoir of techni~al expertise . Hence, there will 
be no bottleneck s on the technical front. 
DemtlDd for shipbuilcling: Demand from Indian shipowners is likely to increase 
substantially in coming years, and this should mean a big market ' for shipbuilders in India. 
7. At the same time, the Comminee was also apprised of the constraints and problems 
facing the Indian Shipbuilding ind1.stry which have prevented it from emerging as a 
competitor in the international market. These ore: 
(A) Availabilily of raw materials: The Secretary, Ministry of Surface Transport told the 
Committee that a large proportion of raw materials and inputs used in the construction of

ships are not available in India and have to be imported. This imported content can go up to 
as much as 75% for large vessels or ships, sometimes. These important inputs range from 
special steel required for the ship, to special nuts and bolts. This raises the eost of 
construction of a ship in India to at least 30---40% more than elsewhere. Hero other 
shipbuilding countries like Japan and South Korea have an advantage as they are able to get 
these essential raw material inputs [rom their domestic sources. 
(B) Productivity: While labour cost may be low in India the productivity of labour in 
India in the shipbuilding sector is far lower than that in other major shipbuilding countries . 
The Committee was told that while there have been improvements in labour productivity, 
and for example as compared to ~.992 when it was 300 man-hours per Dwr it is now 192 
man-hours per Dwr, out productivity is still :almost three times lower than South Korea and 
Japan. Similarly, in South Korea, the completion of a bulk carrier takes about 8-10 months

(C) Lack of regular flow of adequate orders in both Nos. as well as in series 
construction resulting in idle manpower, and cost and time overruns. 
(D) The scheme of granting of subsidy to public sector shipyards constructing ocean 
going ships announced in September, 1993 expired in September, 1995, as a result of which 
shipyards are unable to procure further orders. This situation has to be contrasted with the 
wide range of subsidies, financial or otherwise, that are l?eing extended to national 
shipbuilders in the major shipbuilding countries like China . Japan, South Korea , U.S.A., 
Germany, Tai wan etc., which enable the shipyards of these countries to offer lower prices. 
(E) State Governments levy sales tax upto 8% on ships constructed in Indian yards. This 
pushes up construction . costs. 
8. The Committee was inforrncd due to a continuous recession in the Shipping industry 
during the 80's, shipbuilding activity all over the world was hit by a lack of orders, and 
unremunerative p rices of ships, and as a result, a number of shipyards in many countries had 
to close down. The Indian Shipyards too did not escape the impact, and suffered 
considerable losse.s. The. average capacity utilisation of the two largest shipyards, viz. 
Hindustan Shipyard Ltd. and Cochin Shipyard Ltd. was 32.07% and 11% respectively during 
the 8th Plan Period. 
SHIPREPAIR INDUSTRY IN INDIA 
9. Unlike shipbuilding, the shiprepair sector in India is a profitable activity and is 
characterised by full utilisation of capacity. This however, is mainly due to the limited 
capacity existing in the Indian shipyards. Thus as against an estimated demand of 9,420 
drydock days per annum, a capacity of 3,850 drydock days per annum only is available in 
the country. Apart from an overall quantitative limit on capacity, the shiprepair facilities 
available in India also do not extend to the entire range of vessels. 111e main types of vessels 
which are repaired in Indian yards are offshore vessels, passenger vessels, liner vessels, a 
limited type of bulk carriers, etc. Specialised vessels like acid carriers and gas tankers are 
not repa ired in Indian yards which have not acquired the experti se for this. 
10. The Committee was told in a written memoranda by the Ministry of Surface 
Transport that, due to this limited capacity , a large number of Indian ships are being 
repaired outside the country with a huge foreign exchange outgo , the Committee was told 
that presently over 50% of Indian merchant ships are repaired overseas which results in a 
outgo of U.S. S 50 million. The Committee was also told that a number of !ndian ships get 
repairs done abroad on foreign Yfrds which arc on route. The principal competitors to the
