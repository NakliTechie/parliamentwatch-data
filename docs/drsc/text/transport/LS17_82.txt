R E P O R T
 
Roads are the very arteries of the nation, carrying the lifeblood of trade and commerce to all corners of the country,
 promoting and sustaining a robust national economy.  They also play an important role in national integration, which is
 particularly important in a country of the size of India.
 
2.         The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways is responsible for development, formulation and implementation of
 policies relating to Road Transport and National Highways.  Its main function relate to road development and maintenance
 including Border Roads, Planning and co-ordination and Road Transport. The Ministry is administratively divided into two

D R A F T R E P O R T
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/82ndreport.htm[22-10-2018 12:03:03]
 wings namely Transport Wing and Roads Wing.
 
3.         Transport Wing is concerned with the formulation of broad policies relating to Road Transport, Motor Vehicles Act,
 legislation relating to Road Transport Corporations, besides making arrangements for movement of motor vehicular traffic with
 the neighbouring countries.
 
            Roads Wing is mainly concerned with the matters relating to:-
(i)                  Advising Government on all general policy matters relating to Highways;
(ii)                Development & Maintenance of roads declared as National Highways;
(iii)               Roads other than National Highways in Union Territories;
(iv)              Administration of Central Road Fund (CRF) pertaining to State Roads other than rural roads;
(v)                Evaluation of standards for roads and bridges and formulation of specifications;
(vi)              Performance of road research;
(vii)             National Highways Authority of India and National Institute for Training of Highway Engineers.
 
4.         The following autonomous bodies function under the administrative control of the Ministry of Road Transport &
 Highways: -
 
(a)                National Highways Authority of India (NHAI); and
(b)                National Institute of Training of Highway Engineers (NITHE).

9.         While making presentation before the Committee during his evidence, the Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport &
 Highways brought out that the National Highway system in the country suffers from deficiencies of capacity constraint,

 inadequate pavement crust etc.  The resource requirement for removing deficiencies during Tenth Plan is around Rs.1,65,000
 crore.  However, the Tenth Plan allocation is only Rs.59,490 crore.  The Committee was further informed that the length of
 National Highway network other than NHDP is 51,290 kilometers and the pro-rata funds for maintenance and development for
 these highways are inadequate.  The Secretary also expressed the apprehension that National Highways Development Project
 (NHDP) may slide if funds assured for the project are not provided in future.
 
10.        According to the Tenth Five Year Plan, roads are the key to the development of an economy.  However, the allocations
 made for the road sector do not indicate their importance in the economic development of the country.  The Committee notes
 with concern that there is a huge resource gap of around Rs.1,06,000 crore in the road sector during the Tenth Plan.  A good
 road network constitutes the basic infrastructure that propels the development process through connectivity and opening up the
 backward regions to trade and investment.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to achieve the objective of
 Tenth Plan in road sector, the budget allocation of Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should be substantially increased at
 RE stage.  The Committee notes that the Government has not been able to attract private investment as anticipated.  Therefore,
 the budgetary allocations of the Ministry should be adequately increased during the remaining years of the Tenth Plan so as to
 reduce the resource gap in this sector to some extent. 
 
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
11.        India has a vast road network of 3.32 million kilometers of which the National Highways and the State Highways
 together account for 1,95,000 km.  As on 31
st March 2004, the length of National Highways network is only 65,569 km
 constituting about 2% of the total road network in the country and carrying about 40% of the total traffic across the length and
 breadth of the country.
 
12.        The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways is primarily responsible for the construction and maintenance of National
 Highways (NH). All roads, other than National Highway in States, fall within the jurisdiction of respective state governments. 
 The development and maintenance of National Highways are being done on agency basis.  The National Highways Authority
 of India (NHAI), State Public Works Departments (PWDs) and the Border Road Organisation (BRO) are the main agencies.
 Out of the total National Highway length of 65,569 km, about 14,279 km have been entrusted to National Highways Authority
 of India (NHAI) for improvement under National Highway Development Project (NHDP), Port Connectivity Schemes and
 other schemes.  The 7,457 km length of new National Highways is yet to be entrusted to agencies.  The balance lengths of
 about 39,129 km and 4,704 km of non-NHDP sections of National Highways are mainly entrusted to State PWDs and Border
 Road Organisation (BRO) respectively, as agencies of the Ministry. 
 
13.        Ministry of Road Transport & Highways has also decided to upgrade stretches of NH-34 from km 31 at Barasat and 398
 Km at Raiganj in West Bengal by availing assistance from Asian Development Bank.  While the total project size is around
 Rs.1085.70 crore, the outlay for the year 2004-05 is Rs.113.50 crore.  Separately, Rs.3210 crore has been earmarked for NHAI
 for Externally Aided Project out of which Rs.2568 crore is being provided as Grant and Rs.642 crore as EAP-loan.
 
14.        National Highways (Original) Works are capital works on Non-NHDP highways such as two laning, four laning,
 construction of bye-passes, construction of Rail over-Bridges, construction of Bridges, widening of roads etc. Budgetary
 provision for National Highway Works (O) for the year 2004-05 is Rs.1487 crore out of which Rs.1338.80 crore is for the
 States and Union Territories other than Sikkim and North Eastern States and a sum of Rs.148.20 crore has been earmarked for
 2004-05 for Sikkim and North Eastern States.

 a massive programme to develop highways through the National Highways Development Project (NHDP) in the country.  The

 under NHDP is about 14,279 km.  The details relating to NHDP are as under:-
(i)

 Chennai and Kolkata.  Total length of GQ is 5846 km.  As on 31 st March 2004, four-laning of 2082 km has
 already been completed and work is under various stages of implementation on the entire balance length of
 3764 km under 78 different contracts.

24.        The Committee observes that the main objective relating to the road sector for the Tenth Plan is balanced development
 of the total network.  The task would include widening of roads, improvement in riding quality and strengthening and road
 safety measures to cater to the growing demand for road services.  However, the Committee notes with dismay that out of
 43,833 kilometer of non-NHDP National Highways, only 1.95 per cent are four or more laned whereas 62.55 per cent of such
 National Highways are two-laned and 35.49 per cent of such National Highways are only single/intermediate laned.  The
 Committee cannot accept the wishful thinking of the Ministry that "this is intended to be implemented mostly through private
 sector participation on Build Operate Transfer (BOT) basis" without being supported by any specific facts.  Going by the past
 records, the Committee also feels it is not suggestive that such course will be available to the Government.  The allocation
 made for these National Highways is also meagre  when compared to the allocations made for National Highways under
 NHDP.  There is a mismatch between NHDP and non-NHDP Highways in terms of lanes, surface quality and funds. 
 Therefore, the Committee recommends that Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should give equitable weightage to the
 non-NHDP National Highways and provide adequate budgetary allocation required for their development as envisaged in the
 Tenth Plan.  A balanced approach should be adopted for increasing the lane width and improving the riding quality of these
 National Highways.  This will ensure that those parts of the country which are not connected with National Highways under
 NHDP also get due attention.  The development of National Highways in these areas will also help improve the economic
 development of such places.
 
VALUE ADDITION TO THE LAND ADJOINING NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
25.        During the course of the evidence of the representatives of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, the Committee
 observed that construction of National Highways results in appreciation of economic value of land on both sides of the road.  
 Hence while creating world-class roads, the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways adds value to the lands adjoining the
 roads and there is a price appreciation of these lands.  As a result, the owners of the land adjoining National Highway get
 advantage without investing any money. 
26.        The Committee feels that in view of the resource crunch being faced by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways,
 there is a genuine need for generation of additional revenue by the Ministry.  Whenever any National Highway is constructed, it
 leads to huge value addition to the land adjoining the National Highway and these benefits of addition in value have so far been
 enjoyed by private agencies only.   The Government should also get advantage of the appreciation of land value because it is
 the taxpayer's money that has led to the value addition of that property.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 Government may explore the possibility of imposing some cess/tax on land adjoining National Highways which is being
 utilized for commercial purpose on annual basis.  This could serve as a permanent source of income for the Government and the
 revenue so generated could be ploughed back only for the construction and maintenance of National Highways in the country. 
 Provision of ducting at the time of construction of National Highways for laying telephone and electric cables also leads to
 value addition and can be explored for generating more funds for the road sector. These measures may go a long way to meet
 the resource gap for maintenance and construction of National Highways in the country.

29.        During the course of the evidence, the Committee brought to the notice of the representatives of the Ministry of Road
 Transport & Highways and BRO that high-intensity explosives are being used while constructing the roads in the hilly areas. 
 This often results in landslides which lead to washing away of roads, bridges and residential areas.  The Committee sought the
 response of BRO in the matter.  Replying to the query, the representative of the BRO agreed with the view expressed by the
 Committee.  The Committee was informed that so far the executing agencies in the hilly areas were using high-intensity
 explosives.  The Committee was further informed that in view of the adverse effects of the high-intensity explosives, the
 Government of India has directed the manufacturing agencies to try for other alternatives.  The BRO was trying to switch over
 to some other system in this respect.  
 
30.        The Committee is of the opinion that hilly areas have a delicate ecological system and there is a need to reduce blasting
 in these areas so that the hill slopes do not destabilize or fall.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the BRO should
 switch over to using  low-intensity explosives while making roads in such areas.   The BRO should also consider constructing
 tunnels in hilly areas, wherever required.
 
31.        The Committee also enquired from the representatives of BRO as to whether they had any Research and Development
 facility within their own organisation to address such problems.  The Committee was informed that BRO had no specific
 Research and Development facility available with them.  However, it was mentioned that BRO had a huge experience of over
 forty years in road construction in hilly areas.
 
32.        The Committee is aware that Border Roads Organisation is a well disciplined, motivated construction organisation in
 the country equipped with proven capability and expertise developed over more than forty years in the area of construction of
 roads, national highways, permanent bridges etc. in the border and hilly areas.  However, the Committee is surprised to know
 that no Research and Development facility is available in the BRO.  The Committee is of the opinion that R&D should be an
 integral part of any organisation working in a field where technological advancements are bound to take place.  Practical
 experience of BRO of making roads in hilly areas is no doubt an added advantage for them but having an institutional
 mechanism for research and development is absolutely essential in the contemporary age.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the BRO should develop a Research and Development facility of their own so that they can have access to the
 modern technology and they could stay in touch with technological advancements in the field of making roads on hilly areas. 
 
DEVELOPMENT OF NEWLY DECLARED NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
33.        From a perusal of the documents submitted by Ministry of Road Transport & Highways for examination of Demands
 for Grants of that Ministry, the Committee observed that 7457 km of state road in various states had been declared as new
 National Highways on 25
th February 2004.  However, the newly declared lengths of National Highways were yet to be handed
 over to the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways by the State Governments.  In this context, the Secretary of the Ministry,
 during the course of the evidence, stated as under:-
 
"The total range of 7457 km of state roads were declared National Highways and those roads are yet to be handed over
 to us by the State Government.  The procedure is, the State Government makes a request.  We do not take over on our
 own.  The State Government requests and then we take it over.  So far roads have not been handed over".
           
34.        The Committee is concerned to note that certain lengths of state roads had not been handed over to the Ministry of Road
 Transport & Highways for taking over the roads for development even after a period of nearly six months after their declaration
 as National Highways.  The Committee feels that once the roads are declared as National Highways, the physical possession of
 the roads should be immediately taken over by the central agencies for their development as National Highways.  The
 Committee recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should adopt a proactive approach in this regard. 
 Before declaring a state road as National Highway, such lengths of the road must fulfil the criterion for declaration as National
 Highway.  The guidelines for declaration of state roads as National Highways should also be so revised that the State
 Governments fulfil the required criterion before any road is declared as National Highway.

CENTRAL   ROAD FUND
35.        Funds for development and maintenance of National Highways are provided by the Central Government on yearly
 basis. Budgetary allocation, however, had fallen short of the requirements. In recognition of the need for funds for the road
 sector the Union Budget for 1998-99 provided for a levy of additional excise duty and additional customs duty of Rs.1.00 per

40.        During the course of oral evidence of the representatives of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, the Members
 of the Committee sought information regarding the introduction of High Security Registration Plates by the Ministry.  The
 Committee enquired about the need for the introduction of such registration plates.  In response, the Committee was informed
 that a Technical Standing Committee to advise the Ministry in various matters relating to Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989
 was set up in 1996.  Based on recommendation made by the said Committee in May 1999 and keeping in view the increase in

 the number of cases of theft of motor vehicles and poor rate of recovery thereof, the Ministry accepted the recommendation to

            i)          Prescribing detailed technical specifications for the HSRP.
            ii)         Notifying the testing agencies to test the same.

ROAD SAFETY
47.        Road Safety is a area of serious concern.  An allocation of Rs.29.00 crore was made for this sector in BE 2003-2004
 which was reduced to Rs.28.16 crore at the RE stage.  However, the actual expenditure during the year was to the tune of
 Rs.28.24 crore.  Now an amount of      Rs.33.20 crore has been allocated in BE 2004-05.  Under this scheme, a number of
 activities such as publicity and awareness campaign on road safety, grants-in-aid to Non Governmental Organisations (NGOs)
 to create awareness on road safety/administer road safety programmes, assistance in the form of equipment for accident relief
 and refresher training to drivers in the unorganized sector are being undertaken.
           
48.        The Committee brought to the notice of the Ministry that in recent year, more than 80,000 persons had been killed in
 accidents on Indian roads annually.  The Committee further pointed out that the Delhi-Jaipur and Delhi-Agra Highways were
 the most accident prone of the five highways leading out of Delhi.  The Committee enquired as to whether Ministry of Road
 Transport & Highways had conducted any survey to identify the factors which have been the cause of higher rate of accidents
 on the two highways and taken remedial measures. 
 
49.        In response, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways that a study had been
 conducted by a private consultant for the NHAI to identify the requirement of improvements related to road safety on NH-2
 (Delhi-Agra section) and NH-8 (Delhi-Jaipur section).  The consultant has suggested some of the remedial measures for
 enhancing the road safety on these two highways such as road markings, delineators, road signs, improvement of junctions,
 improvement of median openings, provision of service road etc.  The total estimated cost of these works as submitted by the
 consultant is Rs.121.22 crore.  The reports are being scrutinized and further action for execution of these works will be taken
 thereafter keeping in view the availability of the resources.   The Committee enquired from the Ministry regarding the steps
 taken for increasing public awareness about road safety.  In response, the Committee was informed that the Ministry works
 with the different State Governments and NGOs in the following areas:-
·
               refresher training for heavy vehicle drivers
·               evacuation of accident victims from the accident spot to the nearest hospital by assisting States and NGOs with
 ambulances
·               removal of vehicles involved in accidents on National Highways by providing a crane to States and NGOs
·               checking overloading of vehicles
·               NGOs and institutions are also given financial assistance for conducting refresher training to drivers of heavy
 commercial vehicles in the unorganized sector
·               providing non-recurring financial assistance to State Government / NGOs for setting up model driver training
 schools.
·               publicity and awareness on road safety through campaigns in print and electronic media.
50.        The Committee observes that the mortality rate due to the road accidents in the country is too high.  The rate of
 accidents has gone up after the improvement in the riding quality of our highways.  The Committee feels that so far the
 measures taken by the Government to contain road accidents have proved to be inadequate.  Hence there is an urgent need to
 re-devise the strategy for taking remedial measures and minimize road accidents.  The Committee recommends that the
 Government should introduce road safety devices and launch awareness programmes for the public and transport users in a big
 way.  The Committee takes note that a consultant has suggested some remedial measures for enhancing the road safety on some
 highways.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should adopt a proactive
 approach in this regard and there should be wider and effective use of fast improving applications of information technology
 and communication technology to monitor technical deficiencies on the roads.  The Committee suggests that the Ministry of
 Road Transport & Highways should involve their R&D wing and before constructing National Highways, road safety features
 should be incorporated in the design of the road at the detailed project report stage itself. 
 
51.        During the course of the evidence, the Committee observed that there had been frequent digging of National Highways
 by various local and central agencies.  This exercise is mostly undertaken for laying of cables etc.  However, the roads are not
 laid properly after that and are merely filled up with mud.  In some cases the process goes on in a very haphazard way.  Such
 portions of the road sink inside during rains.  The drivers of the vehicles passing through the roads are not aware of the puddles
 and the potholes so created.  This often leads to serious accidents which result in loss of precious lives and property.
           
52.        The Committee is of the view that with the development and improvement in riding quality of the roads in the country,

OBSERVATION/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
AT A GLANCE
 
PLAN PROVISIONS
According to the Tenth Five Year Plan, roads are the key to the development of an economy.  However, the allocations
 made for the road sector do not indicate their importance in the economic development of the country.  The Committee notes
 with concern that there is a huge resource gap of around Rs.1,06,000 crore in the road sector during the Tenth Plan.  A good
 road network constitutes the basic infrastructure that propels the development process through connectivity and opening up the
 backward regions to trade and investment.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to achieve the objective of
 Tenth Plan in road sector, the budget allocation of Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should be substantially increased at
 RE stage.  The Committee notes that the Government has not been able to attract private investment as anticipated.  Therefore,
 the budgetary allocations of the Ministry should be adequately increased during the remaining years of the Tenth Plan so as to
 reduce the resource gap in this sector to some extent. 
(Para 10)
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (NHDP)
The Committee notes that about 90% of the Golden Quadrilateral is proposed to be completed by December 2004.  The
 Committee recommends that the Government should take all necessary steps to make sure that the works proposed to be
 completed by December 2004 are completed within that period.  However, the Committee is not satisfied with the reasons
 given by the Ministry for delay in completion of NHDP programme.  The reasons are general in nature and could have been
 anticipated well in advance whereby the time of one year could have been saved.  This clearly shows the lack of proactive
 approach on the part of the Ministry. The Committee also recommends that the Government should evolve a monitoring
 mechanism to ensure quality of construction, making officials accountable for failure, transparency in implementation process,
 penalising defaulting contractors etc. so that the remaining part of the project is also completed within the revised targeted
 period.
(Para 20)
STATUS OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS NOT COVERED UNDER NHDP
The Committee observes that the main objective relating to the road sector for the Tenth Plan is balanced development
 of the total network.  The task would include widening of roads, improvement in riding quality and strengthening and road
 safety measures to cater to the growing demand for road services.  However, the Committee notes with dismay that out of
 43,833 kilometer of non-NHDP National Highways, only 1.95 per cent are four or more laned whereas 62.55 per cent of such
 National Highways are two-laned and 35.49 per cent of such National Highways are only single/intermediate laned.  The
 Committee cannot accept the wishful thinking of the Ministry that "this is intended to be implemented mostly through private
 sector participation on Build Operate Transfer (BOT) basis" without being supported by any specific facts.  Going by the past
 records, the Committee also feels it is not suggestive that such course will be available to the Government.  The allocation
 made for these National Highways is also meagre  when compared to the allocations made for National Highways under
 NHDP.  There is a mismatch between NHDP and non-NHDP Highways in terms of lanes, surface quality and funds. 
 Therefore, the Committee recommends that Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should give equitable weightage to the
 non-NHDP National Highways and provide adequate budgetary allocation required for their development as envisaged in the
 Tenth Plan.  A balanced approach should be adopted for increasing the lane width and improving the riding quality of these
 National Highways.  This will ensure that those parts of the country which are not connected with National Highways under
 NHDP also get due attention.  The development of National Highways in these areas will also help improve the economic
 development of such places.
(Para 24)
VALUE ADDITION TO THE LAND ADJOINING NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
The Committee feels that in view of the resource crunch being faced by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways,
 there is a genuine need for generation of additional revenue by the Ministry.  Whenever any National Highway is constructed, it
 leads to huge value addition to the land adjoining the National Highway and these benefits of addition in value have so far been
 enjoyed by private agencies only.   The Government should also get advantage of the appreciation of land value because it is
 the taxpayer's money that has led to the value addition of that property.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 Government may explore the possibility of imposing some cess/tax on land adjoining National Highways which is being
 utilized for commercial purpose on annual basis.  This could serve as a permanent source of income for the Government and the
 revenue so generated could be ploughed back only for the construction and maintenance of National Highways in the country.

 approach in this regard and there should be wider and effective use of fast improving applications of information technology
 and communication technology to monitor technical deficiencies on the roads.  The Committee suggests that the Ministry of
 Road Transport & Highways should involve their R&D wing and before constructing National Highways, road safety features
 should be incorporated in the design of the road at the detailed project report stage itself. 
(Para 50)
The Committee is of the view that with the development and improvement in riding quality of the roads in the country,
 the speed of the vehicles has also gone up.  Non-uniformity of the quality of the surface because of indiscriminate digging and
 non-compliance of rules by other departments while digging, presence of slow moving vehicles particularly in densely
 populated areas, straying of animals in the carriageways lead to fatal accidents.  Therefore, the Committee feels that availability
 of service roads/underpasses in densely populated areas is of immense importance.  The Committee further feels that laying
 ducts is not only beneficial for value addition and resource mobilization but also for avoiding indiscriminate digging.  The
 Committee is of the view that lack of telecommunication facility on the National Highways is also responsible for higher death
 rate.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should ensure the availability of adequate
 telecommunication for availing medical facility.  The Committee further recommends that in view of the growing number of
 accidents, adequately equipped trauma centers should be opened at reasonable distances on National Highways.
            (Para 52)
           
The Committee is of the view that if ever the roads are required to be dug up, they must be restored to their earlier
 position.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should make it mandatory
 that the quality of National Highways should be restored by the authority undertaking the digging work to a workable condition
 so as to be able to cope up with the traffic load till repair work is undertaken by the NHAI or concerned PWD.  The violators in
 this respect should be punished adequately. 
Para 54)
MODEL DRIVER TRAINING SCHOOL
The Committee also notes that in the recent past, there have been a large number of cases of accidents involving school
 buses wherein a number of school children have lost lives or suffered grievous injuries.  The Committee is of the opinion that in
 view of the high rate of accidents due to the fault of the drivers, it is extremely essential that driver training schools are set up in
 as much states as possible.  However, the Committee feels that this is one area where instead of asking for additional funds, the
 Government should attempt to invite private sector investment.  The Government should concentrate more on regulating the
 quality of training in driving skill being imparted by these schools.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 Government should make efforts to attract private sector investment in the opening of more and more driver training schools in
 the country.  The Committee is also of the view that unskilled drivers with licenses are like doctors with fake degrees and both
 are equally dangerous to the society.  The Committee is of the view that a substantial number of drivers are getting licenses
 using unscrupulous means with the connivance of corrupt officials.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
 Government should suggest all license issuing authorities to have stringent norms for issuing license to the drivers. 
(Para 58)
DIFFERENTIAL PRICING SYSTEM FOR PRIVATE VEHICLES
During the course of the oral evidence, the Committee observed that the automobile population in the country had been
 growing disproportionately with respect to the infrastructure created and the roads were under undue pressure.  In view, the
 Committee was of the opinion that there was a need to adopt a policy which discourages owning of multiple vehicles.  The
 efforts of the Government should focus on developing the public transport.  In order to discourage owning multiple vehicles,
 the Government should adopt a methodology whereby the rich and the affluent are asked to pay more for keeping more than
 one vehicle.  Acquiring more than one vehicle in a family should invite financial disincentives.  This would certainly
 discourage owning of multiple vehicles.  Further, it would also help release the pressure on the surface of the roads and also

(Para 59)
RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT
The Committee feels that there is an urgent need to undertake studies with regard to modernization and capacity
 building of road transport sector in the country with respect to its present and future scenario. The Committee is in agreement
 with the view expressed by the Secretary with regard to the inadequacy of the allocation for Research and Development
 activities of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the allocation for
 Research and Development should be adequately increased at RE stage.
