REPORT
Jammu and Kashmir holds a special place in the collective hearts of the nation. This picturesque
State nestled amidst the Himalayas holds the captive imagination of the country as a ‘paradise on
earth’. Images of its pristine landscape, house boats on the Dal Lake, lush valleys, the deep blue
lakes springing forth from Himalayan glaciers, the stark bareness of the cold deserts of Leh have
been etched in the minds of generations of Indians. Scenic shots of Leh etc. have captured the
beauty of Jammu & Kashmir in full cinematic glory, making Jammu and Kashmir an alluring
destination, to be visited by tourists, both domestic and international.
2. The tourism industry in Jammu and Kashmir is known across the world for its scenic landscape,
beautiful valleys, lakes, snowcapped mountains and religious places. The State of Jammu and Kashmir has
three distinct regions viz., Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh. All the three regions have immense potential for
tourism from both domestic as well as international tourists. Its impact is visible in the service industry
sectors such as transport, hospitality, horticulture, handicraft and the small scale industry.
3. With the Himalayas as its background, Kashmir’s natural landscape has made it a popular destination
for adventure tourism in South Asia. Jammu is renowned for its ancient temples, Hindu shrines, gardens and
forts. The Hindu holy shrine of Amarnath in Kashmir attracts millions of Hindu devotees every year, as does
the Vaishno Devi shrine in Jammu region. Jammu’s historic monuments feature a unique blend of Islamic
and Hindu architectural styles. Ladakh, the third region of the State has emerged as a major hub for adventure
and eco-tourism. This part of the Greater Himalayas popularly called ‘Moon on Earth’ comprises of naked
peaks and deep gorges. It was once an integral part of the silk route to Asia from the subcontinent.
4. Given its mountainous terrain and landlocked location, tourism is the primary economic activity of the
State and is the bread earner for a major part of the State. Jammu and Kashmir largely depends on the natural
resources that this place has to offer. Visitors’ spending generate income for both public and private sectors,
besides generating employment opportunities. Given that tourism is integral to the development of the State
of Jammu & Kashmir, the Committee decided to take up the subject ‘Development of Tourism in Jammu &
Kashmir’ for examination and report thereon.
5. In this connection, the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism
and Culture heard the views of the Ministry of Tourism; State Government of Jammu and Kashmir; and
Travel and Tour operators, Hoteliers etc. on the subject at Srinagar on 25th June, 2016. The Committee also
heard the views of the Secretary, Ministry of Tourism; Ministry of Finance; and State Government of
Jammu and Kashmir on the subject at Delhi on 14th July, 2016. The Committee further heard the views of
the Ministry of Tourism; State Government of Jammu and Kashmir; and Travel and Tour operators, Hoteliers
etc. on the subject at Srinagar on 11th July, 2018.

STATE TOURISM POLICY
7. A State Tourism Policy is essential to ensure the planned development of tourism in the region. The
Committee was informed by the Secretary, Ministry of Tourism that States have been encouraged to formulate
a State Policy for tourism to give direction to the growth of tourism in the State and to ensure its sustainability
in the years to come. The Committee was informed by the representatives of the State Government of
Jammu & Kashmir in July, 2018 that the State had formulated a State Tourism Policy which was being
finalised.
8. The Committee feels that the formulation of State Tourism Policy is integral to the growth,
promotions and sustenance of tourism and related activities in the region. The Tourism Policy
should give equal opportunities to all the stakeholders involved in tourism related activities like
the local people, private sector and NGOs. It should include provisions to promote effective
communication and greater intra-State coordination within J&K, i.e., Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh.
As tourism is mainly a private sector driven industry, it is important for J&K Government to act as
a facilitator with respect to tourism. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of
Tourism, Government of India should pursue with the  State Government  to finalise the State
Tourism Policy at the earliest. The Committee further recommends that the State Tourism Policy,
upon release, must be placed in public domain.
9. The Committee may be informed of the details of historical monuments and heritage sites in
J&K and the maintenance of the UNESCO Heritage Sites in the State. The Committee may also be
apprised of the present condition of the Pari Mahal. The Committee recommends that the same
may be included in the Action Taken Replies.
DISTRIBUTION OF FUNDS
10. The Comm ittee was informed by the Ministry of Tourism about the various policies which have
been introduced to provide funding towards the development of Swadesh Darshan (Annexure-I) for
integrated development of tourism based theme circuits and PRASHAD in Jammu & Kashmir (Annexure-V).
It was stated that there have been limited allocation of funds under the Swadesh Darshan Scheme.
However, given its status, the State has been allocated special funds under Prime Minister Development
Plan (2016) (Annexure-IV) .
11. It has been brought to the attention of the Committee that the State Government of J&K has not
submitted any proposals for funds during 2017-2018 and 2018-2019 for the development of tourism in

3
Ladakh region under the PRASHAD/Swadesh Darshan. Furthermore, the Committee was informed that
there have been no proposals for projects under Buddhist Circuit in Ladakh.
12. The Committee expresses its displeasure that the State Government has not submitted any
proposals for the development of tourism in Ladakh region continuously for two years. The Committee
reiterates its view that development of tourism is integral to the holistic development of the State.J
& K deserves to be prosperous and economically able, to ensure a better life for its residents. The
Committee urges upon the State of J&K to submit proposals for the development of tourism in
Ladakh region, at the earliest. Furthermore, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of
Tourism must coordinate with the State Government to ensure that sufficient funds and due publicity
are given for the promotion of tourism in Ladakh region.
13. As Ladakh region offers multiple tourism options which cater to the taste of a vast array of
tourists from varied age groups, the Committee recommends that an elaborate tourism development
plan should be conceived for Ladakh which accounts for focussed  promotion of rural, theme based
and adventure tourism, besides opening of new destinations for the tourists.
SEASONALITY
14. The Committee is aware that in Jammu and Kashmir, every year, there is a shift of the Secretariat and
all other Government offices, from Srinagar to Jammu and vice versa . This bi-annual shift is known as
‘Darbar’ move. From May to October, all Government offices are housed in Srinagar which is the summer
capital of the State and from November to April, the offices are housed in Jammu which is the winter capital
of the State. The Jammu and Kashmir High Court also moves from Srinagar to Jammu in winters and from
Jammu to Srinagar in summers.
15. Jammu & Kashmir is blessed with temperate climate with four distinct seasons. Due to its geographical
position, most parts of the State are inundated with snow. This becomes a major drawback in the development
of capital infrastructure in the region.  The Committee was informed that the working season in the region
is extremely short and limited, given the terrain of the region, administrative difficulties and lack of advanced
machinery in the region, resulting in delay in completion of projects.
16. The Committee understands that the seasonality of the region, along with the other variables,
may contribute to the delay in completion of projects in the region. The Committee is of the view
that the time table followed by the Ministry of Finance towards funding various activities in the
region may not be conducive to the peculiarities of the region. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Ministry of Tourism, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and other
stakeholders may  impress upon the Ministry of Finance to chalk out a plan to ensure a more
sustainable and efficient way of releasing funds to J&K and other similarly affected States.
CONNECTIVITY
(i)  Air
17. The Committee is pleased to note that the airports at Leh and Srinagar have been upgraded and made
functional (Annexure-V). The Association of Tour, Travel and Hotel operators of the State informed the
Committee that the exorbitant price of air tickets is a major dampener. Escalating airfares of ‘low cost’
carriers during the peak season are a major deterrent to increasing footfall in the region. The airfares are

4
equivalent to that of the nearby foreign destinations and given the sensitivity of the region, tourists would
prefer to visit nearby foreign destinations at the same airfare rather than visit J&K.
18. In this regard, the Committee recommends that necessary measures must taken to keep the
air fares for J & K during the peak tourist season, under reasonable limit. The Committee also
recommends that the number of flights from Srinagar to Leh and from Srinagar to Kargil may be
increased.
19. Given the mountainous terrain and the frequency of landslides in the region, the Committee
recommends that Pawan Hans Services may be further augmented by pressing additional helicopters
into service, for tourist facilitation, to lesser known destinations like Gurez, Bangus, Kishtwar and
Kargil. UDAN scheme should also be devised in such a manner as to connect Srinagar with other
major cities especially in the peak tourist season.
20. The Committee could understand that the Sheikh-ul-Alam Airport (International airport) at
Srinagar is not functional. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism must initiate
talks with the Ministry of Home Affairs, Ministry of Civil Aviation and the Airports Authority of
India, to facilitate the operation of international flights from Srinagar. The Committee recommends
that the infrastructure provided at Srinagar Airport must be improved.
(ii)  Railways
21. Railways are an economical and romantic way of exploring the country.  Rail journeys are affordable
and increase the connectivity of the region with other parts of India. Domestic tourists are primarily train-
friendly and prefer to travel by trains due to better connectivity and affordability.
22. The Committee was informed that the Ministry of Tourism provided financial assistance to the tune of
12 crores @ ` 4 crore per coach to the Ministry of Railways for the manufacture of three glass top coaches,
for developing rail tourism in the country. The ‘Glass Top Coach’ have been designated for the route
between Budgam to Banihal and Baramulla to Srinagar.The Vistadome Coach boasts of a glass roof which
has electrically controlled opalescence, i.e., it can be made transparent or opaque at the turn of a switch –
360 degree rotatable, pushback chairs and automatic shading compartment doors.
23. The Committee recommends that Tourist Trains may be introduced to J&K from various
metros of the country. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Tourism must
coordinate with the Ministry of Railways for better railway connectivity with the valley, besides
operating additional tourist trains from all Metro Cities to J & K. Furthermore, the Committee
recommends that the Ministry of Tourism must coordinate with the Ministry of Railways to facilitate
the speedy completion of the Vistadome Coaches. These Coaches, with the glass tops, would be a
wonderful addition to the railway services in the region, by giving its passengers a 360 degree view
of the surrounding regions.
(iii)  Road
24. Jammu & Kashmir is a place of religious prominence and a popular pilgrimage destination. However,
the conditions of the roads in the region leave much to be desired. The Committee understands that the
mountainous terrain of the region makes building of capital infrastructure a herculean task. Roads, thus,
serve to become the cheapest and most reliable mode of transportation.

5
25. The Committee, in this regard, notes that the region is prone to frequent landslides,  road caving etc.
Many of the tourists are forced to travel by foot, while carrying the old and disabled on hand held palanquins.
Moreover, the Committee noticed that the roads lack proper signages and safety markers at bends or around
the thinner mountainside roads.
26. The Committee strongly feels that the National Highways should take initiatives to improve
the roads in Jammu & Kashmir. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of
Tourism may impress upon the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways to expedite the upgradation
of Jammu-Srinagar highway which is long pending. Furthermore, the Ministry of Road Transport &
Highways, through its agencies in the region, may also coordinate with the concerned State
departments in order to ensure that the roads including the National Highways are properly
maintained; signageare put in place and to ensure that fluorescent safety markers are installed
along the roads to reduce the incidence of accidents in the region.
27. The Committee also recommends the Ministry of Tourism to impress upon the Ministry of
Road Transport & Highways and the Government of J&K to improve road infrastructure in pilgrim
and border towns and backward areas of the State where the road network is poor and the existing
roads are unable to cater to the ever increasing vehicular movement.
28. The Committee observes that for all the above mentioned works related to connectivity, no
timelines or follow up mechanisms are in place. Delays in completion of projects increase the costs
and woes of people. The Committee recommends that an effective mechanism may be set up to
ensure that the respective Ministries take these recommendations seriously and come up with
specific and concrete timelines to follow up on the progress made and funds utilized.
HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
29. The presence of trained and skilled workforce is necessary for the success of a destination as a tourist
destination. The Committee was informed of the dearth of trained individuals in the tourism sector in the
State. The Committee is pleased to note the construction of the Institute of Hotel Management (IHM) in
Srinagar. However, there is a lack of qualified and trained professionals in tourism management.
30. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism must collaborate with the State
Government in order to introduce contemporary courses by the Indian Institute of Tourism and
Travel Management (IITTM) in the region. The students must be given due exposure in other
States. The Committee recommends that training must not be restricted to theoretical knowledge,
but must include internships and regular interactions with industry pioneers, in order to gauge the
present requirement of the sector. Local Universities and professional colleges should be encouraged
to introduce courses on Tourism studies and foreign language courses, in collaboration with national
and international institutes of Tourism studies.
31. The Committee recommends that the State Government may devise programs to introduce
skill/ craft as a subject under Socially Useful Productive Work (SUPW) in the curriculum, in order
to educate children of the rich traditional handicrafts of the State, develop skills in the State and
promote such activity. This will go a long way in ensuring a livelihood in the State, whilst ensuring
the longevity of these prized handicraft skills.
32. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Tourism must work with the Ministry

6
of Finance to provide some exemption to handicrafts under implementation of GST in order to
ensure a market for the handcrafted products which are presently being cornered by the cheaper
Chinese products.
MEDIA AND PROMOTION
33. The Committee was informed by the various stakeholders of tourism in the region that a major hindrance
to increasing the footfall in the region is the constant negative publicity received by the State as a troubled
State. This perception overshadows the true narrative of the region, as not all parts of the State are in
conflict or affected by it.
34. The Committee was also informed that the travel advisories issued by various countries acted as an
impediment in attracting foreign tourists. The Committee feels that many a times, the advisories are based
on ill-informed facts and unnecessary assessment of local conditions. The Committee feels that the print
and electronic media, both domestic and international, have an important role in projecting the favourable
situations existing in J & K.
35. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should organize their
own publicity campaign in order to remove the negative impression which are based on incorrect
facts. The Ministry should also involve the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting and the Ministry
of External Affairs for the removal of advisories imposed by various countries against travel to
Jammu & Kashmir.
36. The Committee recommends that the State Government, Ministry of Tourism and the various
other stakeholders must endeavor to remove the negative publicity in the press by finding innovative
means to engage with the world at large via the social media. In this age of Youtube and social
platforms like Instagram and Snapchat, social media influencers may be engaged to promote a
more positive image of the State. Furthermore, the Committee recommends that the State
Government must engage with ASSOCHAM, CII and FICCI to invite and encourage companies to
host meetings and visits in the region. The Committee feels that websites of the Ministry of Tourism
and its various arms should be made interactive and user-friendly. Mobile applications and social
networking sites like Facebook, Twitter, Snapchat, Instagram etc. should be harnessed as a quick
and cost effective medium for tourism marketing.
BUDDHIST CIRCUIT
37. The Committee could understand that the Ladakh region of the State is predominantly Buddhist.
However, it was informed that the Leh and Ladakh region of the State have not been included in the purview
of the Buddhist Circuit. The Committee feels that the Buddhist Circuit is integral to the promotion of the soft
power of India via its culture. The scenic landscape and terrain are a perfect backdrop for the Buddhist
Festivals in the region which are a major tourist attraction and are very popular the world over.
38. The Committee recommends that the State Government must take efforts to co-ordinate
with the Ministry of Tourism, in order to include Ladakh and Leh under the Buddhist Circuit in the
country and develop the tourist destinations in the region.
TOURIST FACILITIES
39. The Committee observed during its visit to Jammu and Kashmir that the State requires further

7
input in terms of improving upon tourist facilities in the region. The tourist destinations must be
equipped with safe drinking water stations, clean and hygienic washrooms, rest stop, car stops etc.
The Committee recommends that tourist facilities proposed under the Swadesh Darshan scheme
must be completed at the earliest. Furthermore, the Committee recommends that the State
Government must put in place a system to ensure that these tourist amenities, once operational,
are well maintained and at par with global standards. The creation of adequate and suitable
infrastructure in terms of accommodation, transport, communication links etc. and other essential
amenities are the foundation for the success of tourism in the State.
40. The Committee recommends that in order to improve the maintenance of existing tourist
destinations, adoption/management/sponsorship programs should be promoted so as to create a
sense of ownership in the minds of locals and corporates alike, for monuments, religious sites and
tourist destinations.
41. During the visit of the Committee to Srinagar, it was informed that there was an urgent need
to introduce a Laser Show on the Dal Lake and restart the Sound and Light Show at Shalimar
Gardens. The Committee may be apprised of the status of implementation of the Sound and Light
Show at Dal Lake. The Committee feels that these facilities may definitely attract more tourists
while serving to narrate the story of Kashmir. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of
Tourism should extend financial support to the Government of J & K to restart the above mentioned
projects.
SAFETY OF TOURISTS
42. Safe and secure travel is always the top priority for travelers. The Committee feels that security of
tourists must be given top priority. The Committee was informed by the Tour and travel operators and
Hoteliers that the travel bans imposed by various Governments, both international and domestic on travel to
the State create an air of negativity in the region. The Committee, during its visit to Srinagar, observed
that the situation was more peaceful than what was reported in the news and media. The Committee
feels that this is a matter which must be broached with caution as it involves issues of national
security.
43. The popularity of the region as a ‘dham’ pilgrimage site is offset by large crowds travelling on foot.
Often, the elderly, women and children have to be carried on indigenous palanquins which are neither sturdy
nor reliable.  The result is a chaotic situation of unregulated crowds which cause regular stampedes which
leave many injured and prove fatal for others.
44. The Committee, in this regard, recommends that the State Government must work on crowd
management in coordination with the concerned temple authorities, to formulate a plan to regulate
the crowds in the area. Furthermore, the State Government of J&K must work to ensure the
provision of regulated assistance, in order to prevent overcrowding by vendors.
45. The Committee recommends that the State Government must endeavour to establish an
evacuation plan and procedure, with signage and instructions at regular intervals, to help and evacuate
the tourists in the event of a stampede or a natural calamity.
46. The Committee is pleased to note that the State Government has implemented Tourism
Police in the State. The Committee recommends that the Tourism Police must be trained regularly

53. The sensitive nature of Jammu & Kashmir must be taken into consideration during the development of
tourism in the region. The implementation of GST on tourism in the region would have manifold effects,
mostly negative on the economy of the region. The Committee was informed by the stakeholders that
sourcing of products and materials is an issue. Unlike other States, the residents of Jammu & Kashmir and
its business owners spend huge amounts of money on procuring essentials, most of which have to be flown
in, which increases their capital expenditure. Most wholesale traders insist on payment in cash which is a

9

unsustainable.

NEED FOR GREATERCENTRE- STATE COORDINATION
56. The development of J&K has been integral to the overall development policy for the nation. In this
regard, the Committee was informed that the Prime Minister’s Reconstruction Package (PMRP) was instituted
as early as 2004 and a great deal of money was sanctioned under different kind of projects which included
a project for rural tourism, tourism authorities and one for the Circuits amongst others. The Committee was
further informed that the package also included compensation for businesses destroyed/ damaged by the
floods in 2014.
57. The Committee was also informed that under the Product/Infrastructure Development For Destinations
and Circuits, the Ministry of Tourism extended Central Financial Assistance to the State Governments/Union
Territory administrations for tourism projects identified in consultation with them for the improvement of
existing tourism products and also for developing new ones. Under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme of
Product/Infrastructure Development for Destinations and Circuits, 100%  Central assistance is given for
development of destinations/circuits, including mega projects, to world standard and also for Rural Tourism
infrastructure development. The Ministry of Tourism provides financial assistance up to ` 5.00 crore for the
development of destinations and ` 8.00 crore for circuits. The upper limit of financial assistance has been
increased to ` 25 crore and ` 50 crore for development of identified mega destinations and circuits respectively,
under the schemes to promote rural tourism in the country. The thrust is for development of tourism
infrastructure at the identified rural tourism sites so that socio-economic benefits percolate down to the
rural community. A maximum of ` 50.00 lakhs is sanctioned for each project under this scheme for
development of tourism related infrastructure. The Committee was informed that the scheme had been
restructured under PRASHAD and Swadesh Darshan. The details of amount sanctioned and funds provided
under the Swadesh Darshan have been included in Annexure I. The details of amount sanctioned and the
funds provided under the PRASHAD are provided in Annexure III.
