5.         The Committee notes that while comparing B.E. 2006-07 and B.E. 2007-08, it is observed that most of the major
 schemes under the Ministry of Culture have got  increased allocations.  The Committee feels that preservation and
 protection of vast cultural resources of the country is a task gigantic in nature and requires huge investments.  But, the
 Committee is at dismay that the Government has always treated it as non productive sector thereby inviting apathy of the
 Finance Ministry which is always reducing the allocations made for the Ministry of Culture at Revised Estimates stage.  
 The Committee strongly feels that this sector is catalytic for economic growth and not only that but also preservation of our
 culture means preserving our national identity which should not be compromised at any cost.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the allocations made for the year 2007-08 should not be reduced at RE stage by the Finance Ministry and
 the Ministry of Culture should try to spend the allocations to the fullest extent. 
 
6.         Transition from the 10
th Five – year Plan to the 11th Five Year Plan is an occasion for deep retrospection.  It is also
 an opportunity to learn valuable lessons from past successes and failures.  The new strategies for the 11 th Plan should be
 built on the already demonstrated strengths and successes during the past decades of planned development, while avoiding
 the pitfalls. Before embarking upon the new Plan it is necessary to compare the past performance as well as the future Plans
 undertaken by the Ministry. In this context the Ministry informed the Committee that “the Culture Sector has been classified
 as non-developmental sector.  It is not surprising that this sector has got neither the profile nor the priority it deserves in the
 Nation’s overall agenda for development.  It may be relevant to mention here that Art and Culture got a share of only 0.02%
 of the total 10
th Plan Allocations.”
 
7.         During the Tenth Plan Period, major institutions under the Ministry like the Sangeet Natak Akademi, Sahitya
 Akademi and Lalit Kala Akademi completed 50 years of their existence and organised Golden Jubilee Celebrations.  It was
 an occasion of not only to celebrate the achievements of decades but also to re-orient their activities by re-focussing their
 objectives.
 
8.         An allocation of Rs.1720.00 crore was approved by the Planning Commission for the Central Sector – Art and
 Culture for the Tenth Five Year Plan (2002-07).  Of this, an amount of Rs.1168.43 crore constituting about 68% was
 utilized by the Ministry of Culture during the year 2002-06.  An Annual Plan outlay of Rs.470 crore was allocated by the
 Planning Commission for the various activities of the Ministry of Culture during 2006-07.  It is pertinent to mention that
 many of the Plan activities in respect of the Ministry and its organisations were to be deferred during the Tenth Five Year
 Plan period as there was reduction of Annual Plan outlay of each year at the stage of Revised Estimates.
 
9.         Consequent upon the identification of the major thrust areas for the Eleventh Five Year Plan (2007-12) keeping in
 view the vision for Eleventh Plan by the Ministry, the following new schemes are proposed to be implemented from 2007-
08 for which token budgetary provisions have been made.  The details of the schemes are being worked out by the Ministry,
 namely:-

(ii)        Awareness creation in the area of Intellectual Property Right(IPR) and Setting up of a National IPR Cell for

(iii)       Plan Scheme for promotion and Dissemination of Awareness about Indian Cultural and Heritage.\

10.       Our culture is the most vibrant and rich culture in the world.  The Committee feels that culture has to be harnessed
 together with tourism and showcased to the world.  The right modal mix between culture and tourism would yield valuable
 foreign exchange by inviting more and more tourists; promote our artists and artisans many of whom are at the verge of
 starvation; preserve our local/ regional culture, art and craft ; last but not the least, it will also enthuse private sector to
 invest more and more in these activities.  The Committee strongly recommends that the approach of the Ministry of Culture,
 Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission should change during the 11
th Five Year Plan and all of them should
 together take adequate care and ensure that none of the Plan activities in respect of Ministry of Culture and its organizations
 is deferred due to reduction in Annual Plan outlay at the stage of Revised Estimates. 
 
11.       The year 2007-08 being the first year of the Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-12), the Planning Commission has
 allocated a sum of Rs.557.00 crore for various developmental activities of the Ministry of Culture.  Even though, this
 Ministry had proposed an outlay of Rs.905.41 crore for the first year of the Eleventh Plan, on the basis of the
 recommendations of the Working Group constituted by the Planning Commission and also receipt of proposals for the
 Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-12) including Annual Plan 2007-08, from various cultural organizations/divisions under the
 Ministry of Culture, the allocation for the Annual Plan 2007-08 has been pegged only at Rs.557.00 crore.  As regard Non-
Plan funds, a sum of Rs.375.61 crore has been provided during the year 2007-08.  The Plan provision for the Annual Plan
 2007-08 includes Rs.50.00 crore for the building Projects of the attached and subordinate Bodies of the Ministry of Culture.
 
12.       The Ministry has been allocated only a sum of Rs.400.00 crore under Plan at R.E. 2006-07 level, thereby effecting a
 reduction of Rs.70.00 crore from the original allocation.  On the basis of the recommendations of the Standing Committee
 for reviewing of budgetary allocation in the month of December instead of the month of September, the matter was taken up
 with the Ministry of Finance for their views/comments.  In this regard, they have shown their inability to alter the reviewing
 system of budgetary allocation, since preparation of budget documents for presentation of annual budget on 28
th February
 starts from December and it is, therefore, necessary to complete their R.E. exercise before this which is done during
 October-November every year. The  Planning Commission is of the opinion that inadequacy of resources under Non Plan
 Activities does not justify conversion of such activities to the Plan side.  It has been therefore, desired that the requirement
 of funds for the regular upkeep and maintenance activities and for protection and conservation activities may realistically be
 assessed and sought from the Ministry of Finance.
 
13.       The Committee strongly recommends its earlier observations with regard to budgeting pattern of the Ministry of
 Culture and reiterates its recommendations made at para 11 of the 103
rd Report on the Demands for Grants for the year
 2006-07 wherein it was stated that :-
 
"The Committee is of the view that fixing of the Revised Estimates ceiling by the Ministry of Finance on the basis of
 the level of expenditure attained upto the month of September/October creates problem for the Department of
 Culture.   The Committee observes that most of the cultural functions and festivals take place in the month of
 October and November, after the monsoon.  The Archaeological Survey of India also incurs major expenditure after
 the monsoon.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government must review the budgeting procedure and
 evolve some mechanism to ensure that the Plan allocation for the Department of Culture is not reduced.  The
 Committee recommends that if the review of the budgetary allocation is done in the month of December, instead of
 the month of September then the cut in the budgetary provision could be avoided.  "
 
 
 
Vacant posts in various premier Institutions functioning under the Ministry of Culture:

14.       The Committee took note of the fact that a large number of vacancies are existing in various cadres in different apex
 institutions/bodies functioning under the Ministry of Culture.  When the Secretary was requested to explain the position
 thereon he stated that in the ASI there were sixteen vacancies of Deputy Superintendent Archaeologist Surveyors, out of
 which they have advertised for eight posts and these are going to be filled.  The Secretary also informed the Committee that
 ASI does not have single museologist on its roll to look after 41 site museums working under it.  It also does not have single
 conservation architect and even conservation engineers are also not there in its panel resulting major problem in various
 activities.  Now, the Ministry is deciding to outsource these posts.  Particularly, when the Committee asked about the reason
 for not appointing a museologist, the Secretary, informed that there is a ban on creation of posts as well as filling up of
 posts.  The Secretary admitted before the Committee that out of thirty-three there are six organization which do not have an
 executive head at present.  The Committee had earlier recommended for appointment of Director-General, National
 Archives immediately and till now the said post is lying vacant.  The Director-Generals post is actually under the purview
 of the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC).  The UPSC advertised for this post and subsequently interviews were
 held on 21.2.2006, but none was suitable for the post.  The Ministry again requested UPSC to re-advertise this post. 
 Subsequently the post was re-advertised by the UPSC on 27.7.2006 but, till now no interview has been held.   Similarly, in
 the Anthropological Survey of India a person was selected and the proposal was sent to the ACC for appointment, but when
 the ACC was deliberating on   the process of approving the appointment of the selected person, the UPSC withdrew the
 recommendation that it had made for the candidate because of the reason that the qualification of the selected person was
 not adequate.   In the case of Indian Museum, Kolkata, the Ministry has completed the process of selection and it has gone
 to the ACC for approval and the appointment will take place very soon.  As far as the Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Institute
 of Asian Studies, Kolkata is concerned the post for the head of the executive was re-advertised twice.  The last time UPSC
 had recommended two candidates. Out of these two, when Ministry started selecting one, it was found that certain
 guidelines laid down by the Department of Personnel and Training for the Constitution of the Search Committee had not
 been followed. So the Search Committee had to be reconstituted to confirm to the guidelines given by the DoPT and now a
 person has been selected and will be appointed very soon.  In the Rampur Raza Library, the post of Director is lying
 vacant.  An interview was held on 30
th June 2006 by the Selection Committee but no suitable candidate was found. 
 
15.       Regarding the reason for non-filling up various key posts the 
 Secretary, Ministry of Culture expressed concern and helplessness and submitted before the Committee that UPSC
 generally takes about nine months to one year for filling up these posts.  Secondly, the Government have imposed a ban on
 recruitment;  there is a circular to this effect saying that only one percent of the total strength could be filled, and that too,
 through a Screening Committee, from year to year basis, and accordingly meeting will be held for filling up the direct
 recruitment posts.  The Screening Committee, however, has not met even for the year 2001-02 and 2002-03.   Meetings of
 the Screening Committee for both these years have been held recently and the Ministry of Culture has allowed some of the
 institutions to fill-up the vacancies arising in the year 2001-02 and 2002-03. The Meeting of the Screening Committee for
 the year 2003-04 has not yet been held.  The instructions of the Government are that apart from the posts which are there
 and which are not recommended by the Screening Committee, the rest of the posts have to be abolished.  So every year, the
 Ministry is actually abolishing the posts.  The Secretary informed the Committee that during Special Investigation Unit
 inspection at the National Library, Kolkata, it was recommended for abolition of certain posts, to which the National
 Library has not agreed, and these posts have not been abolished so far, thereby creating major problem. 
 
16.       The Committee notes the sorry state of affairs prevailing in various premier institutions of art, culture and literature
 and several of them are functioning headless.  The Committee takes strong exception to the fact that Screening Committee
 is not meeting regularly.  The Committee is also surprised to notice the lethargic approach of UPSC towards filling up these
 vacant posts which should have been done rather without any loss of time.   The Committee therefore, recommends that the
 Ministry should propose an amendment to the rules and a composite method of recruitment should be followed instead of
 direct recruitment method.  The Committee strongly feels that these key posts should be taken out of the purview of the
 UPSC and appointment should be made out of a panel of persons of the institutions having requisite experience and such
 other persons having eminence in various concerned fields.  As regards the posts of Deputy Superintendent Archaeologist
 Surveyors in the ASI is concerned the Committee expresses its displeasure in advertising the posts in peacemeal instead of
 in one go and recommends that all the vacant posts should be filled up without further loss of time. The Committee finds
 that despite the earlier recommendations of the Committee to appoint a person who is an expert in archaeology or eminent
 historian as the Director-General of ASI the Ministry of Culture continues to appoint civil servant from IAS as the Director-
General of ASI which is resulting in the neglect of this premier institution. The Committee reiterates its earlier
 recommendations to appoint eminent archaeologist or historian as the Director General of ASI.

17.       The Archaeological Survey of India, the biggest attached office under the Ministry of Culture has been provided with
 a major step up in the plan as well as non-plan outlay for the year 2007-08 for sustaining its various activities.  ASI has
 envisaged certain new schemes to address the issue of preservation and conservation of monuments.  It proposes to take up
 the conservation of at least 800 monuments/sites.  Besides, ASI proposes to implement a new scheme to provide visitor’s
 amenities at all World Heritage Monuments and those monuments visited by large number of tourists.  Another major
 initiative is to assist state protected monuments and unprotected monuments through the State Governments.  Modernising
 archaeological exploration by state of the art technologies is another area of priority of the ASI in the Eleventh Plan.  On
 account of these major and much needed initiatives, the Annual Plan Allocation of ASI has been kept at Rs.98.00 crore
 which includes Rs.8.00 crore for externally aided project/scheme of conservation and development of Ajanta- Ellora
 Complex through Japan Bank of International Corporation.  In case of Non-Plan provision for 2007-08 crore has been made
 to provide adequate funds to meet the committed liability such as salaries, D.A. etc. of all organisations under Ministry of
 Culture.
 
(a)        Conservation and Preservation of Monuments
 
18.       Conservation, preservation and maintenance of the centrally protected monuments and sites are the prime
 responsibilities of ASI.  The total number of individual structures being maintained by the ASI is over five thousand. 
 During the year 2006-07, 700 schemes under plan and 500 schemes under non-plan for conservation, preservation,
 environmental development and providing tourist amenities at monuments were taken up.
 
19.       During 2006-07, more than eight hundred monuments were taken up for conservation and structural repairs,
 chemical preservation and environmental development besides day-to-day maintenance of three thousand six hundred sixty
 seven monuments. ASI has been extending services and expertise to State Governments and other agencies by undertaking
 Civil Deposit Works of ancient structures other than the protected ones.            Public-private partnership is encouraged and
 some important corporate houses have contributed funds to the National Culture Fund for the developmental activities of
 Protected Monuments.  MOUs have been signed with Indian Oil Corporation, Steel Authority of India, World Monuments
 Fund, Indian Hotels Company Ltd.   The agreement for phase-II of Ajanta Ellora Conservation and Tourism Development
 Project with loan from JBIC, is finalised and ASI’s component being 901 Million Yen (Rs.36.87 crores) Conservation of
 monuments is multidisciplinary in nature.  ASI has entrusted scientific studies and investigations of some of the monuments
 to the specialized agencies in the country.

22.       The Committee recommends that conservation preservation work undertaken by ASI including phase II of Ajanta
 Ellora project should be completed within a definite time frame and in a targeted manner.  The details and outcome of these
 may be provided to the Committee separately. As regards exploration and excavations, the Committee feels that selection of
 new sites is a welcome step but this should also be done in a systematic and time bound manner.  Moreover, findings of
 excavations should also be recorded simultaneously so that the report is finalized by the end of the excavation work itself. 
 The Committee strongly recommends that the pending reports on excavations undertaken by ASI should be finalized and
 published without any loss of time.
23.       The Committee notes that conservation of our heritage is important, both for encouraging tourism as well as for
 attracting visitors from overseas.  The importance of cultural tourism in the context of development of both culture and
 tourism cannot be overemphasized.  Archaeological Survey of India, which is a flagship organization of the Ministry of
 Culture is engaged in conservation, preservation and maintenance of monuments.  Unless and until substantive allocation of
 funds is made to ASI towards undertaking the massive task of maintenance and conservation of heritage, ancient
 monuments and historical sites, the heritage preservation in a sustained manner and promotion of ancient cultural heritage
 could not be achieved.  Unfortunately, this aspect of heritage preservation/promotion has received very little attention it

(c)        National Mission on Monuments
24.       Though we have an extraordinarily rich, vast and diverse cultural heritage, there is no comprehensive database on the
 archaeological heritage in terms of monuments and antiquities.  So far, 3667 monuments and sites are protected by the
 Archaeological Survey of India and another 4000 are protected by the State Departments of Archaeology.  However this
 constitutes only a fraction of country’s monumental heritage.
25.       Like the built heritage and sites, our country is rich in antiquarian wealth not only in quantity but also in its
 diversified varieties both in time and space.  But there again, there is no comprehensive national database enlisting the total
 antiquarian wealth of the country.  The available information is scattered all over of which there is no complete record.
26.       Realising the above, Ministry of Culture has proposed the constitution of a National Mission on monuments and
 Antiquities.  The National Mission was formally launched by Minister (T & C) on 19.3.07.  The Mission would start its
 activities immediately.  To start with, the Mission would take up the documentation of monuments and antiquities in the
 States of Rajasthan, Delhi, Assam and Kerala.
27.       The mandate of the mission includes;
(i)
                  Prepare National Register of Built Heritage, Sites and Antiquities.
(ii)                Set up State level data base on built heritage, sites and antiquarian wealth for information and dissemination
 to planner, researchers etc. and for better management of such cultural resources.
(iii)               Promote awareness programme concerning the benefits of preserving the historical and cultural aspects of
 built heritage sites and antiquities.
(iv)              Extend training facility and capacity building to the concerned state departments, local bodies, NGO’s
 universities, museums, communities, etc. in the field of conservation of built heritage, and preservation and
 management of antiquarian remains.
(v)                Help in developing synergy between institutions like ASI, State Departments, concerned institutions and
 NGO’s to generate close interaction.  The Mission would work in very close association with State
 Departments of the Culture, NGO’s.  Universities and other organizations active in the field of heritage and
 its conservation.

(d)        Encroachment of Monuments
 
29.       The Committee inquired from the Ministry about the number of monuments which have been encroached upon in an
 unauthorized manner and remedial action taken by the Ministry to remove these encroachments.  The Secretary replied to
 the Committee that there were 249 monuments where there were encroachments, out of which the Ministry have cleared 70
 in the last five years.  Now, so far as encroachments are concerned, under the Act itself, the Superintending Archaeologist
 has the power to ask somebody to remove the encroachment.  But if the person who has been asked does not remove the
 encroachment, then, under  the Public Premises (Eviction of Unauthorized Occupants) Act, 1971 powers have been given to
 the Superintending Archaeologist to actually evict encroachers.  But the actual eviction has to be done only with the
 assistance of the State Government and District Collector.  So, unless the Collector does not provide for the Police and other
 things, it is not possible to remove these encroachments. 
 
30.       The Committee then desired to know whether the ASI is having sufficient number of staff to guard all the
 monuments.  The Ministry informed that there are 3500 monument attendants and 789 persons from private security
 agencies. There are 294 CISF attendants guarding the Red Fort and about 165 guarding the Taj Mahal.   The Secretary
 submitted before the Committee that "There is an urgent need for creation of manpower at the Archaeological Survey of
 India to guard these monuments."
 
31.       The Committee expresses its anxiety over the incidents of encroachments upon the land of Centrally Protected
 Monuments.  The Committee is more concerned about the failure of Archeological Survey of India to get back the land
 from the encroachers.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that a complete assessment should be made
 regarding encroachment of monuments/sites under the protection of Archaeological Survey of India all over the country. 
 Prompt action ought to be taken against the encroachers with the help of State Governments/Local Administrations and the
 matter should be pursued at the highest level i.e. Chief Minister.    The Committee also recommends that number of
 monuments attendants should be suitably increased by recruiting more personnel on urgent basis to check the incidents of
 encroachments as well as thefts. 
 
(e)        Other issues
 
32.       Air Pollution Monitoring Laboratory at Agra is continuously monitoring the ambient air quality in and around Taj
 Mahal to study possible impact of air pollutants on the monument and to formulate conservation measures accordingly.  In
 addition, weather parameters such as wind velocity and direction, temperature, relative humidity and rainfall are being
 recorded continuously as these parameters have a bearing on the behaviour and distribution of pollutants in the
 environment.  It has been found by this laboratory that the level of air pollutants i.e., sulphur-di-oxide and Nitrus Oxide
 gases are generally within the permissible limits whereas the suspended particulate matter is always at a very high level
 except during the rainy season.  The deposition of Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM) on the shimmering white marble of
 the Taj Mahal imparts yellow tinge to the marble surface.  To restore the pristine glory of the Taj Mahal, as a conservation
 measure, the clay pack treatment which is non-corrosive and non-abrasive is carried out for the removal of the acretionary
 deposits.
 
33.       The Salimgarh Fort has been notified as a monument of national importance vide Notification No.SO989 (e) dated
 11.9.2005 and is under the jurisdiction of Delhi Circle of the Archaeological Survey of India.  The Northern Railway has
 made a proposal for realigning the railway tracks passing through Salimgarh Fort in view of their proposed project for a
 new Yamuna Bridge (in lieu of the existing bridge) on Delhi-Shahdra section on Northern Railway. The realignment of the
 railway track passing through Salimgarh Fort would involve making two deep cuts on the ancient Salimgarh Fort wall.  As
 this would constitute a violation of the provisions of the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act,
 1958 and rules made thereunder, the ASI has regretted that it has issued NOC for realigning the railway line passing
 through Salimgarh Fort.  The Salimgarh Fort along with the Red Fort has been proposed for inclusion in the list of World
 Heritage Sites in 2007.  ASI has appointed a Consultant for preparing a Comprehensive Conservation Management Plan for
 Red Fort (including Salimgarh Fort).  The Consultant has finalized the draft report which is under discussion.  Conservation
 works would be initiated after the plan is finalised.
34.       The Committee expresses its concern that Taj Mahal, the world famous monument at Agra is becoming yellowish

(c)  National Mission on Monuments:
            The Committee recommends that the National Mission of Monuments and   Antiquities is an important Mission and
 has a laudable goal to achieve but however, the modalities for the Mission should be clearly planned and targets should be
 fixed for achieving the stated objective so that the allocation made for this purpose is not wasted.    The Committee also
 recommends that in addition to the four States chosen initially for the purpose activities of the Mission should also be
 extended to other States which are economically backward but culturally very rich. 
(Para 28)
(d) Encroachment of Monuments

            The Committee expresses its anxiety over the incidents of encroachments upon the land of Centrally Protected
 Monuments.  The Committee is more concerned about the failure of Archeological Survey of India to get back the land
 from the encroachers.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that a complete assessment should be made
 regarding encroachment of monuments/sites under the protection of Archaeological Survey of India all over the country. 
 Prompt action ought to be taken against the encroachers with the help of State Governments/Local Administrations and the
 matter should be pursued at the highest level i.e. Chief Minister.    The Committee also recommends that number of
 monuments attendants should be suitably increased by recruiting more personnel on urgent basis to check the incidents of
 encroachments as well as thefts. 
(Para 31)
 
 
(e)  Other issues
 
            The Committee expresses its concern that Taj Mahal, the world famous monument at Agra is becoming yellowish
 due to deposition of Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM).  The Committee recommends that while undertaking any
 conservation activity at Taj Mahal abundant cautions should be taken to retain the original glory of the shimmering white
 marble used in this.  
 
            The Committee takes exception to the fact that ASI without assessing the consequences issued No Objection
 Certificates (NOC) for realigning the railway line passing through Salimgarh Fort, which the Ministry is proposing to be
 included in the list of World Heritage Sites in the year 2007 in a routine manner.  This shows the apathy and callousness of
 the Organization which now regrets for issuing NOC and admits that realignment would involve making two deep cuts on
 the ancient Salimgarh Fort Wall.    The Committee strongly recommends that the NOC issued by ASI should be revoked
 forthwith and this kind of incidents should not be repeated in future.   
(Paras 34 & 35)
 
Observing Centenaries and Anniversaries of Important national personalities and events
 
            The Committee observes that in comparison to the total plan and non-plan outlay i.e. Rs.932.61 crore of the whole
 Ministry, the allocation made for the purpose of observing centenaries and anniversaries of the important national
 personalities and events is quite substantial amount i.e., 65.92 crore.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that it should
 be spent carefully and such events and occasions should also be used optimally for the promotion of tourism.
(Para 37)
 
National Archives of India
 
            The Committee recommends that the allocation for National Archives which has been almost doubled in the BE
 2007-08 in comparison to the RE 2006-07 should be utilized for the purpose of acquiring more and more documents from
 the States.  The Committee also recommends that activities like, reference, digitization of rare and precious archival
 records, conservation of archival records of national importance and translation thereof should be done in committed
 manner. 
(Para 40)
Sahitya Akademi
 
            The Committee desires that the allocation to the tune of Rs.1.25 crore made for the activities of the Sahitya Akademi
 will be spent by the Akademi meaningfully. 
(Para 43)
Sangeet Natak Akademi
 
            The Committee notes with unhappiness that out of Rs. 9.00 crore allocated in RE 2006-07, the Akademi could spend
 only Rs.6.41 crore.  The Committee recommends that the increased allocation made this year for the Sangeet Natak
 Akademi in the BE 2007-08 should be used meaningfully for promotion of performing arts.  The Committee also
 recommends that the work relating to Museum of performing arts should be initiated at the earliest.
(Para 46)
 
National School of Drama

The Committee is happy to note that National School of Drama has got Rs.11.00 crore for undertaking various
 activities but simultaneously cautions that the amount should not remain under spent as it is made clear by the Ministry that
 out of theRs.11.00 crore available to the School at RE 2006-07, it could spend only 7.42 crore by 28th February, 2007. 
(Para 49)
Lalit Kala Akademi
 
            The Committee is distressed to point out that this premier institute which is engaged in promotion of visual and
 plastic arts in India like painting, graphic, sculpture and ceramic, etc. is not being able to spend even the meager allocation
 made to it during 2006-07 which is quite clear from the fact that it has spent even less than fifty per cent of its allocation. 
 The Committee recommends that functioning of the Akademi should be strengthened and non-utilisation of the allocated
 funds should be viewed seriously by the Ministry for which corrective measures should be initiated in a time bound manner.
 The Committee should be informed of the measures taken in this regard at the earliest.
(Para 51)
Raja Rammohun Roy Library Foundation 
 
The Committee recommends that in accordance with the recommendation made by the Knowledge Commission at
 least one library should be opened at the level of District Headquarter and since Raja Rammohun Roy Library Foundation is
 the nodal organization for undertaking such task adequate budgetary provision should be made for the purpose. 
(Para 54)
Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts
 
            The Committee takes strong note of the fact that planned allocation made for Indira Gandhi National Centre for the
 Arts at the stage of B.E. 2006-07 was just Rs.50 lakh whereas at the RE stage it was increased to Rs.3.00 crore and out of
 this amount the Centre could spend only Rs.30 lakh.  The Committee does not find any satisfactory explanation for non-
utilization of the allocated funds and the reasons for the vast increase at the RE stage during the year 2006-07.  The
 Committee also notes that there is a major increase in the allocation for the IGNCA again in the BE 2007-08.  The
 Committee hopes that this year the increased allocation will be utilized by the Centre optimally. The Committee also notes
 that there is some adverse report of CAG on the functioning of IGNCA and complaint has been lodged in the CBI against
 some IGNCA Trustees.  The Committee therefore, desires that copies of all these reports may be furnished to the
 Committee for its perusal at the earliest and inform about the action taken thereon.    
(Para 56)
Budgetary provision for the North Eastern States
 
            The Committee notes that the major portion of the plan allocation of the Ministry is released through various
 autonomous and subordinate organizations under the Ministry of Culture, most of which are scattered all over the country
 and their location is far away from the North-East.  This becomes an inhibiting factor in the implementation of the
 programmes in the North-East.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that efforts should be made to find suitable local
 partners such as Universities, local academies etc. in the North-Eastern Region itself, which will go a long way in
 improving utilization of the funds earmarked for the North-East.
(Para 61)
