REPORT
The Ministry of Shipping encompasses within its fold the port and shipping sectors which include
major ports, shipbuilding and ship-repair, national water-ways and inland water transport system. The
entire maritime transport sector comes under the purview of Ministry of Shipping. India has 12 major
ports and about 200 non-major ports. Approximately 95% of the country's trade by volume and 68%
by value are moved through maritime transport. Therefore, shipping and ocean resources, ship design
and construction, ports and harbours, issues relating to human resource developing, finance, ancillaries
and new technologies need to be developed in the light of the emerging scenario. The Ministry of
Shipping has been entrusted with the responsibility to formulate policies and programmes on these
subjects and their implementation.

20. The salient features of the Demand for Grants 2018-19 of the Ministry of Shipping are outlined
below:
20.1. Sagarmala:
An allocation of ` 600 crore for Sagarmala programme for supporting the Phase-1 of Coastal Economic
Zone (CEZ) development, development of coastal berths, funding of unique and innovative projects and
coastal community development projects in maritime States. The allocation also includes ` 250 crore as
capital expenditure for Sagarmala Development Company for providing equity support to project SPVs.
20.2. Development of Major Ports: For the year 2018-19 an outlay of `4588.75 crore ( `323.57 crore as
GBS and ` 4265.18 crore as Internal and External Budgetary Resources (IEBR) has been provided for
the Port Sector.
• Allocation of ` 323.57 crore as GBS for the ports mainly includes:
• Capital Dredging for V .O. Chidambaranar Port Trust, Tuticorin ( ` 67.71 crore): This project,
which was approved by the Cabinet has already been completed. Out of the total grant of GBS
of ` 224.01 crore, four installments of GBS to the Port amounting to ` 118.94 crore have
already been released ( ` 9.5 crore during 2013-14, ` 54.44 crore during 2014-15, ` 25 crore
during 2015-16, ` 15 crore during 2016-17 and ` 15 crore during 2017-18). A balance amount
of ` 105.07 crore is still to be reimbursed out of which an allocation of ` 67.71 crore has
been made in BE 2018.

8
• Construction of four lane Port Connectivity road - Mormugao Port Trust ( ` 35 crore) : This
work is in progress and the total port share for the work is ` 187.00 crore and the Government
has already released GBS of ` 90.00 crore. The work is to be completed by 2019. This is
part of the obligation of the port towards the project.
• Coastal Road Protection Work - Chennai Port Trust ( ` 23 crore) : Total cost of the project
is ` 63 crore. Till date, an amount of ` 40 crore has already been released to the Port ( ` 15
crores during 2014-15, ` 10 crore during 2015-16, `5 crore during 2016-17 and `10 crore
during 2017-18). This is part of the obligation of the port towards the project which is already
completed.
• Construction of Subway flyover of Cochin Port Trust ( ` 3.25 crore): SFC has approved the
project relating to construction of a sub way/Fly over in front of the International Container
Transshipment Terminal (ICTT) Gate at Vallarpadam at a cost `30 crore. Till date an amount
of `23.45 crore has already been released to the port ( `10 crore during 2014-15, `4 crore
during 2015-16, `4.45 crore during 2016-17 and `5 crore during 2017-18.) Further an amount
of `3.30 crore would be released by the end of this year under additional allocation in RE
2017-18. Balance amount of ` 3.25 crore has been allocated in BE 2018-19 as part of the
obligation of the port towards the project which is already completed.
• Rail connectivity to International Container Transshipment Terminal project of Cochin Port
Trust - Rail over Bridge (RoB) ( ` 31.61 crore): Revised cost of the project (3rd RCE) is
`409.83 crore. Till date an amount of `378.22 crore has been released to the port including
`5 crore allocated under BE 2017-18. The balance amount of `31.61 crore is yet to be released
to the port, allocation for which has been made in BE 2018-19 for ensuring timely completion
of the project.
• Subsidy for Maintenance Dredging to KoPT ( ` 155 crore): Central Government assistance to
KoPT for channel and river dredging approved for the years 2016-17 to 2019-20 is
` 998.08 crore. The Central share for dredging subsidy to KoPT for the year 2016-17 is about
` 270 crore which is likely to be claimed by the port after certification of dredging expenditure
by the Audit during 2018-19. An outlay of ` 155.00 crore has been provided as subsidy
towards River and Channel Maintenance Dredging in Hoogly and Haldia River by Kolkata Port
Trust (KoPT) in BE 2018-19.
• Swacch Bharat Abhiyan ( ` 8 crore): An Outlay of ` 2 crore each for Four Major Ports viz.
KoPT, ChPT, MoPT and CoPT who do not have sufficient internal resources is considered
necessary for taking up various activities under the Swacch Bharat Abhiyan.
20.3. Grants to Inland Water Transport Authority of India: An outlay of ` 500 crore has been provided
to Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) for infrastructure development and regulation of National
Waterways. This includes Jal Marg Vikas Project (JMVP) on NW-1, development of NW-2, NW-3,
NW-4 and NW-5 and newly declared NWs which are considered to be viable. The provision also includes
the payment of interest on bonds raised by the IWAI.

20.4. Shipping and Ship Building: `30 crore for payment of subsidy to Indian Shipyards for manufacture
of ships.
20.5. Directorate General (Shipping): `35 crore for Capital expenditure on new E-Governance project,

20.6. Indian Maritime University:An allocation of `30 crore for procuring different kinds of simulators,

20.7. DGLL: An allocation of `60 crore for schemes like establishment of Lighthouses at Dhanushkhodi,

are being implemented for improvement of marine aids to navigation.
20.8. Assistance to ALHW: An allocation of `140 crore for development of sea route to Baratang island
in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, extension of new dry dock at Port Blair and acquisition of two tugs.

PROJECT UNNATI
45. As per the Annual Report (2017-18) of the Ministry, an exercise was undertaken to prepare a
Quantitative Benchmarking Module which covered the operational, financial, human resources and efficiency
related parameters for benchmarking of efficiency and productivity of Major Ports in India against
international standards and define Key Performance Indicators for the ports and terminals. The study
covered marine operations, stevedoring, jetty operations vessel operations, yard performance, Labor
productivity, Cargo storage (containers & dry bulk only), rake operations (loading/unloading of rakes),
maintenance (Equipment uptime and breakdowns), Gate-In and Gate-out operations safety, customs and
penetration of IT.
46. The benchmarking study focused on identifying how efficiently capacity is utilized and underlying
operational performance metrics across commodities. The low berth productivity and crane productivity
across container terminals at Major Ports along with potential to drive 15-20% higher volumes of coal
across ports, just by replicating 'best demonstrated performance' consistently was studied. Potential to
double volumes of POL by replicating BDP and reducing non working time and high costs of labour and
maintenance dredging across ports was also analyzed. On the basis of the quantitative and qualitative
benchmarking carried out, a clear roadmap for improvement for each port has been laid out covering
changes in the areas of core business processes, equipment, organization structure, people skills, information
technology and infrastructure.

17
47. A total number of 116 new initiatives for 12 Major Ports have been identified which would increase
the volume of traffic significantly and also avoidance of capital expenditure. The roadmap for improvement
has been suggested along with the timelines, approach and methodology for implementation. All the 116
recommendations are to be implemented by December 2019. Out of these, 86 have already been
implemented. The implementation of these initiatives will further improve the efficiency and performance
of the Ports.
48. The Committee hopes that the implementation of the above stated initiatives will further

75. The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, stated thus:

SHIP BUILDING FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE POLICY FOR INDIAN SHIPYARDS

84. The Government of India has introduced a ` 4000 Crores Financial Assistance Policy (FAP) to
shipyards for 10 years for contracts secured between 01.04.2016 and 31.03.2026 (including these
dates).
85. Financial assistance will be granted to Indian Shipyards equal to 20% of the lowest of "Contract
Price" or the "Fair Price" of each vessel or actual payments received in respect of the vessel built by
them for a period of at least 10 years commencing 2016-17.
86. This rate of 20% will be reduced by 3% every three years. The quantum of financial assistance
for a vessel shall be the product of the applicable rate of financial assistance prevailing on the date of
Contract, and, the lowest of the contract price or the fair price when converted in Indian Rupees,
provided that, at the time of release of financial assistance, if the actual payment received for a vessel
is lower than the contract price, such payment shall replace the contract price in the formulae for
computation of the financial assistance.

2017 and amended in October 2017. The updated webportal also has been rolled out in October 2017.

SHIP BUILDING INDUSTRY
93. The main problems before the ship building industry are as follows:
(i) Stressed assets in Shipping and Ship building.
(ii) High working capital requirements and borrowing costs.

27
(iii) Poor order book position of Indian Shipyards.
(iv) Stiff competition from Korean, Japanese and Chinese shipyards which are supported by
their governments.
94. The new policy initiatives taken by Ministry of Shipping to revamp the ship building industry in
the country are as follows:
(i) The Government approved the Ship building Financial Assistance Policy for Indian Shipyards
on December 09, 2015 to encourage domestic shipbuilding and to provide a level playing
field vis-a-vis foreign shipyards,
(ii) Further, the Government approved on December 09, 2015 to grant a Right of First Refusal
(RoFR) to Indian Shipyards for construction of vessels to be procured by Government
departments/agencies including PSUs for governmental purposes or for their own use till
2025. Similarly, for repair of vessels by the above mentioned departments, RoFR is to be
granted to Indian shipyards. Indian shipyards will have to match the lowest rate offered by
the foreign bidder. From 2025 onwards, only Indian built vessels are to be procured by
these agencies for governmental purposes or for own purpose. All these departments and
agencies shall undertake bulk tendering for their entire vessels related requirements with
deliveries starting from 2017-18.
(iii) Upon pursuance by Ministry of Shipping, Government of India has vide Gazette Notification
No. 112 dated April 13, 2016 notified an updated Harmonized Master List of Infrastructure
Sub-Sectors including a new sub-sector, "shipyards" under the category of "Transport".
The "Shipyards" have been defined therein as a floating or land-based facility having requisite
facilities for carrying on shipbuilding/repair/breaking activities. With this inclusion, shipyards
will be able to avail flexible structuring of long term project loans, long term funding from
Infrastructure Funds at lower rates of interest and for a longer tenure equivalent to the
economic life of their assets, relaxed ECB norms, issuance of infrastructure bonds for
meeting working capital requirements.
(iv) In order to alleviate the stress in the shipbuilding sector, an Inter Ministerial Group (IMG)
was constituted on 28.04.2017 under the Chairmanship of the then Additional Secretary
(Shipping). The following issues were discussed in the meetings of IMG:
• Improving order book position of Indian Shipyards
• Provision of cheaper finance to Indian Shipyards and Shipping Sectors
• Taking over of stressed assets
• Restructuring of loans
• Streamlining the tax structure
The report of IMG was sent to D/o Financial Services on 07.08.2017 for necessary action.

28
(v) RBI has issued guidelines on scheme for sustainable structuring of stressed assets (S4A).
The S4A envisages determination of the sustainable debt level for a stressed borrower and
bifurcation of the outstanding debt into sustainable debt and equity/quasi-equity instruments
which are expected to provide upside to the lenders when the borrower turns around.
However, the provisions of the scheme are quite stringent such as having operating profit
in the previous years. Most of the shipyards may not be able to follow S4A norms. Ministry
of Shipping had requested RBI for relaxation of norms of the S4A Scheme for Indian
shipyards to alleviate their stress.
(vi) A Maritime Development Fund (MDF) is being proposed to be set up to raise long term
funds in the domestic and international markets at favoutable rates and on-lend such funds,
in both foreign currency and Indian rupees, to the maritime sector including shipbuilding
and shipping sectors at competitive rates. MDF shall supplement the present financing
options available to the sector from Banks. The proposal of MDF is being worked out in
consultation with EXIM Bank and stakeholders.
(vii) Exemption in duties:
(i) The custom duties on import of goods for use in the manufacture of ships has been
exempted by the Government.
(ii) The customs duties on import of goods including capital goods for use in repair of
the vessels and ships have been completely exempted.

growth due to its spin-off effect to other industries. The Committee appreciates the new policy
initiatives taken up by the Ministry of Shipping to revamp the ship building industry.

150. One of the Members of the Committee stressed upon the need to control the dumping of pollutants
into the seas and waterways by the vessels in the form of sewage, oil leakages etc.
151. As per the written reply furnished by the Ministry, the role of DG (Shipping) in management of
environment pollution due to effluents etc. in sea is as follows:
(i) All sea going vessels registered under Indian Merchant Shipping (M.S) Act, 1958 come
within the ambit of Directorate General of Shipping (DGS), whereas vessels registered
under Inland Vessel Act plying within inland water ways and within the port limits come
under the ambit of respective state boards and port authorities.

40
(ii) The following rules have been formulated for foreign going Indian flag vessels on the basis
of Marine Pollution (MARPOL) 1973/1978 convention for handling different category of
waste viz. oil / oily bilge water, sewage, garbage, Noxious Liquid Substance (NLS) chemicals
and harmful substances carried in packaged form. Informatively these rules are modeled on
MARPOL convention 1973/1978.
(a) Merchant Shipping (Control of Pollution by Noxious Liquid Substance in Bulk)
Rules, 2010.
(b) Merchant Shipping (Prevention of Pollution by Harmful Substances carried by Sea in
Packaged Form) Rules, 2010.
(c) Merchant Shipping (Prevention of Pollution by Sewage from ships) Rules, 2010.
(d) Merchant Shipping (Prevention of Pollution by Garbage from Ships) Rules, 2010.
152. Periodic surveys are carried out for verifying compliance with the provisions stipulated in the
above mentioned rules and in case of any violation, pollution prevention certificates is withdrawn and
necessary penalties as specified under part XVI of M.S.Act, 1958 as amended are imposed on the
violators.
(iii) DG (Shipping) orders have been issued on similar lines for Indian River-sea and coastal
vessels.
(iv) Port State Control Officer (PSCO's) Inspection for foreign flag vessels visiting Indian ports
on risk based selection method.
(v) Port Reception Facilities are provided by Major Ports as per Section 356-I of M. S. Act,
1958 as amended and DGS orders issued from time to time.
Role of State Governments in management of environment pollution due to effluents etc.
in National Waterways.

initiatives taken up by the Ministry of Shipping to revamp the ship building industry.

The Committee recommends that steps should be taken to assuage the concerns of the

The Committee observes that the Government should give priority to Cochin Shipyard
since it is the most important ship building facility of the Government. The Cochin Shipyard
should get the first option to undertake any defence related projects. The private players may
be allowed only when the Government PSU shows its inability. (Para 113)
The Committee takes note of the interventions being made by the Ministry to handle
future cargo growth through inland waterways. (Para 120)
Cargo movement through inland waterways is cheaper, more reliable and less polluting
than transporting goods by road or rail. The Committee has noted the projects proposed to be
undertaken during 2018-19 for development of Inland Waterways and is of the view that the full
potential of movement of goods via inland waterways has not been fully exploited in India over
the year. The Committee recommends that necessary funds may be sanctioned to IWAI for
carrying out the projects work without any delay. (Para 124)
The Committee urges the Ministry to ensure that sufficient manpower is available in
IWAI. (Para 130)
The Committee takes note of the steps taken by the Ministry to tap the vast potential of
inland waterways through the development of ferry services, Ro-Ro services and cruise operations.
(Para 135)
The Committee feels that river cruise tourism in India is still in its infancy. Promoting
river cruise tourism will give a fillip to the development of inland waterways in the country and
will also boost tourism in India. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Shipping and
IWAI should take innovative steps to promote river cruise tourism by attracting tourists to the
concept and by giving necessary funding for its development. (Para 137)

The Committee hopes that the maritime training institutes offer the required training to
