INTRODUCTION 
Maritime transport constitutes a fundamental pillar of national infrastructure, 
underpinning the economic growth, trade expansion, and social advancement of the 
country. It plays an instrumental role in shaping the pace, structure, and trajectory of 
development, ensuring India’s connectivity with global markets and reinforcing its 
position in international trade. 
2. The Ministry of Ports, Shipping, and Waterways (MoPSW) bears the paramount 
responsibility for the formulation and execution of policies and programmes aimed at 
fortifying the maritime sector. Its remit encompasses ports, shipping, inland waterways, 
shipbuilding and ship-repair services, with the overarching objective of ensuring a 
modern, efficient, and globally competitive maritime infrastructure. 
3. To address the complexities and evolving demands of the maritime sector, a 
comprehensive, strategic, and future-oriented policy framework is imperative. In 
furtherance of this objective, the Ministry is actively engaged in: 
 Augmenting port capacity by advancing infrastructure, enhancing berths, and 
modernising cargo-handling mechanisms to facilitate seamless trade and efficient 
logistics. 
 Empowering the shipping and waterways industries to assume greater 
responsibility in handling sea-borne trade through indigenous vessels, thereby 
reducing reliance on foreign carriers and reinforcing national maritime 
sovereignty. 
 Encouraging sustainable investment in port infrastructure, recognizing that the 
development of ports demands substantial capital investment, entails prolonged 
gestation periods, and is subject to multifaceted externalities. 

2 
 
4. Historically, port development has been state-driven, necessitated by the 
considerable financial outlay, uncertain returns, and strategic implications inherent in 
such large-scale projects. The Ministry’s proactive approach in navigating these 
challenges is pivotal in fortifying India’s maritime capabilities and ensuring that the 
nation remains at the forefront of global maritime trade. Through infrastructure 
modernization, progressive policy initiatives, and steadfast commitment, the Ministry of 
Ports, Shipping, and Waterways is spearheading the transformation of India’s maritime 
sector, making it a driving force for economic prosperity and global trade leadership.

GOALS AND OBJECTIVES 
6. The Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF), published by the Ministry 
of Ports, Shipping, and Waterways for FY 2025-26 outlines the strategic objectives, 

4 
 
financial allocations, and expected outcomes for key maritime initiatives under the 
Ministry. The framework focuses on enhancing maritime infrastructure, promoting 
shipbuilding, strengthening research & development, and improving logistics 
connectivity. The principal objectives are as follows: 
i. Research & Development (allocated budget ₹0.71 crore): The allotted 
budget will focus on promoting studies on sediment management, tsunami-
resilient breakwaters, and dredged sediment valorisation. There is also a 
planned development of draft forecasting models for River Hooghly and 
siltation control measures for Mumbai & JNPT ports. 
ii. Sagarmala Programme (allocated budget ₹866 crore): The Sagarmala 
Programme aims at augmenting port connectivity, modernization of 
infrastructure, and promoting maritime tourism. Under this initiative, one 
major road project is set to be completed, with an anticipated reduction in 
transit time for goods by 0.5 hours, improving overall port logistics. 
Infrastructure modernization efforts include the addition of 1 MTPA cargo 
handling capacity, the construction of a new cruise terminal, and the 
development of seven Ro-Ro passenger jetties. Additionally, 20 skill 
development courses are planned, targeting the certification of 6,000 
individuals. 
iii. Shipbuilding and Research (allocated budget ₹350 crore): The aim is to 
construct and deliver 37 vessels, with the resulting generation of ₹1,837 
crore in business revenue, reflecting a 292.5% growth from the previous 
year, and create2,99,900 additional man-days of employment, marking a 
24.98% increase. Furthermore, an increase of 31,000 gross tonnages (GT) 
in ship production capacity, representing77.5% growth, is targeted.

MOVEMENT, EFFICIENCY AND COOPERATION  
8. Container Movement Timing Recommendations 
8.1 The Committee recommends immediate attention to the challenges facing 
maritime container movement schedules due to global disruptions and trade 
volatility. The Committee strongly urges the Ministry to implement comprehensive 
digital tracking platforms for real-time monitoring of container movements across 
all major ports. Furthermore, the Committee recommends establishing a dedicated 
Maritime Response Unit to address contingencies arising from geopolitical 
disruptions along key shipping routes. The Committee believes these measures are 
essential to mitigate extended lead times currently affecting Asia-Europe shipments 
and to reduce congestion at strategic port hubs. 
Port Efficiency Recommendations 
8.2 The Committee notes the ambitious targets under Maritime India Vision 2030 
and recommends accelerated implementation of mechanization initiatives at all 
major ports to achieve the sub-20-hour turnaround time goal. The Committee 
strongly advises prioritizing budget allocation for modernizing port infrastructure, 
particularly automated cargo handling systems and digital documentation 
processes. The Committee further recommends quarterly performance reviews of 
port efficiency metrics with standardized benchmarks to ensure measurable 
progress toward capacity enhancement targets. 
Inter-Ministerial Port Connectivity Recommendations 
8.3 The Committee emphasizes that inadequate multi-modal connectivity 
remains a significant bottleneck affecting port efficiency. The Committee 
recommends establishing a dedicated Inter-Ministerial Coordination Cell to 
expedite port connectivity projects under PM Gati Shakti. With reference to 

10 
 
Paradip Port, the Committee recommends immediate completion of pending 
railway linkage projects and upgradation of NH-53A to facilitate smoother cargo 
evacuation. The Committee strongly urges synchronized planning between the 
Ministries of Railways, Road Transport and Highways, and Ports, Shipping and 
Waterways to ensure infrastructure development aligns with projected cargo 
volumes. The Committee recommends that the Paradip Port development model be 
replicated across other major ports to enhance seamless connectivity and reduce 
logistics costs. 
8.4 The Committee notes that the allocation of ₹0.71 crore for Research & 
Development (R&D) in port development is strikingly low, especially considering 
the critical role that technological advancement plays in ensuring the 
competitiveness and efficiency of the country’s maritime infrastructure. The 
Committee strongly recommends a significant augmentation of R&D funding to 
bring out innovation in port infrastructure, automation, and environmental 
sustainability.

CENTRAL SECTOR SCHEMES-an analysis 
Sagarmala Programme 
15. The Sagarmala Programme, the Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways' 
flagship Central Sector Scheme, promotes port-led development by harnessing 
India’s coastline, waterways, and strategic maritime position. Projects are 
implemented by Central Ministries, State Governments, State Maritime Boards, 
and Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs), with the Ministry also supporting high-
impact, low-return projects. For FY 2025-26, ₹ 810 crore has been allocated for 
port infrastructure, coastal berths, road and rail links, fishery harbours, floating 
jetties, skill development, cruise terminals, and Ro-Pax ferry services. Under the 
Sagarmala Scheme, the Ministry is developing the National Maritime Heritage 
Complex (NMHC) at Lothal, Gujarat, as a one-of-a-kind project dedicated to 
India’s maritime heritage. The Ministry is enhancing urban waterways transport 
(Ro-Pax services) to improve cargo movement, reduce travel time, ease road 
congestion, and cut pollution. It is part-funding 26 fishing harbours, with 9 
completed at ₹ 144.5 crore, and upgrading five major harbours—Kochi, Chennai, 
Visakhapatnam, Paradip, and Mallet Bunder. Additionally, 50 floating jetty 
locations have been identified in Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, 
Karnataka, and Kerala, while ₹ 56 crore has been allocated for maritime 
infrastructure in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep.

19. Balancing Capital Expenditure and Maintenance Requirements 
The Committee observes imbalance between capital investments (₹994.33 
crore for JMVP) and maintenance expenditure (₹267 crore) which in its opinion 
reflects a critical concern in infrastructure development strategy. The Jal Marg 
Vikas Project, currently extended to 2026 with World Bank support, represents a 
substantial commitment to expanding the inland waterway infrastructure along 
National Waterway-1. While the project has demonstrated progress in creating 
multi-modal terminals at Varanasi, Sahibganj, and Haldia, evidence suggests 
implementation challenges persist, with certain tenders being cancelled due to 
significantly higher bids than estimated values. The Committee notes that 
maintenance expenditure forms the backbone of any sustainable infrastructure 
network, particularly for waterways where regular dredging and channel 
maintenance are essential for ensuring navigability. The current allocation of ₹267 
crore for maintaining existing waterways appears insufficient, especially when 
considered against the extensive network of 111 National Waterways. The 
experience from European waterway management indicates that sustained 
maintenance funding is crucial for ensuring the reliability and performance of 
inland navigation infrastructure. The Committee therefore recommends a more 

19 
 
strategic approach involving implementation of a Life-Cycle Cost Assessment 
(LCCA) framework for waterway infrastructure that balances initial capital 
expenditure with long-term maintenance requirements. This framework should 
incorporate detailed assessment of annual maintenance needs for each operational 
waterway segment, ensuring sufficient allocation for critical activities like dredging 
campaigns, navigation aids maintenance, and terminal upkeep. The Ministry should 
establish a minimum maintenance-to-capital expenditure ratio of 1:2 for mature 
waterways, gradually transitioning to 1:1 as the network stabilizes, ensuring 
sustainable operation while continuing necessary expansion. 
20. Reconsidering Debt Financing Strategy for IWT Projects 
The Committee notes the allocation of ₹76.50 crore toward interest payments on 
IWAI bonds  and raises concerns about the sustainability of debt-based financing 
for inland waterway projects. While debt instruments provide immediate access to 
capital necessary for infrastructure development, they create rigid fiscal obligations 
that constrain future budgetary flexibility. The current financing structure of the 
JMVP, with its reliance on both international loans (₹2,512 crore from IBRD) and 
government bonds, exemplifies this mixed approach. 
Debt financing offers certain advantages, including preservation of operational 
autonomy and prevention of ownership dilution. However, it also introduces 
financial covenants that may restrict operational flexibility and increases overall 
financial risk exposure. The experience from the financing survey of the inland 
waterway transport sector indicates that while loans on favorable terms remain a 
preferred financial instrument, there is growing interest in alternative financing 
approaches, particularly among larger operators. 
20.1 The Committee therefore recommends a more balanced financing strategy that 
incorporates diversified funding sources beyond traditional debt instruments. The 
Ministry should develop a Waterway Infrastructure Finance Framework that 

20 
 
includes: (1) Project-specific revenue bonds tied to direct income streams from 
waterway operations, reducing dependency on general budgetary allocations; (2) 
Dedicated Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) specifically for waterway 
projects with stable revenue potential, allowing broader market participation; and 
(3) Blended finance models combining concessional loans, grants, and commercial 
financing to optimize the capital structure for different project components. 
21. Enhancing Effectiveness of North-Eastern Region Investments 
The Committee notes the allocation of ₹421 crore for North-Eastern Region 
infrastructure development targeting NW-2 (Brahmaputra), NW-16 (Barak), and 
the Indo-Bangladesh Protocol route which represents a significant opportunity to 
enhance regional connectivity. However, the unique geographical and logistical 
challenges of the region necessitate more nuanced implementation strategies to 
ensure these investments yield meaningful economic returns. 
In its opinion, stakeholder consultations in the North-Eastern Region reveal limited 
awareness about environmental safeguards and coordination challenges among 
different agencies involved in riverine infrastructure. Local communities prioritize 
rapid development but lack understanding of the quality standards necessary for 
sustainable waterway operations. Field observations indicate that structural 
interventions like piers, bridges, and river training works can pose navigational 
hazards when not properly coordinated among stakeholders. 
In order to maximize the effectiveness of the ₹1,010 crore five-year NER project 
(2020-2025), the Committee therefore recommends that the Ministry implements a 
Regional Waterway Development Consortium model that brings together state 
governments, local bodies, technical institutions, and private stakeholders in a 
structured governance framework. This consortium should establish a unified 
project monitoring system with standardized reporting protocols and performance 

21 
 
indicators for all NER waterway projects, enabling real-time tracking of 
implementation progress and rapid intervention where needed. 
21.1 Furthermore, the Ministry should initiate quarterly multi-stakeholder 
coordination forums in each NER state to harmonize river training works, terminal 
development, and navigation infrastructure planning. These forums should 
specifically address the coordination issues between state Water Resources 
Departments (WRD) and Inland Water Transport (IWT) authorities identified 
during stakeholder consultations. 
22. Structuring Framework for Enhancing Private Sector Participation 
The Committee further notes the limited engagement of the private sector in inland 
waterway development which in its opinion represents a missed opportunity for 
accelerating infrastructure development while reducing fiscal burden. Despite 
allocations of ₹301 crore for private participation under PPP mode within the 
JMVP framework, actual private investment has remained below expectations. The 
Ministry’s emphasis on nurturing public-private partnerships is indicative of 
governmental recognition of this gap, but systematic structural interventions are 
needed to translate this intent into action. 
22.1 The Committee therefore urges the Ministry to establish a comprehensive 
Inland Waterway Investment Framework specifically designed to attract private 
capital across multiple segments of the value chain. This framework should include 
distinct incentive structures for three categories of private participation: (1) 
Infrastructure developers focusing on terminal construction, navigation aids, and 
specialized cargo handling facilities; (2) Vessel operators and logistics providers 
offering transportation services on established waterways; and (3) Technology 
partners bringing innovations in vessel design, navigation systems, and 
environmental solutions. 

22 
 
Financial mechanisms should be calibrated to address specific barriers faced by 
different types of private investors. For infrastructure developers, enhanced 
Viability Gap Funding with coverage up to 40% for waterway projects in 
challenging geographies would reduce entry barriers. For vessel operators, 
accelerated depreciation provisions and priority sector lending status would 
stimulate fleet modernization. For technology partners, a dedicated Waterway 
Innovation Fund providing matching grants for pilot deployments would accelerate 
adoption of new solutions. 
22.2 Furthermore, the Ministry should reform the concession framework for 
waterway operations to offer more attractive risk-sharing arrangements. This 
includes implementing flexible concession periods tied to actual traffic achievement 
rather than fixed timelines, establishing minimum revenue guarantees for pioneer 
routes, and creating streamlined regulatory clearance mechanisms for waterway 
projects through a single-window system. 
  
23. Ship Building and ship repair- The Ministry informed the Committee that to 
promote indigenous shipbuilding industry in India, Ministry of Ports, Shipping and 
Waterways has brought in Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Policy (SBFAP) scheme for 
Indian shipyards to procure orders from domestic as well as international market and to 
be competitive in international market for securing global orders. The scheme offers 
financial assistance to Indian Shipyards for shipbuilding contracts signed between April 
1, 2016 to March 31, 2026 with rate of financial assistance starting from 20% in 2016 and 
diminishing to 11% in 2026 with a budget allocation of ₹4000Crore. Also, financial 
assistance of 30% is given for vessels where main propulsion is achieved by means of 
green fuels such as Methanol/ Ammonia / Hydrogen fuel cells and financial assistance of 
20% for vessels with electric means of propulsion or vessels fitted with hybrid propulsion 
system. An amount of ₹ 138Crore has been allocated for SBFAP for FY 2025-26. Funds 
are projected based on tentative delivery of ships by shipyards. 

23 
 
24. Current State of SBFAP Implementation and Fund Utilization 
The Committee notes the allocation of ₹138 crore for shipbuilding assistance in FY 
2025-26 which represents a modest commitment when viewed against the backdrop 
of the scheme's total allocation of ₹4,000 crore that was intended to span from 2016 
to 2026. This relatively small annual allocation however raises serious concerns 
about the pace and scale of implementation. Evidence suggests that despite the 
policy being operational for nearly nine years, barely 10% of the total corpus has 
been utilized, indicating structural impediments in policy implementation. Since its 
inception, the SBFAP has facilitated 313 vessel orders across 39 shipyards, with a 
cumulative value of approximately ₹10,500 crore. However, financial assistance 
disbursed stands at only ₹337 crore for 135 completed vessels. This significant gap 
between allocated funds and actual disbursement suggests systemic issues in fund 
absorption capacity within the industry. The progressive reduction in financial 
assistance percentages—from 20% in 2016 to the current 11%—appears to have 
contributed to diminishing industry interest in the scheme, particularly as 
competing shipbuilding nations like China and South Korea continue to provide 20-
30% subsidies to their shipyards. 
24.1 The Committee therefore suggests that the Ministry undertakes a 
comprehensive performance audit of the SBFAP to identify specific bottlenecks 
preventing optimal fund utilization. The audit should examine whether procedural 
complexities in application processing, unrealistic valuation mechanisms, or lack of 
shipyard capacity are inhibiting fund absorption. The web application for 
processing applications, despite improvements made after industry feedback, may 
require further streamlining to facilitate easier access to financial assistance. 
25. Strategic Realignment for Green Shipbuilding Technologies 
The Committee observes the current SBFAP framework includes provisions for 
enhanced incentives—30% for vessels powered by green fuels (methanol, ammonia, 

24 
 
hydrogen) and 20% for those with electric or hybrid propulsion systems. However, 
without a dedicated allocation specifically earmarked for these sustainable 
technologies within the ₹138 crore budget for FY 2025-26, there remains a serious 
risk that traditional shipbuilding might continue to dominate fund utilization. As 
global shipping increasingly focuses on decarbonization, India risks falling behind 
in developing indigenous capabilities for green vessel construction. The implications 
extend beyond environmental considerations to encompass future market 
competitiveness, as regulatory pressures and market demands increasingly favor 
low-emission vessels. The shipping ministry's recognition of green vessels in its 
policy framework indicates awareness of this shift, but implementation requires 
more targeted financial commitment. 
25.1 The Committee further notes the upcoming SBFAP 2.0, with its proposed 
allocation of ₹18,000 crore and enhanced subsidies of 25% for specialized vessels 
and 30% for green vessels, represents a significant step forward. However, this 
policy requires immediate operationalisation with a clear timeline for 
implementation and specific allocations for different vessel categories. The Ministry 
should establish immediate ring-fencing of at least 40% of the current year's 
allocation (approximately ₹55 crore) exclusively for green vessel construction, 
creating a dedicated funding stream that ensures India builds capacity in this 
emerging technology area. 
25.2 Implementation of Milestone-Based Disbursement Mechanism 
The Committee notes that the current practice of projecting SBFAP funds based on 
tentative ship delivery schedules introduces significant inefficiencies in fund 
utilization. Shipbuilding projects, particularly those involving complex vessels, are 
vulnerable to production delays, supply chain disruptions, and technical challenges 
that can extend timelines considerably. This creates a mismatch between projected 
fund requirements and actual disbursement schedules, contributing to the observed 

25 
 
pattern of underutilization. The construction of specialized and green vessels, which 
involve more complex technologies and often require sourcing components from 
global supply chains, are particularly susceptible to such delays. This problem is 
likely to be exacerbated as Indian shipyards expand their capabilities to build more 
sophisticated vessels with lower emission profiles. 
The Committee feels implementing a milestone-based fund release mechanism 
would fundamentally transform how financial assistance is distributed. Under this 
approach, shipyards would receive predetermined percentages of the total 
assistance amount upon completion of defined construction milestones, such as steel 
cutting, keel laying, launching, and final delivery. This approach would provide 
shipyards with steady cash flow throughout the construction process, rather than 
concentrating financial support at project completion. 
 
25.3 The Committee therefore urges the Ministry develops a standardized set of 
verifiable milestones for different vessel categories, with appropriate weightage 
assigned to each stage. For instance, the steel cutting stage might trigger release of 
15% of the total assistance, keel laying another 25%, launching 30%, and final 
delivery the remaining 30%. This structured approach would maintain financial 
pressure on shipyards to progress systematically while providing them with the 
liquidity needed to finance ongoing construction. 
26. Comprehensive Recommendations for Policy Enhancement 
Based on the analysis of current SBFAP implementation and the proposed 
transition to SBFAP 2.0, the following recommendations are provided to enhance 
the effectiveness of India's shipbuilding assistance framework: 
26.1 Immediate Fund Reallocation and Optimization 

26 
 
The Ministry should conduct an urgent review of anticipated fund requirements for 
the remainder of FY 2025-26, identifying shipyards with confirmed orders that can 
demonstrate clear progress toward delivery milestones. Unutilized funds from the 
broader ₹4,000 crore allocation should be systematically redistributed to support 
these viable projects, ensuring maximum resource utilization before the current 
scheme expires in 2026. 
This reallocation should prioritize yards that have demonstrated consistent delivery 
performance and those engaged in constructing specialized or green vessels that 
align with future maritime needs. The experience gained from the previous 
shipbuilding financial assistance scheme, which has supported 135 delivered vessels, 
provides sufficient data to identify high-performing shipyards that can effectively 
utilize additional funding. 
26.2 Establishment of Green Shipbuilding Innovation Fund 
Beyond simply allocating a portion of existing funds to green vessel construction, the 
Ministry should establish a dedicated Green Shipbuilding Innovation Fund within 
the SBFAP framework. This fund would finance not only vessel construction but 
also research and development activities focused on adapting international green 
shipping technologies to Indian conditions. 
The proposed SBFAP 2.0, with its enhanced subsidies for green vessels, creates an 
opportunity to institutionalize this approach. The fund should support collaborative 
projects between shipyards, research institutions, and technology providers to 
develop standardized designs for green vessels suited to Indian operational contexts. 
This would create a pipeline of technically validated vessel designs that Indian 
shipyards could offer to domestic and international clients at competitive prices. 
26.3 Development of Integrated Monitoring Framework 

27 
 
To address the persistent challenges in fund disbursement and project tracking, the 
Ministry should implement an integrated digital monitoring framework that 
provides real-time visibility into project progress across all SBFAP-supported 
shipbuilding activities. This system should incorporate automated milestone 
verification through digital documentation, physical inspections, and timestamped 
visual evidence of construction progress. 
The framework should include a quarterly performance dashboard accessible to all 
stakeholders, including the Ministry, shipyards, and oversight bodies. This 
dashboard would track key performance indicators such as funds allocated, 
milestones achieved, disbursements made, and potential bottlenecks. Regular 
performance reviews based on this data would enable proactive intervention where 
projects show signs of delay.
