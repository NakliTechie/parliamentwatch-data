REPORT
1. The Indian civil aviation sector is poised to emerge as the third largest aviation market in
the world by 2020. The rapidly growing aviation sector in the country handles 2.5 billion
passengers across the world in a year. Indian aviation market with an annual growth of 18 per cent
in domestic sector is expected to generate 2.6 million jobs in next ten years. Entry of private
players in the early 90s made the aviation sector more competitive with far greater connectivity to
many more cities in the country and abroad.
2. The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation and implementation of
national policies and programmes in the civil aviation sector. The Ministry oversees the development
and regulation of civil aviation in the country including establishment of new aerodromes,
maintenance and upgradation of existing aerodromes, regulation of carriage of traffic by air and
ensuring civil aviation safety and security.
3. The civil aviation sector is monitored and regulated mainly by two separate organizations
under the Ministry namely Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation
Security. The Commission of Railway Safety which is also under the administrative control of the
Ministry of Civil Aviation deals with matters pertaining to safety in rail travel and operations and
performs certain statutory functions specified in the Indian Railway Act and the Rules framed
thereunder. Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following Public Sector Undertakings/Companies/
Autonomous Bodies under its administrative control:–
(i) Air India Limited and its subsidiaries viz., Hotel Corporation of India Limited, Air
India Charters Limited, Air India Air Transport Services Limited, Air India Engineering
Services Limited, Airline Allied Services Limited and Vayudoot Limited.
(ii) Airports Authority of India
(iii) Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited
(iv) Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi
(v) Airports Economic Regulatory Authority of India

6. The Committee notes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation was able to spend
Rs.40.673.04 crores (anticipated) as against the allocation of Rs.49,267 crores for the
Eleventh Plan period resulting in a huge saving. The Committee finds that except Indira
Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi which spent Rs.41.00 against the budgetary allocation of
Rs.42.00 crore for the plan period, all other organizations under the Ministry could not
spend the allocated money fully. This is a disturbing scenario. The Committee notes that Air
India was able to spend Rs.25,603 crores only against the allocation of Rs.32,730.71 for the
Eleventh Plan period, again saving a sum of Rs.7,127 crores. The Committee is surprised
to note that when the national carrier was knocking the doors of financial institutions to
bail it out, it was unable to spend money it had been allocated during the Eleventh Plan
period.

3
7. Another case of under-spending is that of Airports Authority of India, the leading
infrastructure provider for aviation industry in the country. AAI could spend Rs.11,873.41
crores against the allocation of Rs.12,964.21 crores for the plan period. The Committee is
not able to understand the logic behind Airports Authority of India’s inability to spend even
the budgetary support of the Government; they could spend roughly 50% only. The
Committee feels that such under utilization of funds by AAI could have definitely slowed
the pace and process of developing airport infrastructure at various places including
Chennai and Kolkata. A curious case in this respect is that of the Hotel Corporation of
India which was able to spend Rs.43.75 crores against Rs.75.00 crores. The Committee feels
that time has come to assess the performance of this PSU in a broader perspective and it
should not be allowed to become a liability to pave the way for its privatization.  Air India
Charters Limited is another example in this respect. They could spend only Rs.2088.16
crores against the plan outlay of Rs.2475.26. Neither the Low Cost Carrier of the
Government is expanding its services nor able to spend the allocated plan outlay. The
Committee would like to be informed about the reasons for such under-utilization of funds
by various organizations of the Ministry of Civil Aviation and the remedial measures taken
to prevent such happenings in future. The Committee hopes that the Ministry will ensure
that the organizations under its control were able to spend their allocations to the maximum
possible extent in the Twelfth Plan. The Committee feels that any laxity on spending of
allocation will have the direct impact on aviation sector of the country.

9. The Committee notes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation was not able to spend fund
allocated in respect of DGCA, BCAS and AAI – three main organizations. It is not a
welcome sign since allocation under these heads constituted major share of the allocation
under Plan, excluding Air India. The reasons submitted by the Ministry for under-utilization
of funds such as delay in clearances to various projects, delay in acquisition of land for
airport projects and delays in getting necessary regulatory and statutory approvals may be
understandable but not acceptable, if repeated every year, For example, the project of
construction of office buildings for DGCA and BCAS could not be taken up for last two to
three years. The Committee feels that failure to spend the allocated moneys by these
organizations shows a lack of proper planning for timely execution of major programmes/
projects. Delay on account of various administrative problems and procedural difficulties
cannot be acceptable as excuse as these could be visualized at initial stages and precautions
taken well in time. The Committee recommends that the long pending construction of
headquarters for DGCA and BCAS be started at the earliest in coordination with agencies
concerned and be completed in a fixed schedule.  The Committee further recommends that
the Ministry should impress upon all the organizations under its purview to spend the
allocations cent percent in a planned manner.

19. To a specific query on the status of turnaround plan for Air India and the status of equity
infusion for Air India and its impact on the financial performance of the beleaguered company it
was informed that Air India prepared a Turn-around Plan (TAP) in June 2010 and the same
included an Operational Restructuring Plan combined with Financial Restructuring Plan (FRP).
Since the FRP involved a commitment from the Govt. in the form of equity infusion and guarantee

7
for loans, the TAP/FRP was presented to the GOM on 22 nd June 2011. The GOM appointed a
Group of Officers (GoO) in the Finance Ministry to examine the TAP/FRP and furnish their
recommendations/observations. After discussions/deliberations, the GoO recommended  a moderated
growth Scenario VII with targeted PLF of 73% by 2015 and 75% by 2020. The recommendations
of the GoO were submitted to the GOM in the meeting held on 28
th October, 2011.
20. RBI was approached for their regulatory forbearance and their views on the dispensation
sought by lenders. RBI has agreed to most of the dispensations requested for by the lenders and
the same has been discussed with all the lenders. Based on the discussions with the lenders, the
Cabinet Note for Restructuring has been processed for Equity infusion. This is under consideration
of the CCEA.
21. As regards the status of equity infusion for Air India and its impact on the financial
performance of the company it was informed that from 2009 till March 2012 Government has
infused Rs. 3200 crores and based on the Financial Restructuring Plan prepared by SBI Caps, a
further Equity support from GOI would be to the extent of Rs. 30231/- crores. During 2012-13
budget, Government has sanctioned Rs. 4000 crores towards equity infusion. After taking into
account all the recommendations/suggestions given by SBI Caps/Lenders/GoO towards financial and
operational restructuring, Air India would be able to achieve positive EBIDTA in FY 2013 and would
start generating cash profits from FY 2019. The tangible net worth of Air India would turn positive
in FY 2027.
22. When the Committee raised the outstanding issues relating to ‘Merger’ yet to be resolved
and about the road map ahead in this regard, it was informed that the main outstanding issues yet
to be resolved related to HR like pay parity, level mapping and seniority integration of manpower
at all levels. These outstanding issues have been referred to the Justice Dharmadhikari Committee.
23. To a query on the reconstitution of review groups appointed for rationalization and
harmonization of PLI and the progress made so far to resolve the matter it was informed that PLI
is also inter alia  one of the issues referred to the Justice Dharmadhikari Committee.
24. When it was asked about the main recommendations of the Dharmadhikari Report and on
fixation of any time limit to take action thereon particularly keeping in view the deteriorating
condition of Indian Airlines after merger of IA and AI, it was further informed that Justice
Dharmadhikari Committee has submitted its report to the Government on 31
st January, 2012.
However, the Committee has thereafter been requested to examine its recommendations on issues
related to governing structure of the pensionary scheme, harmonized working conditions and
allowance to be paid to Cabin Crew and Engineers. A committee of three officers has also been
constituted to examine the report and also to determine the milestones for its implementation.
25. When the issue of reforms in expenditure governance in Air India was raised, the CMD Air
India replied that a ten per cent cut on the reimbursements given to senior executives has already
been implemented and foreign travel of officers for various types of inspection and audits have
been reduced. He further added that stay arrangements of pilots and cabin-crew are being
rationalized and deployment of cabin crew and pilots has been modified to the extent that their stay
in hotels for longer periods has been minimized. In-flight service has been remodeled on the basis
of duration of flights.
26. To a query on the share of Air India in the aviation market, the Ministry replied that Air
India’s share in domestic market is estimated at 16.3 % (till January, 2012) as against 17.1% for
the year 2010-11. Air India’s market share in international markets to /from India is estimated at
18.7% inclusive of Air India Express (till January, 2012) as against 20.5% for the year 2010-11.
The national carrier improved its passenger load factor from 66.1 % (2010-11) to 68.5% (2011-
12) and the overall load factor stood at 60.26% for 2011-12 as against 58.2% in 2010-11.

27. During the deliberations of the Committee, issue of withdrawal of services from profitable
routes by Air India was raised. The CMD in his reply stated that a study on withdrawal of services
by Air India from 31 routes over a period of time was commissioned and a report on this has been
received by the Company. He informed the Committee that all these routes were loss making and
the report will be submitted to the Committee for its perusal.
28. Regarding the status of Air India’s entry into Low Cost Carrier (LCC) sector it was
informed that it has been planned to convert the old fleet of A320 aircraft with 145 seats (20
Business class and 125 Economy seats) into all economy configuration of 168 seats as to counter
competition from LCC and to gain greater penetration in the low fare market segments which has
been growing significantly over the past five years or so, i.e. by offering more capacity and
thereby increasing revenues.
29. When the issue of giving priority to Air India in allocation of Bilaterals, the Secretary
supplemented that the Ministry of Civil Aviation has recently through an Inter-Ministerial Group
finalized a bilateral allocation policy under which unutilized traffic rights are allocated to Indian
carriers. Allocation for summer schedule has already been started. He assured the Committee that
Air India is being given due consideration for their operational requirements first, before taking up
the cases of other private carriers.
30. The Committee notes that the national carrier had to undergo a tough period during
the last year mainly on account of merger related issues, ever increasing ATF prices,
employees’ resentment over non-payment of dues, allotment of profitable routes to private
carriers, its over-ambitious aircraft acquisition plan, inability to raise funds from the open
market and the reluctance of financial institutions’ and Oil Marketing Companies to help
the ailing Air India. The Committee finds that though the company was able to increase its
passenger load factor it witnessed a dip in share in domestic and international aviation
markets.
31. The Committee notes that a large number of amendments to Air Service Agreements
with foreign countries were signed for expansion of international air services by the
national carrier during the last year. The Committee finds that Air India operates almost
500 departures daily offering more than 78000 seats per day connecting about 150 domestic
city-pairs and 125 international city-pairs with a 143 strong fleet but still not in a position
to attain a visible competitive edge vis-à-vis  other airlines. The Committee notes some
serious efforts being made both by Air India management and the Government for taking
the national carrier out of the current mess. There are improvements on some parameters
indicating that things have started moving into required direction, which is why the
Government has decided to support the airlines’ recovery process. But in view of the several
challenges and over all slow down in the Sector, Committee would like to caution the Air
India management and the Government to be very careful while implementing its
restructuring/revival plans. The greatest challenge it must overcome on priority is that
relating to HR issues created due to ill-conceived merger plan. The Committee hopes that
allocation of unutilized bilaterals/traffic rights to Air India will benefit it in regaining its
lost market share in international market.
32. The Committee feels that infusion of government equity to the tune of Rs.30,000
crore over a nine-year period, induction of 27 Boeing 787 Dreamers, proposal to hive off
Air India’s Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO) business and its Engineering
Services as two wholly-owned subsidiaries will yield the much desired results in the long
run.

Airports Authority of India
33. Airports Authority of India is developing airport infrastructure along the length and breadth
of the country including remote and far flung areas. It manages 118 airports and 25 civil enclaves
and also provides CNS-ATM facilities at 11 other airports. The main function of AAI inter-alia

38. When it was asked about the steps taken to carve out the ATC operations from the control
of AAI, the Committee was informed that in December, 2007, AAI had engaged KPMG to conduct
a pre-feasibility study for the corporatization of AAI’s Air Navigation Services (ANS). The need for
this study was based on the findings of earlier Committees including Tata Committee 1976, Justice
Lahoti Committee-1996, Julka Committee-1997, Naresh Chandra Committee-2003 and the Roy Paul
Committee-2006. Key objectives of the study were to analyze options and develop a roadmap for
corporatization of ANS. Following were the key recommendations of the study:
– Consolidate ANS under Member ANS, as part of AAI and establish an ANS
Oversight Committee to monitor its performance.
– Segregate ANS from AAI and establish it as a separate Government of India
Enterprise. The new ANS entity shall have stringent performance parameters and
monitoring mechanism.
• This was further reiterated in the Report of the Committee under the Chairmanship of
Shri Ajay Prasad, 2008 which recommended clear demarcation of the dual functional
responsibilities of AAI as an ‘Aerodrome Operator’ and of an Air Navigation Service
Provider. Taking note of these recommendations, a roadmap has been prepared by
Ministry of Civil Aviation for segregating ANS from AAI and incorporating it as a
separate entity. Member (ANS) has already joined the Board of AAI in October, 2010
and ANS function has been consolidated under him. KPMG have been retained as
advisor in July, 2011 to assist AAI in the corporatization of ANS, and the engagement
timeline is for 15 months. Project Management Unit (PMU) comprising of AAI and
KPMG officials was set-up for the realization of the engagement objectives.

84. When the Committee raised the issue of instances of over pricing of air tickets reported to
the DGCA it was informed that Air fares are market determined and take into account all relevant
factors including the cost of operation, characteristics of services, etc.  Generally scheduled airlines
offer different fare buckets for each flight, in line with the practice followed in other parts of the
world.  As the lower fare buckets get sold out, the passengers pay progressively higher fare with
the increase in demand for seats. Air fares are not being regulated by the Government and Airlines
are free to charge air fares as per the market forces. Although, filing and approval of tariff is not
required, yet DGCA has the power to intervene in cases where he is satisfied that the operator has
established excessive or predatory tariff or had indulged in oligopolistic practices. Accordingly Rule
135 of Aircraft Rules has been amended.
85. The Committee was further informed that with a view to maintain transparency in tariff
publication, DGCA has taken following steps:-
(i) Scheduled domestic airlines have been directed to display established tariff route-wise
and fare category-wise in their websites, on monthly basis and also to notify any
significant and noticeable changes to DGCA within 24 hours of effecting such a
change.
(ii) A T ariff Analysis Unit has been set up in DGCA to monitor air fares of domestic
airlines on a regular basis at periodic intervals.
86. It was further informed that during the regular random check of airfares, it has been
observed that airfares offered by the airlines are well within the fare band uploaded by them on
their respective websites. However, trend of airfares moving towards higher bucket is observed
during week-ends, holidays, etc. when the demand of seats is comparatively high.
