Recommendation/Observation 
1. The Committee recommends that wherever possible, the Ministry/ASI should develop 
the area in the vicinity of the ancient monuments as green space with amenities for tourists 
since doing so will not only curb encroachment of that area but also bolster touri sm in the

(Para 35A)  
Reply of Ministry

Review of existing provisions for grant of permission required for construction or 
repairs within the prohibited/ regulated areas of Centrally protected monuments  
Recommendation/Observation 
2. The Committee fails to see why a person living in the Regulated Area of a CPM 
should be required to seek permission from the Competent Authority under the AMASR Rules 
for carrying out innocuous maintenance activities such as filling of cracks, whitewashing , 
replacement of windows etc. The Committee feels that such uncalled -for interference makes 
lives harder for people living nearby the monuments and could slowly cause feelings of 
anguish towards the monument itself. Given the large number of monuments acro ss the 
country and the limited budgetary resources available with the Government, it is pertinent 
that people residing near the monuments are encouraged to look after the monuments and 
protect them. Besides, placing such unnecessary curbs and implementing them is a wastage 
of limited Government resources and turns the focus away from where it should be - actually 
protecting and preserving the monument itself. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Ministry may undertake a thorough review of the AMASR  Act and Rules and omit any 
provisions that provide for seeking permissions for minor works where the same is not 
actually needed and streamline the process for applying for permission in case where the 
same is actually needed. Steps may urgently be taken to make it possible for the entire 
process for seeking permission for construction activities in prohibited and regulated areas, 
to be completed online so as to not cause any hassle to bona fide residents living near the 
monuments.

The Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains (AMASR) Act, 1958 
Recommendation/Observation 
1. In light of the observations made by various experts on the subject and the lack of any 
specific reasoning behind the limit of 100 metres and 300 metres around the CPMs as 
prohibited and regulated areas, the Committee is of the strong view that the Ministry must 
undertake a holistic, in -depth review of the restrictions imposed by the AMASR Act on 
construction activities near the CPMs since it does not seem logica l to have restrictions on a 
uniform amount of area across the highly diverse types of monuments under the protection of 
the Central Government. The Committee takes serious notice of the observation made by one 
of the experts that the 2010 amendment was bro ught about without any inputs from the ASI 
and recommends that the review of the existing legislation should be carried out in 
consultation with existing and former senior officials of ASI, and other subject experts. The 
Committee recommends that the area to be deemed as prohibited and regulated area should 
not be set in stone for all the monuments and should instead be decided on a case -to-case 
basis, with due consideration to the individual requirements of the monument/site. There is 
no denying the fact t hat there needs to be a mechanism for keeping a check on the 
construction activities in the vicinity of a historical monument but utmost care must be taken 
to ensure that doing so causes least possible disruption in the lives of people who have had 
been li ving in harmony with the monuments since decades, duly factoring in the specific 
requirements for such checks given the unique nature of the individual historical monuments. 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should urgently work towards creating a  
regulatory framework that has provisions for the least possible restrictions on construction 
activities in a manner suitable to the specific requirements for individual monuments while 
ensuring that no structural or visual damage is caused to the monument  by any construction 
activities and does not unnecessarily impinge on the rights of people living nearby the 
monuments. 
(Para 14)  
 Reply of Ministry  
1.1 The issue has been taken into consideration under proposed amendment in 
consultation with other experts also. 
 
 
 

5 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
2. The Committee agrees with the concerns raised by one of the experts regarding the 
prohibitions imposed by the AMASR (Amendment) Act, 2010 on appointment of any person 
who has held any post with the ASI or the Ministry of Culture, as the Chairman or member of 
the NMA. The Committee feels that having the services of personnel who have had actual 
field experience working for the preservation and protection of the historical monuments and 
sites, could help NMA fulf ill its mandate in a more effective manner but many people with 
such experience would be those who have had worked with the ASI or Ministry of Culture 
and thus would be deemed ineligible to be appointed as the Chairman or member of the NMA 
as per the exist ing provisions of the AMASR Act. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry may revisit the rationale behind the restrictions imposed by Section 20F(3) of the 
AMASR (Amendment and Validation) Act, 2010 and consider the pros and cons behind 
imposing such a restriction on the appointment of Chairman and members of the NMA. 
Further, the Committee notes that the NMA has also been given the responsibility of 
classification and gradation of monuments in consultation with ASI. The Committee 
recommends that NMA may complete the classification and gradation exercise at the earliest. 
The Committee feels that doing so, alongwith preparation of heritage byelaws for all CPMs, 
would permit the Ministry to come up with provisions for restrictions best suited for each 
monument instead of blanket bans that fail to serve the intended purpose. 
(Para 15)  
Reply of Ministry  
2.1 The issue has been taken into consideration under proposed amendment. Actions are 
also initiated for enabling the work of preparation of Heritage Bye-laws at faster rate.  
 
Recommendation/Observation 
3. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may introduce the concept of 
Transferable Development Rights to enable people having properties in the vicinity of the 
monuments to monetize their properties. The Committee feels that this would act as a 
deterrent to encroachment of monuments, especially in case the monument is located in a 
congested urban area.  
(Para 16) 
Reply of Ministry  
3.1 Transfer of Development rights is not within the jurisdiction of MoC. 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
4. The Committee takes note of the extensive list of suggestions made by the expert 
regarding the new Rules/ Regulations/ Policies/ Legislation/ Amendments that can be

review of the existing legislation may factor in feedback from experts in the field of 
conservation, preservation, archaeology, law as well as all the stakeholders who are

(Para 18)

Efficacy of the existing Regulatory Framework to deal with the specific issue of 
encroachment of the Historical Sites and Monuments  
Recommendation/Observation 
5. The Committee notes the existing provisions in place in order to deal with the issue of 
encroachment of Centrally Protected Monuments and the loopholes/ gaps/ glitches pointed 
out by various experts. The Committee agrees with the suggestion made by one of the experts 
that amendments are required in order to ensure that encroachment of monuments are 
removed as quick as possible in fast track courts. The Committee feels that doing so can 
permit the encroachment to be removed befo re any permanent structure is constructed in the 
vicinity of the monument, thus preventing any sort of possible damages to the monument.  
(Para 33)  
Reply of Ministry  
5.1 The issue of removal of encroachments is suitably being addressed in the proposed 
amendment.  
 
Recommendation/Observation 
6. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may undertake a thorough review of the 
AMASR Act as well as the AMASR Rules in terms of the powers available with ASI to tackle 
the issue of encroachment of monuments, keeping in view the various loopholes and gap s 
pointed out by legal experts. The Committee feels that the Ministry should incorporate 
provisions similar to those relating to encroachment in the Indian Forest Act, 1927, in the 
AMASR Act in order to empower ASI to effectively deal with the issue of enc roachment of 
monuments. The Committee further recommends that the AMASR Act should grant power to 
ASI to seal the site of illegal construction till the time the demolition can be done, so that any 
delay in the same does not cause any further damage to the monument and there is no 

7 
 
incentive for encroachers to undertake any illegal construction near the monument. The 
Ministry should request the Ministry of Finance to provide it with separate budget to carry 
out the demolition of unauthorized constructions tha t have taken place in the vicinity of 
Centrally Protected Monuments.  
(Para 35)  
Reply of Ministry  
6.1 The issue of removal of encroachments is suitably being addressed in the proposed 
amendment. As per the AMASR Rules, 1959 the demolition of unauthorised constructions is 
to be carried out at the cost of defaulter.

Suggestions for creation of Regulatory Framework for protection of Historical 
Monuments presently not under the protection of both Central and State Governments 
Recommendation/Observation 
1. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry may consider coming up 
with a legislation to amend the Constitution adding ―ancient and historical monuments 
other than those declared to be of national importance to the Concurrent List, in order to 
permit the Central Government to enforce at least some set of provisions for the basic 
protection and conservation of archaeological monuments and sites which are presently not 
under any sort of protection by Central and State Governments. A separate Herita ge 
Commission may thereafter be established to look after such monuments. Since a 
Constitutional amendment is a time -taking process, the Committee recommends that in the 
meanwhile, the Ministry may come up with policy guidelines which can be adopted by the  
State Governments across the country for looking after neglected archaeological sites and 
monuments. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry may request for certain 
budgetary allocation to allow ASI to carry out urgent conservation measures at such 
miscellaneous monuments, possibly in coordination with concerned State Archaeology 
Departments.  
(Para 23)  
Reply of Ministry  
1.1 ASI has provisions to undertake conservation measures at ancient monuments which 
are not of national importance. Certain budgetary allocation is also made for this purpose.  
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
1.2 The reply furnished by the Ministry does not  state whether it has taken any 
action to frame policy guidelines which can be adopted by the State Governments across 
the country for looking after neglected archaeological sites and monuments . The 
Committee desires to be informed whether the Ministry has taken any action in this 
regard. Further, the Committee may be apprised regarding the budgetary allocation 
made and u tilized by ASI for monuments which are not of national importance and 
what are the guidelines/ rules in place for taking up such conservation works.

encroachment of the Historical Sites and Monuments  
Recommendation/Observation 
2. The Committee is concerned to note that the final notification of declaration of an 
ancient/historical monument/site/remains as of national importance, is not published by ASI 
in most cases. The Committee recommends that a time-bound limit may be prescribed for ASI 
to publish the final notification, alongwith the site map, for monuments under its protection 
since the purpose of the same is to verify the physical structure and to determine the 
portion/part/extent of the monument that is required to be within the protected limits.  
(Para 34)  
Reply of Ministry  
2.1 Declaring any monument as of national importance and publication final notification 
therefor involve consultation with State Government,  Stakeholder, Management Committee 
or sometimes land owners. Such formalities may consume much time -case to case basis and 
are beyond the jurisdiction of ASI/MoC Sometimes, legal issue may crop up. As such it 
appears not feasible to specify a fix time limit to publish final notification. 
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
2.2 The Committee notes that the declaration of any monument to be of national 
importance and publication of final notification therefor is a time -consuming process 
since it involves consultation with State Government, Stakeholder, Management 
Committee or sometimes land owners.  However, the more extensive the tasks, the 
greater is the need for setting time limits.  The Committee feels that having a clear time -
limit for publication of the final notification is required to mitigate the present situation 
wherein the final notification of declaration of an  ancient/historical 
monument/site/remains as of national importance, is not published  at all  by ASI in 
many cases, even after passage of very long time  since publication of preliminary 
notification. The Committee  strongly recommends that t he Ministry may prescribe  a 
well-defined time-bound limit for ASI to publish the final notification  of declaration of 
an ancient/historical monuments/site/remains as of national importance , along with the 
site map, for monuments under its protection.  While deciding upon the time -limit to be 
fixed for this purpose, the Ministry may duly factor in the time-consuming nature of the 
process for publication of final notification. 
 
Preparation of monument -specific conservation plan and bye -laws for all Centrally 
Protected Monuments across the country 
Recommendation/Observation 
3. The Committee opines that the Ministry/ASI/NMA should carry out an exercise to 
prepare monument -specific conservation plans and heritage bye -laws for all Centrally 

10 
 
Protected Monuments across the country, since there cannot be a uniform plan for all the 
monuments and the unique conservation requirements of each monument can only be 
reflected through dedicated conservation plan for each monument. Such an exercise would  
undoubtedly require sufficient manpower and resources. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry should ensure that swift action is taken to ensure that ASI is adequately staffed at all 
times and staffing requirements should be anticipated well in advanc e since recruitment of 
quality personnel is often a time-taking exercise. The Committee further recommends that the 
Ministry should formulate the budgetary requirements for creation of dedicated conservation 
plans for all CPMs and request the Ministry of F inance to grant the same as a special one -
time grant.  
(Para 43)  
Reply of Ministry  
 
3.1 For the conservation of monuments an annual conservation programme is prepared by 
the Circles concerned as per the requirement and priority of the monuments/ sites. B ased on 
the approved annual conservation programme, conservation works are carried out. In  
addition, Site Management Plans (SMPs) for select WH monuments have also been prepared 
by ASI. 
3.2 Promotions held in ASI during 2021 and 2022 (till date) are attac hed at Annexure-I. 
Further, plan for filling up vacant posts and promotion for upcomi ng years are also given at 
Annexure-II. 
3.3 Annual budget for the conservation of monuments is provided by the Ministry of 
Culture to the Archaeological Survey of India. 
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
3.4 The Committee observes that the annual conservation programme prepared by 
the Circles concerned is as per the requirement and priority of the monuments/ sites. 
The Committee, however, feels that the requirements and priority of conservation work 
at the monuments/ sites cannot be effectively gauged unless monument -specific 
conservation plans are prepared for all monuments in the respective circles  and 
therefore, recommends that provisions may be introduced to prepare m onument-
specific conservation plans so as to ensure suitable state of conservation for all Centrally 
Protected Monuments. 
 
3.5 The Committee notes the details furnished by the Ministry regarding the status 
of direct recruitment to the various posts in ASI and is concerned to note  that more  
than 2,000 vacancies exist at various posts in ASI at present. In this regard, the 
Committee may be apprised whether ASI factors in the time taken by the recruiting 
agencies to complete the recruitment process, while repo rting its vacancies to such 
agencies. Given the evident scarcity of manpower available with ASI in view of the large 
number of Centrally Protected Monuments across the country, anticipating the delays 
in the process of recruitment while reporting vacancies / upcoming vacancies, is of 

11 
 
crucial importance. Effective steps, therefore, must be taken in this regard to ensure 
that ASI is adequately staffed at all times  and recruitment process is put in motion well 
in advance for the same,  so as to ensure sufficient  protection and preservation of our 
tangible cultural heritage.

action to frame policy guidelines which can be adopted by the State Governments across 
the country for looking after neglected archaeological sites and monuments . The 
Committee desires to be informed whether the Ministry has taken any action in this 
regard. Further, the Committee may be apprised regarding the budgetary allocation 
made and utilized by ASI for monuments which are not of national importance and 
what are the guidelines/ rules in place for taking up such conservation works. 
(Para 1.2) 
Efficacy of the existing Regulatory Framework to deal with the specific issue of 
encroachment of the Historical Sites and Monuments  
2.2 The Committee notes that the declaration of any monument to be of national 
importance and publication of final notification therefor is a time -consuming process 
since it involves consultation with State Government, Stakeholder, Management 
Committee or sometimes land owners.  However, the more extensive the tasks, the 
greater is the need for setting time limits.  The Committee feels that having a clear time -
limit for publication of the final notification is required to mitigate the present situation 
wherein the final notificatio n of declaration of an ancient/historical 
monument/site/remains as of national importance, is not published  at all  by ASI in 
many cases , even after passage of very long time since publication of preliminary 
notification. The Committee  strongly recommends t hat the Ministry may prescribe  a 
well-defined time-bound limit for ASI to publish the final notification  of declaration of 
an ancient/historical monuments/site/remains as of national importance , along with the 
site map, for monuments under its protection.  While deciding upon the time -limit to be 
fixed for this purpose, the Ministry may duly factor in the time-consuming nature of the 
process for publication of final notification. 
(Para 2.2) 
Preparation of monument -specific conservation plan and bye -laws for  all Centrally 
Protected Monuments across the country 
3.4 The Committee observes that the annual conservation programme prepared by 
the Circles concerned is as per the requirement and priority of the monuments/ sites. 

16 
 
The Committee, however, feels that the requirements and priority of conservation work 
at the monuments/ sites cannot be effectively gauged unless monument -specific 
conservation plans are prepared for all monuments in the respective circles and 
therefore, recommends that provisions may be intro duced to prepare monument -
specific conservation plans so as to ensure suitable state of conservation for all Centrally 
Protected Monuments. 
(Para 3.4) 
3.5 The Committee notes the details furnished by the Ministry regarding the status 
of direct recruitment to the various posts in ASI and is concerned to note that more 
than 2,000 vacancies exist at various posts in ASI at present. In this regard, the 
Committee may be apprised whether ASI factors in the time taken by the recruiting 
agencies to complete the rec ruitment process, while reporting its vacancies to such 
agencies. Given the evident scarcity of manpower available with ASI in view of the large 
number of Centrally Protected Monuments across the country, anticipating the delays 
in the process of recruitme nt while reporting vacancies/ upcoming vacancies, is of 
crucial importance. Effective steps, therefore, must be taken in this regard to ensure 
that ASI is adequately staffed at all times  and recruitment process is put in motion well 
in advance for the same , so as to ensure sufficient protection and preservation of our 
tangible cultural heritage. 
(Para 3.5) 
Security policy for all Centrally Protected Monuments 
4.3 The Committee desires to be apprised whether the Ministry of Culture has taken 
any action to seek sufficient budget from the Ministry of Finance to allow it to post 
security personnel at all Centrally Protected Monuments across the country  and 
whether any action has been taken on the issue of training of guides by the Ministry of 
Tourism. Further, the Committee is of the view that given the extensive knowledge 
possessed by ASI regarding Centrally Protected Monuments across the country, having 
a suitable mechanism for coordination between ASI and Ministry of Tourism will help 
improve the training st andards of Tourist Guides working at such sites. Sensitization 
programmes for guides may also be carried out in coordination with the Ministry of 
Tourism to encourage them to look after the Centrally Protected Monuments while 
guiding the tourists. 
(Para 4.3) 
Improving the mechanism for coordination between ASI and State Government 
Departments  
5.2 The Committee desires to be apprised of the procedure under which ASI 
provides technical expertise and guidance to the State Governments and the details of 
such instances in the last 3 years.
