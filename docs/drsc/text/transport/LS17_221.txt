REPORT
The Ministry of Shipping has been mandated with the responsibility of formulating policy framework
concerning the shipping industry as a whole, devise programmes in keeping with the policy and to
implement the programmes through appropriate delivery mechanism. India is a major maritime nation by
virtue of its long coast line of around 7500 km. The Maritime Sector in India comprises Ports, Shipping,
Shipbuilding and Ship repair and Inland Water Transport Systems. There are 12 major ports under the
direct control of Government of India and about 200 non-major ports.
2. Indian Shipping industry has, over the years, played a crucial role in the transport sector of India's
economy. Approximately 95% of the country's trade by volume and 68% in term of value move through
maritime transport. The rapid economic growth and technological advancements in the shipping industry
as well as in all the related fields pose major challenges and exacting demands on infrastructure creation,
human resource development, facilities for training research and design studies etc. in the areas of port
infrastructure, ship-building and ship-repair, inland water transport, training and research institutes and
the newly created Indian Maritime University.
3. The subject Ship Breaking was allotted to the Ministry of Shipping vide Cabinet Secretariat
Notification dated 31.07.2014 transferring the subject from Ministry of Steel by change in the Allocation
of Business Rules. The subject was being handled in Ministry of Steel since 2005.

Sagarmala Project
41. The Sagarmala project envisaged for developing India's ports to levels comparable with the best
global ports in terms of infrastructure, efficiency and quality of service, increasing the tonnage capacity,
upgrading & creating ship building and ship repair facilities and increase the use of inland waterway for
transportation. The project has already been conceptualized and a Cabinet Note submitted for the
consideration of the Cabinet. Proposed allocation of Rs. 200 crore includes preparation of National
Perspective Plan and detailed matter plans for Coastal Economic Zones and administrative expenditure.
42. The Committee welcomes the new project named Sagarmala for integrated development of
Indian Ports and Shipping Sectors. The Committee hopes that this project will be a key component
in the participation of the Indian Port and Shipping industry in the 'Make in India' Campaign
of the Prime Minister.
43. The Committee desires to know the developments made in the establishment of two new
Major Ports in West Bengal and erstwhile Andhra Pradesh. The Committee also desires to know
the VGF the Central Government sanctioned in each of these ports.
Cargo Traffic at Indian Ports
44. During April to December 2014, Major and Non-major Ports in India handled a total cargo
throughput of 775.18 MT. The traffic grew by 6.79% over the corresponding period of previous year.
The 12 Major Ports handled a traffic of 433.87 MT during April-December 2014, representing an
increase of about 5.04% over the corresponding period of previous year. Of the 12 Major Ports, cargo
handled during April-December 2014 at 11 ports showed positive growth, but New-Mangalore has shown
negative growth. Amongst these 11 major ports, growth in throughput at Mormugao was the highest at
19.89% followed by Kamarajar Port (14.27%) VO Chidambaranar (9.82%), Kandla (7.22%), Mumbai
(5.78%) and Paradip (5.24%).
45. While the commodities  viz.,  Coal, Fertilizers, Containers, and POL are showing steady growth,
there has been decline in handling of Iron Ore due to restriction imposed by the Government on Iron

10
Ore mining. The other general traffic continued to grow. Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust (JNPT) continued
to be the leading container handling port in the country with a share of about 48% followed by Chennai
(25%) and the remaining share of 27% being handled by other Major Ports.
46. During the deliberations on Demands for Grants, many Members of the Committee raised the issue
of non-availability of enough storage space for perishable items like seafood, vegetables, fruits etc. in
the Port premises for both in- bound and outbound cargo. As a result thereof, such cargo preferably goes
to the minor ports and major ports are facing cargo loss and revenue loss due to non- availability of
enough air-conditioned storage space in the Major Ports.
47. As per the Outcome Budget of the Ministry, during the Eleventh Five year Plan (2007-12), the
traffic at Non-Major ports increased at an annual rate of close to 14.75%. Non-Major ports handled
around 44% of the total maritime freight traffic of the country during April to December 2014. Four
maritime States viz., Gujarat, Maharashtra, Orissa and Andhra Pradesh together, accounted for close to
97% of the total traffic by the non-major ports in the current year i.e., during April-December, 2014.
48. The Committee notes the performance of the Ports in terms of handling cargo traffic
during the last two years. The Committee is concerned to note that many of the Major Ports are
losing its business due to the presence of other Minor Ports in its close vicinity. The Committee
recommends that Major Ports should be equipped with modern facilities for handling of cargo so
that they can compete with the Non-Major Ports in the aspect of attracting more cargo.
49. The Committee understands that all the Major Ports have enough land available with them
and hence, providing proper storage facilities should not be a problem for them. Therefore, the
Committee recommends that all the Major Por ts should make sure that air-conditioned storage
facilities are available, so that there will not be any loss of cargo in the Ports. The Ministry
should make the necessary allocation at the RE stage and the Committee may be apprised of
the progress made in this regard.
Port Efficiency
50. Efficiency at Ports has an important bearing on the transaction cost of the shipping lines. Major
ports have improved their efficiency of operation particularly in terms of Turnaround Time (TRT). As
per the data furnished by the Ministry, amongst the 12 Major ports, improvement in Turnaround Time
during 2013-14 in comparison to 2012-13 was discernible in most of the major ports. The average
Turnaround Time (Port A/c) improved from 4.24 days in 2000-01 to 2.25 days in 2013-14. The average
pre-berthing detention time has decreased from 11.04 hours in 2000-01 to 5.59 hrs in 2014-15. The
average output per ship per berth day has shown a major increase in this decade. It has increased from
6961 tonnes in 2000-01 to 12342 tonnes in 2014-15.
51. The Committee takes note of the improvement in Turnaround Time registered during
2013-14 in majority of the Major Ports and the increase in average output per ship per berth day.
The Committee hopes that the Major Ports would sustain this momentum in 2015-16 also.
52. To improve the efficiency of Ports, the XII Plan document laid stress on the need for efficient
electronic data interchange. It is necessary for Port Community System (PCS) to integrate the electronic
flow of document/information and function as centralized hub for all the Major Ports of India and also
stakeholders like shipping lines/agents, surveyors, stevedores, banks container freight stations, custom
house agents, importers and customs. Further, Non-Major ports should also gradually integrate with the
centralized Port Community.

Tariff Authority on Major Ports (TAMP)
54. As per the information furnished by the Ministry, recently, the Ministry of Shipping has issued a
Tariff Policy for Major Port Trusts, 2015. Under Section 111 of the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963, under
this new Policy, the Major Port Trusts have been given flexibility to determine their own tariff within
the Annual Revenue Requirement. These guidelines would enable the Major Ports to change tariff based
on market condition and will provide flexibility.
55. The Ministry has informed the Committee that M/s Deloitte Touche India Pvt. Ltd. (Deloitte) have
been appointed as Consultant by Indian Port Association (IPA) on behalf of the Ministry of Shipping to
undertake the Study of reviewing the role of Tariff Authority for Major Ports (TAMP) and its relevance
in the present context. The Consultant M/s Deloitte has, after initial consultation with stakeholders,
submitted its Preliminary Report to the Ministry of Shipping on 28th February, 2015 and made a
presentation to the Ministry of Shipping on the preliminary report on 5th March 2015. Deloitte has to
consult the other stakeholders including users / user associations and is expected to submit its final report
to the Ministry of Shipping by 30.4.2015.
56. The Committee in its 170th Report on Modernization of Major Ports had observed that with the
coming of minor ports in the country, the pace of development has slackened for the Major Ports. Since
minor ports are governed by the Indian Ports Act, 1908 and are outside the purview of the Tariff
Authority of Major Ports (TAMP), a single comprehensive Act will provide a level playing field to the
Major Ports in terms of infrastructure development, better railway networking etc. The Committee had
also noted that the tariff structure differs to a great extent for major and minor ports of the country,
which needs to be bridged. The Committee had highlighted the need for TAMP in the first instance to
establish some common accounting procedures amongst Ports and move towards fixing uniform principles
for fixation of tariffs; this would go a long way towards promoting inter-port competition.
57. The Committee observes the flexibility given to the Major Ports to change tariff based on
market condition, as per the new Tariff Policy. The Committee hopes that this initiative would
enable not only more functional autonomy but also permit a level playing field to compete with
the minor ports who are not under the TAMP. The Committee finds that its repeated
recommendations in this regard have been considered by the Ministry while reviewing the TAMP
norms. The Committee recommends that the revision of TAMP should be made effective at the
earliest. The Committee reiterates that the Ministry should consider constituting a port promotion
council to take initiative on the developmental activities as well as the proper marketing of the
services available with the Port and setting up a tribunal to hear complaints against port tariffs.

Legislative Measures
139. As per the information furnished by the Ministry, the following are the legislative measures taken
by the Ministry in 2014-15:
AFS Convention provisions incorporated in Merchant Shipping Act, 1958:
The Merchant Shipping (Amendment) Act, 2014 to give effect to the Anti Fouling Systems (AFS)
Convention 2001 of International Maritime Organization (IMO) was passed by both Houses of Parliament
and the Act was published in the Gazette of India on 10th December 2014. This legislation incorporates
the provision of AFS Convention in India domestic legislation i.e., Merchant Shipping Act 1958.
140. The Committee, in its 197th Report, had made recommendations on the Merchant Shipping
(First Amendment) Bill, 2014 on the anti-fouling systems for control of chemical pollution in the
seas. The Committee understands that the legislation has been implemented w.e.f 1st April, 2015
and hopes that this legislation would help in controlling the use of harmful anti-fouling systems
on ships.
Maritime Labour Convention provisions incorporated in Merchant Shipping Act ,1958
141. The Merchant Shipping (Second Amendment) Act 2014 to give effect to the Maritime Labour
Convention (MLC) 2006 of International Labour Organization (ILO) was passed by both Houses of
Parliament and the Act was published in the Gazette of India on 10th December 2014. This legislation
incorporates the provisions of MLC 2006 in Indian domestic legislation viz., the Merchant Shipping Act
1958.
142. The Committee, in its 202nd Report, had made recommendations on the implementation
of Maritime Labour Convention 2006 for the benefits of the Indian seafarers. The Committee
notes that seafarers are facing various problems, cheating and other atrocities even from ship
owners and life threat from pirates etc. throughout their career. Even though there are provisions
for their protection under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, they have not proved to be effective
in addressing their grievances. The Committee, therefore, recommends for an early
implementation of the Maritime Labour Convention 2006.

organizations. The Chair asked the Secretary to make a presentation on the Budget allocations, expenditure,
achievements, non-utilization of funds, the adequacy of budgetary provisions made to each head etc. The
Committee wanted to know about the areas where the Ministry failed to fully utilize the funds, the details
of fund allocations to the port sector, shipping sector, capacity addition of ports, issues related to
Shipping Corporation of India, Dredging Corporation of India, Indian Maritime University, development
of Inland Waterways, issues related to Kolkata Port and Haldia, Cochin Port, Mumbai Port, road projects
in the Ports, use of Port land for the benefit of general public, the schemes for infrastructure development
in enhancing port capacity, providing boost to the ship building industry etc.
3. The Secretary gave an overview on the budget allocation made for the Ministry and the achievements
made during the previous years. He briefed the Committee about the reasons for under utilization of
funds, the achievements made during the 12th Plan, issues related to the major ports and other organizations
under the Ministry, their future plans etc. The Secretary gave a comparative table on the expenditure

48
made under various sectors during the period from 5th Plan to 11th Plan. He explained to the Committee
that Railways got the highest allocation of 51.53%, followed by Road Transport with 32.54% allocation.
However, Civil Aviation got 9.46% and the least allocation was given to Water Transport which was mere
6.47%.
4. The Secretary apprised the Committee that about 101 navigable main rivers are available in the
country for navigation. However, indiscriminate construction of cross-structures in them are causing a
major hindrance to navigation. Therefore, Cabinet has approved introduction of a Bill in Parliament to
bring some regulation of such structures across the rivers in the country. He stated that availability of
sufficient rakes is a major problem faced by the Ports, especially the Major Ports located in the eastern
coast. To address this issue, an Infrastructure Committee has been constituted under the Chairmanship
of the Minister for Shipping and the Committee holds periodic meetings to review the status.
5. The Members of the Committee highlighted the need for scanners of large sized containers at
Major ports, controlling pollution caused by oil spilling, asbestos during ship breaking etc.
6. Members of the Committee also raised various queries. The Secretary replied to the points raised.
The Chair directed that written replies to the points not answered, may be sent within a week.
