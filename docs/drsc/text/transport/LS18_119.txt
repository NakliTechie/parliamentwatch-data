REPORT
 
            The Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for overall policy planning and coordination of programmes of
 various activities of the Central Government agencies, State Governments/Union Territories and the Private Sector
 for the development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Ministry plays a crucial role in coordinating and
 supplementing the efforts of the States/Union Territories Governments, catalyzing private investment, strengthening
 promotional and marketing efforts and in providing trained manpower resources. The activities of the Ministry
 mainly consists of the following functions:-
 
·
        Framing of policy matters on development of tourism

·        Grant of Central Financial Assistance to States/UTs for Development of Tourism Infrastructure
·        Encouragement of Public Private participation in tourism infrastructure development
·        Promotion and marketing of Tourism Products within the country and abroad
·        Providing incentives in the accommodation sector
·        Regulation of tourism industry
·        Investment facilitation
·        Human Resource Development and Training
·        Research, analysis, monitoring and evaluation
·        International Cooperation of promotion and development of India Tourism
 
2.         The administrative head of the Ministry is the Secretary (Tourism).  The Secretary also acts as the Director
 General (DG) Tourism. The office of the Director General of Tourism {now merged with the office of Secretary
 (Tourism)} provides executive directions for the implementation of various policies and programmes.  Directorate
 General of Tourism has a field formation of 20 offices within the country and 13 offices abroad and one sub-ordinate
 office/project i.e. Indian Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM)/Gulmarg Winter Sports Project (GWSP).
 The activities of IISM/GWSP have been revived and various ski and other courses are conducted in the J&K valley. 
 In addition to the Headquarters Organization at New Delhi, the Directorate General of Tourism has 20 India tourism
 Offices in the country (including 5 Regional Offices), situated at New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Guwahati
 and other offices are located at Agra, Bhubaneswar, Aurangabad, Bangalore, Kochi, Hyderabad, Imphal, Jaipur,
 Khajuraho, Naharlagun, Panaji (Goa), Patna, Port Blair, Shillong and Varanasi.  There are 13 India tourism Offices
 Overseas, which include 5 Regional Offices at, New York, Frankfurt, Dubai, Sydney and Tokyo.  The other India
 tourism Offices are located at Los Angeles, Toronto, London, Paris, Amsterdam, Milan, Johannesburg and
 Singapore. The overseas offices are primarily responsible for tourism promotion and marketing in their respective
 areas and the field offices in the country are responsible for providing information service to tourists and to monitor
 the progress of field projects.

21.       While examining the expenditure pattern of the Miistry of Tourism, the Committee desired to know about the
 details of the various centrally sponsored schemes.  The Ministry informed the Committee that centrally sponsored
 schemes like, product infrastructure and Destination Development, Infrastructure Technology, Assistance for large
 Revenue Generating Projects and Market Research etc. are extended to States on the condition of providing matching
 grants by them or bearing the committed liability. 
 
22.       The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism is implementing a number of centrally sponsored schemes like
 Product Infrastructure and Destination Development, Information Technology, Assistance for large Revenue Generating
 Projects and Market Research etc., where States have to provide matching grants or bear the committed liability. The
 Committee understands that some of the States are finding it difficult to bear the requisite liability under the centrally
 sponsored scheme. The Committee is ofthe opinion that implementation of the centrally sponsored schemes is greatly
 hampered when the State Governments do not come forward with the proposals for tourism infrastructure development
 projects or find it difficult to provide their monetary share thereon. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the funding
 pattern of various centrally sponsored scheme should be reviewed at the earliest and action plan be formulated to provide 100
 percent grants for centrally sponsored scheme of the Ministry during the 11
th Five Year Plan.
 
DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM INFRASTRUCTURE AND DESTINATIONS
 
23.       The Committee has been informed that during the 10 th Plan, a paradigm shifts in tourism planning and
 development have yielded quantum increases in tourist footfalls.  The Ministry of Tourism has focused on
 infrastructure development in certain key destinations–Ajanta-Ellora, Bodhgaya-Rajgir-Nalanda, Kurukshetra,
 Mahabalipuram.  An integrated approach with all backward-forward linkages was adopted in these destinations. 
 This has yielded larger tourism volumes and revenues, thereby contributing significantly to the national economy
 and the rural sector in particular. This thrust for infrastructure development has been achieved in combination with
 care for the carrying capacity of destinations, visitor satisfaction and local community sensibilities.
 
24.       In the 10
th Five Year Plan, the eight schemes providing Central assistance for tourism infrastructure
 development from previous plan period were merged into two schemes, i.e., Integrated Development of Tourist
 Circuits; Product/Infrastructure and Destination Development.  Recognizing the need to regulate tourism product
 development, these two schemes were further merged into one scheme for Product/Infrastructure Development for
 Destinations and Circuits with the following components, namely, Major Destinations and Circuits development;
 Rural Tourism infrastructure development.
 
25.       The Committee was informed that at the beginning of the 10
th Plan the Central Financial Assistance to the
 States had not been able to create a major impact in terms of creation of world-class tourism infrastructure. 
 Therefore, in order to provide quick and substantial impact, the revised scheme was taken up  to identify key tourism
 circuits in the country on an annual basis, and develop them to international standards, with the objective of
 providing all infrastructure facilities required by the tourists within these circuits.  The circuits prioritized/sanctioned
 for development during the 10th plan period are given at Annexure-IV.
 
26.       In the 9 th Plan, destination development had tended to be somewhat ad hoc, with inadequate prioritization. 
 During the 10 th plan period, limited destinations in the States / UTs were identified on yearly basis for integrated
 intensive development. While developing the destinations, the strategy followed, was to ensure that the
 developments take place in a regional planning framework that is not restricted to the immediate environs of the site

18
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/119threport.htm[22-10-2018 12:24:10]
 but extends to the ‘region of influence’ of the attraction or cluster of attractions including the primary staging area
 and travel nodes.  It was ensured that all development work was based on professionally prepared master plans,
 ideally using the best expertise and contained components that are conceived, to satisfy the preferences and
 requirement needs of a clearly identified target market.  Efforts were made to see that the master planning approach
 included all stakeholders in the planning process – all levels of government and administration, various government
 agencies and Ministries, the local community, elected representatives, and members of the tourism industry or
 related industries. Destinations were carefully selected in consultation with the State Governments, based on the
 tourism potential. 
 
27.       An outlay of Rs.520 crore has been allocated in BE 2007-08 for development of tourist infrastructure as
 against Rs.422.50 crore allocated in BE 2006-07. The Ministry of Tourism informed that the year-wise number of

 resources are still being spread thinly across States, therefore, it is proposed to continue with the recently revised
 Scheme with focus on attaining the world’s best standards through:
 
(i)
                Improvements in the existing tourism product.
(ii)             Development of new tourism products.
(iii)           Integrated infrastructure development of the tourist sites.
 
28.       The Committee enquired about the number of Circuits , Destinations and Rural Tourism Projects selected and
 developed by the Ministry of Tourism during the 10 th plan and the quantum of funds released to States/UTs, the
 Ministry of Tourism informed that Ministry has sanctioned 1157 projects till date with sanctioned amount of
 Rs.2012.91 crore and released amount of Rs.1558.25 crore. Out of the sanctioned projects,  441 projects have been
 completed. During the  financial year 2006-07, Ministry of Tourism has sanctioned 78  projects for development of
 Destinations  with sanctioned amount of  Rs. 344.03 crore,  34 projects under the category of Circuits were also 
 sanctioned with sanctioned amount of Rs. 259.98  crore and 30 projects for Rural tourism development under the
 scheme were also sanctioned with sanctioned amount of Rs.16.61crore respectively. The Ministry of Tourism has
 also released Rs.446.59 crore during the current financial year.
 
29.       When the Committee desired to know the reasons for slow progress of projects, the Ministry of Tourism
 informed that implementation of the projects is primarily the responsibility of the concerned State /UT
 Governments. The main hurdles in implementation of the projects are delay in preparation of DPRs,  availability of
 land,  timely award of work to implementing agencies, coordination among various agencies etc. If these issues
 could be resolved by the concerned States, the projects can be implemented with fast pace.
 
30.       As regards status of creation of infrastructure projects to world class standards, monitoring and to prevent
 delays in the completion of the projects, the Ministry of Tourism stated that the projects sanctioned by the Ministry
 of Tourism for different States/UTs are expected to be completed within a maximum period of 36 months.  During
 the current financial year, 142 infrastructure projects were sanctioned for various States/UTs which are at different
 stages of implementation. Emphasis has been given for development of tourism infrastructure to world standard. The
 progress of implementation of tourism projects sanctioned for various States/UTs is reviewed from time to time at
 the level of the Secretary (Tourism) in the meetings with the State Government officials and other stakeholders. The
 officers of the Ministry of Tourism including the domestic field offices of India Tourism visits the project sites to
 ensure speedy implementation of the projects. In addition, the Financial Advisor posted in the Ministry also
 monitors intensively project implementation in the States. A monitoring cell has also been set up to ensure proper
 implementation and evaluation of the projects. The Ministry of Tourism is releasing 80% of the sanctioned amount
 as first instalment so that the States do not face paucity of funds in timely completion of the projects. A list of such
 prioritized circuits/destinations which are under implementation during the current financial year is at Annexure V.

 Rs.2012.91 crore and an amount of Rs.1558.25 crore was released. Out of the sanctioned projects only 441 projects or
 38.11% works could be completed till date and there still remain a shortfall of 716 projects. The Committee is not satisfied

77.       Our country accords top priority to the tourism sector and the new tourism policy seeks to ensure safety and
 security of the tourists inspite of that the cases of Cheating, fleecing and maltreatment of tourists bring a bad name to
 the country. India is counted as one of the unsafe destinations by international tourists. Often there are reports of
 atrocities and harassment of foreign tourists by unscrupulous elements. That foreign tourists are sometimes

file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/119threport.htm[22-10-2018 12:24:10]
 physically harassed, robbed and even murdered can not be denied. There are also reports of exploitation of
 international travellers by some unauthorised tour operators. One cannot deny occurrence of such incidences in a
 vast country like ours. But even if a very small number of such cases get publicised through the media, they tarnish
 the country’s image.
 
78.       Tourists travel to various destinations for relaxation, pleasure and exploration. Nothing should vitiate the
 atmosphere. Otherwise the tourists will go away. The policy suggests that a section of the State police be earmarked
 to act as tourist police with special training for the job. On the Central Government’s advice States like Goa, Kerala,
 Jammu & Kashmir, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh have set up Tourist Police

 Territories to deploy Tourist Police at important tourist centres/destinations. Ministry of Tourism also carries out
 social awareness campaign from time to time in print and electronic media to sensitise the service providers so that
 the tourists are not harassed.

“Security and law and order is a State subject. Nevertheless, we have been advising all the State Governments

 retired army personnel and engaged them as tourism police. But they don’t have police power. Some other
 State Governments like Jammu and Kashmir, Mumbai, Kerala, etc. have engaged local police officers and
 they have been brought under the Tourism Departments. They function as regular police force and they have
 got full police powers also. We have been recommending to all the State Governments to go in for tourism
 police system. We have been advising them that whenever there is any offence against any tourist, in that

DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM IN ANDAMAN & NICOBAR AND LAKSHADWEEP ISLANDS
 
82.       The emerald islands of Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands are like a glittering jewel and have
 gained popularity as unique tourist destinations and a paradise for naturalists.  The precious heritage of ecology and
 culture of these islands is supported by an extremely fragile ecosystem.  Andaman & Nicobar is a group of 572
 emerald islands, islets and rocks out of which 36 islands are inhabited.  The Andaman’s and Nicobar Islands have
 brilliant tropical flora and are thickly forested with evergreen, deciduous rainforest and tropical trees with mangrove
 swamps at the water edge. The sparkling clear water is excellent for snorkeling with increasing opportunity for
 fabulous Scuba diving for beginner and experienced people. The Islands are also a paradise for bird watchers. The
 coral islands of Lakshadweep consists of 36 islands out of which 10 islands are inhabited. Considering the limited
 infrastructure, the administration of these islands propose to promote low volume high value tourist inflow to these
 islands because of the fragile eco-system of the rain forest and under water corals.

 the Administration of Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep Islands, Tour/travel Association and the

 Administration. There is no specific policy of Ministry of Tourism for development of tourism in the islands.

            The Committee notes that the matter of improving connectivity to important tourists destinations and heritage sites in
 the country has been discussed in the Committee on Empowerment of Infrastructure which has recommended for setting up
 of Inter-Ministerial Groups of Secretaries for suggesting an action plan for developing road system in the country. The
 Committee feels that the physical infrastructure is the main foundation and particularly, the road network is vital for the
 promotion and development of tourism as almost 70% of tourists in the country travel by roads. The Committee
 recommends the Ministry to raise the matter of connecting important tourists Destinations/cultural heritage sites in the
 country with national highways of four lane capacities in the meetings of Inter-Ministerial Groups.                        (Para 33)
ASSISTANCE FOR LARGE REVENUE GENERATING PROJECTS
            The Committee notes with dismay that there is a heavy shortfall in the utilization of funds allocated under the
 scheme and Ministry has not furnished any reasons for the shortfall in achieving physical and financial targets during 2006-
07. An amount of Rs.1.02 crore was spent upto December 2006 out of the approved outlay of Rs.40 crore during 2006-07
 and just a single project was sanctioned under the scheme by the Ministry. The Committee recommends the Ministry to
 create sufficient facilities under the scheme for setting up of the hotels, convention centres, golf courses, special tourist
 trains, passenger shipping etc. through private initiative with Government acting as a facilitator and catalyst apart from part
 funding such projects.                                                          (Para 35)
CAPACITY BUILDING FOR SERVICE PROVIDERS
            The Committee is constrained to note that out of the budgeted allocation of Rs.15 crore the Ministry could spend 
 only Rs.2.692 crore by the end of the December 2006. The Committee take a serious note of non-achievement of financial
 and physical targets by the Ministry and desires to know the reasons for the same. The under-utilization of funds would
 have an adverse impact on the capacity building in the unorganized sector. The Committee recommends the Ministry of
 Tourism to take remedial action to ensure the optimum utilization of allocated funds.        The Committee also recommends
 the Ministry to develop a system of skill assessment and certification provider without any formal training or
 education.                                                                                                      (Para 38)
HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
            The Committee notes that the hospitality and service sector has been accorded priority with the growth and
 diversification of tourism activities. The Committee further notes that there are five institutes of repute being run in the
 private sector apart from there are 125 institutes of hotel management in the country offering hospitality programmes in
 private sector.  The Committee observes that as a sizeable number of private IHMs have emerged in the country to offer
 courses in hotel management programme there is a need to provide a institutional and integrated course in development of
 the country’s human resources programme. The Committee is pleased to note that although the guidelines for affiliation of
 these private institutes have been put-in place spelling out the eligibility requirements for affiliation though these institutes
 are lacking in training and other infrastructural facilities. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to grant a
 statutory/deemed university status to NCHMCT for achieving excellence in the tourism sector and to standardized the
 quality of education and training through affiliation and academic supervision through an Act of
 Parliament.                                                                        (Para 44)
            The Committee is constrained to note that there is huge demand-supply mismatch of manpower servicing the tourism
 industry resulting in poor quality of service offered to the tourists. The Committee feels that there is a need to develop the
 required human resources in various segments of the tourism industry consequent upon the rapid growth in the tourism,
 rapidly changing technology and a more demanding competitive market. The Committee recommends the Ministry to
 address the issues related to human resources development in the tourism sector with particular emphasis on qualified
 tourists guides, involving the quality of human resources, their condition of works, training and opportunities, the role of
 private sector for efficient administration and successful operation of the quality of manpower.                                          
                                       (Para 48)
PROMOTION OF DOMESTIC TOURISM
            The Committee notes that the request of the Ministry of Tourism for budgetary allocation of Rs.188.75 crore has not
 been accepted by Government and a provision of Rs.165 crore has been made for the year 2007-08 which is similar to that
 of last year’s allocation. The Committee feels that it will dampen the spirit of the Ministry and also put them in difficulty to

18
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/119threport.htm[22-10-2018 12:24:10]
 continue their efforts sincerely. The Committee expects that the cut in budgetary allocation will be restored at the RE stage.
 The Committee notes that the infrastructure development and destination management is the backbone for sustained growth
 of tourism in the country as it provides critical connectivity between the tourism product and target source market for both
 domestic and international tourists. The Committee finds that the creation of tourism infrastructure has multiplier effect in
 terms of overall economic growth, employment generation, preservation of art, culture and heritage. The Committee,
 therefore, urges upon the Ministry to have proper planning for development of tourism infrastructure in economically
 backward States but having abundant tourism potential by creation of assets viz. roads, telecommunication, medical
 facilities, sanitation, removal of garbage from these tourists sites etc. Integrated approach with all backward and forward
 linkages along with mass awareness campaigns in domestic market should be adopted in these destinations for getting larger
 tourism volumes and revenues, thereby contributing significantly to the national economy.                                       (Para 54)
OVERSEAS PROMOTION
            The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism through its overseas offices, attempts to promote India as a
 preferred tourist destination and to increase the number of foreign tourists arrivals in the country. The Committee observes
 that there is strong competition in the tourist generating markets for attracting foreign tourists to their countries. The
 Committee recommends the Ministry to review the functioning of the overseas marketing offices and, if need be relocate
 some of them in view of the emerging tourism requirements of the prospective tourists besides revamping the India tourism
 website to make it more user friendly. The Committee appreciates the work done by the Ministry in the form of Incredible
 India campaign and recommends the Ministry to reinforce the campaign in the global tourist market with more vigor and
 zeal.  The Committee also recommends that Incredible India campaigning should also be done in various regional languages
 to boost domestic tourism.                                 (Para 57)
            The Committee is constrained to note that our country lags behind several other less endowed countries in terms of
 attracting tourists although the  foreign tourists and foreign exchange earnings through tourism (in US Dollars), have
 registered a growth of 53.1% and 69.8% respectively. The Committee also notes that the major constraints for growth in
 foreign tourists arrivals in the country are inadequate budgetary support for promotion and marketing, non-availability of
 accommodation at the major ports of entry and other major tourist centres in the country during season, fear about health
 and hygiene conditions, perceptions relating to safety and security and expensive packages as compared to many South East
 Asian countries. The Committee recommends the Ministry to lay emphasis on safety, security and hygiene aspects along
 with other promotional activities through all major print and visual media at overseas markets and ask for adequate
 budgetary support from the Ministry of Finance and inform the Committee of the outcome
 therefrom.                                                            (Para 60)
NATIONAL TOURISM PROMOTION BOARD
            The Committee notes that tourism is not single but involves multi-sectoral activities requiring close coordination
 with various agencies viz., the Ministries of Civil Aviation, Culture, Textiles, Environment and Forests, Railway, Home,
 Finance, Shipping & Road Transport and Highways and External Affairs etc.  The Government has brought the subject of
 tourism under the scope of the Committee on Infrastructure (COI) for initiating policy on creation of world class
 infrastructure in a time bound manner. The Committee is of the opinion that inspite of the efforts, the tourism is still in the
 appalling state of coordination. The Committee is not satisfied with the replies of the Ministry on the issue of the setting up
 of a National Tourism Promotion Board for better coordination with all agencies/ stakeholders and expand its scope towards
 inter-sectoral convergence of resources and policy integration and desires that the whole issue be re-examined and
 Committee be informed of the outcome thereof with reasons for conclusions.
     (Para 63)
SHORTAGE OF HOTEL ACCOMMODATION
            The Committee observes that the Ministry of Tourism has assessed the requirement of hotel rooms in Delhi and NCR
 in the wake of the Commonwealth Games 2010 and taken a series of steps and incentives to augment the
 accommodation/infrastructure in this region. The Ministry of Finance has also declared a Tax Holiday for 5 years for setting
 up new hotels in Delhi and NCR region w.e.f. 1
st April 2007. The Committee also notes that the Ministry of Tourism has
 taken up the matter of creation of infrastructure with the State Governments and other land owning agencies like Ministries
 of Civil Aviation, Railway and DDA for creation of land banks, allocation of hotel sites on revenue sharing basis and
 granting extra FSI/FAR for hotels, permitting extra commercial usage in the hotels and a capital subsidy scheme as an
 incentive for accommodation infrastructure upto 3 star hotels, which have now yielding results. The Committee 
 recommends the Ministry to impress upon all the land owning agencies and the State Government for allotting land on
 annual rental basis for promotion of hotels in the country and Ministry of Finance be pursued for extending Tax Holiday
 scheme for construction of budget hotels under 3 star category all over the country so as to promote the tourism in a big
 way.

                                                    (Para 68)
RATIONALIZATIONS OF TAXES ON THE TOURISM INDUSTRY
            The Committee notes with concern that there exists multiplicity of taxes and the quantum of each levied by different
 States and Union Territories does affect the growth of tourist traffic to the country by making tourist packages more
 expensive as compared to other neighbouring countries. The Committee is also constrained to note that the luxury tax on
 hotels and VAT on food and beverages and on Airline Turbine Fuel (ATF) and high rate of taxation on tourist transport
 vehicles have deterring impact on the growth of tourism. The Committee notes that the issues related to very high and
 variable rate of luxury tax and taxation on transport sector have been referred to Empowered Committee of State Finance
 Ministers for looking into the implementation of VAT all over the country and Ministry of Tourism has mooted a proposal
 regarding introduction of Zonal Permits for a minimum of 3 States in order to rationalize the tax on tourist transport and to
 facilitate smooth movement of inter-state travel. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to pursue the matter
 of rationalization of taxes on the tourism industry more vigorously with the Ministry of Finance for suitable
 recommendation so that the taxes are rationalized appropriately to promote the tourism sector in the country in a big
 way.                               (Para 71)
INTRODUCTION OF VISA ON ARRIVAL SCHEME
            The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism has mooted a proposal regarding introduction of visa on arrival
 and electronic visa scheme for selected countries but due to the prevailing global security scenario and present security
 concerns in the country, the same was not agreed to by the Ministry of Home Affairs. The Committee agrees to the fact that
 security reasons are the prime concern and cannot be compromised but at the same time it also feels that granting visa-on-
arrival to the foreign tourists at airports will certainly boost the global tourist arrivals in the country. The Committee is of the
 opinion that the scheme should first be introduced with certain friendly countries on experimental basis and then it can be
 extended to other countries as well. The Committee recommends the Ministry of Tourism to further pursue the matter with
 the Ministry of Home Affairs and Ministry of Civil Aviation for desired results.
                                      (Para 76)
SPECIAL TOURISM POLICE
            The Committee notes with concerns that incidents of cheating, fleecing criminal acts and harassment of foreign
 tourists bring a bad name to country and have an adverse impact on the foreign tourists arrival. The Committee feels that
 the issue related with safety and security is vital in creating goodwill and the Union Ministry of Tourism cannot wash its
 hand off stating that policing is a State subject. The Committee is of the view that the role of media, travel agents and tour
 operators is paramount in establishing goodwill even in the critical situation. The Committee welcomes the steps taken by
 Government of Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Rajasthan,
 Uttar Pradesh and Delhi for deployment of Tourism Police for ensuring safety and security of the tourists. The Committee
 recommends the Ministry to take initiatives in other States as well for deployment of special Tourist Police so as to address
 the safety and security related issues properly.
                                    (Para 81)
DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM IN ANDAMAN & NICOBAR AND LAKSHADWEEP ISLANDS
            The Committee notes that as per its recommendations the Ministry of Environment and Forests has reduced the No
 Development Zone to 50 mtrs. instead  of 200 mtrs. in Andaman and Nicobar islands.  The proposals for relaxation in No
 Development Zone for Lakshadweep islands upto 5mtrs. is under finalisation. The Committee feels that this move will
 definitely facilitate the development of world class tourism infrastructure in the islands and coastal areas besides attracting
 high value and low volumes tourists traffic in these islands.
                                                                                              (Para 90)
            The Committee notes that the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep islands have the potential of growing
 as unique tourist destinations for Eco-Tourism and Island Tourism but due to variety of limitations particularly the
 inadequate connectivity, the tourism in these islands has not grown as required both in the scale and quality.  At Andaman
 the tourists have to largely depend on the infrastructure at Port Blair whereas the facility created at the other islands remains
 unutilized for want of poor inter-islands connectivity. The Committee is constrained to note that inspite of the limited
 resources, the Administration of these islands have not sent any proposals to the Ministry of Tourism for release of the
 central financial assistance for development of the infrastructure as well the connectivity. It clearly shows the lackadaisical
 attitude of the Administrations of these islands. The Committee, therefore, urges the Administrations of these islands to
 prepare concrete proposals complete in all respect for release of central financial assistance under different schemes of the
 Ministry of Tourism for promotion and development of infrastructure as well as connectivity. In this way they could

18
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/119threport.htm[22-10-2018 12:24:10]
 encourage the growth of hotel accommodation and mitigate connectivity problems between different islands. The
 Committee recommends the Department of Tourism  to take up the matter with Administration of these islands for sending
 the proposals so as to create conducive environment of investment to seek larger flow of funds from private sector
 participation in developing the necessary infrastructure and accommodation and then privately manage it through a
 transparent process.                             (Para 92)
The Committee notes that in order to revive the domestic tourists traffic, the Ministry of Tourism has requested the
 DoPT for extension of LTC facility by air for the central government employees for a further period of 2 years beyond
 24.11.2005. The Committee recommends the Ministry to pursue the matter for extension of LTC facility in the Andaman
 and Nicobar Islands for the central government employees with the DoPT and Ministry of Tourism should introduce
 attractive LTC packages for these employees visiting Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
