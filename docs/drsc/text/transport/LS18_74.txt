4.         The Committee appreciates that except for a few slippages, the Golden Quadrilateral programme is largely on

CENTRAL ROAD FUND (CRF)
(Recommendation at Para 30 and 32)
 
8.         The Committee in its 59
th Report on Demands for Grants 2002-03 of the Ministry of Road Transport and
 Highways had commented upon the savings under Central Road Fund during 2001-02 and recommended that proper
 monitoring for utilisation of funds be ensured.  The Committee appreciates that there has been a major improvement in
 the rate of utilisation of Central Road Fund by the States/UTs as compared to the earlier years.  The Committee notes
 with satisfaction that the funds allocated for maintenance and development of National Highways to the States/UTs was
 expected to be fully utilised by them during 2002-03.  The Committee recommends that there should be no let up in the
 measures adopted for the complete utilisation of the funds by the States/UTs and the said trend should continue in the
 future also.
 
9.         The Committee is of the view that since the allocation out of the CRF for the State Highways is done by the
 Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, it becomes the responsibility of the Ministry to ensure proper utilisation of
 the funds.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should develop
 some mechanism to ensure that the funds released out of the CRF for State Highways are utilised for that purpose only.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
10.       The States/UTs are required to furnish the progress report and Utilization Certificate in the format prescribed
 vide the Ministry’s guidelines dated 13th July, 2001.  Furnishing of Utilization  Certificate authenticated by an officer of
 the rank of Under Secretary or above of the State/UT and progress report giving the physical and financial progress of
 the CRF works as per the Ministry’s guidelines mentioned above is mandatory for consideration of further release of
 funds under CRF.  Thus it is ensured that the funds released out of the CRF for State Highways are utilized for that
 purpose only.

FEE/TOLL TAX ON BRIDGES AND NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
(Recommendation at Para 42)
 
15.       The Committee is not convinced with the reasons advanced by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for
 collecting toll on roads and bridges in perpetuity.  The Committee feels that the Government is not justified in
 burdening the common people with the development as well as the maintenance of the roads in the country.  The
 Committee had also commented upon this matter in its 43
rd Report on Demands for Grants 2000-2001 of the then
 Ministry of Surface Transport.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that once the cost of construction of a
 road/bridge plus interest is recovered, the collection of toll should be stopped.   The Committee also feels that there
 exists a need for the rationalisation of the toll tax being charged on the National Highways.  The Committee
 recommends that persons travelling on smaller stretches of the National Highways where toll is being collected should
 be charged a lower amount than the persons using the entire length of that National Highway.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
16.       The policy of levying users fee on roads and bridges was adopted to defray the cost of construction and
 maintenance due to budgetary resource constraints.  Levying of users fee is based on the principle of upgradation of the
 facility resulting in reduced vehicle cost, reduced travel time and enhanced safety.  Development and maintenance of
 highways being a continuous process it was decided to levy fee in perpetuity to have constant flow of fund.  The total
 amount of bridge fee as collected is spent in the State from which it is levied.  Thus, this fee is utilized for improving the
 National Highways in the State itself.  In view of the above the Committee may kindly reconsider their

17.       The Users’ fee on National Highways is being collected on “Open Tolling” system.  It means the tolled portion is
 not access controlled.  The users’ fee is charged on the basis of the distance between two tollgates.  The location of
 tollgates is decided on the basis of convenience of traffic, location of major access roads, travel pattern and cost of toll
 collection.  In the ‘Open Tolling’ system it is difficult to implement differential rates of users’ fees.  The users’ fee on
 the basis of distance traveled can be charged in the “Closed Tolling” system where the accesses are controlled.  This is
 not yet feasible in the country.  In view of the above the Committee may kindly reconsider their recommendation.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
18.       The Committee does not agree with the reply of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for collecting toll
 on roads and bridges in perpetuity.  Development and maintenance of highways is no doubt a continuous process but the
 Government cannot absolve itself of its responsibilities in this respect by shifting its entire burden on the common
 people.  In order to help the Government overcome the budgetary financial constraints, the toll should be collected till
 such time the cost of construction of a road/bridge plus interest is recovered.  The Committee, therefore, reiterates its
 earlier recommendation that once the cost of construction of a road/bridge plus interest is recovered, the collection of
 toll should be stopped.
 
19.       The Committee  takes note of the reply of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways with respect to the
 Committee’s recommendation regarding charging of users fee on the basis of distance travelled.  The Committee feels
 that in the present system of tolling, persons traveling in smaller stretches of the National Highways where toll is being
 collected are in a disadvantageous position.  Such a situation cannot be allowed to go on forever.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should initiate the steps to develop a system
 whereby the users’ fee could be charged on the basis of the distance travelled.
 
PORT CONNECTIVITY PROJECTS
(Recommendation at Para 46)
 
20.       The Committee feels that connectivity of the ports to the National Highways is of great importance from the
 point of view of commercial and industrial production.  The Committee is surprised to note that Mumbai Port Trust has
 not been included in the port connectivity projects.  The Committee has been informed that there is no proper road link
 to Mumbai Port and the only available road route to the port is in a poor condition.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should also include the Mumbai Port in their port
 connectivity project.   For this purpose, they can also approach the Mumbai Port Trust for their assistance in the project.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
21.       Major ports are to be connected under Port Connectivity Programme as mandated to NHAI.  Feasibility for
 connection of Mumbai Port was examined and it was observed that there was hardly any scope for the improvement of
 approach road to the Mumbai Port as it passed through heavily populated area of Mumbai city.  The only option was to
 construct a new elevated road which would have to pass through long stretch of salt pans and marshy land.  Mumbai
 Port Trust also advised that unless the matters were sorted out, further detailed study should not be carried out.  In view
 of the fact that construction of such a long section of elevated road will be extremely costly and such a huge investment
 might not justify the benefit which will accrue and this would also involve a large extent of land acquisition and
 environmental issued, it was decided not to take up this stretch at this stage.  However, on the advise of the Standing
 Committee the matter is being taken up once again with the Mumbai Port Trust.

 by the Government besides highway design to check the road accidents are as under:

ROAD TRANSPORT
(Recommendation at Para 58)
 
32.       The Committee feels that with the development and improvement in riding quality of the roads, danger to the
 lives of the villagers and cattle has also increased on those roads.  Inspite of the construction of underpasses at the
 traditional crossing points, the incidents of accidents on such roads has been rising.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that while undertaking the development of the roads, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should

 survey for assessment of users convenience should also be conducted in this regard.  
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
33.       The four/six laning of National Highway Project considers the provision of under passes and over bridges at
 congested and populated areas for the safety of the habitants in the adjoining areas.  The four/six laning project is
 prepared on the basis of detailed engineering.  The requirement of under passes/over bridges is assessed on the basis of
 number of population and vehicles.  Location of these structures is decided on the basis of engineering requirements and
 also the most preferred point(s) of crossing.  These factors are considered while preparation of the detailed project report
 of the improvement works.  Hence it would be seen that the recommendation of the Hon’ble Standing Committee is
 being followed.

PROBLEM OF OVERLOADING
(Recommendation at Para 65)
 
35.       The Committee is of the view that the problem of overloading of vehicles has been growing steadily inspite of
 the measures adopted by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.  The Committee feels that overloading of
 vehicles has not only been causing early destruction of several road stretches but also resulting in accidents leading to
 loss of life and property.  The Committee is constrained to note that certain state governments have been regularising
 overloading for financial gains.  The Committee appreciates the States of Karnataka, West Bengal, Meghalaya, Uttar
 Pradesh, Tripura and Gujarat for offering tax rebate to popularise the use of multi axle vehicles.  The Committee
 recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should further take up the matter with the concerned state
 governments so as to check the problem of overloading. Also the legislation proposed for this purpose should be
 introduced at the earliest.  The Committee also reiterates the recommendations made in its 59
th Report that the Ministry
 of Road Transport & Highways should come up with publicity efforts, on large scale, addressing to the truck drivers and
 the agencies engaged in transportation business, including general public.  Themes dealing with dangers of overloading
 should form an important part of the publicity campaigns undertaken in this regard.  Also awareness campaigns should
 be launched including advertisements in print/electronic media. 
 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
36.       Motor Vehicles Act has strict provisions for tackling overloading.  Enforcement of these provisions is
 responsibility of the State Government. The Transport Development Council in its 30th meeting on 16.1.2003 had
 discussed this matter and it was agreed that:
 
a)      Strict enforcement of the provisions relating to overloading under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and Central
 Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989.
b)      The State Governments are not to issue special cards/passes which legalize overloading.
c)      The decision of the 28th meeting of TDC held on 10.10.1998 for states to give a 25% tax rebate to popularize
 use of multi axle vehicles, which has been implemented so far by a few States like Karnataka, West Bengal,
 Meghalaya, Uttar Pradesh, Tripura and Gujarat, may be carried out by other States also.
d)         Keeping in view a number of court cases, the registering authority shall ensure that the vehicles are registered
 only as per the load ceilings notified and in case erroneously a higher load rating has been incorporated, then
 the same shall be got rectified.
e)      Non-renewal of registration and denial of permit to habitual offenders of overloading.
 
Subsequently, this matter has been continuously taken up with the State Governments.
 
37.            MOS(RT&H) and Secretary(RT&H) have written a number of letters to the State Governments pointing out
 that the practice of issuing special cards/ token, which have the effect of legitimizing overloading should be stopped and
 that the Central Government would be constrained to stop the releases from the Central Road Fund to the State
 Governments.   MOS(RT&H) has written to Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh on 3rd April, 2003 and subsequently to
 Chief Ministers of Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Orissa and Haryana on 19.4.2003.  Similarly,
 Secretary(RT&H) has written to all states on 4th March, 2003.  Subsequently, Secretary(RT&H) has again addressed the
 Chief Secretaries of several states, namely Madhya Pradesh & Rajasthan(21.3.2003), Uttar Pradesh, Orissa & Gujarat
 (7.4.2003) and again to Rajasthan, Orissa & Uttar Pradesh (17.4.2003) and Haryana and Uttar Pradesh (on 20.4.2003
 and 29.4.2003 respectively).
 
38.       The Committee’s suggestion regarding making appropriate amendments in the Motor Vehicles Act to tackle
 overloading in an effective manner has been noted.    In fact, a proposal recommending amendment of sec. 194 & 200 of
 the M.V. Act removing the powers of State Governments to compound the offence of overloading has been sent to M/o
 Law for vetting as a part of the comprehensive proposal for amendment of M.V. Act.

COMPUTERISATION OF ROAD TRANSPORT SECTOR
(Recommendation at Para 70)
 
41.       The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways in respect of
 computerization of Road Transport Sector.  However, the success achieved in the computerization programme leaves
 much to the desired.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways should
 impress upon the States/UTs for expediting both front-end and back-end computerization programme of the road
 transport sectors.  The Ministry should also explore the possibility of providing financial assistance/incentives to the
 States for completing the computerization programme at the earliest.  This would not only help in putting a curb on the
 tampering of licenses by unscrupulous elements but would also ensure the existence of uniform and foolproof system
 for driving licenses by the various states. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
42.       The matter has been pursued with the State Governments. It has been learnt that the Ministry of Information
 Technology has launched a scheme to assist development of at least one pilot site in every State. The Ministry has made
 available free of cost, the standardized software for the front end and back end operation of the smart card based Driving
 License and Registration Certificate.  There is no scheme under this Ministry to provide further financial assistance to
 the States/UTs under the Scheme.  However, it has been observed that wherever computerization of RTOs has been
 taken up, revenue gain has been to the tune of 20-40%.  Secretary(RT&H) has written to various Chief Secretaries of the
 States/UTs to provide finance for these programmes as this would pay back the cost quite quickly.  Government agrees
 with the observation of the committee that implementation of the computerization programme by states would help in
 bringing about a uniform & foolproof system for driving licenses.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
43.       The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.
 
PERFORMANCE BUDGET
(Recommendation at Para 71)
 
44.       A perusal of the Performance Budget 2003-04 of the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways indicates that the
 Performance Budget lacks the information regarding targets/achievements about the specific schemes undertaken by the
 Ministry during the previous year.  In the absence of the above details, the Committee finds it difficult to assess the
 progress of the projects.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Performance Budget should comprise of
 information indicating the targets fixed with specific parameters, actual physical achievements and slippages alongwith
 the reasons for the same.  The physical/financial achievements should be clearly indicated together with the reasons for
 shortfall, if any, in respect of the projects so that the Committee can make proper assessment of the projects executed by
 the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.
