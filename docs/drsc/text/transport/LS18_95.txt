APPROACH IN THE 10
TH
 PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2005-2006

(Recommendation at Para No.9)
5.
The Committee notes that the budgetary allocation has always been less than the actual requirement and to adjust the shortfall, the allocation has to be subsequently increased.  The Committee is unable to comprehend the
reasons for keeping lower outlays when the actual requirement is far higher.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that every effort should be taken to make realistic estimation of allocation in subsequent plans keeping
in view all the anticipated demand for development of the tourism sector as a whole.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
6.         The Ministry of Tourism has always projected its budgetary requirements based on the actual needs and proposed for revision at the time of formulation of Revised Estimates, if any additional requirement is felt
necessary.  However, the Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance are the final authorities for the allocations of funds in the Annual Plans of the Ministry and they have allocated less Budgetary support than the
actual requirements and projected plans.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
7.The Committee is aware of the fact that the Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission are the final authorities for the allocation of funds in the Annual Plans of the Ministry.  However, the Committee recommends
that the attention of the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance may be invited to the observations made by Committee for  allocation of Budgetary support as per actual requirement and projected plans.  Their
response may be submitted to the Committee for its consideration.
(Recommendation at Para No.13)
 
8.         
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism has been allocated a higher amount to the tune of Rs.786.00 crore in the B.E. 2005-06 which is almost 57 per cent over and above the approved outlay for the year
2004-05.  However, the Committee expresses its concern over the under utilisations of funds by the Ministry during the year 2004-05 and notes that the Ministry could spend only 79 per cent of the allocation i.e. Rs.395.1
crore out of the budgeted allocation of Rs.500 crore upto February 2005.  The Committee also notes that larger part of the budgeted amount was spent at the fag end of the financial year, especially, in the months of
February and March, which is much against the financial norms.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the programmes and policies should be implemented in a manner, which ensures regular infusion of
funds in various projects/schemes uniformly in all quarters of the financial year.  The Committee expects that the Ministry of Tourism will be in a better position to suitably revise the plan outlays in future. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
9.      The Ministry of Tourism during the year 2004-05 has utilized its 92.30% out of the total budgetary allocation of Rs.503.50 crore.  The Ministry of Tourism has spent Rs.347.95 crore i.e. 69.11% of its total budget
by 31.12.2004.  The reasons for the underutilization during the year 2004-05 were due to late restoration of the cut of Rs.100 crore in B.E. 2004-05 of the Ministry of Tourism by the Ministry of Finance, and non-
approval of re-appropriation proposals.  However, the suggestions of the Committee will be kept in view while formulating its future Annual Plans.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
10.       
The Committee notes the reply furnished by the ministry.
(Recommendation at Para Nos. 18, 19, 23 & 24)
 
11.    The Committee notes that with an aim to improve the existing destinations and circuits and to develop new destinations and tourists circuits of world class standard, the Ministry of Tourism has merged the existing
two scheme of Integrated Development of Tourist Circuit and Product/Infrastructure and destination development into one single scheme as Product/Infrastructure Development of Destinations and Circuits.  However,
the Committee is surprised to note that the tourism potential and uniqueness of some of the important States like Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Tamilnadu, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh and  Uttranchal for convergence of
resources and development of Circuits and Destinations of international importance has not yet been fully explored by the Ministry.  The Committee recommends that more Circuits and Destinations  region-wise should
be identified in consultation with the State Governments and developed in an integrated and holistic manner for the purpose of convergence of resources and world-class benchmarking, the related Ministries and
Department of State Governments should have sub-plans in their annual plans so that tourism related infrastructure projects receive the highest priority.
 
12.    The Committee also recommends that purposeful domestic and international publicity and marketing support should also be provided to all important tourist Circuits and Destinations so as to create interest of the
tourists in the Indian tourism products besides promoting India as a major tourists destination.
 
13.    The Committee notes with serious concern that the Ministry of Tourism sanctioned 2526 numbers of small isolated projects spread throughout the length and breadth of the country during 8
th
 and 9
th
 plan period,
out of which, 708 projects remained still incomplete.  The Committee feels that delay in implementation of projects not only led to slow progress and cost overrun but at the same time, deprive the tourists of the desired
facilities.  The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism will converge all the resources required for development of tourists infrastructure instead of spreading it very thinly in small isolated projects.  The Committee
recommends that to meet the developmental objectives and convergence of resource, the State and Central Government should work in synergetic manner and provide best facilities at the most economical price besides
addressing the issues of civic governance, management of sites and services, ensuring safety and security and hassle free comfortable stay for the tourists at these projects sites.
 
14.    The Committee also recommends the Ministry to adopt a proactive approach and take care of the hindrances resulting in delay and put in place some institutional mechanism to achieve better coordination, effective
monitoring and timely implementation of these pending projects.                    
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT

sites/spots identified by the State Governments/Union Territories.  Development of tourist sites is an ongoing process and based on the proposals received from the State Governments/UTs the Ministry of Tourism is
continuously assisting the State Govts/UTs in this process to create world class tourist infrastructure in the country, equipped with best facilities, services, etc. required by the tourists.
 
16.       The Ministry has been promoting and marketing the newly developed/created destinations/circuits sites through its publicity support material and marketing campaigns with a view to create interest among the

HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
(Recommendation at Para No.36)
 
23.       The Committee notes that there are 24 Institutes of Hotel Management and 6 Food Crafts Institutes catering to the requirements of tourism and other industries for trained manpower.  The Committee is of the view
that with the vast expansion and modernisation of the hospitality sector, the Hotel Management and catering education programme gained tremendous popularity and there is great demand for trained manpower not only
in the field of hospitality sector but also it spanned to other sectors like Hospitals, Call Centres, Banks etc.  The Committee observes that as number of hotels/restaurants are going to increase in the future, availability of
trained manpower is a must to meet the growing demand for excellence in the tourism sector.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to set up Institutes of Hotel Management in each State for meeting shortage of
trained manpower in the tourism and other sectors.  The Committee further recommends that in view of the mushrooming of the number of such institutions mostly in the private sector which does not have desired
training level and infrastructure facilities, a statutory status to the National Council for Tourism & Hospitality Education should be granted to better regulate and networking of all resources for institutions in the tourism
and hospitality sectors.

PROVIDING A CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS OF TOURISM
(Recommendation at Para No.39)
 
28.       The Committee notes that a proposal to include Tourism in the concurrent list has been mooted by the Ministry for giving Constitutional recognition to tourism and development of tourism in a systematic manner.

�
s tourism products will grow only
when the suitable legislative and regulatory support is provided.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry of Tourism to build a strong national consensus for creating awareness about the role of tourism and
harness its benefits as an instrument of economic development and employment catalyst besides removing the hurdles for its growth and every
effort should be taken by the Ministry to include tourism in the Concurrent List of the Constitution.

UNIFORMITY AND RATIONALISATION OF TAXES ON TRANSPORT AND HOSPITALITY SECTOR
(Recommendation at Para No.43)
 
31.       The Committee notes that the major barrier to the growth of tourism in the country is a multitude of Central and State level taxes.  The Committee is of the opinion that uniform taxation is very necessary for
promotion of tourism and recommends the Ministry of Tourism to make consistent effort for building a nationwide campaign on rationalization of taxation issues both in transport and hotel sectors.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
32.       The Ministry of Tourism is aware that one of the fundamental problems plaguing the Indian Tourism sector is a multitude of Central and State Taxes, which make India as an expensive destination as compared to
other competing tourist destinations.  This issue was taken up for discussion during the State Tourism Ministers Conference held in New Delhi on 4
th
 and 5
th
 March, 2005.  Simultaneously the matter has been referred to
the Inter-State Council and on their advice, detailed views of the State Governments and Union Territories have been sought.  The matters like reduction in service tax on tour operators and excise duty on Aviation Turbine
Fuel are being taken up with the Ministry of Finance.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
33.       The Committee appreciates the efforts being made by the Government to rationalize the tax structure concerning the different departments of tourism sector.  However, it suggests them to strive to evolve consensus
on the subject. 
DOMESTIC TOURISM AND PUBLICITY
(Recommendation at Para Nos. 48 & 49)
34.       The Committee notes that during the year 2003, around 6 million Indians have visited abroad against a total incoming flow of 2.73 million foreign tourists, and more and more countries are queuing up to attract the
Indian tourists, which has led to foreign exchange outflow.  The Committee observes that by diverting Indian tourists going abroad to Indian destinations, massive potential for domestic tourism can be explored and this
requires focussed approach for its growth.  The Committee is of the view that the growth of global tourism depends upon the domestic tourism and it is easier to target the domestic tourism market as compared to the
international market.  The Committee feels that a relative change in the marketing strategy is required for tapping the domestic and international tourism markets and recommends that special emphasis must be laid for
accessing the market by consolidation of efforts to arrest the increasing trend of outbound tourists.
 
35.       The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry for launching a multi-pronged promotional campaign in all key markets across the world under the brand name of 
�
Incredible India
�
.  The Committee hopes
that the campaign will continue to focus on the diverse and varied tourism products of the country and will be able to create awareness about varied attractions amongst the travelers.  The Committee notes that the outlay
on publicity for 2005-06 has been increased to Rs.70 crore which is almost five times as compared to that of last financial year.  The Committee hopes that with the increased outlay, the Ministry of Tourism will be able to
make effective publicity to promote domestic tourism.  However, the Committee recommends the Ministry to chalk-out a concrete plan for targeting domestic middle class tourists.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
36.       The concern expressed by the Committee has been noted.  Efforts are being made during the current year with the increased budgets to tap the domestic tourism market.  A Marketing strategy is being
worked out to consolidate efforts and arrest the increasing trend of outbound tourists.
37.       It may, however, be pointed out that the cost of domestic travel is much higher than cost of travel to neighbouring countries.  To counter the effects of Tsunami on Andaman & Nicobar Islands special efforts
are being made to promote tourism to Andamans through publicity and advertisement.  The Ministry of Tourism website has brought out special packages offered by the trade on its site to attract visitors to
Andaman.  In addition, during the current year the following promotional material is being produced: -

Ministry of Transport and others besides the concerned State Governments.  The Incredible India campaign is also proposed to be continued in the key markets across the world focussing on new themes. 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
39.       The Committee notes the extraneous factors hindering the growth of tourism in the country as detailed by the Ministry.  The Committee feels that the Incredible India campaign aims at promoting India as a global
tourist destination and there is no specific slogan or initiative to boost domestic tourism. Therefore, the Committee recommends that a concrete plan be chalked out for domestic tourists taking into account their problems
and affordability.
OVERSEAS PROMOTION AND MARKETING
(Recommendation at Para Nos. 53 & 54)
40.       The Committee is unhappy to note that there is a heavy shortfall in the utilization of funds for Overseas Promotion and Marketing. The Committee also notes that the Ministry has not given any reasons for under
utilization of funds. The Committee observes that while revising allocation little consideration has been paid to the actual expenditure incurred in the past. The Committee hopes that the Ministry will be able to utilize the
funds allocated during 2005-06 optimally.
 
41.       The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism has reoriented itself to the overseas market with a view to changing global resources and fierce competition in the tourism generating market and the allocation has
substantially been increased during 2005-06. The Committee expects that number of Asian and particularly Chinese Tourists is expected to grow enormously as the Asia-Pacific region becomes more integrated with the
global economy and recommends the Ministry to develop linkages to tap tourists from these countries especially Japan, Korea and China and create basic infrastructure to facilitate the global tourists arrivals from these
countries.

NATIONAL TOURISM PROMOTION BOARD
(Recommendation at Para No. 58)
46.       The Committee is unhappy to note the appalling state of co-ordination in the tourism sector.  The Committee feels that to synergies the tourism potential in the country, coordination is needed as several Ministries
are involved apart from State Governments, other agencies and Private Sector. The Committee desires the Ministry to create a framework, which facilitate and take everybody on board.  The Committee recommends the

Ministry to reconsider the matter to create National Tourism Promotion Board for coordination with all these agencies in terms of policy decision and in the area of execution as recommended by the Committee in its 79
th
Report on Demands for Grants (2004-05) of the Ministry of Tourism.
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
47.       The issue of setting up of a National Tourism Board has been engaging the attention of the Ministry for quite some time but no final decision could be taken. In the Indian scenario, with clear allocation of
business/subjects amongst various Ministries/Departments of Government of India; and also division of responsibility between Centre and States, the issue of decisions of the 
�
Board
�
 being binding upon other Central
Ministries/Departments, and the State Governments is being examined. The Board has to be envisaged as an autonomous institution with members from Government and other stakeholders in the tourism sector with
administrative and financial powers. We are examining as to how the problems concerning other Ministries/Departments, as also States, like the following will be solved:
(i)
                  
Matters concerning M/o Civil Aviation (Adding air seat capacity, reduction in air fares, etc);
 
(ii)
                
Matters concerning M/o Home Affairs (Visa issues, restricted area permit, immigration problems, etc);
 
(iii)
               
Matters concerning M/o Urban Development (Land use policies of State Governments for setting up of hotels, etc.);
 
(iv)
              
Matters concerning M/o Commerce, D/o Revenue (Import of raw material/equipment for hotels, Custom problems, etc.);
 
(v)
                
Matters concerning M/o Culture (Entry Fee to Monuments, maintenance of monuments, putting monuments to alternative use, etc.); and
 
(vi)
              
Matters concerning Taxes (Luxury tax on hotels, taxes on transport vehicles, sales tax on food & beverages, sales tax on ATF, etc., Multiple channels for clearance of tourism projects, safety/security of tourists,
etc.)
