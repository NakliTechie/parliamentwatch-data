INTRODUCTION 
 
 I, the Chairman, Department -related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, 
Tourism and Culture, having been author ised by the Committee, have the honour to present on 
its behalf this Report on the Demands for Grants (2026 -27) of the Ministry of Road Transport 
and Highways (Demand No.86). The Report has been prepared following a structured scrutiny 
of the Ministry's bu dgetary proposals for the financial year 2026 -27, in accordance with the 
mandate vested in the Committee under Rule 270 and related provisions of the Rules of 
Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council of States. 
 
2. The Committee has examined the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in view of the 
central and catalytic role that Road Transport and Highway Infrastructure occupies in India's 
economic architecture. With a National Highway network exceeding 1,46,000 km and an 
annual capital outlay of Rs.  2,94,167.45 crore proposed for 2026 -27, the Ministry of Road 
Transport and Highways administers one of the largest infrastructure programmes in the world. 
The quality, pace and efficiency of its expenditure has a direct bearing on logistics costs, 
industrial competitiveness, agricultural market access, regional integration and the living 
standards of communities traversed by the national highway network. 
 
3. The Committee is of the considered view that the future trajectory of India's highway 
programme will be determined not merely by the quantum of capital mobilised but by the 
rigour with which it is planned, targeted and executed. The transition from a phase of rapid 
network expansion to one that prioritises quality assurance, long -term asset preserva tion, 
logistical efficiency and road safety represents a maturing of infrastructure policy. The 
Committee's scrutiny has accordingly been directed not only at the adequacy of financial 
allocations, but at the alignment between budgetary intent and ground -level outcomes with 
particular attention to recurring patterns of under -utilization in maintenance and research, the 
pace of completion of ongoing projects and the equity of regional distribution of infrastructure 
investment. 
4. The present Demands for Gran ts reflect several significant policy transitions. The 
introduction of Performance Based Maintenance Contracts (PBMC) as a distinct capital head, 
the closure of new project awards under Bharatmala Pariyojana in favour of need -based 
development under Nation al Highways (Original), the impending transition to cashless tolling 
through GNSS and ANPR technologies from April 2026 and the notification of the Cashless 
Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025 are among the substantive shifts that have 
informed the Committee's examination. The Committee has sought to assess whether these 
policy innovations are accompanied by the institutional capacity, budgetary provisioning and 
monitoring frameworks necessary for effective implementation. 
 
5. During the course of its examination, the Committee held oral evidence sessions with 
the Secretary and senior officials of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways. The 
Committee scrutinized the Detailed Demands for Grants (Demand No.86), the Annual Report 
(2025-26), the Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF) 2026-27, the Salient Features 
of the Budget 2026 -27 and the Statement of Budget Estimates along with accompanying 
Remarks and Explanatory Notes furnished by the Ministry. The Committee also received 
written respo nses to a detailed questionnaire covering financial performance, scheme -wise 

iii 
 
progress and policy implementation. The Committee places on record its appreciation for the 
comprehensive documentation and oral submissions provided by the Ministry during this 
examination. 
 
6. The Committee's examination encompassed five principal thematic areas : the overall 
financial structure of the Demands and its alignment with stated policy objectives; scheme -
wise scrutiny of major programmes including National Highways (Ori ginal), NHAI 
investment, Bharatmala Pariyojana, SARDP -NE, Externally Aided Projects, Parvatmala 
Pariyojana and Multi-Modal Logistics Parks, a review of past demand versus fulfilment across 
three financial years, cross -cutting tests of budget realism, regio nal equity, efficiency of 
outcome linkage and institutional governance and a focused assessment of transport sector 
reforms including vehicle scrapping, the BH Series registration, Advanced Driver Assistance 
Systems and digital enforcement. The Committee's  observations and recommendations across 
these areas are detailed in this Report. 
 
7. This Report synthesizes evidence received from the Ministry, the detailed budgetary 
documentation and the Committee's own analysis of multi -year financial trends. It places fiscal 
prudence, outcome accountability and equitable regional development at the centre of its 
assessment. The Committee's recommendations are framed in a spirit of constructive 
engagement and are intended to support the Ministry in strengthening the p lanning, execution 
and monitoring of its programmes. The Committee expects that the Ministry will give careful 
and sympathetic consideration to these recommendations and that appropriate action taken 
notes will be communicated to the Committee in a timely manner. 
 
8. The Committee places on record its sincere appreciation of the assistance rendered by 
the officers of the Rajya Sabha Secretariat in facilitating the examination of the Demands for 
Grants and of the cooperation extended by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and 
the National Highways Authority of India in providing detailed documentation, responses to 
the Committee's questionnaire and oral submissions during the evidence sessions. The 
diligence of the Ministry's officials in placing comp rehensive data before the Committee has 
materially contributed to the depth of this examination. 
 
9. The Committee considered and adopted this Report at its meeting held on 23 rd March, 
2026.

through Ring Roads and Bypasses; enhanced connectivity to ports and industri al nodes to 
facilitate trade; and a heightened emphasis on road safety and user convenience.

1.9 The Committee notes with concern the persistent pattern wherein initial Budget 
Estimates for maintenance are set at levels that subsequently require drastic correction. In 
2024-25, the Ministry projected a requirement of Rs. 2,500 crore (BE), revised u pward 
by 86.4% to Rs. 4,660 crore (RE). In 2025 -26, the BE was set at Rs. 4,555.00 crore, RE 
was increased to Rs. 4,895.02 crore, yet actual expenditure up to January 2026 stood at 
Rs. 3,744.31 crore, leaving approximately 18% to be utilized in the final two months. The 
Committee is of the considered view that such fluctuations indicate that maintenance 
budgeting is reactive rather than grounded in a scientific assessment of asset 

5 
 
deterioration. Relying on Supplementary Demands for routine maintenance disru pts the 
planning of systematic repair cycles. 
1.10 During oral evidence, the Ministry stated that budget utilisation has been 
consistently above 99% in recent years, with 90% utilisation expected in the current year 
with Rs. 870 crore pending. The Committe e observes that while the aggregate capital 
head shows high utilisation, this figure masks significant sub -head level variations: 
Highway Research at 80.59% under -utilization in 2025 -26; MMLP at under 20% 
utilisation; and Parvatmala at 53% utilisation. The  Committee is of the view that 
aggregate utilisation figures, while broadly positive, should not mask specific areas of 
persistent under-absorption. 
1.11 The Committee further observes that the maintenance expenditure figure of Rs. 
5,000 crore under the Re venue head, when read alongside the Rs. 1,000 crore PBMC 
provision, totals Rs. 6,000 crore. However, the Ministry during oral evidence stated that 
maintenance expenditure stands at Rs. 10,000 crore for 47,000 km, projected to increase 
to Rs. 11,000-12,000 crore in the following year. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry place before the Committee a detailed reconciliation clarifying the 
composition of the total maintenance expenditure figure, distinguishing between the 
Revenue head provision, the Capi tal PBMC provision and any NHAI maintenance 
expenditure routed through separate heads, so that the Committee may assess the 
total maintenance effort on the National Highway network in a comprehensive and 
transparent manner. 
1.12 The Committee recommends th at the Ministry move towards a 'Zero -Based 
Maintenance Budgeting' framework, wherein annual budget projections are 
grounded in empirical network condition data. The Ministry may conduct a 
structured Network Condition Survey using Network Survey Vehicles as  a standard 
precursor to each budget cycle, with the resulting International Roughness Index 
(IRI) data informing the quantum of the maintenance demand in a transparent and 
verifiable manner. 
C. Highway Research: Fund Utilisation 
1.13 The Committee has not ed with attention the persistent pattern of under -utilisation in 
the Highway Research head. In 2025 -26, the Ministry sought a BE of Rs. 219.00 crore. 
At the RE stage, this was reduced by 45.6% to Rs. 119.00 crore. Actual expenditure up to 
January 2026 was Rs. 42.50 crore, representing an under-utilisation of 80.59% against the 
original BE. The Ministry submitted that it does not have an R&D organisation of its own 
and relies on external institutions such as IITs and CSIR, with proposals requiring 
modification and amplification before finalisation. 

6 
 
1.14 The Committee is of the view that the inability to spend even the reduced RE 
allocation reflects a capacity constraint within the Ministry to vet and guide research 
proposals. The repeated surrender of R&D fun ds hinders the adoption of cost -effective 
and sustainable technologies in the highway sector. The Committee recommends that 
the Ministry establish a standing R&D Facilitation Cell comprising domain experts 
from the highway engineering sector, to serve as a  bridge between practical field 
requirements and proposals from institutions such as IITs and CSIR, with the 
objective of strengthening the quality and readiness of research submissions and 
improving the pace of utilisation of the Research budget. 
D. Treasury Single Account Implementation 
1.15 In 2023 -24, actual expenditure under the NH(O) head was Rs. 72,191.98 crore 
against a Budget Estimate of Rs. 73,113.08 crore. The Ministry attributed the saving of 
Rs. 87.64 crore to the operationalisation of the TSA system, citing that the existence of 
only one Central Nodal Agency for a scheme utilised by multiple sub -agencies caused a 
lack of coordination and choked fund flows. While the Ministry has since allowed 
multiple CNAs, the Committee notes that administrati ve reforms must be pilot -tested to 
prevent disruption to ongoing infrastructure projects. The Committee recommends that 
the Ministry codify a Standard Operating Procedure for Digital Payments under the 
TSA regime, with structured capacity-building workshops for the finance personnel 
of sub -agencies, to ensure that TSA objectives are achieved without inadvertent 
disruption to fund flows. 
E. Vendor Invoice Submission 
1.16 In 2023-24, a saving of Rs. 58.22 crore occurred under the Maintenance head due to 
non-submission of bills by vendors in a timely manner. The Committee observes that this 
is not merely a vendor compliance issue but a matter of contract management, where the 
absence of strict digital billing discipline allows vendors to delay submissions, dist orting 
the Ministry's quarterly expenditure planning. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry incorporate a structured Billing Cycle Provision in future contract 
templates, providing a defined window from milestone completion within which 
vendors are ex pected to submit invoices, complemented by a Bill Tracking Module 
on the Data Lake portal for greater visibility of outstanding claims. 
F. Expenditure Bunching 
1.17 The Committee analysed expenditure trends for 2025 -26 based on actuals up to 
January 31, 2026. The data points to a significant accumulation of expenditure in the last 
quarter (Q4). For the Tribal Sub -Plan, Rs. 3,978.42 crore representing 25.8% of the 
Revised Estimate remained unspent. For NH(O) Works, Rs. 13,293.41 crore (20.2% of 
RE) remained unspent. The Maintenance head had Rs. 810.69 crore (17.8% of RE) 

7 
 
pending. Of particular note, the Multi -Modal Logistics Parks head had 81.2% of its 
allocation still unspent as of January 31, 2026. 
1.18 The Committee apprehends that the accumulation of near ly one -quarter of the 
capital budget for expenditure in the final two months creates a risk of hasty spending 
that may compromise due diligence in bill verification and quality control. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry institute a quarterly expen diture 
discipline framework with mandatory reporting of sub -head wise utilisation to the 
Committee at the end of Q2 and Q3, so that end -of-year expenditure bunching is 
identified and addressed in advance. 
G. Centre-State Fund Utilisation 
1.19 The Committee notes consecutive savings of Rs. 38.13 crore in 2023 -24 and 2024 -
25 under the head for Roads in UTs, with the remarks stating 'No demand from NCT of 
Delhi.' The Committee views the lapse of funds allocated for the national capital's road 
infrastructure du e to absence of demand as a matter warranting closer attention in the 
context of Centre-State coordination. 
1.20 During oral evidence, the Ministry informed the Committee that state road 
development through CRIF has an allocation of Rs. 10,400 crore for th e next year, with 
Rs. 7,600 crore of Rs. 10,000 crore already utilised in the current year. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry engage proactively with the NCT of Delhi and other 
Union Territory administrations that have recorded zero or negligible demand, to 
identify the specific administrative impediments preventing the absorption of 
allocated funds and to institute corrective measures.

CHAPTER II: MAJOR POLICY SHIFTS 
A. Transition to Performance Based Maintenance 
2.1 The Committee notes that a new Capital head for Performance Based Maintenance 
Contracts (PBMC) has been opened with an outlay of Rs. 1,000 crore. The Ministry plans 
to bring the balance length of the National Highway network under maintenance 
contracts, aiming for 100% coverage. During oral evidence, the Ministry stated that 
47,000 km is sanctioned for maintenance at Rs. 10,000 crore with 6,000 km pending 
sanction, and that maintenance expenditure is projected to increase to Rs. 11,000 -12,000 
crore in the following year. 
2.2 While the Committee welcomes this structural shift to long -term asset preservation, it 
considers it important to ensure that the transition from Short Term Maintenance 
Contracts is managed in an orderly manner and that the reclassifi cation of heavy 
maintenance from Revenue to Capital does not obscure the true maintenance position. 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry place before Parliament a considered 
Transition Plan mapping the phased migration from Short Term Maintenance 
Contracts to the PBMC model, and that the Service Level Agreements embedded in 
PBMC contracts be subject to systematic monitoring through the Data Lake portal. 
B. Rationalisation of Bharatmala Pariyojana 
2.3 The Ministry informed the Committee that no further  projects are being taken up 
under the Bharatmala Pariyojana umbrella, with future development prioritised under 
National Highway (Original) works based on traffic requirements and project -based 
appraisals. This indicates a move towards a flexible, need -based planning approach. 
Against a Phase -I target (including residual NHDP) of 34,800 km, 26,425 km has been 
approved and awarded with 21,785 km completed as of December 31, 2025. 
C. Digitalisation of Enforcement and Services 
2.4 The implementation of the 'O ne Nation, One Challan' initiative and integration of 
eChallan with ITMS across 33 States/UTs represents a move from manual intervention to 
technology-driven compliance. The Ministry informed the Committee during oral 
evidence that cash toll collection wil l be discontinued from April 2026 in favour of UPI 
and FASTag, that 18 toll plazas have been converted to barrier-free operation, and that all 
4-lane and above toll plazas will transition to barrier -free. An annual pass scheme of 200 
trips at Rs. 15 per tr ip has been introduced, with 54 lakh people having used the scheme 
and 1 lakh passes issued. 
 
 

9 
 
D. Quality Improvement and Contractor Accountability 
2.5 The Ministry informed the Committee during oral evidence that a comprehensive 
quality reform framework h as been introduced, comprising: a rating system for DPR 
consultants, contractors and supervision consultants; fixed -cost contracting for DPR with 
30% weightage for quality rating; additional performance security and penalty 
provisions; tightened eligibilit y criteria; authority-based experience certificates replacing 
private company certifications; automated roughness index testing through Network 
Survey Devices for over 1 lakh lane kilometres; drone -based monthly progress 
monitoring in pilot stage; mandatory automated intelligent compaction machinery for 675 
km of greenfield projects; mandatory precast for non -critical items; and mobile quality 
control vans deployed in pilot across four states with planned expansion to 12 additional 
states. 
2.6 The Ministry further stated that stringent criteria for project commencement have 
been introduced: 90% Right of Way and resolution of all claims are required before the 
appointed date. Projects delayed more than 3 years have been reduced from 152 (April 
2024) to 107 an d are projected to reach 71 in the current year. Projects unable to start 
after award have been reduced from 197 (April 2025) to 37. 
E. Dispute Resolution and Contractual Reform 
2.7 The Ministry is moving towards replacing arbitration with conciliation and 
introducing no -litigation incentives in Model Concession Agreements. New model 
concession agreements for BOT and HAM have been finalised. The Committee notes 
these reforms as a positive development for reviving private sector confidence. 
F. Asset Monetisation 
2.8 The Committee observes that approximately 16% of NHAI's capital envelope (Rs. 
30,000 crore) for 2026 -27 is targeted to be raised from Asset Monetisation. During oral 
evidence, the Ministry informed the Committee that cumulatively over Rs. 1.52 lakh 
crore has been raised through monetisation efforts as of November 2025: Rs. 58,000 
crore through TOT by 2025 -26 and Rs. 43,600 crore through InvIT, with a target of Rs. 
15,000 crore for the current year. EPFO and public sector pension funds invested Rs. 
3,000 crore in InvIT and a new public InvIT launch is planned at Rs. 10,000 per unit with 
average returns of 15-18% per annum. 
2.9 While the cumulative proceeds validate the mode l, linking core capital expenditure to 
market-dependent instruments introduces an element of funding volatility. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry explore the creation of a Monetisation 
Reserve Fund mechanism, wherein proceeds from asset monetisat ion realised in 

10 
 
excess of annual targets during favourable market conditions are set aside, 
providing a buffer in years where market sentiment is less conducive.

CHAPTER III: SCHEME-WISE ANALYSIS 
A. National Highway (Original) — NH(O) 
3.1 The National Highway (Original) scheme serves as the Ministry's primary instrument 
for development and expansion of the National Highway network. Unlike project-specific 
umbrellas, NH(O) is flexible, prioritising works based on traffic requirements, in ter-se 
priority and road condition. The scheme covers network capacity augmentation, 
widening, strengthening, critical infrastructure such as missing links and bypasses, and 
rehabilitation of bridges and culverts. The proposed BE for 2026 -27 is Rs. 1,02,50 4.97 
crore, incorporating the Tribal Sub -Plan of Rs. 10,000 crore and the North East 
component of Rs. 17,558 crore. 
3.2 In 2024 -25, the allocation saw a substantial mid -year revision from a BE of Rs. 
78,349.00 crore to an RE of Rs. 99,824.58 crore, with ac tual expenditure of Rs. 
99,791.35 crore, indicating normalisation post -TSA stabilisation. The Committee notes 
from oral evidence that 10,000 km of paved shoulder development has been planned, of 
which 8,000-9,000 km has been sanctioned. 
3.2A The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Road Transport and 
Highways may consider factoring an "Impact -First" assessment approach in 
addition to the conventional "kilometres built" metric. Under this approach, capital

measurable improvements in road safety indices, reduction in average travel time 
on treated corridors, improvement in the Logistics Performance Index for 
connected districts, and verifiable enhancement in last -mile connectiv ity to 
economic nodes. The Committee notes that India's logistics cost has been assessed at

measurable citizen-level benefits beyond aggregate construction statistics. 
B. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) Investment

construction of 27,597 km of National Highways with a total capital cost of Rs. 7.72 lakh

approximately 13,228 km correlates directly with the high requirement for Capital GBS.

C. Bharatmala Pariyojana

stretches, which represent the most challenging terrain in the North East, receive 
focused financial attention under a unified programmatic framework. The 
Committee notes that SARDP -NE is in its tapering phase and the institutional

CHAPTER V: ROAD SAFETY AND ENGINEERING 
A. Unquantified Safety Outcomes 
5.1 The Committee notes with grave concern that India reported approximately 4.73 lakh 
road accidents and 1.70 lakh  fatalities in 2024, with over -speeding accounting for 68.4 
per cent of accidents in the most recent published data. National Highways, despite 
constituting approximately 2 per cent of the total road network, account for a 
disproportionate share of fatalit ies, with over 52,600 deaths recorded on National 
Highways in 2024 alone. Against this backdrop, the Committee notes that the targets for 
'Reduction in road accidents' and 'Reduction in fatalities' are listed as unquantified 
placeholders (' -%') in the OOMF  for 2026 -27. The road safety programme has been 
allocated Rs. 400 crore, and the OOMF targets removal of 1,000 black spots. The 
Committee is of the view that without specific, quantifiable reduction targets, the efficacy 
of the road safety programme canno t be objectively assessed. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry adopt specific, measurable annual reduction targets 
for road accidents and fatalities within the Outcome Budget framework, so that 
outcome-linked accountability is established and the ef fectiveness of road safety 
expenditure can be assessed against pre-declared benchmarks. 
B. Black Spot Rectification 
5.2 The Committee notes from oral evidence that 16,540 black spots have been identified 
from 2016-2023 data. Short-term measures (traffic calming, signages, lighting) have been 
completed for 14,138 black spots. Long -term measures (design improvements, flyovers, 
underpasses) have been taken for 6,650 black spots. The Electronic Detail Accident 
Record System (EDAR) is now providing near real -time accident data for black spot 
identification. 
5.3 The Committee observes that there is no data to verify whether previously treated 
black spots remain accident -free. The OOMF target of removing 1,000 black spots does 
not include a post -rectification verification mechanism. The Committee recommends a 
structured Post-Rectification Safety Audit for treated black spots, and that locations 
where accident rates have not reduced to an acceptable threshold be reclassified for 
higher-order engineering solutions suc h as grade separation or geometric 
realignment. 
C. Over-Speeding Enforcement 
5.4 The Committee notes from the Annual Report that 68.4 per cent of road accidents in 
2023 were attributable to over -speeding, a figure that has remained persistently high 
across successive years. Spot -speed cameras have known behavioural limitations as 
drivers decelerate only near camera locations. The Committee recommends 

19 
 
progressive deployment of Section Control (average -speed camera) systems on 
Access-Controlled High -Speed Corridors as a complement to spot -speed 
infrastructure, and that vehicle -to-vehicle communication technology, for which 30 
MHz frequency has been allocated by the Department of Telecommunications, be 
prioritised for high-accident corridors such as the Yamuna Expressway. 
D. School Zone Safety 
5.5 The Committee notes that School Zone Road Safety was a key theme identified in the 
National Brainstorming Seminar on Road Safety. Highway stretches passing through 
school zones are particularly hazardous during peak hours due to the absence of traffic 
calming infrastructure. The Committee recom mends that Safe School Zone 
provisions, including traffic calming measures, speed tables, rumble strips and 
signage, be incorporated as a standard design requirement for National Highway 
stretches passing through inhabited areas where schools are in proxim ity, and that 
such provisions be in place before a completion certificate is issued. 
E. Ambulance Availability and Road Safety Concerns from Oral Evidence 
5.6 During oral evidence, the Committee raised specific concerns regarding: ambulance 
availability an d response time for accident victims on highways; safety concerns 
regarding sleeper buses due to limited reaction time in accidents; and encroachment on 
National Highways, identified as a major concern by the Committee. 
5.7 The Committee notes the Ministry 's response that the PM Rahat scheme now 
provides the statutory framework for cashless treatment and that the ADAS mandate from 
October 2027 for new vehicle models and January 2028 for existing models will 
strengthen vehicle -level safety. ADAS features inc lude Driver Drowsiness Alert, Lane 
Departure Warning, Emergency Braking System and Electronic Stability Control. Bus 
body certification has been made mandatory, and e -rickshaw standards have been 
mandated in two phases. 
5.8 The Committee recommends that the Ministry engage with IRDAI to explore the 
feasibility of insurance premium incentives for ADAS -equipped vehicles as a 
market-based complement to the regulatory mandate, encouraging early voluntary 
adoption particularly among fleet operators for whom the upfront cost may be a 
consideration. 
F. District Road Safety Committees 
5.9 During oral evidence, the Committee raised the concern that two separate district road 
safety committees exist, one headed by the District Magistrate and one involving 
Members of P arliament, leading to institutional confusion and duplication. The Ministry 
committed to examine and clarify this overlap. The Committee recommends that the 

20 
 
two district road safety committees be merged into a single body with a clearly 
defined composition and mandate, and that the Ministry communicate the revised 
structure to States. 
G. National Highway Safety Patrol 
 
5.10 The Committee notes that India recorded approximately 4.73 lakh road 
accidents and 1.70 lakh fatalities in 2024 as per the Transport Research Wing of the 
Ministry, with National Highways alone accounting for over 52,600 fatalities. The 
absence of a continuous on -ground presence on National Highways for accident 
response, traffic discipline and as set protection remains a critical institutional gap. 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry examine the feasibility of 
establishing a dedicated National Highway Safety Patrol, drawing upon successful 
institutional models such as the Railway Protection Force which has demonstrated 
effectiveness in asset protection and passenger safety across the railway network. 
Such a patrol, even if established initially on a pilot basis on high -accident corridors 
and expressways, would strengthen real -time accident re sponse during the critical 
golden hour, enhance enforcement of speed discipline and lane discipline on access -
controlled corridors, deter encroachment and damage to highway assets, and 
complement the digital enforcement infrastructure being deployed under the 
Intelligent Traffic Management System. The Committee is of the view that the 
present dependence on State Police forces, whose jurisdiction and priorities extend 
well beyond highway safety, is insufficient for the scale of the National Highway 
network, and a dedicated institutional mechanism warrants serious consideration.

A. Vahan-Insurance Data Integration 
7.1 The Committee notes that the Vahan/Sarathi database holds over 41 crore vehicle

identify uninsured vehicles. The Committee recommends a Real -Time API

B. Standardisation of Enforcement Devices 
7.2 The Committee notes that 25 states have implemented ITMS. However, the legal 
admissibility of e-Challans generated by these systems is contingent upon the calib ration 
accuracy of speed cameras and sensors, which currently lacks a nationally notified 
standard. The Committee recommends notification of a National Calibration

complemented by a periodic independent calibration audit framework. 
C. Public Grievance Analytics 
7.3 The Committee notes that the Ministry received over 46,000 public grievances in 
2025. While individual resolution rates may be high, the sheer volume suggests systemic 
issues at the project or contractor level not addressed by individual case resolutio n. The 
Committee recommends deployment of analytical tools, including AI -based pattern 
recognition, on the Ministry's public grievance database to identify concentrations 
of complaints relating to particular highway stretches or contracting agencies, 
serving as a trigger for targeted engineering or management review. 
D. Green Highway Accountability 
7.4 The Committee notes the Ministry's emphasis on Green Highways. Plantation along 
National Highways is a significant ecological commitment, but monitoring the survival

CHAPTER IX: SUMMARY OF RECOMMENDATIONS 
The follow ing is a consolidated summary of the recommendations made by the 
Committee in the preceding chapters. For full context, the relevant chapter and paragraph 
references are indicated. 
Recommendation 1 (Para 1.11) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry place 
before the Committee a detailed reconciliation clarifying the composition of the total 
maintenance expenditure on the National Highway network, distinguishing between the 
Revenue head provision (Rs. 5,000 crore), the Capital PBMC provision (Rs. 1,000 crore) 
and any NHAI maintenance expenditure routed through separate heads. The discrepancy 
between the Rs. 6,000 crore reflected in the Demands for Grants and the Rs. 10,000 crore 
figure stated during oral evid ence must be explained so that the Committee may assess 
the total maintenance effort in a comprehensive and transparent manner. 
Recommendation 2 (Para 1.12) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry move 
towards a 'Zero -Based Maintenance Budgeting' framew ork, wherein annual budget 
projections are grounded in empirical network condition data rather than incremental 
precedents. The Ministry may conduct a structured Network Condition Survey using 
Network Survey Vehicles (NSVs) as a standard precursor to each budget cycle, with the 
resulting International Roughness Index (IRI) data informing the quantum of the 
maintenance demand in a transparent and verifiable manner. The persistent pattern of 
reactive mid-year corrections, including the 86.4% upward revision i n 2024-25 and the 
subsequent 6.95% reduction in 2025-26, underscores the necessity of this reform. 
Recommendation 3(Para 1.14): The Committee recommends that the Ministry establish 
a standing R&D Facilitation Cell comprising domain experts and experienced 
practitioners drawn from the highway engineering sector. Such a cell would serve as an 
enabling bridge between the Ministry's practical field requirements and the research 
proposals emanating from institutions such as IITs and CSIR, with the objective of 
strengthening the quality and readiness of research submissions and thereby improving 
the pace of utilisation of the Research budget. The Committee notes that in 2025 -26, 
actual expenditure was Rs. 42.50 crore against a Budget Estimate of Rs. 219 crore, 
representing an under -utilisation of 80.59%, which the Ministry itself attributed to the 
need for modification and amplification of proposals from external institutions. 
Recommendation 4(Para 1.15): The Committee recommends that the Ministry codify a 
Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Digital Payments under the Treasury Single 
Account (TSA) regime, so that the procedural expectations placed on sub -agencies are 
clearly documented and consistently understood. Structured capacity-building workshops 
for the finance personnel of concerned sub -agencies, including State PWDs, NHAI and 
NHIDCL, would be a worthwhile investment to ensure that the public finance reform 
objectives of the TSA are achieved wit hout inadvertent disruption to the flow of funds to 

28 
 
active construction projects, as occurred in 2023 -24 resulting in a saving of Rs. 87.64

Recommendation 5 (Para 1.16) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry 
incorporate a structured Billing Cycl e Provision in future contract templates, providing a 
defined window from milestone completion within which vendors are expected to submit 
invoices. Complementing this with a Bill Tracking Module on the Data Lake portal 
would lend greater visibility to out standing claims, enabling the Ministry to anticipate 
expenditure patterns with greater accuracy and reducing the scope for unplanned savings 
arising from administrative delays in the billing chain, such as the Rs. 58.22 crore saving 
in 2023-24 attributed to delayed vendor bill submission. 
Recommendation 6(Para 1.18): The Committee recommends that the Ministry institute 
a quarterly expenditure discipline framework with mandatory reporting of sub -head wise 
utilisation to the Committee at the end of Q2 and Q3,  so that end -of-year expenditure 
bunching is identified and addressed in advance. The Committee notes that as of January

81.2% of the MMLP allocation remained unspent, conce ntrating substantial volumes of 
expenditure in the final two months and creating risks to quality control and bill 
verification. 
Recommendation 7(Para 1.20): The Committee recommends that the Ministry engage 
proactively with the NCT of Delhi and other Unio n Territory administrations that have 
recorded zero or negligible demand in consecutive years, to identify the specific 
administrative impediments preventing the absorption of allocated funds and to institute 
corrective measures. The consecutive lapse of R s. 38.13 crore in 2023 -24 and 2024 -25 
under the head for Roads in UTs, attributed to 'No demand from NCT of Delhi,' 
represents a lost opportunity for infrastructure upgradation in the national capital. 
Recommendation 8 (Para 2.2) : The Committee recommends t hat the Ministry place 
before Parliament a considered Transition Plan mapping the phased migration of legacy 
stretches currently under Short Term Maintenance Contracts (STMC) to the Performance 
Based Maintenance Contract (PBMC) model. The Service Level Agr eements embedded 
in PBMC contracts must be subject to systematic monitoring through the Data Lake 
portal, so that the quality assurance objectives of this structural shift are demonstrably 
achieved. The Committee further recommends that the reclassificatio n of heavy 
maintenance from Revenue to Capital expenditure be accompanied by transparent 
reporting, ensuring that it does not artificially inflate capital expenditure while 
diminishing the visibility of the revenue-side maintenance position. 
Recommendation 9(Para 2.9) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry explore 
the creation of a Monetisation Reserve Fund mechanism, wherein proceeds from asset 
monetisation realised in excess of annual targets during periods of favourable market

16% of NHAI's capital envelope (Rs. 30,000 crore) for 2026 -27 is contingent upon

validate the model, annual targets remain subject to market conditions. 
Recommendation 10 (Para 3.4) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry place 
before the Committee a WIP Liquidation Plan for NHAI's active construction pipeline,

at risk of cost escalation due to delays and specify the financial implications of Interest 
During Construction (IDC) for eac h such stretch. The Committee is concerned that the 
widening gap between awarded and completed lengths may balloon IDC and overall 
project costs if not addressed through a structured liquidation strategy. 
Recommendation 11(Para 3.6): The Committee recommen ds that the Ministry engage 
with the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways to establish a joint Port Connectivity 
Mission, with a senior Nodal Officer designated to coordinate the remaining port 
connectivity roads under Bharatmala. Port Connectivity Ro ads stand at only 45%

should be specifically mandated to facilitate the resolution of C oastal Regulation Zone 
(CRZ) clearances and land acquisition matters that are impeding progress. 
Recommendation 12 (Para 3.8) : The Committee recommends a structured Review of 
Long-Pending Projects in the North East, specifically identifying stretches that h ave 
remained at a standstill for extended periods under the SARDP -NE programme. Where 
the complexity of terrain or security considerations renders conventional contracting 
impractical, the Ministry may consider, in consultation with relevant authorities, w hether

a more effective approach. The Committee notes that while SARDP -NE has achieved 
91% physical completion (5,864 km of 6,418 km), the remaining stretches represent t he

Recommendation 13 (Para 3.11) : The Committee recommends that the Ministry 
expedite the formulation and notification of comprehensive National Standards for 
Ropeway Safety and Operations, which would provide the regulatory certainty that this

complementing conventional land acquisition with Land Pooling Models developed in 
close partnership with State Governments, as a means of accelerating the development of 
Multi-Modal Logistics Parks. The Committee further recommends that rail connectivity 
approvals be secured as a pre -condition to the award of MMLP contracts, to reduce the 
risk of mid-project coordination issues with other infrastructure agencies. The Committee 
notes that of 35 MMLP locations identified, only 5 have been awarded, and financial 
utilisation in 2025-26 was under 20% of the Rs. 400 crore allocation. 
Recommendation 15 (Para 3.16) : The Committee  recommends that the PM Rahat 
(Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims) scheme be assigned quantified outreach

objectively assessed. The Committee further recommends that a Live Public Dashboard 
reflecting real -time geographic coverage of empanelled hospitals be created and 
integrated with the existing 108 ambulance network, so that fi rst responders are in a 
position to direct accident victims to empanelled facilities within the critical golden hour. 
The Committee notes that while the scheme has been launched with 35 of 36 states 
onboarded, the OOMF currently lists the target for victim s who availed treatment as

achievement, incorporating a realistic growth traject ory rather than repeating aspirational 
figures that are not supported by demonstrated execution capacity. The credibility of the 
Outcome Budget framework rests on targets that are both ambitious and demonstrably 
achievable. 
Recommendation 17(Para 4.5): The Committee recommends that the Ministry explore 
according the Frontier Highway (NH -913) in Arunachal Pradesh (1,840 km) a distinct

materially contribute to th e momentum of this project, which is intended to arrest

impediments in each case, whether land acquisition, forest clearance, contractor default  
or inter-agency coordination, and the proposed remedial measures. During oral evidence, 
the Committee raised specific concerns regarding long -delayed projects including the 
Gandak river bridge, NH -66, and projects on the Bangalore -Tumkur and Goa -Karnataka 
border corridors. The Ministry itself acknowledged that 80% of current issues relate to 
old legacy projects. A systematic review would enable the Committee to monitor 
progress on a periodic basis. 
Recommendation 19 (Para 5.1) : The Committee recommends that  the Ministry adopt 
specific, measurable annual reduction targets for road accidents and fatalities within the 
Outcome Budget framework. The Committee notes that the targets for 'Reduction in road

400 crore with a target of removing 1,000 black spots. Outcome -linked accountability, 
wherein the effectiveness of road safety expendi ture is assessed against pre -declared and 
verifiable reduction benchmarks, would considerably strengthen the governance of the 
road safety programme and lend greater credibility to the Ministry's commitment to 
reducing the human cost of road accidents. 
Recommendation 20 (Para 5.3) : The Committee recommends a structured Post -
Rectification Safety Audit for treated black spots, conducted at a suitable interval after 
treatment, to assess whether the engineering interventions deployed have produced a 
sustained improvement in safety outcomes at each location. Where the audit reveals that 
accident rates have not reduced to an acceptable threshold, the Ministry may consider 
reclassifying the location and exploring higher -order engineering solutions such as grade 
separation or geometric realignment. The Committee notes that while 14,138 black spots 
have received short-term treatment and 6,650 have received long -term treatment, there is 
no verification mechanism to confirm whether rectified spots remain accident-free. 
Recommendation 21 (Para 5.4) : The Committee recommends the progressive 
deployment of Section Control (average -speed camera) systems on Access -Controlled 
High-Speed Corridors, in addition to or as a complement to existing spot -speed

be a practical step towards aligning highway design standards with the pedestrian safety 
needs of the communities through which these roads pass, particularly during peak school 
hours when such stretches are most hazardous. 
Recommendation 23(Para 5.8): The Committee recommends that the Ministry engage

noting that the existence of two parallel committees creates institutional confusion and 
dilutes accountability for road safety outcomes at the district level. The Ministry 
committed during oral evidence to examine this overlap, and the Committee expects this 
matter to be resolved and the revised structure communicated to all States. 
Recommendation 25(Para 6.3): The Committee recommends that the Ministry explore 
making the Certificate of Deposit (CD) a fully tradeable and digital instrument, which

AITP rules, and give full effect to the intent of the permit without requiring additional 
physical infrastructure.

the Faceless Protocol to commercial transport services, particularly to processes such as 
Inter-State Permit Variations and route permissions, which continue to require human 
intermediation and create scope for discretionary delays. The Ministry may identify the

FASTag platform, contributing to both road safety and financial accountability without 
requiring additional enforcement personnel. The current isolation of the Vahan database 
from insurance records means that enforcement agencies cannot automatically identify 
uninsured vehicles, which constitutes a public safety and financial liability concern.

public grievance database, which received over 46,000 grievances in 2025. Where such

contracting agency, the Ministry may consider this as a trigger for a targeted engineering 
or management review, thereby allowing the grievance mechanism to serve not only as a 
redressal channel but also as an early warning system for underlying quality or 
governance concerns that are not apparent from individual case resolution alone. 
Recommendation 32 (Para 7.4): The Committee recommends the introduction of geo -

rates and ensuring that the e cological commitments of the Green Highway programme 
translate into durable, measurable outcomes on the ground. The Committee notes that 
monitoring the survival rate of saplings has historically been a weak area, with instances 
of replanting dead trees before inspection.

Dig Once Policy across the National Highway network, to preserve the integrity of newly

standard highway cross -section design, and encouraging all utility agencies to 
concurrently lay their infrastructure during the construction phase i tself, would be a 
forward-looking approach that the Ministry may formalise through appropriate 
guidelines. The Committee notes that the Ministry is already piloting Optical Fibre Cable 
infrastructure along 1,900 km of expressways, providing a basis for wider adoption. 
Recommendation 34(Para 8.1): The Committee recommends that the Ministry examine 
the feasibility of establishing a minimal regional field presence on the transport side, at 
least at the zonal level, to reduce the current complete dependence on States for 
enforcement, regulatory compliance and monitoring functions. The Ministry's own 
admission during oral evidence that it has no field presence on the transport side and is 
entirely dependent on States exposes a significant institutional constraint . A zonal 
presence, even if modest, would enable the Ministry to exercise direct oversight over 
critical regulatory functions and improve the quality of ground -level data available for 
policy decisions.

responsible or compensating the State Government. The Ministry committed to examine

alignment and design stages, so that ground -level knowledge is incorporated before 
project finalisation. Improved DPR quality has a direct bearing on project cost, timeline, 
quality outcomes and the prevention of mid -project design changes that cause delays and 
cost escalation. The Ministry's introduction of fixed -cost contracting for DPR with 30% 
quality weightage is a welcome step, and the workshop mechanism would complement 
this reform. 
Recommendation 38(Para 8.5): The Committee recommends th at the Ministry develop 
a systematic anti -encroachment protocol for National Highways, with specific focus on 
newly constructed bypasses and access -controlled corridors where encroachment in the 
early stages of operation undermines the intended traffic man agement and safety design. 
Access control measures should be incorporated at the design stage itself for all new 
highway projects. Encroachment on National Highways was identified as a major issue 
by the committee during oral evidence, and the Ministry com mitted to address 
encroachment removal on bypasses and to implement access control measures.

of logistics efficiency. The Committee notes that India's logistics cost stands at

trajectory.

institutional energy on liquidating the construction backlog and reduce the fiscal 
exposure arising from Interest During Construction. 
Recommendation 42 (Para 3.6A): The Committee recommends the constitution of a "Last 
Mile Task Force" to implement a "Reverse -Completion" protocol for the residual 13,015

identifying and resolving the specific land acquisition, legal or clearance impediments 
blocking the completion of the final segments that are pr eventing entire corridors from

Recommendation 43 (Para 3.8A): The Committee recommends that the Ministry examine

sensitive zones in the North East.
