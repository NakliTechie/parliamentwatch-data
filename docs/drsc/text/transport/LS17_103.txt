R E P O R T
Ours is one of the few nations in the world having rich Cultural heritage.  Its culture is deeply rooted in history and
 has a pluralistic ethos providing creative expression, value-sustenance and belief patterns to many communities, which
 constitute the contemporary Indian society.  Increasingly it is being realized that the role of culture lies not only in the
 context of promotion of economic growth but also in developing creativity in the artists thereby promoting them as a
 social force.  The role played by the Department of Culture as a nodal government agency is therefore very crucial for the
 whole country.
2.         The mandate of the Department of Culture is to preserve and promote all forms of art and culture.   The functional
 spectrum of the Department is rather wide, ranging from generating cultural awareness at the grassroots level to
 promoting cultural exchanges at an international level.  Along with programmes for the preservation of India’s ancient
 heritage, the activities of the Department encourage and disseminate a variety of contemporary creative arts as well. 
 Department’s aim is to develop ways and means through which the basic cultural and aesthetic sensibilities of the people
 remain active and dynamic.  The activities are carried out through to attached offices namely, i.e., Archaeological Survey
 of India and National Achieves of India;  six subordinate offices and 35 autonomous institutions under the Department.

 buildings of the attached/ subordinate offices of this Ministry, submits its bills at the fag end of the financial year adding
 up to increased expenditure in the last quarter of the financial year.   
8.         Keeping in view the general pattern of expenditure of the Ministry, the budget circular emphasizing the need to
 augment the expenditure from the very beginning of financial year are issued regularly. Further, instructions reiterating to
 avoid heavy expenditure during the last quarter/ last month of the financial year, phasing out of the expenditure on an
 even manner from the very first quarter of the financial year, and above all, to make a real assessment of the requirement
 of funds under each scheme/ organization before the onset of formulation of budgetary proposals are also issued to
 various Divisions/ Organizations of the Ministry. Adequate care has been taken so that no scheme/ organization suffers
 for want of funds.  The evaluation methods evolved by the Ministry to gauge the level of expenditure from time to time
 under each scheme/ organization and to augment the expenditure resulting in full utilizations of funds has been found to
 be successful.
9.         However, fixing of the RE ceiling by Ministry of Finance on the basis of the level of expenditure attained by this
 Ministry up to the month of September/ October which usually leads to reduction of substantial provision of the Budget,
 as compared to the funds provided under BE, jeopardizes the progress of many activities, especially under Plan. This
 leads to deferment of many developmental activities to the next financial year.
10.       The Secretary Department of Culture during the course of oral evidence deposed before the Committee :
“Sir, now I would like to point out some of the constraints that we are facing. Firstly, there is the problem
 of seasonality of the functioning of the ASI. The Budget discussion takes place in December. It is fixed at
 RE stage on the basis of expenditure up to the month of September. Therefore, the ASI can never show a
 very sizeable expenditure at that point of time because most of the expenditure of the ASI takes place after
 the monsoon. So, their BE is always reduced. This is one of the problems we are facing. Of course, last
 time, the relaxation was given by the Department of Expenditure. Similarly, in case of Grants in Aid, we
 are facing a problem. As far as purchase of books and other artefacts are concerned, we have expert
 committees, which meet after every second quarter. These committees recommend and approve purchase
 of books. In this area also, there is a problem.  …Sir, the ASI has to take up conservation work in a big
 way. Out of our total non-Plan Budget of Rs.350 crores, Rs.177 crores is the non-Plan Budget of the ASI.
 Almost fifty per cent of this Rs.177 crores is the budget amount used for conservation of monuments.
 Actually, conservation work has been put under the head 'non-Plan'. Therefore, there is no chance of any
 increase in the conservation budget as it is put under the non-Plan expenditure. It is our view that
 conservation work should come under Plan, not under non-Plan. If this is shifted to Plan, then, at least,
 there will be money for conservation work of the ASI. ”
11.       The Committee is of the view that fixing of the Revised Estimates ceiling by the Ministry of Finance on the basis
 of the level of expenditure attained upto the month of September/October creates problem for the Department of Culture.  
 The Committee observes that most of the cultural functions and festivals take place in the month of October and
 November, after the monsoon.  The Archaeological Survey of India also incurres major expenditure after the monsoon. 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government must review the budgeting procedure and evolve some
 mechanism to ensure that the Plan allocation for the Department of Culture is not reduced.  The Committee recommends

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
(a)        Release of the revenue collected as admission fees to Archaeological Survey of India :
12.       The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) was established in 1861.  It is an attached office of the Ministry of
 Culture.  Since its establishment, 144 years ago, the ASI has grown into a large organization having its network
 throughout the country.  At present, the ASI has declared 3663 monuments, which are of national importance in the
 country, including 17 properties inscribed on the World Heritage List by UNESCO.  The major activities of the ASI are
 maintenance, conservation and preservation of centrally protected monuments/sites and remains.  In addition to the above
 activities, it conducts archeological exploration and excavation regularly.  It also keeps developing epigraphical research,
 setting up and reorganization of site museums and training in archaeology.

14.       In response to query upon financial requirement and provision of budgetary allocation for managing of the affairs
 of ASI, the Secretary, Department of Culture during his presentation submitted before the Committee that :-
 
“The ASI gets quite a bit of foreign domestic tourists. They get an annual income of Rs.53 crores from that.
 But, unfortunately, the ASI, being a Government organisation, it goes into the Government coffers. This
 money is not really ploughed back to the ASI for preservation of any of the monuments. Out of 3667
 monuments, at present, we have 118 monuments which are ticketed. So, there is a tremendous potential for
 this. If this money could be somehow ploughed back for conservation and preservation of monuments,
 then, it would be helpful.”
15.       The Committee feels that execution of conservation and preservation works need an excellent expertise and timely
 funding of projects.  But with its meager resource, it is difficult for the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) to protect
 and maintain all the 3667 monuments/sites.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that 100 per cent of the revenue
 collected as entry fees from ticketed monuments should be released to ASI for stepping up the activities and to augment
 resources of ASI.   Coordinated efforts should also be taken by the Department to bring more monuments under central
 protection and also efforts should be made to raise the number of monuments for ticketing purpose to generate more
 revenue.  The Committee strongly feels that there exists a requirement for creation of a non lapsable fund out of the
 amount generated by the ticketing of monuments which can be used for preservation and development of monuments in a
 dedicated manner.

(c)        Conservation and preservation of monuments by the ASI:
19.       Conservation, preservation and maintenance of the centrally protected monuments and sites are the prime tasks of
 Archaeological Survey of India.  The total number of individual structures being maintained by the Archaeological
 Survey of India is over five thousand.  During 2005-06, more than 700 monuments were taken up for conservation and
 structural repairs, chemical preservation and environmental development besides day-to-day maintenance of 3667
 monuments.  Of these 172 monuments were taken for scientific preservation and 168 monuments were selected for
 environmental development in and around the site.
20.       The Committee wanted to know from the Department that whether the Archaeological Survey of India, the sole
 custodian of the national monuments, has decided to allow corporate participation in its conservation efforts and whether
 it is also a fact that the initial corporate participation would be for conservation of 5000 monuments under the ASI's direct
 protection.  The Department of Culture in their reply stated that the participation of the Corporate sector in the
 conservation of monuments is facilitated only through the National Culture Fund administered by Ministry of Culture.
 Whereas funding is allowed from the corporate sector, conservation works are carried out under the direct supervision of
 ASI.    As regards details of the ASI projects supported through NCF, there are private sponsoring agencies namely, Aga
 Khan Foundation and Oberoi Group of Hotels funding Humayun’s Tomb, New Delhi with Rs. 2.25 Crores; Apeejay
 Surendra Hotels funding Jantar Mantar with Rs. 10 lac; Indian Hotels Company Ltd. (Tata Group) funding Taj Mahal,
 Agra with Rs. 1.87 Crores.  Likewise, there is public sector participation from Pune Municipal Corporation, Pune funding
 Shaniwarsada, Pune with Rs. 34.18 Lac; Indian Oil Corporation signing MoU for Hampi (Karnataka), Konark (Orissa),
 Kanheri Caves (Maharashtra), Khajuraho Group of Monuments (Madhya Pradesh), Warangal Fort (Andhra Pradesh) and
 World Monument Fund funding Jaisalmer Fort with US $ 4, 39,000.  The Secretary, Department of Culture during the
 course of his oral evidence on 28
th April, 2006 also informed the Committee that the Steel Authority of India has adopted
 the Lodi Garden and Qutab Minar is getting fund from National Culture Fund.  The Department is also trying to get
 private sector investment for protection and maintenance of other monuments in the country. 
21.       The Committee is happy to note that more and more corporate houses are coming forward to help the ASI in
 conservation of monuments by way of contributions to National Culture Fund. The Committee recommends that the
 Department of Culture should continue its efforts to ensure more involvement of  private sector companies/firms.   But at
 the same time the Committee also cautions that private involvement should not lead to commercial use of the monuments
 putting their safety and sanctity at stake.

NATIONAL MUSEUM AND MUSEUM SECURITY
25.       The National Museum, New Delhi is a premier institution in the field of art and culture in India.  It is doing
 important work of acquisition, conservation, exhibition and educational activities.
26.       The Committee enquired about the measures, the Department of Culture has taken recently to strengthen the
 museum security, besides training of security personnel to operate on these security gadgets since the security of
 Museums is an important areas of concern in view of the prevailing security scenario in the country.  The Department of
 Culture informed the Committee that in view of the prevailing Security scenario of the country and also taking into
 consideration of the current level of deployment of CISF, the security has been reviewed and resurvey carried out jointly
 by CISF and representative of National Museum.  To revamp the security system 121 personnel have been recommended
 in the re-survey for which the report has been submitted to the Ministry of Culture for approval. The existing electronic
 gadgets have also been reviewed and upgradation plan of the Security Gadgets has been submitted and the follow up
 action regarding procurement/installation is underway.
27.       The Committee wanted to know about the reasons for variation in the budgetary provision made for the National
 Museum, as an amount of Rs.17 crore has been allocated in BE 2005-06 which was reduced to Rs. 5 crore in RE 2005-06
 under planned allocation.  Whereas, during the year 2006-07 an amount of Rs. 11 crore has been allocated for the same
 purpose.
28.       The Department of Culture explained that in the Budget of 2005-06 Rs.17.00 crores was earmarked to National
 Museum under Plan.  Out of this Rs.8.00 crore was kept for modernization of major museums in the metro cities to bring
 them upto international standards.   The money kept for the purpose could not be spent, as there was a major cut in the
 overall Budget of Ministry of Culture at RE stage.  The Budget of National Museum under Plan was further reduced, as
 some major international exhibitions planned during the year could not be organized due to non-finalisation of terms with
 the host countries.  Due to cut at RE stage the modernization project of major museums in the country had to be
 postponed.  During the year 2006-07, Rs.11.00 crores have been kept under plan because of major international
 exhibitions and upgradation of museum galleries and buildings to international standards.  During 2006-07 additional
 expenditure is also expected on enhancement of security arrangements in National Museum.
29.       The Committee agrees with the Department that Museum security is the major area of concern and therefore
 enhancement of security arrangements is very much required.  The Committee while recognizing the importance of
 security arrangements of National Museum also recommends that adequate steps should be taken to enhance the security
 of the State Museums also.  The Committee considers that the cut imposed by the Finance Ministry at RE stage is
 unreasonable due to which modernization project of major museums in the country had to be postponed and some major
 international exhibitions planed during the year could not be organised and recommends the Department  to take up the
 matter with the Finance Ministry to avoid such cuts in future.
