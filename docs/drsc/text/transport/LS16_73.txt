REPORT
           
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Government on the observations/recommendations as
 contained in its Sixty-sixth Report on Demands for Grants (2003-04) (Demand No. 91) of the Department of Tourism which
 was presented to the Parliament on 10th April 2003.
 
2.         Action Taken Replies have been received from the Government on 17 th July 2003 in respect of all the
 observations/recommendations contained in the Report.  These have been categorized as follows:-
 
(i)                  Recommendations/observations that have been accepted by the Government:
 
Para Nos. 15, 20, 24 & 29                                                                     Total - 4
 
(ii)                Recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the Government replies:
 
Para Nos. 34 & 45                                                                                Total – 2
 
(iii)               Recommendations/observations in respect of which replies of the Government have not been accepted by the
 Committee.
 
      Nil                                                                                                   Total - 0
 
(iv)              Recommendations/observations in respect of which final replies of the Government are still awaited:
 
Para Nos. 7, 9, 26, 40, 50, 51, 55, 56 & 59                                               Total - 9
 
3.         The Committee desires that final replies in respect of the recommendations, which have been categorized as
 interim replies by the Committee should be furnished to it at the earliest.
 
4.         On scrutiny of the said Action Taken Replies, the Committee has made observations on each item of the action
 taken/proposed to be taken by the Ministry.  The recommendations of the Committee contained in the Sixty-sixth Report, action
 taken thereon by the Government and recommendations/observations of the Committee on each item of action taken have been
 given in the succeeding paragraphs.
 
THE APPROACH IN THE 10 TH PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2003-04 (Recommendation at

5.         The Committee is happy to note that the plan allocation of Rs.225.00 crores of the Department of Tourism was not
 subjected to any reduction at R.E. stage during 2002-2003 and the outlay for the year 2003-2004 has also been hiked
 substantially from Rs.225.00 crores to Rs.325.00 crores.  The Committee hopes that the maximum amount of plan outlay would
 be utilised properly and substantially during the first five months of the current financial year so that the Department will be in
 a position to make demand for more allocation of funds at R.E. stage   for development of tourism infrastructure and promotion
 of tourism in the country.
 
6.         The Committee notes that for the financial year 2002-03, the expenditure upto January 2003 has been only 55% i.e.
 Rs.124.876 crores out of the total plan allocation of Rs.225 crores.  Though, the Department of Tourism have assured that the
 total allocation would be spent by the end of the financial year i.e. 31st March 2003 the Committee however, is unable to
 understand as to how the Department would be able to spend the remaining 45% of the plan allocation within a short span of
 just 2 months of the financial year.  The Committee therefore, disapproves the tendency of spending funds desperately towards
 the end of the financial year. The Committee recommends that the Department should evolve some mechanism of uniform
 utilization pattern through out the year so as to avoid incurring expenditure at the fag end of the financial year.
 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
7.         The Department of Tourism has formulated schemes of Integrated Development of Tourist Circuits,
 Product/Infrastructure and Destination Development and Assistance for Large Revenue Generating Products during the year
 2002-03, the first year of the Tenth Five Year Plan. Schemes were formulated in consultation with the Planning Commission. 
 Being restructured schemes SFC meetings were called for approval and release of funds.  It took time in finalisation of these
 schemes.  State Governments/UT Administrations were requested to submit detailed projects proposals for these schemes.  As
 the State Governments submitted the proposals after preparing detailed project reports, there was delay in release of funds to
 the State Governments.  But the Department of Tourism utilized the allocated funds of Rs.225 crores during the year 2002-03. 
 In fact the Department of Tourism sought additional grants of Rs.34.50 crores for completion of old projects.  Additional funds
 of Rs.25.00 crores were provided in March 2003 and these were fully utilized.  The recommendation of the Committee will be
 followed by the Department and efforts will be made for uniform utilization of funds during the year 2003-04. 
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
8.         The Committee notes that the Department of Tourism has formulated schemes of Integrated Development of
 Tourist Circuits, Product/Infrastructure and Destination Development and Assistance for Large Revenue Generating
 Products during the year 2002-03.  However, the Committee desires that State Governments may be given some
 reasonable time and  requested to submit project proposals for the schemes within that stipulated time limit only for
 avoiding delays in finalisation of project proposals.  The Committee also recommends that old pending projects must
 also be completed by the Department of Tourism and if need be, additional grants may also be provided.
 
INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF TOURIST CIRCUITS
(Recommendation at para 15)
 
9.         The Committee is happy to note that Department of Tourism is taking a holistic approach for development of these six
 tourist circuits in the country on each year and providing necessary infrastructural facilities within these circuits and
 convergence of resources in coordination with States/Private sector.  The Committee also notes that Department of Tourism has
 decided that in future funds will not be provided to small isolated projects, which are not of much importance for the
 development of tourism in the country and concentrate on the development of important travel circuits alone.  The Committee
 recommends that more tourist circuits region wise covering more than a State/UT may be selected and developed on the basis
 of the tourism potential of the places according to international standards and aggressive marketing efforts may be taken up in
 order to attract more and more tourists to these places.

RURAL TOURISM
(Recommendation at para 24)
 
17.        The Rural Tourism is a new market concept to tap tourist traffic as it can form the perfect vehicle for achievement of the
 national objective of achieving partnerships between local communities, government and industry.  Besides, it would help
 generate employment at local level, dispensing with the need of migrations to distant places for earning livelihood thereby
 leading to economic betterment. The Rural Tourism can also provide a solution to the increasing trends of industrialization and
 help in countering the stress of urban life styles and other urbanization syndromes. The Committee feels that global tourism
 growth potential can be harnessed as a strategy for Rural Development and therefore, recommends that the Department of
 Tourism should develop a strong platform around the concept of Rural Tourism for achieving the national objectives in better
 perspective.
 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT

 unorganized sector consisting of small hotel, dhabas and restaurant and other eatable joints spread all over the country

OVERSEAS PROMOTION AND MARKETING
(Recommendation at para 34)
 
29.        The Committee observes that though the allocation for overseas promotion and publicity has been increased
 substantially during the year 2003-04 still there is a significant gap between the Government of India’s spending and the world
 average expenditure on travel and tourism which can not be abridged keeping in view the crunch of financial resources. The
 Committee also notes that Department of Tourism has reoriented itself to the overseas market with a view to changing global
 scenario and fierce competition in the tourism generating markets. The Committee however, recommends that Department of
 Tourism should consider new strategy in the changing patterns of consumer behaviour and adopts integrated marketing
 communication approach to secure the future of the industry and its potentiality for job creations. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 
30.        The Department of Tourism has noted the above recommendation and also taken into account the present world travel
 trends and changing patterns of consumer behavior while developing its Promotional Plan for the current financial year.  The
 Plan adopts an integrated marketing communications approach and the elements include advertising, sales promotion, public
 relations and direct marketing for positioning India in key markets under the brand name ‘Incredible India’.
 
31.        In the changing world tourism scenario where mass markets are getting fragmented and destinations are shifting away
 from mass marketing to segmented marketing, the Department lays emphasis on use of information technology and Internet as
 this medium has become a catalyst in this paradigm shift.
 
32.        The plan also envisages a centralized advertising campaign to be launched directly from the Headquarters  in various
 key markets.  The Indiatourism Offices overseas through their annual Action Plans would supplement this campaign as well as
 compliment with promotional activities like participation in fairs and exhibitions, organising of seminars and workshops, direct
 mailing, hosting of media personalities and opinion makers under the Hospitality programme, web marketing, dissemination of
 information through the CD-ROMs and brochures etc.  The main objective behind this strategy is to carefully integrate and
 coordinate various communication channels so as to deliver a clear, consistent and compelling message about India’s various
 tourism products under the brand line ‘Incredible India’.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
33.        The Committee notes that the Department of Tourism has developed the promotional plan keeping in view the
 changing global scenario and competition in the tourism generating markets.  The Committee appreciates the
 Department of Tourism for adopting an integrated marketing communications approach, components of which include
 advertising, sales promotion, public relations and direct marketing for positioning India in key markets under the
 brand name ‘Incredible India’ as per its recommendation.  The Committee wishes that it will help augmenting the
 tourism potential and in view of this desires not to pursue the matter further.

(c)        Coordination Between Department of Tourism and State Governments
(Recommendation at paras 55 and 56)          
           
46.        The Committee notes with dismay that during the 9th Five Year Plan, the Department of Tourism sanctioned 1563
 projects involving an amount of Rs.372.43 crores to the State Government/UTs for tourism projects out of which only 571
 projects have been completed and 992 projects remained incomplete. Non-completion of projects not only led to cost over runs
 of the project but also the tourists potential in the country could not be exploited fully in the absence of the facilities which
 could have been made available otherwise.  The Committee is unable to understand the reason put forth by the Department of
 Tourism for delay i.e. lack of coordination between the State Government and different other Ministries in the implementation
 of projects, besides low level of monitoring and review of progress.  The Committee is of the opinion that involvement of
 Members of Parliament in identification, development and monitoring of tourist destinations in their respective state, can avoid
 delay and cost overrun in execution of tourism projects. In this connection, the committee reiterates its earlier recommendation
 made in 55th Report, which is reproduced below;
 
“The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should involve the Members of Parliament in the
 identification and development of places of tourist interest in their respective states.  The Committee is of the view that,
 as elected representatives of the people, Members of Parliament can also help the Department in identifying tourism
 sports in their states and inform the Department of Tourism which can take steps to develop such tourism areas.  The
 Committee also recommends that the Department of Tourism should, by way of taking the initiative, request the
 Members of Parliament to identify places, which can be developed as tourist spots.  The Members of Parliament should
 also be kept informed of the status of the tourism projects in their respective states.”
 
47.        The Committee therefore recommends that Department of Tourism should directly involve the Member of Parliament in
 identification, development and monitoring process of tourists destinations/circuits in their respective states and have a task
 force which will coordinate between different Ministries as well as State Governments and respective Members of Parliament
 in that state for monitoring the progress of physical targets every quarter or so.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
48.        The recommendations received from the Members of Parliament regarding identification and development of tourist
 circuits and destinations will be considered in consultation with the concerned State Governments. 
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
49.        The Committee notes that Department of Tourism have agreed to examine the proposals received from Members
 of Parliament in consultation with the concerned State Governments. The Committee recommends that the matter may
 be taken up with State Governments/UTs at the earliest. 
 
TOURISM DEVELOPMENT IN NORTH EASTERN REGION
(Recommendation at para 59)
 
50.        The Committee observes that Department of Tourism couldn’t utilise the funds allocated for the North-East region
 during 2002-03 for the development and promotion of tourism in the region. The Committee notes that a budgetary allocation
 of Rs.32.5 crores has been made during 2003-04 for various projects to be undertaken in the North Eastern Region including
 Sikkim. Despite incurring a lot of expenditure for development of tourism in North Eastern Region no perceptible improvement
 has been observed on the ground and a lot more has to be done to fully explore the tourism potential of this region. Therefore,
 the Committee recommends that the tourism related developmental work undertaken in the North East region should be given
 the highest priority and recommends that the funds allocated for the North East region should be optimally utilised in that
 region only.
