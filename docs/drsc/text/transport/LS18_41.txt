        Tourism is not only a major social  phenomenon of the modern society with enormous economic
 consequences but also  the world’s largest export industry, which accounts for about 8% of the total
 world’s exports, more than 30% of international trade in services, 10.7% of the  global work force and
 about 12% of world GDP.   It is also dubbed as the ‘Largest Smokeless Industry’ and contributes
 towards preservation of clean environment.
 2.Tourism is presently India’s second  largest net foreign exchange earning export industry after
 textiles/garments.  The estimated foreign exchange earning during January to December 99 were
 $2916.01 millions as compared to $2819.62 millions during the same period of  1998 showing an
 increase of 3.4%. Foreign tourist arrival has gone upto 2471848  during 1999 registering a growth of
 4.8% as compared to 1998.
 3.Tourism is of a particular significance  so far as India is concerned since it contributes to the
 environment and to the  national integration as it helps in the interaction amongst social and cultural
 lives of the people.   Over 160 million domestic  tourists visiting different parts of the country every
 year return with a  better understanding of the people living in the other regions of the country  and the
 cultural diversity of India.
 4.Tourism not only has the capacity to  generate large-scale employment opportunities in remote and
 backward areas but  also provides employment to a large number of women, both educated and
 uneducated.   In fact women are employed  in a large number in hotels, airlines services, travel agencies,
 handicrafts making, cultural and other tourism-related activities.
 5.The Ministry of Tourism functions as the  nodal agency for development of tourism in the country by
 coordinating and  supplementing the efforts of the State/Union Territory Governments, catalysing
 private investment, strengthening promotional and market efforts and in  providing trained manpower
 resources.
 6.The Ministry of Tourism has under its administrative control the following public sector
 undertaking/autonomous bodies:-
a)  India Tourism Development Corporation Limited;
b) National Council for Hotel Management and Catering  Technology;
 c) Indian Institute of Tourism & Travel Management;
d) Institutes of Hotel Management, Catering Technology and  Applied Nutrition at different locations in
 the country;
e)  Food Craft Institutes at different locations;
f)   Indian Institute of Skiing & Mountaineering;
g) National Institute of Water Sports.
 7.In  addition to the Head-quarters Organisation at New Delhi, the Directorate  General of Tourism has
 21 tourism offices in India and 18 tourist offices overseas.

10.     From a perusal of the Demands for Grants of  the Ministry of Tourism for 2000-2001, the
 Committee found that plan outlay of  the Ministry for the year 1999-2000 had been reduced from
 Rs.160.50 crores to  Rs.110 crores at RE stage.   Also the  plan outlay for the year 2000-2001 has been
 kept at Rs.135.00 crores which is less by Rs.24.50 crores as compared to BE for the year 1999-2000.
 11.     On being asked about the reasons for  reduction of plan outlay for 1999-2000 at RE stage and
 lower BE for the year  2000-2001, the Ministry of Tourism have stated that due to resource  constraints,
 not only BE 1999-2000 has been reduced but also proposed budget  allocation for the year 2000-2001
 has been subjected to drastic cut and the  Planning Commission has approved allocation of Rs.135.00
 crores only. The  Government    has neither been able to  enhance its own investment in the tourism
 sector nor mobilise private  investment to the desired extent due to the capital structure of industry,
 longer gestation period, low rates of return and certain infrastructural and  procedural problems.
 12.    The Committee has been  consistently recommending not to reduce the plan outlay of the Ministry
 at RE  stage. Moreover, while examining the Demands for Grants 1999-2000, the Committee  in its 37th
 Report had specially recommended a proportionate or at  least 20% increase over the previous year’s
 budget allocations.   However, to the surprise of the Committee,  plan outlay of Rs.135 crores has been
 approved for the year 2000-2001 against  the BE of Rs.160.5 crores for the last year.
 13.    The  Committee further observed that out of Rs.135.00 crores for the year 2000-2001,  Rs.49.75
 crores have been allocated for overseas campaign, thereby leaving only  Rs.85.25 crores for Human
 Resource Development, Tourist Infrastructure and  Domestic Campaign etc. in the country.
 14.         Tourism has a huge potential of employment opportunity in  rural and backward areas without
 causing migration to distant places, foreign  exchange earning industry, a tool for sustainable human
 development including  poverty alleviation, environmental conservation and regeneration, job creation
 and advancement of women and other disadvantaged groups.
 15.    The  Committee, therefore, again recommends that adequate funds be made available to  the
 Ministry of Tourism and there should be no reduction in the plan outlay at  RE stage.   The
 Committee is of the view that an amount Rs.85.25 crores is not sufficient to meet the requirement
 for  Domestic Campaign, Tourist Infrastructure, Training (Human Resource  Development) and
 other Expenditure and recommends that it should be increased at RE stage.
16.    The Committee is also concerned to note  that the Government policy has so far not been able to
 attract the private  investment in tourism sector to the desired extent.   The Committee is of the view that
 the Government alone, through  its limited resources, will not be able to meet the requirements of funds
 for  proper development of tourism sector.   The Committee, therefore, further  recommends that the
 Government, while acting as a facilitator and coordinator  and encouraging involvement of private
 sector in the development of tourism,  should take steps to remove the infrastructural and
 procedural hurdles which the private sector has to face and ensure the availability of land,
 electricity, water supply, sewerage system and do away with the multiplicity of  permits and
 approvals.
17.    The Committee notes that as against the  proposed outlay of Rs.5370 for the 9th Plan Period, only a
 minuscule  amount of Rs.485.75 crores was approved.   The Committee also notes that the Ministry of
 Tourism has so far been  allocated Rs.455.46 crores out of the total plan outlay of Rs.485.75 for the
 entire 9th Five Year Plan period. Now about Rs.30.29 crores remain  for the last financial year of the
 9th Plan Period i.e.  2001-2002.   The Committee feels that  this amount of Rs.30.29 crores would not be
 sufficient for the implementation  of various schemes already under consideration of the Ministry of
 Tourism.   The  Committee, therefore, recommends for substantial enhancement of the Plan Outlay
 of the Ministry of Tourism for the year 2001-2002 and that Planning Commission and Ministry of
 Finance should intimate the Ministry of Tourism about the  quantum of plan outlay well in
 advance, so that Ministry of Tourism may be able  to project its demands properly for the year
 2001-2002.  
CENTRAL ASSISTANCE FOR  TOURISM INFRASTRUCTURE & PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT  
18.     Since most of the tourist attractions and delivery systems are  within the purview of State/UT
 Governments, a large part of the Central  Government investment for the improvement and creation of
 tourist facilities is channelised through the State Governments on a cost-sharing basis.   In order to
 ensure integrated development of  tourism infrastructure including basic amenities and other facilities,
 the  Ministry of Tourism provides assistance to State/Union Territory Governments for the
 implementation of composite projects.   A total amount of Rs.29.10 crores has been allocated under this
 Head which  includes Rs.18 crores for integrated development of Tourist/Pilgrim Centres,  Special

 Shows/Flood  Lighting, Adventure Tourism etc. and Rs.25 lakh for Externally Aided Projects.
 19.Central assistance for infrastructure development is provided  to the States based on two types of
 funding patterns – (1) Normal Funding and  (2) Equity Scheme.   The most popular  pattern of funding,
 which has been continuing over successive plan periods, is the normal funding. The Ministry of
 Tourism almost meets the entire expenditure  except the cost of land and interior decoration in the
 construction  projects.   A new method of funding,  namely ‘Equity Scheme’, had been introduced during
 1992-93 in which the  assistance from the Ministry of Tourism is limited to 28% of the project  cost.  
 12% of the project cost is to be  provided by the State Government/Union Territory and the balance 60%
 has to be  raised as loan from financial institutions/banks.   This method of funding is applicable to
 bankable projects requiring large investments.

 21.     From a perusal of the above data, it is clear that performance  under both the schemes has not
 been satisfactory.   Against the actual expenditure of Rs.9.51 crore during 1998-99,  only Rs.6.50 crores
 are expected to be utilised during 1999-2000 whereas the BE  1999-2000 was Rs.32.20 crore.   For the

 Commission has provided only Rs.25.00 lakhs for this scheme for the year 2000-2001.  
 22.     The Committee had, in its 37th report on the Demands for Grants (1999-2000) of the Ministry of
 Tourism, identified the condition of  transferring the title of the land from State Government to the
 Government of  India as one of the major bottlenecks resulting in delay in the completion of  projects
 and had recommended the waiving off of this condition for development  of tourism in the country.   The
 Ministry  has informed that the Ministry of Urban Affairs & Employment, and also  Planning
 Commission, have supported the waiving of the condition of  transferring the title of land to
 Government of India.   The Ministry of Finance, however, has agreed  to substitute the condition of
 transfer of land by long-term lease.   However, many States have expressed their  reservation even on
 this requirement.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that  the matter may be taken up with
 the concerned State Government and the Ministry  of Finance to sort it out expeditiously so as to
 arrive at an amicable solution and the funds earmarked for implementation of various projects
 under this scheme are fully utilised without any further delay.  
23.      The Committee was also  informed that the State Governments are reluctant to approach Ministry
 of  Tourism for funds under the Equity Scheme since they have to share 12% of the  project cost and
 have also to obtain funds from financial institutions on loan  basis.   The Committee feels that the
 scheme is not bearing the desired fruits since it has not been able to encourage the participation
 of the States.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that in view of the indifferent attitude of
 the States, the viability of the scheme may  be reassessed since it is leading to non-utilisation of
 funds every year.   The Government should also try to modify the  scheme, in consultation with the
 State Governments, so that it becomes more  acceptable to the States and encourage their
 participation in this centrally  assisted financial scheme.

WAYSIDE AMENITIES
25.     The Ministry of Tourism allocates funds every year to the State  Governments for creating wayside
 amenities for tourists.   These include tourist reception centres,  restaurants/cafeterias, drinking water
 facilities, public conveniences, kiosks,  general store and parking area etc. The Ministry of Tourism
 prioritises projects in consultation with the State Governments and release funds on  receipt of the
 specific proposals prioritised during the financial year.   An amount of Rs.4.00 crores has been
 allocated for the purpose during the year 2000-2001.
 26.    The  Committee feels that to keep pace with the fast developing highway tourism in the
 country, a well-knit network of wayside facilities on the roads connecting  important tourist
 centres is a must.   The Committee also feels that the allocation of Rs. 4 crores for wayside
 amenities in the BE 2000-2001 is simply not enough and recommends that the  allocation under
 this head must be increased.   The Government should also explore the possibility of involving the
 participation of private parties/voluntary agencies for the maintenance of  toilet/drinking water
 facilities and other civic amenities created under this scheme.  
PILGRIM TOURISM
27.     The Committee has been informed that for the development of  infrastructure facilities including
 basic amenities in the identified  tourist/pilgrim centres, the Ministry of Tourism has decided to provide
 financial assistance to the State/Union Territory Governments for the  implementation of composite
 projects in 50 selected centres.   The maximum assistance will be Rupees one  cores, subject to matching
 contribution by the State Governments, and it will  be provided at the rate of Rs.50 lakh for a period of
 two years.   The Ministry of Tourism has decided to set  up a ‘Pilgrim Tourism Development Board’ to
 advise the Ministry in all matters relating to Pilgrim Tourism.
28.    The  Committee is of the view that in India, there are a large number of pilgrim centres and if
 basic amenities/facilities are provided to the tourists/pilgrims, it  will encourage the arrival of the
 tourists at these places.   The Committee recommends that the ‘Pilgrim  Tourism Development
 Board’ may be set up immediately and the State Governments may be requested to provide the
 matching financial contributions so that  development work may take place at the selected tourist
 centres in their States on priority basis.        
MEGA-TOURISM PROJECTS/SPECIAL TOURISM AREAS 
29.     Mega Tourism  projects have brought revolution in tourism sector in Maxico and Indonesia and
 tourist arrivals have gone up substantially there.   In India an ambitious scheme for identification and

 Sindhudurg (Maharashtra), Muttukadu-Mamallapuram (Tamil Nadu) and Diu  as Special Tourism Area
 for integrated development.   The intensive development of these areas was envisaged in  coordination
 with the concerned State Governments and private sector.   The Ministry of Tourism has proposed an

HERITAGE HOTELS
33.     An amount of Rs.2 crores has been allocated for Heritage  Hotels/refurbishment of
 monuments/Heritage sites.   The concept of Heritage Hotels was introduced with a view to  convert the
 old palaces, havelies, castles, forts and residences built prior to  1950 into accommodation units as
 these traditional structures reflect the  ambience and life-style of the bygone era and are immensely
 popular with the  tourists.   The Committee was informed  that till the end of September 99, as many as
 62 properties have been classified  as Heritage Hotels providing a room capacity of 1816 rooms.   At
 present, 53 heritage properties are  approved as projects, which on completion would add 1188 rooms in
 the heritage  category.   The Heritage Hotels outside  the metropolitan areas are eligible for interest
 subsidy @ 5% in respect of  loans from Central and State financial institutions.  
34.    The  Committee appreciates the concept of Heritage Hotels introduced by the Ministry  of
 Tourism and feels that these hotels can serve as a good instrument for  promoting tourism.   The
 Committee,  therefore, recommends that the concept of Heritage Hotels should be promoted more
 intensively so as to ensure that old properties, which are landmarks of  our heritage, are not lost
 due to decay but become financially viable  properties providing additional room capacity for the
 tourists.

 for National Institute of Water Sports and Rs.5 lakh for other programmes.   The existing facilities

36.         Trained/skilled manpower is essential  for attaining quality and efficiency in service.   Ministry
 of Tourism has set up 21 institutes of Hotel Management  and 13 Food Craft Institutes in different part
 of the country.   The Hotel Management & Catering  Institutes and Food Crafts Institutes serve the

 and  catering industry to produce a professional work force.   The total turn out of all the hotel and
 catering technology schools in India is about 7,000 students per year as against the actual requirement
 of 20,000 persons per year.   Thus, a huge gap is anticipated between the  supply and demand of the
 trained manpower in this sector.   The Committee was informed that the Ministry  has commissioned a
 manpower survey through National Council for Hotel  Management and Catering Technology to
 determine the qualitative and  quantitative requirement of the trained manpower in the country.   The
 Council will also assess the capacity of  the private institutes for providing the trained manpower.
37.     The Committee was informed that in order to  strengthen the existing training infrastructure, the
 Ministry proposes to set  up a Hospitality Education Grid comprising of National Culinary Institute and
 Advance Hotel Management Institute, a branch of Indian Institute of Tourism  & Travel Management
 and Headquarters of National Council for Hotel  Management and Catering Technology at NOIDA.   An
 allocation of Rs.5 crores has been made in BE 2000-2001 for the proposed  Grid.   The entire complex
 will be designed  in such a manner that certain common facilities such as the library, computer  centre
 and other amenities will be utilised by the faculty and students of  different disciplines.   The National
 Culinary Institute will provide training to chefs, with special emphasis on  research and documentation
 of variety of Indian cuisine and food production  whereas the Advance Hotel Management Institute will
 focus on advanced  management training for the hotel and hospitality industry.  
38.      The Committee feels that the  availability of trained manpower is an essential pre-requisite
 for promoting  any tourist destination.   The Committee  hopes that the Grid will produce highly
 skilled manpower for hospitality and travel industry for specialised functions and upgrade the
 qualities of the  tourism product in order to make India a competitive tourist destination in all
 respects and thus help in promoting tourism to the country.   The Committee recommends that all
 necessary  steps should be taken to complete the project as per schedule and it should not  be
 allowed to suffer due to non-availability of funds.  
39.    The  Committee takes note of the various steps being taken by the Government to  increase the
 availability of trained manpower by expanding the infrastructure  of Institutes of Hotel
 Management and setting up of new Food Craft  Institutes.   However, the Committee is of the view
 that due to the paucity of funds, the efforts made by the  Government will not be sufficient to
 bridge the gap between the demand and supply of trained manpower and therefore, the private
 sector has to play an important role in this regard.   The  Committee takes exception to the fact
 that the Government is not aware of the  capacity of the private institutes to provide the trained
 manpower in this sector.     The Committee feels that in the absence of such data, the  Government
 will not be able to devise proper strategy to meet the requirement  of trained manpower.  
 Recognising the role  of private sector in providing the trained manpower, the Committee
 recommends that the assessment regarding capacity of private institutes to provide trained

 should evolve  attractive packages for ensuring greater involvement of private sector in this field.

MARKETING & PUBLICITY
42.     An amount of Rs.57.75 crores has been  approved for marketing and publicity during 2000-2001,
 which includes Rs.49.75  crores for Overseas Promotion and Rs.3 crores for Domestic Campaign.
 Information Technology plays a major role in the promotion of tourism.   The Committee was informed
 that a lot of  steps have been taken to exploit Information Technology and electronic media as important
 tools for the development of tourism in India. The Ministry has established a Tourist Information
 Network (TOURNET) for providing up-to-date  information to the tourists visiting the country.   The
 network is presently functional in the Government of India  Tourist Offices in India.   It is proposed to
 upgrade this TOURNET software into a multi media package and  establish it in the Government of
 India Tourist Offices abroad. It is also  proposed to assist the State/UT Governments to achieve
 technological  upgradation of their information systems on tourism by providing assistance  upto 50% of
 the cost for the development of multi-media packages, purchase of  computers and video presentation
 equipments. Some of the steps taken/proposed  to be taken are as under:-  
(a)         An official web site www.tourisminindia.com  has been created.  
(b)         PCs/Internet/E-mail connectivity has been provided to all  the officers at New Delhi, 13 out of
 21 Tourist offices in India and all the  Tourist offices abroad.  
(c)        Interactive Voice Response System (IVRS) has started  working in the Regional Tourist Offices
 in India at Delhi and Chennai.   It is also being provided in the offices at  Mumbai and Calcutta.  
(d)         The CD Rom called ‘Destination India’ has been  produced.   Thematic CD-ROMs on important
 subjects of tourist interest like Wild Life, Railways, N-E States, Convention  and Conferences are also
 being developed.  
(e)         Touch screen based Information Kiosks are being installed  shortly at various important places
 in the four Metropolitan Cities at Delhi,  Mumbai, Calcutta and Chennai.        
43.    The  Committee feels that the potential of the information technology in marketing  and
 promotion of tourism in India has not been exploited to the desired extent.   The Committee find
 that most of  the projects in this field such as installation of Touch screen based information
 Kiosks at Delhi, Mumbai, Calcutta and Chennai, developing thematic  CD-ROMs on Wild Life,
 Railways, North-Eastern States, providing IVRS at Mumbai  and Calcutta etc. are still underway
 and hopes that the completion of these  projects will spread awareness among domestic and foreign
 tourists and help in promoting tourism in India.  
44.         Ministry of Tourism performs its main  marketing function through a network of 18 Tourist
 Offices located  overseas.   These tourist offices perform  promotional activities including advertising

 Government should also explore the possibility of utilising  the services of professional agencies
 abroad to boost the image of India as tourist destination instead of having separate tourist offices
 overseas.   The Committee recommends that immediate  necessary action may be taken upon all the
 recommendations of the World Tourism  Organisations and the action taken upon them may be
 brought to the notice of  the Committee.

47.         Today eco-tourism is being promoted as a  means of ‘giving nature value’ and hence of
 achieving ‘sustainable  development’.   Tourism has contributed  to the protection of natural environment
 and cultural heritage.   The Ministry of Tourism has informed that  they have formulated a
 comprehensive Eco-tourism Policy and guidelines in  consultation with all the concerned agencies.   The
 Committee feels that there are numerous places scattered  throughout the country, such as mountains,
 wildlife and beach resorts, which  hold unlimited varieties of flora and fauna to entertain eco-friendly
 tourists.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that special attention should be  given on the
 development of eco-tourism, which will not only boost tourism but  also help protect landscape,
 fauna and flora from destruction by modern development.   India offers a huge  potential for
 adventure tourism like trekking, mountaineering, river rafting,  winter sports, water sports etc.
 and efforts should also be made to attract  tourists to these places.

GENERAL OBSERVATION
58.    The Committee has failed to understand as to why the Government  has been ignoring such an
 important sector and not providing sufficient funds,  which are required by the Ministry for the
 development of tourism in the  country.   The Committee expresses its anguish over the treatment being
 meted out to the tourism industry for which a  meagre allocation is being approved and that too is being
 subjected to drastic  cuts every year at RE stage. Although during April 99 to December 99, the
 estimated foreign exchange earning was Rs.8817.91 crores, for the last three  financial years, the plan
 outlay for the tourism, the second highest foreign  exchange earning sector, has been more or less equal
 to the cost of one fighter  plane. The Committee, therefore, recommends that keeping in view the role of
 tourism in the future of Indian economy, Ministry of Finance and Planning  Commission should make
 available sufficient funds to the Ministry of Tourism so  that it could take adequate steps for the
 promotion and development of tourism  in the country.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT  A GLANCE  
The Committee has been consistently recommending  not to reduce the plan outlay of the Ministry at RE
 stage. Moreover, while  examining the Demands for Grants 1999-2000, the Committee in its 37th  Report
 had specially recommended a proportionate or at least 20% increase over  the previous year’s budget
 allocations.   However, to the surprise of the Committee, plan outlay of Rs.135 crores has been
 approved for the year 2000-2001 against the BE of Rs.160.5 crores for  the last year.                         
                                
 (Para 12)  
The Committee further observed  that out of Rs.135.00 crores for the year 2000-2001, Rs.49.75 crores
 have been  allocated for overseas campaign, thereby leaving only Rs.85.25 crores for Human  Resource
 Development, Tourist Infrastructure and Domestic Campaign etc. in the  country.   
         (Para 13)  
The Committee, therefore, again recommends that adequate funds be made available to the Ministry of
 Tourism and  there should be no reduction in the plan outlay at RE stage.   The Committee is of the view
 that an amount  Rs.85.25 crores is not sufficient to meet the requirement for Domestic  Campaign,
 Tourist Infrastructure, Training (Human Resource Development) and  other Expenditure and
 recommends that it should be increased at RE stage.   (Para 15)
 The Committee is also concerned  to note that the Government policy has so far not been able to attract
 the  private investment in tourism sector to the desired extent.   The Committee is of the view that the
 Government alone, through its limited resources, will not be able to meet the  requirements of funds for
 proper development of tourism sector.   The Committee, therefore, further recommends that the
 Government, while acting as a facilitator and coordinator and  encouraging involvement of private
 sector in the development of tourism, should  take steps to remove the infrastructural and procedural
 hurdles which the  private sector has to face and ensure the availability of land, electricity,  water
 supply, sewerage system and do away with the multiplicity of permits and  approvals.  (Para 16)

 a  large number of Tourists both Indian and foreign, no consideration has been  given to the Andaman &
 Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands.   These island offer tourist attraction in a  big way and there is
 immense potential for development of tourism.   However, very little appears to have been  done for the
 promotion of tourism in these islands.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that these islands
 should be  developed at a faster pace from the tourism point of view.   The Committee also recommends
 that Andaman  & Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands should also be considered within the  ambit of the
 Government policy for development of tourism in the N.E. region.                                             
         (Para 50)
National Tourism Development Fund  The Committee appreciates the setting up of National Tourism
 Development  Fund.   The Committee recommends that the  fund be made functional and necessary
 formalities therefor be obtained on  priority basis.   Given the financial  constraints the Ministry of
 Tourism is facing at present, the Committee hopes the fund would meet the demands of the
 entrepreneurs for assistance required  for executing tourism projects in the country, which will bring
 improvement in  education and standardise the tourism product to meet international  levels.    The
 Committee further hopes that the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance would release the
 remaining amount of Rs.9.00 crores in the coming year to the corpus of Rs.10.00  crores, as proposed by
 the Ministry of Tourism.   (Para 52)  
India Tourism  Development Corporation
The  Committee observes that there has been under-utilisation of funds to the extent  of 30% in 1998-99.  
 Also plan outlay for  the year 1999-2000 was reduced from Rs.70.00 crores at BE stage to Rs.35.00
 crores at RE stage. Now for the year 2000-2001, the proposed outlay is Rs.60.00 crores.   The
 Committee feels that in  view of the financial performance during the last few years, the proposed
 outlay for 2000-2001 is on the higher side.        
The Committee also observes that the profits earned by ITDC are going  down since the last few years.  
 An  increase of 15% in net profit in 2000-2001 as compared to 1999-2000 is expected, but these are
 only projected and not actual figures.   The Committee notes with concern that no  regular Chairman has
 been appointed for the last two years, which has also  adversely affected the performance of ITDC.   The
 Committee recommends that a full time Chairman of ITDC may be  appointed at the earliest so that
 declining trend in overall performance of  ITDC may be arrested.Paras 55 and 56)

General Observation
The Committee has failed to understand as to why the  Government has been ignoring such an important
 sector and not providing  sufficient funds, which are required by the Ministry for the development of
 tourism  in the country.   The Committee expresses its anguish over the treatment being meted out to the
 tourism industry for  which a meagre allocation is being approved and that too is being subjected to
 drastic cuts every year at RE stage. Although during April 99 to December 99,  the estimated foreign
 exchange earning was Rs.8817.91 crores, for the last  three financial years, the plan outlay for the
 tourism, the second highest  foreign exchange earning sector, has been more or less equal to the cost of
 one  fighter plane. The Committee, therefore, recommends that keeping in view the  role of tourism in
 the future of Indian economy, Ministry of Finance and  Planning Commission should make available
 sufficient funds to the Ministry of  Tourism so that it could take adequate steps for the promotion and
 development  of tourism in the country.
