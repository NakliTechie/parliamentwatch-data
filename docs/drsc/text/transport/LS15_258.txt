REPORT
Culture plays an important role in the development agenda of any nation. It represents a set of
shared attitudes, values, goals and practices. Culture and creativity manifest themselves in almost all
economic, social and other activities. India is a vast and diverse country with a rich cultural heritage that
has been widely appreciated all over the world.
2. The Ministry of Culture is mandated the functions like preservation and conservation of our
cultural heritage and promotion of all forms of art and culture, both tangible and intangible. The Ministry's
task is to develop and sustain ways and means through which the creative and aesthetic sensibilities of
the people remain active and dynamic. The functional spectrum of this Ministry is wide, ranging from
generating cultural awareness at grassroots level to promoting cultural exchanges at international level.
Gandhian Heritage and Convocation of important historical events and centenaries of great personalities
are also the responsibility of Ministry of Culture.
3. The Ministry is the custodian of all the major libraries in the country and through National Archives
of India, it is responsible for maintenance of all archival records of the country. The Ministry also
protects and promotes Buddhist and Tibetan Culture. Capacity building programme in the field of Indian
art and culture is promoted through institutions like School of Archaeology, School of Archives, National
Museum, Institute of History of Art Conservation and Museology, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Institute of
Asian Studies, National School of Drama, Kalakshetra Foundation. On the international front, the Ministry
is responsible for implementing te various UNESCO conventions and for entering into Cultural Exchange
Agreements with partnering countries.
4. The Budget allocations of the Ministry of Culture for the financial year 2018-19 was taken up for
examination by the Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism and
Culture in its meeting held on the 20th February, 2018. The Secretary and other officials of the Ministry
of Culture and its subordinate organizations made a presentation on various aspects of the budget and
functioning of the Ministry. The Committee examined Budget (2018-19) documents and various other
background materials of the Ministry and the reply furnished by the Ministry to the questionnaire.

6. As against total budgetary provision of ` 2738.47 crore at BE 2017-18 which has been reduced
to ` 2666.94 crore at RE 2017-18, the budget for 2018-19 for Ministry of Culture has been pegged at
` 2843.32 crore, consisting of ` 2764.28 crore under Revenue and ` 79.04 crore under Capital heads
for undertaking various prioritized programmes/activities/projects. This budgetary allocation is about
3.83% more than the budgetary provisions at BE 2017-18.
7. The Ministry in its written reply has stated that the Ministry had projected additional amount of
` 16.82 crore at RE (Capital) - 2017-18 for the work related to National Gandhi Heritage Site Mission,
National Archives of India and Anthropological Survey of India. In addition to this, ` 417.76 crore under
RE (Revenue) - 2017-18, which include (i) projections of ` 32.60 crore in the HRA, DA and TA etc.
under the Salary component of Secretariat & attached/subordinate office, (ii) ` 17.00 crore under on-
salary components, (iii) ` 50.00 crore for carrying out routine conservation and preservation works for
the monuments by Archaeological Survey of India, (iv) ` 315.56 crore under Grant-in Aid for Autonomous
Bodies of the Ministry and (v) ` 5.73 crore was sought for grantee bodies. In response to this, as against
BE 2017-18 of ` 2738.47 crore, Ministry of Finance has allocated ` 2666.94 crore as ceiling of RE
2017-18 (Both Revenue and Capital together) against a projection of an amount of ` 3156.23 crore at
RE 2017-18.
8. The Committee is distressed to note that against a projection of ````` 3156.23 crore at RE
2017-18 an amount of Rs 2666.94 crore as ceiling of RE 2017-18 was allocated by the Ministry
of Finance which is a further reduction from budgetary allocation at BE 2017-18. The Committee
notes with dismay that though the Ministry of Culture has demanded more allocation at RE stage
in 2017-18 for works including the preservation of monuments, the Ministry of Finance reduced
the allocation which is a clear indication that the Government is giving least priority to the
activities of Ministry of Culture.

MINISTRY'S PROJECTED DEMAND  vis-a-vis Budgetary Allocation
10. The Ministry in its written reply has stated that they had projected an outlay of ` 3841.02 crore
at BE 2018-19 comprising of ` 3748.52 crore under Revenue and ` 92.50 crore under Capital, on the

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
20. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) was established in 1861. It functions as an attached
office of the Ministry of Culture. ASI is engaged in protection, preservation and conservation of all the
centrally protected monuments of national importance and excavations of historical sites in the country.

9
The ASI has under its protection 3686 monuments and archaeological sites of National Importance in
the country which include 22 cultural sites inscribed in the World Heritage List by UNESCO.

21. The Ministry, in its written reply, has stated that Ministry of Finance has allocated an amount of
` 974.56 crore in favour of Archaeological Survey of India in BE 2018-19 which is an enhanced
allocation compared to ` 922.32 crore in RE 2017-18. The allocated amount of ` 974.56 crore consists

up gradation of toilets, construction of Boundary Wall, construction of visitors amenities like Booking

refurbishing of Adarsh Monuments etc. The total utilization of fund in respect of Archaeological Survey
of India during the year 2017-18 (till January, 2018) is ` 733.95 crore (79.6% of RE 2017-18).
22. ASI has assigned the work of construction of boundary walls and toilet blocks at monuments for
upgradations and new constructions to M/s W APCOS and M/s TCIL. For boundary walls, 228 monuments/
sites have been assigned to W APCOS and 124 to TCIL. For toilet blocks, W APCOS has been assigned
the work for 117 monuments whereas TCIL is given the work for 122 monuments. The Work is in
progress at the assigned sites which is likely to be completed in next four months.
23. The Ministry has in its written reply stated that the efforts have been made for removal of
encroachment from centrally protected monuments/sites following due process of law and at many sites,
the same has been successfully implemented. The exact number of monuments/sites from where
encroachment has been removed is not readily available which would be gathered from the field offices
and submitted in due course.
24. The Committee welcomes the initiative taken to construct boundary walls for ASI protected
monuments and removal of encroachment from centrally protected monuments/sites. The
Committee recommends that every effort should be made to construct boundary walls and
fencing to each of the 3686 nationally protected monuments in a time bound manner. The
Committee hopes that construction of boundary walls will go a long way in preventing further
encroachment of monuments. The Committee recommends that steps should be taken so that
no new encroachment takes places on such sites as removal of such encroachment is a very
cumbersome process. The Committee may also be provided a list of monuments/sites from where
encroachments have been removed.

31. The Ministry has been informed that ASI has identified Adarsh Monuments based on discussion
with respective field officers keeping in view of the significance of the monument, carrying capacity and
scope for providing basic amenities and tourist related other infrastructures identified for Adarsh Monument.
So far, 100 monuments/sites have been declared as Adarsh Monument. No timeline has been fixed for

55. The Ministry in its written reply has stated that the scheme namely "National Mission on Cultural

been launched on 29th March, 2017 with the main objectives of talent hunt as well as collection of
database of the artistes, artisans and various art form across the country. The objective is also to nurture

56. Cultural mapping embraces a broad definition of cultural resources which includes a wide
range of tangible resources such as arts and heritage organizations, cultural occupations and
industries, cultural and natural heritage — among others. It also explores the areas of intangible
cultural assets — the stories, traditions and unique quality of life that shape our identity and
sense of place. It is a dynamic concept. It will help in building better information on the cultural
assets in our country. A cultural asset inventory will be created, i.e., the project will produce a
baseline database of cultural resources in the country. The Committee recommends that
committees may be formed in different parts of the country to provide input and advice to the
mapping project. These committees may be comprised of a wide cross section of rural, urban,
municipal, cultural, business and community representatives and interests so that Cultural
Policy to provide a broad framework to support cultural planning and cultural development may
be framed.

NATIONAL CULTURE FUND
62. National Culture Fund (NCF) constitutes an important departure from the implementation strategies
of the Government, which hitherto was thought to be solely responsible for providing administrative and
financial wherewithal for culture related endeavours in the country. NCF accepts private/public institutions
and individuals as equal partners of the Government in the management of the cultural heritage of India.
It enables the Government to mobilize extra budgetary resources by allowing direct contributions to the
NCF account maintained outside the Consolidated Fund of India. All contributions of NCF are given
100% tax exemption under 80G (2) of the Income Tax Act of 1961.
63. The Committee recommends that the ongoing projects under NCF should be monitored in
regular intervals and the working of the Project Implementation Committee for each project
should also be scrutinized regularly so that delay and pendency can be avoided by timely sorting
out of problems.

ANTHROPOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA
81. Since its inception in 1945 the Anthropological Survey of India, a premier research organization
under the Ministry of Culture, Government of India is dedicated to map the Bio-Cultural Profile of Indian
population utilizing manpower specialized in Cultural and Physical Anthropology, Human Ecology,
Psychology, Bio-chemistry, Linguistics and Folklore; developing the organization as launching pad for
technology based research to be applied in human development; understanding, documenting and preparing
database and disseminating information about bio-cultural diversity of Indian populations through published
research work, seminars, workshops, awareness programmes, outreach programmes, audio-visual
documentation, museum documentation; and development of library for facilitating research in
anthropology.
Major contributions :
• Publication of People of India Project, which provides brief socio-cultural profile on each
of the 4635 communities in India.
• Establishment of High Technology DNA Laboratories at Kolkata and Mysore.
• The Zonal Anthropological Museums (ZAM) located in different locations along with the
length and breadth of the country displays biological, Social and cultural heritage of the
country. These Museums attracts thousands of tourists, scholars and students every months.
Recent research projects of the Anthropological Survey of India:
1. Development and Sustainability
Under the project 'Development and Sustainability'16 villages have been initiated for the study. This
project aims at critically assessing the development projects in the broader framework of their
impacts and sustainability. The studies were carried out at different locations which include both
tribal and non-tribal locations and communities.
2. Bio-Cultural Diversity, Environment and Sustainable Development (Village Studies)
The study of 50 villages was undertaken to elucidate and analyse the changes with respect to
Livelihood Practices, Education, Health and Health Care Issues and Development Initiatives.
3. Man and Environment: Study of Bio-sphere Reserves
Anthropological Survey India has completed the study of all the 18 Biosphere Reserves in India.
Through this study the Anthropological Survey of India intends to understand biological and
cultural diversity as they exist in each of the biosphere reserve together with conservation ethos
embedded in socio-economic practices as pursued by the people.

25
4. DNA Polymorphism of the Contemporary Indian Populations & Ancient Skeletal
Materials
The report "Genomic Diversity of People of India" is ready for publication based on the findings
of the National Project "DNA Polymorphism in Contemporary Indian Population and Ancient
Skeletal Remains". 74 Tribal communities' blood samples were collected for DNA studies. Y-
Chromosome phylogeny and DNA analysis of these communities were also carried out.
5. Paleoanthropology
The Anthropological Survey of India has a rich ancient human skeletal repository with a wide
ranging collection of about thirty (30) human skeletal series representing a vast horizon of culture
from the late Stone Age to the historical period including the Harappan sites.
82. The Committee takes notes of the initiatives and recommends that the Ministry should
ensure that enough fund is allocated under this head and also monitor its utilization. The
Committee notes that some of the Reports of Anthropological Survey of India are ready for
publication. The Committee recommends that the Reports may be revaluated with the help of

CULTURAL MAPPING OF INDIA
Cultural mapping embraces a broad definition of cultural resources which includes a wide
range of tangible resources such as arts and heritage organizations, cultural occupations and
industries, cultural and natural heritage -among others. It also explores the areas of intangible
cultural assets - the stories, traditions and unique quality of life that shape our identity and
sense of place. It is a dynamic concept. It will help in building better information on the cultural
assets in our country. A cultural asset inventory will be created, i.e., the project will produce a
baseline database of cultural resources in the country. The Committee recommends that
committees may be formed in different parts of the country to provide input and advice to the
mapping project. These committees may be comprised of a wide cross section of rural, urban,
municipal, cultural, business and community representatives and interests so that Cultural
Policy to provide a broad framework to support cultural planning and cultural development may
be framed. (Para 56)
The Committee notes there was a gross underutilisation of fund allocated to Cultural
