REPORT
The Indian Civil Aviation sector has witnessed a tough time during the recent years. Hit by
rising fuel costs, uncertain cost regime, rupee depreciation and general slow down in India’s
economy resulting slump in passenger traffic. While the international traffic grew at the rate of
4.3%, the domestic traffic recorded a negative growth of 4.8% during the year 2012. Despite the
fact that the airlines charged higher airfare, most of the airlines incurred losses during this period.
Some of the Government’s initiatives like allowing FDI in civil aviation sector and permission to
import ATF have not yielded the expected results so far. The year also saw the closure of
operations of a private carrier and long labour unrest in the history of national carrier.
2. The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation of national policies and
programmes for the development and regulation of civil aviation in the country and for devising
and implementing schemes for orderly growth and expansion of civil air transport. Its functions
also extend to overseeing the provision of airport facilities, air traffic services, carriage of
passengers and goods by air, safeguarding civil aviation operations, regulation of air transport
services, licensing of aerodromes, air carriers, pilots and aircraft maintenance engineers. In addition
to framing policies, the Ministry also provides guidance to the organizations in the implementation
of policy guidelines.
3. The Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following organizations under its administrative
control:–
(i) Attached/Subordinate Organizations
– Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)
– Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS)
– Commission of Railway Safety (CRS)

7. The Committee finds that the spending pattern of Plan funds has been poor during
2012-13 which needs a lot of improvement. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Civil
Aviation will impress the concerned organizations/undertakings to spend the allocated fund
with proper planning and coordination with respective stakeholders. The Committee notes
that except the investment in Air India, all other organizations were allocated substantially
reduced allocations at RE stage which also could not be optimally utilized. The Committee
feels that reduction of funds at RE stage does not augur well for the much beleaguered
aviation sector. Most of these under-utilizations have been mainly due to finalization of
schemes. The Committee expresses dismay over non-finalization of building plan for BCAS
and DGCA that are pending for so many years. Slow progress in case of NER projects is
a cause of concern to the Committee. The Committee recommends the Ministry to take
necessary steps urgently to finalize/approve these Plan schemes at the earliest to spend the
allocated funds during the current year.

Government has approved the Turn Around Plan (TAP) and Financial Restructuring Plan (FRP) of

EBITDA positive for the year 2012-13 as well.

20. The Committee was further informed that according to the approved Financial Restructuring
Plan (FRP), the Government is to infuse equity of Rs.30231 crores in Air India between the years

the budgetary provision made is only for Rs.5000 crores leaving a deficit of Rs.3574 crores.

MRO SBU
29. The plan of merger of erstwhile Indian Airlines and Air India into NACIL envisaged potential
creation of Strategic Business Units (SBUs) as profit centres in the areas of Full Service Passenger
Airline (PAX), Low Cost Carriers (LCC), MRO etc. The market for MRO business is expected to
grow with the rapidly expanding Indian Aviation Market. It is, therefore, decided that Air India
Engineering Services Limited (AIESL) which was incorporated in 2004 in erstwhile Air India would
be hived off and operationalised as a subsidiary company with management independence.
30. Furthermore, MRO business may help Air India to monetize the value of its current assets
and infrastructure to raise funds to support the parent company. Efforts are being made for taking
approval from FAA/EASA and other regulatory agencies. This will also help Air India to take third
party workload.

Air India has already operationalised its Joint Venture company AISATS, for ground handling at
Bangalore, Hyderabad, Delhi, Mangalore and Thiruvananthapuram. In other airports in the country,
Air India plans to carry out ground handling activities through its subsidiary company, Air India

34. When it was asked about the sorting out of the technical issues of B-787 Dreamliners with
the manufacturer and the expected dates of re-induction of withdrawn Dreamliners into service, it
was informed that B-787 Dreamliners are at present grounded since the FAA has advised that they
are not safe to fly with the existing battery system. M/s Boeing Co., Seattle, USA has proposed
modification/improvements to the Lithium Ion Battery and has re-designed the B-787 Aircraft
Battery System.
35. FAA has approved limited Test Flights for two B-787 aircraft, which will be installed with
prototype version of new Battery Enclosure system. The aircraft is expected to be put back into
service by the end of April, 2013.
36. The Committee notes that despite many labour unrests witnessed during the last
year, the national carrier was able to improve its performance notably both financially and
operationally. It recorded positive EBITDA of Rs.19 crores during the year 2012-13 as
against a negative EBITDA of Rs.2237 crores during the year 2011-12. Air India’s on-time
performance was 84.8% in the case of domestic sector and 74% for international sector as
against the turnaround plan benchmark of 85%. The passenger load factor of Air India was
72% as against the target under turnaround plan of 69.5%. The turnaround plan
benchmark on yield per kilometer was 3.53, against which Air India achieved 3.76.
37. The Committee finds that Air India was able to get Rs.5000 crores as against the
requirement of Rs.8574. The Committee finds that as part of the FRP and as approved by
the Cabinet, the Government of India had given a commitment to infuse the Equity as per
the FRP. Any delay or shortfall in the infusion of the equity would adversely affect the
implementation of Turn Around Plan and FRP, since Air India would be compelled to borrow
short term loans from Banks in order to bridge the equity gap which would entail payment
of interest, which may adversely affect the profitability of the company. In fact, one of the
conditions under which, the regulator (RBI) and the consortium of Banks supported the
FRP, entails timely equity support to Air India by Government of India.
38. The Committee feels that since Air India has started achieving the milestones as
prescribed in the TAP during the last year, further allocation of Rs.3574 crores as equity
infusion will help the national carrier to implement the turnaround plan smoothly and carry
forward the improved performance in the coming years also. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Government should provide the promised equity infusion which has
been accumulated without any interruption during the course of the financial year.
39. The Committee feels that Air India with its wide range of aircraft in its kitty should
expand its operations beyond metro cities. Since majority of the non-metro airports have
been modernized/developed by Airports Authority of India, the national carrier should make
efforts to penetrate those unexplored markets. When the private carriers are in a position
to offer air services to Tier-II and Tier-III cities, Air India should explore offering its
services to such cities using smaller aircrafts. The Committee still feels that Air India’s
entry into LCC segment will definitely strengthen its business. The Committee recommends

10
that the Air India should try to provide better service and products to the passengers which
have lot of scope for improvement.
40. The Committee also hopes that Air India’s entry into ground handling operations will
add much needed financial fillip for the company. The Committee notes that process of
operationalising the MRO SBU is going on in a snail’s pace and the Government and the
Air India do not appear to be taking adequate initiatives to speed up the process. The
Committee is confident that the MRO SBU and the Ground handling activity of AIATSL, if
planned and executed properly, can bring lot of revenue to Air India. The Committee
recommends that the Government should, if need be, make necessary policy changes to

FDI in aviation sector
77. The Committee was informed that the private airlines in the country are in dire need of funds
for their operations and service upgradation to compete with other global carriers. Denial of access to
foreign capital could result in the collapse of domestic airlines, creating a systemic risk for the financial
institutions, and a vital gap in the country’s infrastructure. Hence, Government has decided to permit
foreign airlines to invest in the capital of Indian companies operating scheduled and non-scheduled air
transport services, up to the limit of 49 per cent of their paid-up capital. The purpose of removing the
existing restriction on investment by foreign airlines is to bring in strategic investors, as few private
equity investors would be willing to invest in a risky business like civil aviation.
78. Government has permitted FDI by foreign airlines in domestic scheduled and non-scheduled
carrier up to 49% of their paid up capital. The provision for investment by foreign airlines has been
made subject to conditions which include:
(i) It would be made under the Government approval route;
(ii) The 49 per cent limit will subsume FDI and FII investment;
(iii) The investments so made would need to comply with the relevant regulations of
SEBI;
(iv) A Scheduled Operator’s Permit can be granted only to a Company:
(a) that is registered and has its principal place of business within India;
(b) the Chairman and at least two-thirds of the Directors of which are citizens of
India and;
(c) the substantial ownership and effective control of which is vested in Indian
nationals.
79. When it was asked about the number of proposals received so far, it was informed that
one proposal for FDI by foreign airlines has been received under the new FDI guidelines from
M/s Air Asia Investment Ltd., a company incorporated under the laws of Malaysia, to make an
investment upto 49% amounting to US$ 15 million (equivalent to INR 80.98 Crore approx.) in the
total paid up equity share capital of a joint venture company to be incorporated in India together
with Telestra Tradeplace Private Limited and Tata Sons Ltd. to undertake the business of operating
Scheduled Passenger Airlines in the domestic civil aviation sector.
80. The Committee finds that the much fancied decision of allowing FDI in civil aviation
sector has not find many takers so far, except the one. The Committee desires to know
whether any study had been carried out by the Government before taking such an
important policy decision. The Committee feels that the Government should assess the
consequences of the new policy in the context of problems faced by Indian domestic airlines
particularly the AI. The Committee recommends that FDI may be allowed in such a way
that it should not affect the operational viability of India’s national carrier – Air India and
various airports of India. The FDI in civil aviation sector should aim at developing aviation
hubs within the country and any policy on the contrary should be discouraged.

of Etihad Airways, and for this reason, is aggressively seeking an increase in capacity entitlements.

Government of India have made huge investments in upgrading infrastructure and building
new world class terminals, they are hampered by the competition posed by these well-
established hubs and their aggressive mega carriers. Allowing Abu Dhabi to come up as
another hub  which is only about three hours flight away from the major Indian metros
might have adverse impact on our efforts to establish a world class hub in India.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
ANNUAL PLAN 2012-13
The Committee finds that the spending pattern of Plan funds has been poor during
2012-13 which needs a lot of improvement. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Civil
Aviation will impress the concerned organizations/undertakings to spend the allocated fund
with proper planning and coordination with respective stakeholders. The Committee notes
that except the investment in Air India, all other organizations were allocated substantially
reduced allocations at RE stage which also could not be optimally utilized. The Committee
feels that reduction of funds at RE stage does not augur well for the much beleaguered
aviation sector. Most of these under-utilizations have been mainly due to finalization of
schemes. The Committee expresses dismay over non-finalization of building plan for BCAS
and DGCA that are pending for so many years. Slow progress in case of NER projects is
a cause of concern to the Committee. The Committee recommends the Ministry to take
necessary steps urgently to finalize/approve these Plan schemes at the earliest to spend the
allocated funds during the current year. (Para 7)
ANNUAL PLAN 2013-14
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation was able to get only 56%
of the projected plan allocations for the current fiscal. The Committee feels that such
inadequate allocation will definitely hamper progress of ongoing infrastructure projects and
other aviation related programmes. The Committee hopes that the Ministry will expedite
the approval of various Schemes and persuade Planning Commission for allocation of more
funds at RE stage. The Committee also hopes that the allocated funds will be utilized
judiciously to avoid any reduced allocation at RE stage. (Para 10)
Air India Limited
Ground Handling SBU:
The Committee notes that despite many labour unrests witnessed during the last
year, the national carrier was able to improve its performance notably both financially and
operationally. It recorded positive EBITDA of Rs.19 crores during the year 2012-13 as
against a negative EBITDA of Rs.2237 crores during the year 2011-12. Air India’s on-time
performance was 84.8% in the case of domestic sector and 74% for international sector as
against the turnaround plan benchmark of 85%. The passenger load factor of Air India was
72% as against the target under turnaround plan of 69.5%. The turnaround plan
benchmark on yield per kilometer was 3.53, against which Air India achieved 3.76.
(Para 36)
The Committee finds that Air India was able to get Rs.5000 crores as against the
requirement of Rs.8574. The Committee finds that as part of the FRP and as approved by
the Cabinet, the Government of India had given a commitment to infuse the Equity as per
the FRP. Any delay or shortfall in the infusion of the equity would adversely affect the
implementation of Turn Around Plan and FRP, since Air India would be compelled to borrow
short term loans from Banks in order to bridge the equity gap which would entail payment

22
of interest, which may adversely affect the profitability of the company. In fact, one of the
conditions under which, the regulator (RBI) and the consortium of Banks supported the
FRP, entails timely equity support to Air India by Government of India. (Para 37)
The Committee feels that since Air India has started achieving the milestones as
prescribed in the TAP during the last year, further allocation of Rs.3574 crores as equity
infusion will help the national carrier to implement the turnaround plan smoothly and carry
forward the improved performance in the coming years also. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Government should provide the promised equity infusion which has
been accumulated without any interruption during the course of the financial year.
(Para 38)
The Committee feels that Air India with its wide range of aircraft in its kitty should
expand its operations beyond metro cities. Since majority of the non-metro airports have
been modernized/developed by Airports Authority of India, the national carrier should make
efforts to penetrate those unexplored markets. When the private carriers are in a position
to offer air services to Tier-II and Tier-III cities, Air India should explore offering its
services to such cities using smaller aircrafts. The Committee still feels that Air India’s
entry into LCC segment will definitely strengthen its business. The Committee recommends
that the Air India should try to provide better service and products to the passengers which
have lot of scope for improvement. (Para 39)
The Committee also hopes that Air India’s entry into ground handling operations will
add much needed financial fillip for the company. The Committee notes that process of
operationalising the MRO SBU is going on in a snail’s pace and the Government and the
Air India do not appear to be taking adequate initiatives to speed up the process. The
Committee is confident that the MRO SBU and the Ground handling activity of AIATSL, if
planned and executed properly, can bring lot of revenue to Air India. The Committee
recommends that the Government should, if need be, make necessary policy changes to
enable these two agencies to function on proper business and commercial prudence. The
Committee notes significant headway in implementing the human resources merger
programmes. The Committee recommends that all the outstanding issues must be settled as
early as possible to enable Air India to function as a single entity. (Para 40)
The Committee further recommends that before taking any policy decision in respect
of AI, proper study and analysis of its impact on the functioning and viability of Air India
should be carried out. Air India is our national carrier in which thousands of crores of
rupees of taxpayers’ money has been invested. The Committee, therefore, calls upon the
Ministry and AI to keep this fact always in mind while taking any decision. (Para 41)
The Committee feels that with a committed annual Government equity, Air India can
improve its performance more than the parameters mentioned in TAP in the coming years
and reaffirm its status of ‘Maharaja’. (Para 42)
Hotel Corporation of India
The Committee finds that Hotel Corporation of India was allocated Rs.25.00 crores
(BE) for 2012-13 which was reduced to Rs.I0.00 crores at RE level. HCI was able to spend
the entire revised allocation for its various schemes. Now, it has been allocated with a paltry
sum of Rs.5.00 crores for 2013-14 for all the four units. The Committee feels that such a
nominal investment for these units under HCI does not inspire any confidence. If the idea
is to do away with these properties, Committee does not know. The Committee hopes that
allocation of funds will be mainly utilized to renovate/upgrade Centaur Hotel Srinagar and

23
Chefair Flight Catering Delhi and more funds would be demanded at RE stage and complete
the same within the fixed time-frame. (Para 47)
Airports Authority of India
The Committee finds that the AAI was allocated a budgetary support of Rs.280 crores
(BE) for the year 2012-13 while it has been reduced to Rs.42.00 crores in BE 2013-14. Out
of this, Rs. 32.00 crore is exclusively for development works at airports in Pakyong and
Tirupati. The Committee was informed that AAI’s requirement for various projects under
Grant-in-Aid Schemes is to the tune of Rs.460.21 crores. The Committee feels that such a
drastic cut in budgetary support for the airport infrastructure developer of the country will
definitely slow down the ongoing projects especially in North-Eastern Region. The
Committee hopes that necessary funds will be allocated to AAI at the RE stage so that its
ongoing as well as future projects are not delayed for want of funds. The Committee also
recommends that pending balance of Rs.77 crores be released for the GAGAN project at the
earliest opportunity. (Para 61)
The Committee also finds that out of the required 3500 ATCs to control the Indian
skies only 2300 personnel are available. The Committee hopes that AAI  will take necessary
steps to address the issue by augmenting its training facilities at Allahabad. (Para 62)
The Committee was informed on the deadlock persisting over the development of
Patna airport. The Committee reiterates its observation/recommendation contained in its
174
th Report in respect of Patna Airport last year and hopes that the AAI will pursue the
matter with the State Government of Bihar for ensuring some viable alternate plan taking
into consideration impending danger for the air travelers to Patna. The Committee would
like to have a status note including efforts made, alternate proposals and the results
thereof. (Para 63)
Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited
The Committee finds that as against the projected demands of Rs.5.00 crores under
plan, the Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited was not able to get any budgetary support from
the Government. The Committee feels that necessary budgetary support, if given to PHHL
at RE stage, it can take up its capacity building and skill development/expansion projects
without delay. The Committee feels that PHHL should expand its business beyond off-shore
operations and present itself as a viable air service provider in unexplored tourist
destinations. The Committee also hopes that heliport project at Rohini, Delhi is completed
as per its schedule. (Para 69)
Directorate General of Civil Aviation
The Committee finds that DGCA was allocated Rs.60.00 crores (BE) under plan head
for the year 2012-13 which was reduced to Rs.13.00 crores at RE level. However, an amount
of Rs.4.27 crore only was spent out of the allocated amount. The main reason attributed to
the non-utilization fund was the non-finalization of headquarters building for DGCA. The
Committee hopes that all the land related issues for the building project will be sorted out
with the concerned agencies and the project is taken-up further. The Committee further
hopes that the spending pattern of DGCA will improve during the current fiscal. The
Committee recommends that the process of filling-up of vacancies in DGCA should be
expedited so that the day-to-day functioning of the principal regulatory body of the country
is not affected. (Para 73)

Bureau of Civil Aviation Security
The Committee finds that the construction of new building for BCAS is pending for
a long time due to some land related issues. The Committee hopes that pending issues on
the new building project may be addressed at the earliest.
The Committee also hopes that vacant posts in BCAS are filled-up on priority to

FDI in aviation sector
The Committee finds that the much fancied decision of allowing FDI in civil aviation
sector has not find many takers so far, except the one. The Committee desires to know
whether any study had been carried out by the Government before taking such an
important policy decision. The Committee feels that the Government should assess the
consequences of the new policy in the context of problems faced by Indian domestic airlines
particularly the AI. The Committee recommends that FDI may be allowed in such a way
that it should not affect the operational viability of India’s national carrier – Air India and
various airports of India. The FDI in civil aviation sector should aim at developing aviation
hubs within the country and any policy on the contrary should be discouraged. (Para 80)
Bilaterals
The increase in bilateral capacity put the premium that Ethihad paid in perspective.
The Committee is aware that bilateral negotiations between two countries for increasing
airline seats take place only when the existing allotments become insufficient. The
Committee understands that concerned domestic Airlines have not exhausted those allotted
seats. In such a situation, the Committee is surprised to see this increase in the bilateral
to 36,670 seats a week by the Government. Prima facie this move appears to facilitate one
airline to strike a deal with a foreign Airline for its stake sale at a huge premium. The
Committee feels that the huge premium could be a backhanded way of obtaining access to
the huge Civil Aviation market in India. (Para 82)
The Committee finds that already, India is struggling to create a world class hub at
Delhi, faced with competition from airports like Dubai and Doha in the Gulf and Bangkok,
Hong Kong and Singapore in South East Asia and despite leading airports like Delhi and
Mumbai having been privatized. While Delhi and other private airports along with the
Government of India have made huge investments in upgrading infrastructure and building
new world class terminals, they are hampered by the competition posed by these well-
established hubs and their aggressive mega carriers. Allowing Abu Dhabi to come up
as another hub which is only about three hours flight away from the major Indian metros
might have adverse impact on our efforts to establish a world class hub in India.
(Para 87)
The Committee feels that creating hubs needs time and also a home carrier with a
critical size to be able to sustain the hub. Air India has just started to create a hub in Delhi
as its chosen hub, which needs to be encouraged/supported in its endeavour. The development
of events will, no doubt, have adverse impact on Air India’s efforts in this regard. (Para 88)

(Para 93)
Sale of bilateral by Jet Airways
The Committee was informed that Jet Airways sold three of its slots in London to
the Ethihad Airlines and the Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation has confirmed that the
Ministry is aware of the development. The Committee feels that carriers have no right to
sell the bilateral allotted to them to other airlines that too foreign airline. The Committee
recommends that the Government should take away the slots from Jet Airways and the
airlines should be penalized for selling the national property – bilateral. (Para 94)
Setting up of Aranmula Airport in Kerala
The Committee finds that the proposed Greenfield airport in Aranmula (Kerala) is
120 km. from Trivandrum and 90 km. from Cochin, both are international airports and it
is violative of the Government policy of not allowing an airport within 150 km. Moreover,
the Kochin International Airport and Thiruvananthapuram Airport are brand new airports
and there is no scope for saturation of capacity in these airports in near future. The
Committee notes that the proposed Aranmula Airport is violating the principles of 150 kms.
distance rule and the saturation of the nearby existing airports. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the proposal for construction of Aranmula Airport may be reconsidered by
the Government. (Para 99)
