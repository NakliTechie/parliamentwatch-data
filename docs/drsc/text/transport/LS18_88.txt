9.         The Committee notes that Ministry of Civil Aviation has failed to fully utilize the outlay approved during 2004-
05.  The Ministry anticipates to utilize an amount of Rs.1473.03 crore out of the approved allocation of Rs.1620.98
 crore during the year 2004-05, which exemplifies the poor financial management in the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The
 Committee feels that there is a lack of proper planning in the various organisations of Ministry of Civil Aviation, as a
 result of which the Ministry was unable to utilize optimally the approved outlays at its disposal.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress upon the concerned agencies to make a
 realistic assessment of their financial requirements so that the allocations made do not remain unutilized. 
ANALYSIS OF THE AVIATION POLICY
10.       The Committee notes that the civil aviation sector has grown in the country during the recent years.  As per the
 aviation policy being pursued by the Government, the Ministry of Civil Aviation has taken a number of steps which
 have affected the airlines and the airports in the country.  The Government has taken decision to allow Air India and
 Indian Airlines to proceed with the acquisition process of aircraft. The Government has allowed private domestic
 carriers to fly on international routes also.  With respect to the airport sector, the Government has taken steps for
 modernization and restructuring of the Delhi and Mumbai airports.  The Government has also taken steps towards the
 development of Greenfield airports at Bangalore and Hyderabad. 
 
11.       The aviation policy being pursued by the Government will have a bearing on the various aspects of the civil
 aviation sector in the country in general and the public sector airline companies in particular.   The Committee analysed
 the effect of the aviation policy of the various aspects of the civil aviation sector such as opening of international routes
 to private airlines, requirement of trained manpower, infrastructure and maintenance facilities at airports, modernization
 and restructuring of the Delhi and Mumbai airports, development of Greenfield airports at Bangalore and Hyderabad,
 high cost of air turbine fuel (ATF), review of Route Dispersal Guidelines, impact of Fringe Benefit Tax, requirement of
 an eastern hub at Kolkata etc. The details of the analysis of the Committee have been discussed in the succeeding
 paragraphs of the report.

16.       The Committee noted that the Net Profit of Indian Airlines was estimated to go down from Rs.44.17 crore in
 2003-04 to Rs.8.75 crore in 2004-05 and the projection for 2005-06 was merely Rs.11.35 crore.  The Committee
 desired to know the reasons for this decrease.  The Committee further enquired as to how Indian Airlines proposed to
 improve their position during 2005-06.

 Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) for the year 2004-05 had been compiled by Indian Airlines (IAL) in the
 month of October 2004, on the basis of costs prevalent at that time, wherein a Net Profit of Rs 8.75 crore had been
 projected.  However, the Net Profit of Rs.8.75 crore was lower than Rs.44.17 crore in 2003-04 due to increase in the

19.       The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding the details of the rescue and relief
 operations undertaken by the Indian Airlines as well as the private airlines in the aftermath of the Tsunami waves that
 struck the coastal parts of the country in December 2004.  In reply, the Ministry of Civil Aviation stated that a total
 number 182 relief flights (85 from Calcutta & 97 from Chennai) inclusive of 23 cargo flights were operated by Indian
 Airlines during 27-12-2004 to 31-01-2005 in which total number of 11,102 passengers and 295 infants were brought
 out of Port Blair airport during the above period.  Also during the said periods 5797 passengers mainly relief personnel
 and para-military forces were airlifted to Port Blair from Calcutta & Chennai.  Approximately 500 tones of relief
 material were airlifted into Port Blair by civil relief flight.  Alliance Air has operated 139 Relief flights from Chennai &
 Kolkata during 27-12-2004 to 31-01-2005. 8448 passengers and 245 infants were brought out of Port Blair airport; and
 5067 passengers mainly relief personnel and para-military forces were airlifted to Port Blair from Kolkata & Chennai
 and 358.26 tonnes of relief material was airlifted into Port Blair by Alliance Air relief flights.  Jet Airways has operated
 35 Relief flight from Chennai & Kolkata during 27.12.2004 to 17.1.2005. 2654 passengers and 50 infants were brought
 out of Port Blair airport during the above period and 730 passengers mainly relief personnel and para-military forces
 were airlifted to Port Blair from Calcutta & Chennai.  Approximately 53.12 tonnes of relief material was airlifted into
 Port Blair during the above period by Jet Airways relief flights. Blue Dart has operated 08 Cargo Relief flights from
 Chennai & Kolkata from 2.1.2005 to 10.1.2005 & 89.1 tonnes of relief material was airlifted into Port Blair during the
 above period by Blue Dart.
 
20.       The Committee also desired to know about the number of the flights that were cancelled by Indian Airlines and
 the private airlines after the tsunami disaster.  In response, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation that the number of Alliance Air flights cancelled due to operations of relief flights to Port Blair during
 December 2004 and January 2005 was 28 and 17 respectively.  Ministry of Civil Aviation further informed the
 Committee that Jet Airways has intimated that it had not cancelled any flights to the affected areas as a result of the
 Tsunami disaster.

23.       The Committee desired to know from the Ministry of Civil Aviation as to whether revision of pay scales of the
 employees working in Indian Airlines had become due and what were the reasons for delay in revision of pay scales. 
 The Committee also enquired about the recommendations of Mohan Committee constituted in this regard and the
 reasons for not implementing the recommendations of that Committee.
 
24.       The Ministry of Civil Aviation informed the Committee that the revision of pay scales of employees of Indian
 Airlines Limited had become due w.e.f. 1.1.97.  DPE had, vide  its O.M. dated 25.6.1999, issued guidelines for revision
 of scales of pay w.e.f. 1997 for Board level and below Board level posts, based on the recommendations of Justice S.
 Mohan Committee constituted for the purpose.  
 
25.       The Ministry of Civil Aviation further informed that the issue of revision of pay scales based on the above
 referred O.M. for implementation in the PSUs under the control of that Ministry was examined in a meeting convened
 by the then Secretary on 4.1.2000.  In case of Air India/Indian Airlines, it was decided to examine the matter on receipt
 of specific comments of the managements ensuring fulfillment of all the conditions prescribed in DPE guidelines and
 based on the financial implications/savings likely to accrue in this regard.  Indian Airlines was, however, not in a
 position to undertake a wage revision in view of its financial position.  However, increase in emoluments was given by
 way of revision of PLI which was introduced in 1994.  The matter was, however, again under consideration of the
 Government.         
 
26.       The Committee notes that the revision of scales of pay of employees of Indian Airlines had become due on
 1.1.1997.  However, even after a lapse of a period of more than eight years, the Government is yet to take a decision in
 the matter.  The Committee feels that the revision of scales of pay would contribute towards preventing the exodus of
 trained manpower from Indian Airlines. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should take an
 early decision in the matter so that the employees of Indian Airlines are not deprived of their legitimate right to the
 revision of scales of pay which has been pending for long.

OPENING OF INTERNATIONAL ROUTES FOR PRIVATE AIRLINES
 
30.       During the course of the oral evidence of the Secretary and other officers of the Ministry of Civil Aviation held
 on the 30 th March 2005, the Committee drew the attention of the Secretary to the reports appearing in the press

REVIEW OF ROUTE DISPERSAL GUIDELINES
40.       The Committee had, in its 78

 observed that like Indian Airlines, private airlines should also fulfil their social commitments and not prefer to operate
 mostly on lucrative routes and complete the formality of fulfilling their social obligation by flying to the prescribed
 minimum flights to the North-East region under the Route Dispersal Guidelines.  The Committee had noted that Route
 Dispersal Guidelines had been manipulated by the private airlines to the disadvantage of the backward regions.   The
 Committee had recommended that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should evolve some mechanism to ensure that private
 airlines in the country fulfil their social obligation by operating more number of flights in the North-East region.  The
 Committee had also expressed the view that the Route Dispersal Guidelines should be reviewed with a view to making
 them full proof so that no private airline is able to violate them and any such violation should entail heavy disincentive
 to that airline.

 Committee that the Ministry had constituted a Committee under the Chairmanship of DGCA vide  order dated 23-09-
2003 to review the Route Dispersal Guidelines.  The Committee had been mandated to submit its report within two
 months of issue of the order.  The review committee and its members had been provided with the contents of the
 observations of the Committee in this regard so as to incorporate these views while reviewing the present Route
 Dispersal Guidelines.  The committee had been requested to expedite its report.  During the course of the examination
 of Demands for Grants 2005-06 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation, it was informed that the Committee under DGCA to
 review the Route Dispersal Guidelines had submitted its report on 18.3.2005 which was being examined. 
 
42.       The Committee is of the view that the present aviation policy being followed by the Government has not been
 effective in making the private airlines share the social obligations of the country.  The Committee feels that the private
 airlines have been focusing on lucrative trunk routes only.  These airlines have been operating only on the basis of
 commercial terms and tend to follow the bare minimum of what is mandatory for them under the Route Dispersal
 Guidelines. The Committee recommends that the Route Dispersal Guidelines should be revisited to ensure that the
 private domestic airlines also share the social obligation of connecting remote parts of the country in the true sense and
 not by merely completing the formalities in this regard.

66.       During the course of the oral evidence of the representatives of Ministry of Civil Aviation on the 30 th March
 2005, the Committee was informed that the domestic air transport industry in India has been facing serious problems
 due to high cost of operations. One of the major reasons for this is the heavy cost of Air Turbine Fuel (ATF).  Even
 though the pricing of ATF is now on import parity basis, the rates applicable for domestic operations continue to be
 significantly higher than that of international operations.  The cost of ATF goes up even further due to high rates of
 sales tax levied by the State Governments/Union Territory administration varying from 20 per cent to 36 per cent which
 affects the viability of domestic airline operations. 
67.       Expressing concern over the rising prices of ATF, the Secretary, of the Ministry stated as under:-
"Sir, the issue of prices of fuel is a matter of very great concern not only for the public sector airlines but
 also for the private sector.  Now, Sir, we are talking of prices which are now around 57-58 dollars per
 barrel and for airlines it is now becoming unsustainable.  Last year, when the prices were continuously
 increasing, we tried to absorb it in our own operational expenses by trying to trim costs, by improving
 efficiency, and by improving utilization of aircrafts.  Now we have reached the limits where, I am afraid,
 the airlines will have to come up very shortly with some amount of fare increase to absorb this cost".

IMPACT OF FRINGE BENEFIT TAX ON CIVIL AVIATION SECTOR
           
79.       During the oral evidence of the Secretary and officers of Ministry of Civil Aviation before the Committee on
 30th March 2005, the Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding the impact of Fringe Benefit
 Tax on the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  In response, the Committee was informed by the Secretary of the Ministry that
 Fringe Benefit Tax imposed in the Union Budget 2005-06 would have an adverse impact on the Civil Aviation sector in
 the country.  It was further stated that the tax was not exclusively applicable to the Ministry of Civil Aviation and it
 would adversely affect the aviation industry as a whole.  The Committee asked the Ministry of Civil Aviation to furnish
 a detailed note on the subject.  In their written reply, the Ministry of Civil Aviation informed the Committee as detailed
 below.
 
80.       The Union Budget proposals for the year 2005-06 seek to insert a new Chapter XII-H in the Income Tax Act
 relating to Income Tax on fringe benefits.  This tax would be a new tax under the name of Fringe Benefit Tax which
 will be payable by the employer in respect of fringe benefits provided or deemed to have been provided by an employer
 to his employees during the year.  The value of fringe benefits so calculated, is subject to additional income tax in
 respect of fringe benefits at the rate of 30% as provided in section 115WA.  The fringe benefit tax is payable by the
 employer even where he is not liable to pay income tax on his total income  computed in accordance with the other
 provisions of this Act.

 the employer to the general public as reduced by any amount paid or recovered from the employee;
b)      the actual amount of contribution made by the employer to the approved superannuation fund;
c)      a specified percentage of each of the expenses enumerated as items in para 81 above.
 
83.       In cases where the employer is engaged in the business of carriage of passengers or goods by motor car or by
 aircraft, a lower percentage of expenses on repair, running and maintenance of percentage of the expenses incurred on
 hospitality has been specified for purpose of calculating the liability under the fringe benefit tax.
 
84.       The Ministry of Civil Aviation pointed out that while most heads of expenses specified above are generic and
 are incurred by all businesses i.e., telephones, sales promotion & publicity, entertainment, staff welfare etc., certain
 items are specific to airline industry and their special nature needs to be considered.
(i)
                  Cost of free or concessional ticket for private journeys of employees or their family members: As per the
 proposed Fringe Benefit Tax provisions, this benefit is to be valued at a cost at which the tickets are provided by
 the employer to the Public.  All employees or airlines are entitled to free/concessional passengers in accordance
 with IATA Regulations.  These tickets are purely on the basis of subject to availability. This means that
 employees/families are allowed to travel only when spare seats are available which could not be sold to the
 public at large and that too at the time of departure of the flight.  These seats would otherwise have gone un-
utilized/vacant and hence no value can be attributed to such travel.  As per the proposed provisions of Fringe
 Benefit Tax, these free/concessional tickets have to be evaluated at the fare at which these tickets are offered to
 the public.  Apparently these provisions were intended in case of business which provided free/concessional
 tickets on firm basis as a part of the Leave Travel Facility and these business themselves are not engaged in the
 business of transportation of passengers.

 skopograph, Ceilograph etc., the met-data provided to pilots are sometimes inaccurate as these are collected through
 mere visual observations.  Therefore, a proper MET observatory may be opened at Lengpui Airport.  The Committee
 accordingly recommends that the Aizawl airport should be upgraded from VFR to IFR status and a proper Metrological
 observatory equipped with all modern gadgets like, skopograph, Ceilograph etc., should also be installed there.

ANALYSIS OF THE AVIATION POLICY
 
            The Committee would like to record its appreciation regarding the role played by Indian Airlines and Alliance
 Air in evacuation of people from Andaman & Nicobar Islands after the tsunami disaster and heavy snowfall in parts of
 Jammu & Kashmir in the winter season this year.  The Committee notes that Indian Airlines is expected to earn a profit
 of Rs.8.75 crore by the end of the financial year 2004-05.  The Committee understands the reasons for the low amount
 of profit likely to be earned by Indian Airlines.  The Committee observes that Indian Airlines does not have sufficient

 aircraft available with Indian Airlines is on the higher side.  Also Indian Airlines has to take the lion's share in
 undertaking social responsibilities of the nation.  As part of its social obligation, Indian Airlines also has to fly to those
 unremunerative destinations, which other airlines choose not to fly due to commercial considerations.  The Committee,
 therefore, appreciates that, inspite of the odds being placed heavily against the company, Indian Airlines would be
 ending the financial year 2004-05 with marginal profits.
 
            The Committee notes that the revision of scales of pay of employees of Indian Airlines had become due on
 1.1.1997.  However, even after a lapse of a period of more than eight years, the Government is yet to take a decision in
 the matter.  The Committee feels that the revision of scales of pay would contribute towards preventing the exodus of
 trained manpower from Indian Airlines. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should take an
 early decision in the matter so that the employees of Indian Airlines are not deprived of their legitimate right to the
 revision of scales of pay which has been pending for long.

OPENING OF INTERNATIONAL ROUTES FOR PRIVATE AIRLINES
 
            The Committee is not convinced with the reply given by the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding its decision to
 open up international skies to a particular airline and granting it passage rights to some international routes on the
 opening day of its public offer.  The Committee is of the view that the timing of the decision pushed up the company's
 IPO prospects and the policy adopted by the Government and the clearances granted by the Ministry to the company
 were too close to the public offer of the company to be termed as coincidence.  The Committee feels that grant of
 speedy clearances to the company helped the airline get a good opening for its equity offering.  The Committee is,
 therefore, of the view that the conduct of Ministry of Civil Aviation in granting speedy clearances to the airline and the
 policy changes should be further enquired into by an independent agency.
 
            The Committee is not convinced with the reply furnished by Ministry of Civil Aviation with regard to the
 criteria adopted in selection of scheduled domestic airlines to be allowed to fly on international routes.  The Committee

 Ministry of Ministry of Civil Aviation did not give a fair chance to the other scheduled domestic private airline
 operating in the country. The Committee is also of the view that Ministry of Civil Aviation did not carry out any due
 diligence of the performance record of the existing private sector airlines in terms of fulfilling the social obligations by
 flying to remote and inaccessible areas of the country and utilizing the costly technologies installed at the airports such
 as ILS CAT-III system.
 
            The Committee notes that there is a contradiction between the objectives stated by the Ministry of Civil Aviation

 Aviation to the private airlines. The Committee observes that the basic objective of the Government in allowing private
 airlines to fly to international destinations was providing connectivity to such destinations which the Ministry had not
 been able to provide in the past because of lack of capacity of Air India and Indian Airlines.  However, the Ministry of
 Civil Aviation has granted permission to these private airlines to fly to those destinations which were already being
 serviced by Air India and Indian Airlines.  Hence the stated position of the Government in this respect is in total
 contradiction with the actual decisions of the Government and the basic objective of the Government to provide
 connectivity to new international destinations has been defeated.

REVIEW OF ROUTE DISPERSAL GUIDELINES
            The Committee is of the view that the present aviation policy being followed by the Government has not been
 effective in making the private airlines share the social obligations of the country.  The Committee feels that the private
 airlines have been focusing on lucrative trunk routes only.  These airlines have been operating only on the basis of
 commercial terms and tend to follow the bare minimum of what is mandatory for them under the Route Dispersal
 Guidelines. The Committee recommends that the Route Dispersal Guidelines should be revisited to ensure that the
 private domestic airlines also share the social obligation of connecting remote parts of the country in the true sense and
 not by merely completing the formalities in this regard.

REQUIREMENT OF TRAINED MANPOWER
            The Committee is of the view that as per the policy being pursued by the Government in granting licenses to
 new private airlines, a lot of investment is coming in the airline industry.  Vast expansion of fleet is also taking place in
 the existing national and private carriers.  The Committee feels that there is a mismatch between the pace at which the
 airline fleet is expanding and the human resource being groomed for the same thereby, resulting in tremendous shortage
 of trained manpower.  The Committee recommends that Ministry of Civil Aviation should undertake an objective
 assessment of the requirement of trained manpower for civil aviation sector as a whole in the country on priority basis.

            The Committee is constrained to note that while the current policy of the Government aims at allowing more and
 more airlines to operate in the country, it has not made it obligatory for them to utilize the modern facilities installed at
 the airports with huge investments for the convenience of the passengers.  The Committee is surprised to note that the
 three private airlines i.e. Jet Airways, Sahara Airlines and Air Deccan have not trained any of their pilots for CAT-III
 operations and there is no legal requirement in the Aircraft Rules and Civil Aviation requirements to make it mandatory
 for private airlines to train their pilots on ILS CAT-III System.  The Committee is also not satisfied with the progress
 achieved with respect to the training of the pilots by the National Carriers viz. Indian Airlines and Air India, for
 utilizing the CAT-III System.  The Committee recommends that in order to improve the operational efficiency during
 the extreme foggy conditions, the Ministry should amend 'Aircraft Rules' to incorporate a provision for making training
 of pilots compulsory by the private airlines on CAT-III System so that the investment made by the Government on the
 installation of the system through the tax payers' money is not underutilized.   The Committee hopes that the national
 carriers and the private airlines will optimally utilize the ILS CAT-III System installed in the IGI Airport, New Delhi
 during the period of poor visibility, which will certainly reduce the hardships faced by the passengers.

ALLOTMENT OF PARKING BAYS
            The Committee is constrained to note that before giving licenses to new airlines to commence operations, the
 Government did not deliberate on the infrastructure and maintenance facilities required at the airports after these

 hangers and parking bays are made available for all the airlines providing a level playing field in the matter of allotment

3.         Thereafter, the Committee was apprised by the officials of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that as per the Rule
 134 of Aircraft Rules, 1937 Route Dispersal Guidelines (RDG) have been issued by the Ministry and accordingly air
 connectivity has been provided to North-eastern region. The guidelines stipulate that all domestic operators have to
 deploy at least 10% of their total capacity in the routes connecting North-eastern States, Jammu & Kashmir,
 Lakhadweep and Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Cat-II routes) out of which 10% has to be deployed for connectivity
 with these regions (Cat-II routes).  The Committee enquired from the Ministry about the reason as to why private
 airlines are flying only to the destinations of their own choice.  In view of the difficult terrain and inaccessibility of the
 region by road, there exists a strong requirement for more flight-connectivity to all north-eastern States, though some of
 these airports are non profit making destinations. Accordingly, the private operators should also share the burden and
 provide more flights to all destinations instead of just fulfilling the requirement of Route Dispersal Guidelines.  The
 Ministry official replied that Route Dispersal Guidelines were framed long back and an urgent need has been felt to
 review the Guidelines. They also stated that Naresh Chandra Committee had also recommended for this.  They assured
 that concern of Committee will be taken care of and Route Dispersal Guidelines will be revised very soon.
 
4.         The Committee was informed that new terminal building has the capacity of 12000 passengers per month,
 whereas, the total number of visitors is approximately 4500-6000 per month.  The total number of aircraft movements
 are 46 per week by scheduled operators, i.e. Indian Airlines, Alliance Air and Jet Airways.  The B.S.F. and Indian Air
 Force are operating non-scheduled flights frequently including AVRO, IL76, AN32 and helicopter movements.  The
 Committee was further apprised that the Imphal Airport is equipped with all modern navigational facilities like
 Instrument Landing System (CAT-I) on Runway on VOR/DME, NDB, PAPI and Approach Lighting System.  The
 Runway length is 9010 ft. which is capable of handling Airbus-320 and Boeing 737-800 series aircrafts without any
 load restrictions.  The Runway 04 is the Precision Approach Runway equipped with ILS whereas Runway 22 is the
 usual runway.  Recently, the New Taxiway and Apron was constructed which can accommodate 4 aircraft (1 ATR, IB-
737, Indian Airlines -320, Indian Airlines -300).  Also the Isolation Bay has been constructed and commissioned at
 Imphal Airport for any eventuality. 
 
5.         The Chairman of the Committee emphasised that while constructing the terminal building local art and
 architecture should be kept in mind and building should be designed accordingly.  Civil Aviation and Tourism go hand
 in hand as airport is the gateway for the tourists, which should unveil the rich cultural and architectural heritage of the
 country.  This would also attract more and more tourists and would substantially take care of the capacity under
 utilisation problem of the airports.  The Member of the Committee also emphasised that adequate care should be taken
 with the help of the State Government to open shopping outlets/cafeteria for showcasing local cuisines/handicrafts etc.
 
6.         The Committee was intimated that Imphal being the Hyper-sensitive Airport is equipped with fool-proof security
 systems including the CCTV system.  The peripheral security of the Airport is provided by the CRPF whereas the State
 Police is looking after the Anti-Hijacking Control Measures.  The facilities like Air Traffic Control Communication
 Navigation Surveillance, Fire and Rescue Service (Cat-5 Cat-6) are provided as per International (ICAO)
 specifications.  The total officers and staff strength available at Imphal under AAI is 120 with various disciplines like
 Air Traffic Control, Communication Navigation Surveillance, HRD, Fire and Rescue Services.  The conservancy is
 given a contract through AMC which is well-maintained through modern equipments.
 
7.         While discussing the security aspect at the airport the Committee raised the issue of payment for security at non-
operational airports also.  The Chairman of the Committee also emphasised that in addition to profit and loss
 accountability there should be value audit and development audit in this field.  The Committee also directed the
 Ministry to furnish a written note to the Committee detailing the break-up of security related expenditure and
 developmental expenditure.
