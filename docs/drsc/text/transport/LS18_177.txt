REPORT
Ministry of Shipping was formed in 2009 by bifurcating the erstwhile Ministry of Shipping,
Road Transport and Highways into two independent Ministries. Maritime Transport is a critical
infrastructure for the economic development of a country. It influences the pace, structure and
pattern of development. The Ministry of Shipping encompasses within its fold shipping and port
sectors which also include shipbuilding and ship repair, major ports and inland water transport. The
Ministry has been entrusted with the responsibility to formulate policies and programmes on these
sectors and their implementation.

PERFORMANCE DURING XI  PLAN
3. During the XI Plan the total outlay was Rs.45,331.00 crore and the expenditure incurred
was Rs.16,746 crores as informed by the Secretary during the course of oral evidence while
scrutinizing the Demands for Grants. For Port Sector, as per Working Group Report (Port sector)
the total outlay was Rs. 30,305.16 crores out of which budgetary support was Rs. 3749.00 crores
and Rs. 26556.16 crores was Internal Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR).
4. While deposing before the Committee during the course of oral evidence the Secretary
stated:
…..On the Plan side, as you very rightly said, if you look at the outlay and utilization of
the Eleventh Plan, the outlay was Rs. 45,331 crores and the actual expenditure was Rs.
16,746 crores which is 1/3
rd of the total outlay….
…. We have been under-utilising Gross Budgetary Support to the extent of almost 50 per
cent. The plans which our corporations and ports have been making have also not been
materialising. That is why the figure is low. We need to definitely go into the reasons……
5. The Committee was informed that due to various reasons such as delay in various security
related and environmental clearances, delayed approval of new projects, lack of building material
for Tsunami works of ALHW the funds in XI Plan could not be utilized. Besides, problems of
inadequate draft and other facilities at our Ports, old dredgers, high turn round time, which have
slowed the growth of shipping industry.
6. The Committee notes that in the absence of consolidated data for the XI Plan period
with outlay, actual allocation and expenditure for both the Port and Shipping sector, and
there being no physical targets shown, comment on the performance cannot be made.
However, as admitted by Secretary, Ministry of Shipping, underutilizing the Budgetary
support, due to any reason, in Committee’s view, is acceptable only to a limited extent.
7. The Committee was also informed (in the working group report) that only 1/3
rd of the
total outlay of Rs.30305.16 crore during the 11 th plans outlay, i.e. Rs.10388.87 crore was
allocated to the Ministry of Shipping under the port sector. However, it is equally surprising
that the utilization stands at 59.62% (or Rs.7684.61 crore) only. The Committee finds that
this under-utilization was mainly due to reasons such as delay in clearances, approvals, etc.
These are, no doubt, time-taking exercises but if taken up with proper procedure and
formalities as prescribed, it could be expedited easily and quickly. In view of urgent need
to modernize and expand our port facilities and stiff competition with Private Ports staring
at them, we cannot afford to delay projects at our Ports. The Committee recommends that
thorough home work needs to be done before approaching each plan year projects for
optimum utilization of scarce fund allocation.

12. The Committee notes that the funds at BE 2011-12 was reduced to almost half at RE
2011-12 and the utilization was only 50% of BE. The reason submitted by Ministry of Shipping
such as delay in approval, non-submission of extension of dredging subsidy, non-receipt of
proposal/incomplete proposals from shipyards are of routine nature and not convincing.
13. The Port Sector present really a gloomy picture in case of utilization of outlay for
the year 2011-12 where only 55.16% of total allocation could be utilized upto December,
2011. It is just 0.58% in the case of R & D, 47.40% in IT. The post Tsunami works are
still going and only 35.31% could be utilized during 2011-12 (upto December 2011). ALHW
could utilize only Rs.9.87 crore out of Rs.17.77 (upto December, 2011) which is just 55.54%
of total allocation. A perusal of the statement of expenditure incurred by ports reveals that
there had been shortfall in utilization of funds against the BE/RE during the plan.
14. The Committee is perturbed to note that the spending pattern of the Ministry of
shipping is resulting in cuts in allocations by the Finance Ministry. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry should look into the reasons for low utilization and take
remedial measure by way of proper planning and implementation.
15. The Committee in its earlier reports had also recommended that there is need to
formulate plan and policies in a realistic manner. Besides, all the schemes/plans should be
reviewed to see as to why these are not picking up, particularly subsidy to Shipyards for
which no proposals completed in all respects with proper documentation were received. The
growth of shipping sector is closely linked with the overall development of the country and
therefore, deserves top priority in implementation of plan/schemes by the Ministry of
Shipping.

PORTS
22. Ports provide an interface between ocean transport and terrestrial transport and a gateway
to international trade. There are 12 major ports under the Ministry of Shipping. These 12 ports are
handling about 72 per cent of the maritime cargo of the country.
23. The total capacity of ports in India up to March, 2011 was 1061.79 million tonnes with
670.13 million tonnes for Major Ports and 391.66 million tonnes for Non-Major Ports. As per 12
th
plan projection, Ministry of Shipping has planned to increase the capacity of Major Ports in the
country during 2012-13 to 745.86 million tonnes from 670.13 million tonnes and for Non-Major
ports it is expected to increase to 447.15 million tonnes from 391.66 million tonnes. Major Ports
have identified various projects/schemes or implementation during 2012-13 to create the targeted
capacity. Allocations in BE 2012-13 to port sector is Rs.380.98 crore (plan) and Rs.404.11 crore
in non-plan. In 2011-12, an outlay of Rs.2194.72 crore was made with GBS of Rs.246.54 crore
& IEBR of Rs.1948.18 crore.

28. Under RE 2011-12 for ports additional funds were asked under V oCPT capital dredging
from Rs.35 crore to Rs. 65 crore but the same could not be used since approval of competent

th plan period
could not fulfill the projected quantity of capital dredging and maintenance dredging in the major
ports. A total quantity of 675.25 million cubic meter (MCM) and 429.99 MCM had been planned
for capital and maintenance dredging respectively for all the ports in the country. This quantity
comprised the dredging to be done for fishing harbours also besides the major and non major ports.

to many factors such as delay or failure to takeoff number of port development projects, financial
and environmental constraints, the need to carry out engineering studies to assess the quantum of
dredging and the type of dredging to be performed, poor response from bidders to undertake the
work are some of the reasons attributable to the shortfall. Overall, the ports had done better in
achieving the target in maintenance dredging. The non major ports had done better than the major
ports in capital dredging work.

32. The  Committee considers the ports as the back-bone of the trade and commerce
thereby strongly linked to the economic growth of a country. The Committee observed the
enhancement of provisions to the port sector under Plan Head to Rs.380.98 crore in the
2012-13 compared to Rs. 246.59 crore in 2011-12 . The expenditure during 2011-12 (upto
December, 2011) is Rs. 75.08 crore only. The Committee, however is of the opinion that
already the port sector was far behind from the projected targets of its capacity additions,
modernization of equipments, deepening of drafts and ship channels etc. and even than they
were not able to utilize the allocation. The Committee is not satisfied with the reasons for
low utilization and expresses its concerns that on the delayed projects, resulting in two- fold
loss - more spending due to cost overruns and loss of revenue which otherwise would have
been generated after completion of the extra capacity. The Committee strongly recommends
for prioritized action on the above.

allocations should be utilized to the maximum because many of the expansion projects are
time-taking ones and required continuous monitoring. The Committee also recommends
that the Major Ports should identify projects for deepening of channel/berths, construction/
reconstruction of berths/jetties, procurement of equipment/floating craft, rail and road

GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
115. The Committee is worried about the track record of the Ministry in utilizing the
allocations in the previous years. It is a matter of concern that the underutilization of
allocation in rampant in the Ministry of Shipping. This shows the casual approach to the
implementation of projects. The Committee notes that the end result of such delays will be
cost overruns as well as revenue losses to the organizations.
116. The Committee also recommends that the increased vessel traffic should be utilized
for generating more revenue from the shipping sector. Accordingly, the possibility of
introducing entry/exit toll for the foreign flagged vessels etc. should be worked out by the
Ministry. Also the coastal surveillance need to be strengthened to avoid the accidents and
other such unfortunate mishaps similar to some incidents reported recently.
117. The Committee is of the view that even though the Indian tonnage is growing as per
the projections, the India fleet are not gaining the momentum. Even the fleet acquisition
plans of the SCI has been passing through a very difficult phase. At the same time Indian
ship building industry and ship repair sector are equally struggling to get enough business.
This is happening at a time when Ship Building Orders are given to foreign shipyard and
Indian ships and dredgers are looking overseas destinations for the repair works. The
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Shipping should make an assessment of the
reasons for this contradictory situation and should make remedies for the same.
118. The Committee is concerned over the environmental impact of the ship breaking
activities and the frequent dumping of abandoned vessels and waste materials into the
Indian territorial waters. The Committee recommends for adequate steps to curb the
malpractices in the ship breaking business. The Government should also ensure that old and
unused vessels are not dumped in the Indian Waters.
119. The Committee also notes that Major Ports are getting a raw deal from Government
in many ways. They have to take clearances from both Central and States Governments. In
case of Minor Ports, environmental clearances are taken only from concerned States. The
Committee was also informed that Kandla Port is facing delay in providing road connectivity
for a small stretch of about eight kilometers. This has put Kandla Port in disadvantageous
position vis-à-vis the neighbouring private Minor Port in developing their business. The
Committee is of the view that the interests of Major Ports are not made to languish at the
cost of Private Ports, wherein tax payers crore of Rupees have been invested. The
Committee feels that the Major Ports are loosing business to the Minor Ports; if we
compare the growth rates of the two. The Committee strongly recommends to look into
these aspects and making level playing field for both the Major and Minor Ports.
120. The Committee came to know that in JNPT Port a scanner was setup to scan the
containers a few years back. However the same is not working now. On being asked about
the status of non-functioning of the scanner, the Committee was informed that the entire
management operation and installation of scanner is under the Customs Department and
the Customs Department will install the scanner and operate it. The Committee is of the
view that JNPT and Ministry of Shipping should take immediate action to make the scanner
operational in consultation with the Customs Department. The reasons for the non-
operationlisation of the scanner, till now, may please be furnished to the Committee.
121. The Committee notes that the inland water transport is not being regulated properly
and there are no Rules/Guidelines and if there exists some, it seems it is not being
implemented. The Committee notes one of the worst boat tragedies in Assam, in which
hundreds of people died when a packed steamer carrying over 300 passengers sank in the

23
Brahmaputra river after being caught in a severe storm midstream on 30 th April 2012. The
boat was overloaded, with passengers on its roof. The Committee notes that, it seems, there
is no mechanism in place to check the implementation of safety measures for inland
waterways transport. The Committee recommends that strict enforcement of the Rules
should be ensured by the Ministry to check the recurrence of such accidents.

is of the view that Planning Commission/Ministry of Finance should adopt a realistic view
in the matter to ensure that mandatory allocations for NER from the outlay of Ministry of
Shipping should not remain unutilized. If possible, the above grants be utilized for growth
of Tourism in the region. (Para 114)
GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The Committee is worried about the track record of the Ministry in utilizing the
allocations in the previous years. It is a matter of concern that the underutilization of
allocation in rampant in the Ministry of Shipping. This shows the casual approach to the
implementation of projects. The Committee notes that the end result of such delays will be
cost overruns as well as revenue losses to the organizations. (Para 115)
The Committee also recommends that the increased vessel traffic should be utilized
for generating more revenue from the shipping sector. Accordingly, the possibility of
introducing entry/exit toll for the foreign flagged vessels etc should be worked out by the
Ministry. Also the coastal surveillance need to be strengthened to avoid the accidents and
other such unfortunate mishaps similar to some incidents reported recently. (Para 116)
The Committee is of the view that eventhough the Indian tonnage is growing as per
the projections, the India fleet are not gaining the momentum. Even the fleet acquisition
plans of the SCI has been passing through a very difficult phase. At the same time Indian
ship building industry and ship repair sector are equally struggling to get enough business.
This is happening at a time when Ship Building Orders are given to foreign shipyard and
Indian ships and dredgers are looking overseas destinations for the repair works. The
Committee recommends that, the Ministry of Shipping should make an assessment of the
reasons for this contradictory situation and should make remedies for the same.
(Para 117)
The Committee is concerned over the environmental impact of the ship breaking
activities and the frequent dumping of abandoned vessels and waste materials into the
Indian territorial waters. The Committee recommends for adequate steps to curb the
malpractices in the ship breaking business. The Government should also ensure that old and
unused vessels are not dumped in the Indian Waters. (Para 118)
The Committee also notes that Major Ports are getting a raw deal from
Government in many ways. They have to take clearances from both Central and State
Governments. In case of Minor Ports, environmental clearances are taken only from

30
concerned States. The Committee was also informed that Kandla Port is facing delay in
providing road connectivity for a small stretch of about eight kilometers. This has put
Kandla Port in disadvantageous position vis-à-vis  the neighbouring private Minor Port in
developing their business. The Committee is of the view that the interests of Major Ports
are not made to languish at the cost of Private Ports, wherein tax payers crore of Rupees
have been invested. The Committee feels that the Major Ports are loosing business to the
Minor Ports; if we compare the growth rates of the two. The Committee strongly
recommends to look into these aspects and making level playing field for both the Major
and Minor Ports. (Para 119)
The Committee came to know that in JNPT Port a scanner was setup to scan the
containers a few years back. However the same is not working now. On being asked about
the status of non-functioning of the scanner, the Committee was informed that the entire
management operation and installation of scanner is under the Customs Department and
the Customs Department will install the scanner and operate it. The Committee is of the
view that JNPT and Ministry of Shipping should take immediate action to make the scanner
operational in consultation with the Customs Department. The reasons for the non-
operationlisation of the scanner, till now, may please be furnished to the Committee.
(Para 120)
The Committee notes that the inland water transport is not being regulated properly
and there are no Rules/Guidelines and if there exists some, it seems it is not being
implemented. The Committee notes one of the worst boat tragedies in Assam, in which
hundreds of people died when a packed steamer carrying over 300 passengers sank in the
Brahmaputra river after being caught in a severe storm midstream on 30
th April 2012. The
boat was overloaded, with passengers on its roof. The Committee notes that, it seems, there
is no mechanism in place to check the implementation of safety measures for inland
waterways transport. The Committee recommends that strict enforcement of the Rules
should be ensured by the Ministry to check the recurrence of such accidents. (Para 121)
