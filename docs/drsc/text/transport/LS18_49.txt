REPORT 
 
            The Department of Culture primarily deals with the tangible and intangible heritage.  In a larger perspective, it
 also addresses the issues relating to the history, values and beliefs in conjunction with several other Ministries and
 Departments.  Culture being considered one of the keys to Human Resource Development, in the Five Year Plans,
 suitable emphasis was laid on development of Culture from the grass roots.  Relentless efforts made in various fields of
 Culture have started bearing fruit.  A number of institutions have been actively associated with the promotion,
 preservation and dissemination of the rich cultural heritage of India. 
2.         The Department of Culture was set up in 1985 through the 174
th Amendment of the Government of India
 (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961.  It was part of the Ministry of Human Resource Development before its transfer
 to the Ministry of Culture, Youth Affairs and Sport.  Since 27th May 2000, it forms part of the Ministry of Tourism and
 Culture and is known as the Department of Culture.  The Department is responsible to promote art and cultural mutual
 understanding and goodwill and foster closer relations with foreign countries.  Preservation of ancient and historic
 monuments and records, exploration and excavation of archaeological sites and remains, maintenance and expansion of
 libraries and museums of national importance and observance of centenaries and anniversaries of eminent personalities
 are among its other responsibilities. 
3.         The main functions/activities of the Department can be broadly classified under the following heads:- 
Maintenance and Conservation of heritage, ancient monuments and historic sites.
Administration of Museums and Libraries.
Observing Centenaries and anniversaries of important national personalities and events.
Promoting institutions and organisations of Buddhist and Tibetan Studies.
Entering into cultural agreements with foreign countries and holding of Festival of India abroad.
Anthropology and Ethnology.
Archives and Archival Libraries.
Academies and National School of Drama.
Promotion and dissemination of Culture.
Memorials.
 
4.         The Committee was also informed that while full attention will be given to all the above mentioned sectors,
 activities of the department will be particularly focused on:- 
Conservation and preservation of cultural heritage;
Schemes of the National Cultural fund;
Linkage between education and culture;
Development of a public library movement;
Strengthening of Museums and Art Galleries; and
Promotion of Culture and dissemination. 
5.         The Committee appreciates the decision of the Government to bring Department of Tourism and Culture within
 the purview of one Ministry.    The Committee feels that Culture and Tourism are interdependent and interrelated
 subjects.  India is having enormous monuments, museums, historical spots, and palaces etc., which need to be exploited
 from the tourism point of view.  The Government should have considered to keep both the Departments together very
 long back, so that synergy between the two Departments would have been achieved. Sooner or later now Government
 have realized and brought the two Departments under one Ministry. Most of the cultural sites, monuments, etc. are
 attractive spots of the tourist interest.  The Committee also feels that the coordination between Department of Culture
 and Tourism is very important for proper utilisation of the tourist value of the Cultural spots/monuments. A number of
 times, the Committee has observed that there has been contradiction in the approach of the two departments.  The
 Department of Tourism is interested in having more and more tourists whereas the Department of Culture, particularly
 ASI, discourages the tourists with a view to preserve/protect the monuments.  The Committee recommends that the two
 departments should try to achieve equilibrium between the approaches of preservation/ protection of monuments and
 arrival of tourists to these monuments.  Since the Departments of Tourism and Culture are now the parts of the same
 Ministry, the Committee hopes that a higher level of synergy will be achieved by doing away their differences.

Archaeological Survey of India 
9.         The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), an attached office of the Department of Culture, was established in
 1861.  It is the pioneering organisation in field of archaeological oriented activities.  The main task of the ASI is to
 maintain, conserve and preserve the monuments declared protected under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological
 Sites and Remains Act of 1958 and to set up and recognize the site museums, to develop geographic research and to
 conduct training in archaeology, conducting excavation/exploration of historical sites.  At present, there are 3606
 monuments declared by ASI, which are of national importance.

13.       The Committee is of the view that conservation of our heritage is important, both for domestic tourists as well as
 for attracting overseas tourists.  However the meagre plan budget of the department does not allow it to achieve its
 targets.  The Committee observes that in comparison to the amount proposed for this financial year also, the ASI has
 been allocated a meagre amount for its plan projects. The Committee expresses its concern over the shortage of
 Monument Attendants/Security guards for so many centrally protected Monuments in the country. The Committee also
 noted that in some cases the conservation and protection of monuments are in the hands of low paid security guards
 who are not trained for the purpose. Despite being recommended to this affect by the Parliamentary Committee on
 Human Resource Development for the last two/three years, no sincere effort has been made by the Department to solve
 this problem.  Last year it was informed to the Committee that the Planning commission has asked the Department to
 identify the monuments for de-protection and transfer them to the State Archaeology Departments for maintenance in
 order to reduce its own liability.  This time the Department has informed that the creation of posts is subject to approval
 of the Finance Ministry.  However, in view of the order of the Ministry of Finance imposing a ban on creation of new
 posts, it has not been possible to proceed further in the matter. The Committee feels that the delay in proper manning of
 the historical monuments by the ASI will definitely affect the preservation/ conservation of the monuments and will
 defeat the very purpose of the Department.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that serious efforts should be
 made towards regular appointment of monument attendants/security guards who are well trained to take care of the
 monuments. 
14.       The Committee was informed that severe paucity of funds is one of the main reasons why the ASI has not been
 able to carry out large-scale intensive projects.  This necessitated a step towards finding some alternative
 arrangements.  The Government has setup Cultural Fund during the year 1996 in order to facilitate the infusion of
 private and public sector funds into the field of heritage preservation and also to enable institutions and individuals to
 perform their rightful role in promoting and preserving India’s cultural heritage. Keeping in view this in the mind the
 ASI has also invited private sector participation for the upkeep and maintenance of the old monuments, the ASI has
 signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with Apeejay Group of Hotels through National Cultural Fund for
 environment upgradation and for the illumination of the monument.  Following public/private parties have approached

15.       The Committee is disappointed to note that while the  Government of India have added Rs.85.00 crores to its
 exchequers by way of charging entry fees to monuments from the visitors during the year 2000-2001,  the ASI has been
 allocated an amount of Rs.46.00 crores only to carry out its preservation/conservation activities against its requirements

 feels that visitors will not mind paying even higher fees as they will have some sort of satisfaction that the amount so

 Corporation, Old World Hospitality and Agha Khan Trust etc. for preservation and restoration of monuments in Delhi.

17.            Excavation also one of the major activity of the ASI. The Committee was informed that a number of sites and
 ancient remains, dating from pre-historic to medieval times have been discovered in different parts of the country under
 various schemes, including problem-oriented investigation, Village to Village Survey, salvage operation besides chance
 findings.  The Committee was also informed that in 2000-2001, eighteen small and large-scale excavations were
 undertaken.  Work at some of the sites is in continuation of previous years. 
18.       The Committee came to know that the ASI had not published reports on 37 major excavations sites for 30 odd
 years in spite of the fact that these reports are mandatory.  The Committee enquired from the representatives of the
 Department of Culture/ASI so to why the reports in respect of these excavations had not been published so far. While
 replying in affirmative, the ASI informed the Committee that over the years, the practice of excavating something and
 not publishing the reports had become a chronic malaise.  It was explained to the Committee that sometimes a person
 excavates something and then he goes away to some other place and somebody else takes his place.  This new person
 does not have as much interest in writing the reports on the excavation.  The Committee was also informed that the
 Department was contemplating to devise a programme whereby if some archaeologists have retired, they should be
 asked to submit the reports within three to six months and if there are some serving archaeologists, they should also
 submit it at the earliest. 
19.         The Committee is not satisfied with the reasons submitted by the ASI for non-writing of excavations reports for
 such a long period.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be periodic status reports on the
 excavation works undertaken by the archaeologists so as to add transparency in the functioning of the ASI and the
 utilisation of funds in the excavations works. 
20.       The Committee also noted the amount of Rs.2.36 crores has been allocated for the purpose of restoration/repair
 caused to the monuments due to recent earthquake in Gujarat and the measures proposed to be taken by the ASI for
 restoration/repair of those monuments. 
21.       In reply the Department informed that about 50 monuments in Gujarat, 1 monument in Rajasthan and 2
 monuments in Daman/Diu have been damaged out of which 15 have got major damages.  The restoration of the
 damaged monuments would take two years.  The ASI also sought assistance from the UNESCO to repair the damaged
 monuments.  An amount of Rs. 50 lakhs has been immediately sanctioned for carrying out the necessary repairs. For
 the year 2001-02 an amount of Rs. 2.36 crores has been allocated for the purpose which will be utilised for
 conservation and for repairing the monuments, which were tumbled down, or the monuments requires serious attention
 and conservation.  The ASI also requested the IITS all over the country for engineering assistance, to study the basic
 structure of the monuments with the latest equipment. The Committee was also informed that local architectures, local
 engineers also have come forward to assist the ASI.  All possible efforts have been taken to restore/conservation of the
 damaged monuments. 
22.       The Committee appreciates the promptness shown by the ASI and making a quick assessment to take stock of
 the damages caused to monuments due to recent earthquake of Gujarat.  The Committee also notes that the Department
 has also allocated the amount of Rs.2.36 crores for the purpose in the budget.   The Committee hopes that the
 sanctioned amount will be fully utilised to restore those damaged monuments and however the Committee recommends
 that if necessary the Department may consider increase of the allocation at RE stage to ensure in early completion of
 work of restoration of the damaged monuments.

ANTHROPOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA, KOLKATA
 
39.       The Anthropological Survey of India is a premier research organisation under the Department of Culture.  It

 maintenance, documentation and the study of ethnographic materials as well as of ancient human skeletal remains. 
 During the last 54 years, the activities of the Survey have expanded to a great extent, and to cope with the demands of
 research, seven regional centers, one sub regional center, one permanent field station and eight other field stations have
 been established in different parts of the country.

SYNERGY BETWEEN DEPARTMENTS OF CULTURE & TOURISM
The Committee appreciates the decision of the Government to bring Department of Tourism and Culture within the
 purview of one Ministry.    The Committee feels that Culture and Tourism are interdependent and interrelated subjects. 
 India is having enormous monuments, museums, historical spots, and palaces etc., which need to be exploited from the
 tourism point of view.  The Government should have considered to keeping both the Departments together very long
 back, so that synergy between the two Departments would have been achieved. Sooner or later now Government have
 realized and brought the two Departments under one Ministry. Most of the cultural sites, monuments, etc. are attractive
 spots of the tourist interest.  The Committee also feels that the coordination between Department of Culture and
 Tourism is very important for proper utilisation of the tourist value of the Cultural spots/monuments. A number of
 times, the Committee has observed that there has been contradiction in the approach of the two departments.  The
 Department of Tourism is interested in having more and more tourists whereas the Department of Culture, particularly
 ASI, discourages the tourists with a view to preserve/protect the monuments.  The Committee recommends that the two
 departments should try to achieve equilibrium between the approaches of preservation/ protection of monuments and
 arrival of tourists to these monuments.  Since the Departments of Tourism and Culture are now the parts of the same
 Ministry, the Committee hopes that a higher level of synergy will be achieved by doing away their differences.

LESSER SHARE OF ALLOCATION
The Committee is constrained to note that the share of the Department has not been allocated judiciously and hopefully
 recommends that the balance amount remaining with the Planning Commission should be allocated at the R.E. stage to
 enable it to carry out its normal activities successfully.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA 
(i) SHORTAGE OF MONUMENTS ATTENDANTS
The Committee is of the view that conservation of our heritage is important, both for domestic tourists as well as for
 attracting overseas tourists.  However the meagre plan budget of the department does not allow it to achieve its targets. 
 The Committee observes that in comparison to the amount proposed for this financial year also, the ASI has been
 allocated a meagre amount for its plan projects. The Committee expresses its concern over the shortage of Monument
 Attendants/Security guards for so many centrally protected Monuments in the country. The Committee also noted that
 in some cases the conservation and protection of monuments are in the hands of low paid security guards who are not
 trained for the purpose. Despite being recommended to this affect by the Parliamentary Committee on Human Resource
 Development for the last two/three years, no sincere effort has been made by the Department to solve this problem. 
 Last year it was informed to the Committee that the Planning commission has asked the Department to identify the
 monuments for de-protection and transfer them to the State Archaeology Departments for maintenance in order to
 reduce its own liability.  This time the Department has informed that the creation of posts is subject to approval of the
 Finance Ministry.  However, in view of the order of the Ministry of  Finance imposing a ban on creation of new posts,
 it has not been possible to proceed further in the matter. The Committee feels that the delay in proper manning of the
 historical monuments by the ASI will definitely affect the preservation/ conservation of the monuments and will defeat
 the very purpose of the Department.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that serious efforts should be made
 towards regular appointment of monument attendants/security guards who are well trained to take care of the
 monuments.
(Para 13)
(ii) CREATION OF RECURRING NATURE OF FUND
The Committee is disappointed to note that while the Government of India have added Rs.85.00 crores to its exchequers
 by way of charging entry fees to monuments from the visitors during the year 2000-2001, the ASI has been allocated an
 amount of Rs.46.00 crores only to carry out its preservation/conservation activities against its requirements of Rs.69.90
 crores for the year 2000-2001.  The Committee   is of the view that Government should create a fund of recurring
 nature in which revenue collected by charges of fees from the visitors should be transferred.  The Committee feels that
 visitors will not mind paying even higher fees as they will have some sort of satisfaction that the amount so collected
 will be utilised only for preservation/conservation of monuments.
