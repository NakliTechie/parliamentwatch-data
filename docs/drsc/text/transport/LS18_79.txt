REPORT
           
Ministry of Tourism is the nodal authority for the formulation of national policies and programmes on Tourism and for the
 coordination of activities of various Central Government agencies, State Governments/Union Territories and the Private Sector for the
 development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Ministry of Tourism plays a crucial role in coordinating and supplementing
 the efforts of the State/Union Territories Governments, catalyzing private investment, strengthening promotional and marketing efforts
 and in providing trained manpower resources.  The main activities of the Ministry include all policy matters, i.e. Development Policies,
 Incentives, External Assistance, Manpower Development, Promotion & Marketing and Investment Facilitation etc. Planning, Co-
ordination with other Ministries, Departments, State/ Union Territories Governments; Regulation of Standards and Guidelines;
 Infrastructure & Product Development; Human resource development through institutions and also by setting standards and guidelines;
 Publicity & Marketing; Research, Analysis, monitoring and evaluation; International Co-operation and external assistance are also
 other important functions of this Ministry.
 
2.         The Ministry of Tourism is headed by a Secretary who also officiates as the Director General (Tourism) and is assisted by the

APPROACH IN THE 10th PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2004-05
 
4.         The approach in the 10th Plan aims to stimulate investment and encourage the State Governments to develop unique tourism
 products. It seeks to enhance the employment potential within the tourism sector as well as to foster economic integration through
 developing linkages with other sectors.  The 10th Plan acknowledges the proven advantages of developing tourism for the promotion of
 national integration, international understanding and the earning of foreign exchange besides recognising vast employment generating
 potential of Tourism and furthering the socio-economic development. It also seeks to provide legislative and regulatory support for
 sustainable tourism and protect the interests of both the industry as well as the consumer. It further seeks to involve the rural sector in
 the promotion of all rich areas of tourism viz., rural, heritage, adventure and eco-tourism.  It focuses on the development of competitive
 high quality products and destinations besides addressing the concerns of growth through resolution of contradictions in policy so as to
 achieve inter-sectoral convergence of activities that helps to foster the growth of tourism.

9.         The Committee is happy to note that the plan allocation of the Ministry of Tourism has been revised to Rs.350 crore in R.E.
 2003-04 and the outlay for the annual plan 2004-05 has also been hiked substantially to Rs.500 crore, which is almost 42% over the
 amount of Rs.325 crore allocated in B.E. 2003-04.  However, the Committee is of the view that plan outlay is not sufficient to explore
 the tourism potential available in the country.  The Committee appreciates the fact that keeping in view the earlier recommendations of
 the Committee, the Ministry of Tourism has been able to fully utilize the funds allocated for the first two years of the 10 th Plan period. 
 The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Tourism will also be able to utilize the enhanced allocation for effective implementation of
 the schemes/programmes for development and promotion of tourism as also for creating high quality and dependable tourism
 infrastructure required to sustain the growth.  The Committee recommends that the plan outlay should be utilised in such a way so that
 it can act as a catalyst to attract the additional investments from other sectors for development of tourism in the country.  In this
 process, the Ministry of Tourism will be in a position to make demand for more allocation of funds at R.E. stage.
 
EMPLOYMENT POTENTIAL WITHIN THE TOURISM SECTOR
 
10.        The Ministry of Tourism informed that the objective of annual plan 2004-05 is to enhance the employment potential within the
 tourism sector as well as to foster economic integration through developing linkages with other sectors.  The annual plan 2004-05
 focuses on the following thrust areas for sustainable growth of Tourism in the country:-
·
                       To make tourism as a major engine of economic growth;
·                       To harness the direct and multiplier effects of tourism for employment generation, economic development and
 providing impetus to rural tourism;
·                       To provide a major thrust to domestic tourism which will act as a spring board for growth and expansion of
 international tourism;
·                       To position India as a global brand to take advantage of the burgeoning global travel and trade and the vast untapped
 potential of India as a destination;
·                       To acknowledge the critical role of private sector with government working as an active facilitator and catalyst;
·                       To create and develop integrated tourism circuits based on India’s unique civilisation, heritage and culture in
 partnership with states, private sector and other agencies;
·                       To ensure that the tourist visiting India gets physically invigorated, mentally rejuvenated, culturally enriched,
 spiritually elevated and “feels India within him”;
 
11.        The Committee enquired form the Ministry of Tourism about the measures taken for effective generation of employment for
 travel & tourism sector.  The Committee was informed that India’s travel and tourism share of total employment is at 2.9 percent which
 is extremely less in comparison to other countries of the world.  There is a great potential for creating a large number of new and
 diverse jobs through expanding the frontiers of travel and tourism. The employment potential is the highest in tourism sector as
 compared to any other sectors.  The tourism industry has very strong linkages to the socio-economic progress of the country.  It has got
 a very high revenue capital ratio.  It is estimated that 1 million additional visitors could earn Rs.4300 crore of revenue for the industry. 
 It is also estimated that an investment of Rs.1 million creates 47 direct jobs and 11 indirect jobs, which far surpasses the employment
 potential from agriculture and industry sectors. 
 
 12.       During the course of oral evidence the Secretary informed that
"the Committee that the Ministry is endeavouring to position tourism as a major engine of economic growth and harness
 its direct and multiplier effect for employment generation, economic development and providing impetus to rural
 tourism.  Several schemes are being implemented to strengthen, infrastructure development, marketing and Human
 Resource Development.  These schemes will provide further impetus to generation of employment in this sector."
 
13.        The Committee notes with dismay that India's travel & tourism share of total employment is a meagre 2.9 percent which is far
 too less in comparison to other countries of the world.  This is notwithstanding the fact that employment potential of tourism sector is
 higher than Agriculture and Industrial sectors.  The Committee feels that there is an imperative need to integrate tourism with the socio-
economic objectives of the 10
th Plan so that 3.6 million jobs per year can be created through promotion of domestic and international
 tourism and thereby, India's share of international earnings from tourism can be enhanced. The Committee, therefore, recommends the
 Ministry to take proactive measures to exploit the advantages of tourism in generating employment. This is all the more important
 because tourism generates more employment in comparison to other sectors for every rupee invested.

INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM CIRCUITS
 
15.        This Scheme is basically a merger of all the ongoing schemes for infrastructure development which will also continue during
 2004-05.  The objective of this scheme is to identify important tourist circuits in the country on an annual basis in consultation with
 state/UT Governments and develop them by providing all infrastructure facilities required by the tourists within the identified circuits.
 Improvement of surroundings, construction of budget accommodations, wayside amenities, procurement of adventure sports
 equipments, refurbishment of monuments, construction of tourist reception centres, illumination of tourist sites/monuments and
 mounting of sound and light shows etc. are taken up under this scheme.

ASSISTANCE FOR PRODUCT/INFRASTRUCTURE AND DESTINATION DEVELOPMENT
 
22.        During the financial year 2004-05, an amount of Rs.140 crore has been allocated for Product/Infrastructure and Destination
 Development.  The allocation are proposed to be utilised for completion of works sanctioned during 2002-03 and 2003-04, additional
 works in some tourist destinations and for development of new tourist destinations including rural tourism sites to be identified in
 consultation with the State Governments/UT Administrations during 2004-05. The focus under the scheme is to improve existing
 products and developing new tourism products to world class standards in identified destinations.  The Ministry of Tourism has been
 providing central financial assistance every year to the State Governments for the projects prioritised in consultation with them.
 Selection of destinations for development is made based on their tourism potential in consultation with the concerned State
 Governments/UT Administrations.  Master planning of these destinations is also undertaken so as to develop them in an integrated
 holistic manner.

24.        The Committee notes that the aim of product/infrastructure and destination development scheme is to improve the existing
 tourist infrastructure and create new tourist infrastructure at important tourist places of national and international importance in the
 country.  The Committee, however, notes with dissatisfaction that several new projects for development of infrastructure at identified
 destinations have been sanctioned by the Ministry of Tourism without fixing any time limit for the completion of these projects.  The

 monitoring of the projects.
 
RURAL TOURISM
 
25.        The concept of Rural Tourism has been developed within the product/infrastructure and destination development scheme.  A
 provision of Rs.15 crore has been made during 2004-05 for Rural Tourism Projects.  The objective is to showcase the rural life, art,
 culture and heritage at rural locations thereby, benefit the local community economically and socially and to enable interaction between
 tourists and local population for more enriching tourist experience.
 
26.        The Ministry of Tourism informed that under the scheme, the thrust is to promote village tourism as the primary tourism
 product to spread tourism and its socio-economic benefits to rural and its new geographic regions.  Key rural areas are being identified
 for development and promotion of Rural Tourism.  The implementation is done through a Convergence Committee headed by the
 District Collector.  Activities like improving the environment, hygiene, infrastructure etc. are eligible for assistance. The software
 component i.e. training, capacity & skill building etc. is being met through the UNDP Endogenous tourism project. A maximum of
 Rs.50.00 lakh is sanctioned for each project under this scheme.  The rural tourism sites are identified with care in-consultation with
 state Govts./UT Administrations based on their potential like arts and crafts, tradition, heritage monuments, accessibility from
 established tourist centres etc.

UNIFORMITY AND RATIONALISATION OF TAXES ON HOSPITALITY SECTOR
 
81.        The matter of rationalization of taxes on tourist transport vehicles was discussed in the 30
th meeting of Transport Development
 Council (TDC), which was held on 16th January, 2003.  The meeting was attended by the Ministers and Secretaries of the various State
 Governments.  At the meeting, the State Transport Secretaries generally expressed that the State Government do not favour any special
 dispensations to tourist transport vehicles.
 
82.        The Ministry of Tourism has pursued the matter again with the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and it was agreed that
 the particular issue would be taken up with the States once again.  Accordingly, comments of Transport Authorities in various
 States/UTs have been called for to pursue the matter further.
 
83.        During deliberation in the meeting, the Secretary informed the Committee that;
"the present taxation structure has adverse effects on tourism industry.  This necessitates rationalization, simplification
 and uniformity in the taxation structure.  The present inter-state road taxation is a severe barrier on tourists moving
 from one State to another.  Further States have levied luxury taxes, which has had an adverse impact on tourism.  The
 high and multiplied tax structure reduces the competitiveness of the Indian Tourism Product. A consensus on uniform
 tax structure is therefore necessary."
 
84.        The Committee notes that major impediments to the growth of tourism in India are multiplicity of taxes and lack of conducive
 environment for private sector investment.  The Committee feels that the high and multiplied tax structure reduces the competitiveness
 of the Indian Tourism Products.  The Committee therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism after interaction with all
 appropriate agencies should draw a package/road map and follow up the matter with the Ministry of Finance, so as to evolve uniform
 tax structure for the growth of travel & tourism sector.

PROVIDING A CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS TO TOURISM
 
93.        The Committee desired to know from the Ministry Whether there are any proposal for putting tourism needs in the concurrent
 list.  The Ministry of Tourism informed that the proposal to include 'Tourism' in the Seventh Schedule of Constitution with the
 objective of giving Constitutional recognition to the tourism sector and helping in channeling the development of tourism in a
 systematic manner, and enable Central Government to legislate for tourism development was initiated by the Ministry of Tourism.
 
94.        The proposal to include 'Tourism' in the concurrent list was circulated to all the state Governments/UT Administrations and has
 also been discussed at a Chief Minister's conference and in different forums.  So far, 29 States/Union Territories have agreed to the
 proposal.  The States of Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra and Jammu & Kashmir have, however, opposed the
 proposal.
 
95.        The Ministry of Tourism also informed that to examine and make recommendation for generating consensus and co-ordination
 on issues relating to tourism, a ' Group of Ministers on Tourism Industry and Trade' and a' Standing Committee of Secretaries for
 Tourism' have recently been further strengthened and re-constituted.
 
96.        The Committee is surprised to note that tourism which is such an important sector not only at national but also at international
 level does not figure in any of the three list provided in the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution of India.  The Committee notes with
 dissatisfaction that the Ministry of Tourism could not develop a national consensus emphasizing upon the role of tourism in national
 development and identifying barriers stifling the growth of this sector. The Committee is of the strong view that India's tourism
 performance has failed to match its potential for promoting sustainable tourism products, in the absence of suitable legislative and
 regulatory support.  The Committee, therefore,  recommends the Ministry that concerted efforts should be made to include tourism in
 the concurrent list of the constitution in order to provide constitutional recognition to the tourism sector and enable Central Government
 to legislate for tourism development.

108.      India's wildlife is her most magnificent natural heritage.  It has a virtually unmatched diversity of forest wildlife.  Of the over
 400 parks and sanctuaries which are declared as protected areas, very few are visited by the tourists due to lack of infrastructure.  There
 are many parks and sanctuaries of outstanding natural beauty, with a fairly healthy population of wild animals and rare species of birds,
 mammals.  Wildlife watching in India is more of an adventure than fun.  At times, the sightseeing of endangered animals take days,

 sighting.  Unlike South Africa, where the wildlife is at abundance, the game viewing in India could be an exciting or disappointing

APPROACH IN THE 10th PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2004-05
 
            The Committee is happy to note that the plan allocation of the Ministry of Tourism has been revised to Rs.350 crore in R.E.
 2003-04 and the outlay for the annual plan 2004-05 has also been hiked substantially to Rs.500 crore, which is almost 42% over the
 amount of Rs.325 crore allocated in B.E. 2003-04.  However, the Committee is of the view that plan outlay is not sufficient to explore
 the tourism potential available in the country.  The Committee appreciates the fact that keeping in view the earlier recommendations of
 the Committee, the Ministry of Tourism has been able to fully utilize the funds allocated for the first two years of the 10
th Plan period. 
 The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Tourism will also be able to utilize the enhanced allocation for effective implementation of
 the schemes/programmes for development and promotion of tourism as also for creating high quality and dependable tourism
 infrastructure required to sustain the growth.  The Committee recommends that the plan outlay should be utilised in such a way so that
 it can act as a catalyst to attract the additional investments from other sectors for development of tourism in the country.  In this
 process, the Ministry of Tourism will be in a position to make demand for more allocation of funds at R.E. stage.
(Para 9)
 
EMPLOYMENT POTENTIAL WITHIN THE TOURISM SECTOR
 
The Committee notes with dismay that India's travel & tourism share of total employment is a meagre 2.9 percent which is far
 too less in comparison to other countries of the world.  This is notwithstanding the fact that employment potential of tourism sector is
 higher than Agriculture and Industrial sectors.  The Committee feels that there is an imperative need to integrate tourism with the socio-
economic objectives of the 10
th Plan so that 3.6 million jobs per year can be created through promotion of domestic and international
 tourism and thereby, India's share of international earnings from tourism can be enhanced. The Committee, therefore, recommends the
 Ministry to take proactive measures to exploit the advantages of tourism in generating employment. This is all the more important
 because tourism generates more employment in comparison to other sectors for every rupee invested.

ASSISTANCE FOR PRODUCT/INFRASTRUCTURE AND DESTINATION DEVELOPMENT
           
The Committee notes that the aim of product/infrastructure and destination development scheme is to improve the existing
 tourist infrastructure and create new tourist infrastructure at important tourist places of national and international importance in the
 country.  The Committee, however, notes with dissatisfaction that several new projects for development of infrastructure at identified
 destinations have been sanctioned by the Ministry of Tourism without fixing any time limit for the completion of these projects.  The
 Committee therefore, recommends the Ministry to develop competitive destinations that match international standards in terms of price,
 quality and satisfaction so that these destinations would be able to fully cater to the varied needs of national and international
 travellers.  The Committee also recommends that sufficient allocation to the projects should be made to avoid time and cost overruns as
 a result of which, the investment gets locked in the project.  The Ministry should also fix some physical and financial benchmarks for
 monitoring of the projects.
(Para 24)
RURAL TOURISM
 
            The Committee is of the view that Rural Tourism has great potential and prospects in the country as it helps to revive the local
 peoples' interests and pride in their culture, tradition and values besides generating the tourists interest in the customs of the indigenous
 people as it focuses directly on the local people.  The Committee feels that with a growing interest in the intangible culture of different
 countries (i.e. lifestyle, cuisine, ceremonies, music, religion, beliefs, customs and history), there is an enormous potential to encourage
 global tourism in rural areas. The Committee recommends the Ministry to develop some institutional mechanism for supporting,
 promoting and organizing large number of fairs & festivals and handicrafts & craft melas/bazaars in rural areas which will provide
 exposure, recognition as well as additional flow of income to local artisans.  The Committee further recommends that the Ministry
 should actively coordinate efforts for liaising with other Ministries/Departments of Government of India viz. Ministries of Rural
 Development, Environment and Forests, Textiles & Culture etc. and other concerned organisation/agencies for converging of resource
 from that Ministry/Department and increase allocation for tourism sector through the state budgets as tourism has a greater potential for
 creating a large-scale employment opportunity than any other industry in the country.
(Para 29)
 
ASSISTANCE FOR LARGE REVENUE GENERATING PROJECTS
           
The Committee notes with satisfaction that 6 projects have been sanctioned under the scheme of large Revenue generating
 projects and 100% of plan allocation have been released by the Ministry of Tourism during the year 2003-04.  The Committee
 appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry of Tourism in identifying the projects in consultation with the State Governments and
 timely release of funds for sustainable development and management of tourism products. The Committee hopes that the work on these
 projects would be completed within the specified time schedule since the 100 percent finance have been released by the Ministry. The
 Committee is of the view that while identifying the projects under the scheme, the Ministry of Tourism should have more balanced
 regional distribution of projects. The Committee therefore, recommends the Ministry to evolve some mechanism to ensure better
 implementation, effective control and sustained monitoring of projects.
(Para 32)
CAPACITY BUIDLING IN THE UNORGANIZED SECTOR
 
            The Committee notes the capacity building measures taken by the Ministry for providing basic training to the persons engaged in
 unorganized sector of travel & tourism sector and considers that these measures are taken in the right direction.  The Committee further
 notes that orientation training programmes for customs and immigration staff, police officers posted at the airports etc. have also been
 brought under the purview of this scheme. The Committee is of the view that sensitizing Government staff who come in contact with
 tourists can definitely bring about a positive change in their attitude and recommends that these training programmes should be
 arranged more frequently making them more effective and broad based.
(Para 37)
REVIVAL OF TOURISM IN JAMMU AND KASHMIR
 
            The Committee appreciates the Ministry for sanctioning a package for revival of tourism in Jammu and Kashmir which includes
 financial assistance for up-gradation of infrastructure in the valley viz. Shikara owners, Pony wallas, Houseboat owners and Hotel
 owners etc. through the State Government of Jammu and Kashmir.  This is a land crafted by nature's exquisite artistry and the
 Committee feels that promotion of tourism in the state can help achieve enormous benefits for the state as well as for the country.  The
 State of Jammu and Kashmir, on account of its wide and varied products has been a major driver in the growth of Indian Tourism. 
 Therefore, the Committee recommends that to showcase the beauty and splendour of Jammu and Kashmir and also to meet the
 increased demand of tourists, the Ministry should help resolving financial problems of service providers and provide better
 infrastructure for Jammu and Kashmir.  The Committee also recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should have constant contact

DOMESTIC PROMOTIOIN & PUBLICITY INCLUDING HOSPITALITY
 
            The domestic tourism is the foundation of a viable and sustainable tourism industry.  Much of the growth of global tourism has
 been generated by domestic tourism.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that there are no precise estimates of total
 domestic tourist traffic and observes that the potential for domestic tourism is massive and requires focused approach.  The Committee
 recommends the Ministry to put in place accounting system, which can help to assess the importance and significance of domestic
 tourism. The Committee also recommends that special emphasis on domestic marketing of North-Eastern region as a premier tourism
 products must be laid.   The Committee feels that despite ‘Incredible India campaign’ still there is dearth of information for middle
 class domestic tourists from rural India.  Therefore, information brochures containing all details about stay, taxi/bus fares, details of
 places to see etc. should be widely circulated all over the country through state tourism Departments.  The Committee further
 recommends that provision for budget hotels, specially, targeting domestic tourists from middle income group should be made at all
 important destinations.  It appears to the Committee that specific focused efforts for establishing contact with the expanding middle
 class budget tourists is conspicuous by its absence in the initial information circulated by the Ministry. The initial information brochure
 should be made available to foreign tourists as well giving initial details which would target budget tourists segments.
(Para 46)
OVERSEAS PROMOTION AND MARKETING
 
            The Committee appreciates the concerted efforts taken by the Ministry in positioning and marketing of Indiatourism products
 overseas under the brand "Incredible India".  The Committee feels that upgrading and enhancing of Incredible India brand should
 continue and Ministry should provide increased funds for strategic and marketing promotion across the globe to enhance India's share
 of international arrivals from 0.38% to at least 0.62% by 2007. However, the Committee is constrained to note that eastern and south-
eastern countries viz. Japan, North/South Korea, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and China have been ignored and all efforts of the
 Ministry aim at marketing products only in western countries.  This fact is more evident that there is no Indiatourism office in Thailand,
 Korea and China.  The Committee recommends that Government should also concentrate on the eastern countries to attract tourists
 from these countries to India, including Buddhist pilgrims.  The Committee also recommends to look into the possibility of setting-up
 overseas offices in eastern countries especially in Thailand, Korea and China.
(Para 52)
 
COORDINATION BETWEEN MINISTRY OF TOURISM AND MINISTRY OF CIVIL AVIATION
           
The Committee notes that Ministry of Civil Aviation and Ministry of Tourism are working in close coordination with each other
 for the growth of travel & tourism sector in the country. However, the Committee strongly feels that a restrictive air transport policy
 has a very deleterious impact on the volume of tourist traffic and recommends for a more liberal aviation regimes and a open sky policy
 for the development of tourism.
(Para 57)
 
COORDINATION BETWEEN MINISTRY OF TOURISM AND MINISTRY OF RAILWAYS
           
The Committee notes that the task force on infrastructure for tourism has taken up the matter for revival of project for
 establishment of 100 hotels at railway stations by the Indian Railways. The Committee feels that the cause of domestic tourism could
 better be addressed by promoting amenities and facilities for budget tourists.  In this connection, the Committee reiterates its earlier
 recommendation contained in 66
th Report on Demands for Grants of Department of Tourism (2003-04), which is reproduced below:-
"The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should take up with Ministry of Railways for
 equipping the Railway service to meet the growing travel requirements of domestic and foreign tourists. Besides, better
 hotel accommodation should be provided nearer to railway stations, airports etc. such as to cater to the requirements of
 the NRI’s as the existing ones are not up to the standard of NRI’s/foreign tourists.  The Ministry of Railway may be
 persuaded to introduce ‘Special Tourist Trains’ on the pattern of Rajdhani/Shatabdi trains with fewer compartments to
 run on experimental basis on the Buddhist Circuit, which is proposed to be developed soon. For this purpose possibility
 of getting foreign assistance and Private Sector participation should also be fully explored.  Simultaneously, the
 Department of Tourism must be proactive in their approach for providing the sanitation and public convenience
 facilities at the important tourist destinations.  This may be done with the involvement of State Government/Local
 bodies/Municipal Corporation."
 
            The Committee also recommends that the project "Village on wheels" as announced in the Railway Budget 2004-05 by the
 Ministry of Railways should be linked with rural tourism and the Ministry of Tourism should prepare a comprehensive tour package for
 the budget tourists to experience the exotic rural India.

COORDINATION BETWEEN MINISTRY OF TOURISM AND STATE GOVERNMENTS
 
            The Committee is surprised to note that a large number of tourism projects sanctioned to State Governments/Union Territories
 by the Ministry of Tourism during the 9
th Plan period either remains incomplete or were dropped.  The Committee is of the view that
 Ministry of Tourism failed to activate the State Government machinery which resulted in huge backlog of projects.  The participation of
 the States and their support is essential for tourism to flourish and spread its benefits among the host population with numerous
 benefits.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to enlist the support of the State Governments in the matter and engage the private
 sector for completion of these projects so that delay and cost overrun in execution of tourism projects can be avoided.  The Committee
 also recommends that some institutional arrangements for monitoring progress of the projects should be developed between the
 Ministry of Tourism and State Governments/UT Administrations to evolve a shared approach of action.
 (Para 68)
COORDINATION BETWEEN MINISTRY OF TOURISM AND MINISTRY OF CULTURE
           
The Committee feels that one of the key aspects of Indian tourism is the cultural tourism around the magnificent monuments
 and heritage centres located in different parts of the country.  Both foreign and domestic tourists derive immense pleasure by visiting
 these monuments which stand as glorious embodiment of our culture and heritage.  The Committee therefore, recommends that the
 Ministry of Tourism and the Ministry of Culture should make coordinated efforts for the development of tourism sector in the country.  
 
            The Committee in the foregoing paragraphs have already emphasized the need of the co-ordination among different Ministries of
 Government of India, State Governments/Private Sectors and other agencies.  However, the Committee recommends that Ministry of
 Tourism should have an institutionalized mechanism in the form of 'National Tourism Promotion Board' to co-ordinate with all
 concerned agencies.  This Board should have permanent members and there should be an executive secretariat to assist in its efforts.
(Paras 70 & 71)
 
TOURISM DEVELOPMENT IN NORTH EASTERN REGION
 
            The Committee appreciates the efforts taken by the Ministry for the development of tourism in North Eastern region.  However,
 the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should prioritise the projects, which will help in strengthening of
 infrastructure and product development in North-Eastern region, besides harnessing the national development priorities.  Since, there is
 a separate department for North-Eastern development, the Committee feels that proper coordination between the Ministry of Tourism,
 the Department of North-Eastern Region and North-East Development Council be established to help boost the tourism industry.  The
 Committee is also apprised of the fact that ground realities and law and order situation prevailing in all these North-Eastern States are
 different from each other.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that special state-wise survey should be conducted for individual
 states and tour packages should be developed accordingly.  
(Para 80)
 
UNIFORMITY AND RATIONALISATION OF TAXES ON HOSPITALITY SECTOR
           
The Committee notes that major impediments to the growth of tourism in India are multiplicity of taxes and lack of conducive
 environment for private sector investment.  The Committee feels that the high and multiplied tax structure reduces the competitiveness
 of the Indian Tourism Products.  The Committee therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Tourism after interaction with all
 appropriate agencies should draw a package/road map and follow up the matter with the Ministry of Finance, so as to evolve uniform
 tax structure for the growth of travel & tourism sector.
(Para 84)
DEVELOPMENT OF INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY FOR PROVIDING TOURISM INFORMATION
           
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism has taken a major initiatives in the field of Information Technology with a
 view to benefit tourists.  The Committee feels that internet has a great impact on the marketing of Travel and Tourism and helps greatly
 in promoting tourism destinations and products which are located even in remote areas. The Committee further notes that the present
 official website on tourism has a lot of information but these are essentially static data.  Moreover, the Committee is also not clear how
 this information is getting disseminated to the potential tourists and tourism market.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
 website must provide for interactive space and a call center to make information 24x7x365 complaint from anywhere in the world.
(Para 88)

            The Committee appreciates the effort of the Ministry in promotion and marketing of Tourism products in BIMSTEC countries. 
 The Committee is happy to note that India is a leading country for tourism sector in BIMSTEC co-operation and the year 2004 has been
 designated as 'VISIT BIMSTEC YEAR'. However, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should identify and keep on

PROVIDIDNG A CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS TO TOURISM
 
            The Committee is surprised to note that tourism which is such an important sector not only at national but also at international
 level does not figure in any of the three list provided in the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution of India.  The Committee notes with
 dissatisfaction that the Ministry of Tourism could not develop a national consensus emphasizing upon the role of tourism in national
 development and identifying barriers stifling the growth of this sector. The Committee is of the strong view that India's tourism
 performance has failed to match its potential for promoting sustainable tourism products, in the absence of suitable legislative and
 regulatory support.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry that concerted efforts should be made to include tourism in
 the concurrent list of the constitution in order to provide constitutional recognition to the tourism sector and enable Central Government
 to legislate for tourism development.
(Para 96)
HUMAN RESOURCES DEVELOPMENT
 
The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry in the field of Human Resource Development through the existing
 Institutes of the Hotel Management and Food Craft Institutes, which cater to the growing demand in the service sector. The Committee
 observes that the availability of trained manpower is essential to achieve excellence in the tourism sector.  The Committee recommends
 that to meet the growing demand of trained manpower in the field of Human Resource Development more Institutes be opened up in
 private sector and Ministry should set up a Deemed Central University to monitor and co-ordinate Institutions opening in the tourism
 sector.
(Para 103)
CRUISE TOURISM
 
            The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Ministry of Tourism is working with Ministry of Shipping and concerned Port
 Trusts for the development, promotion and marketing of Cruise Tourism in India.  The Committee feels that the sea travel is becoming
 increasingly popular and there is an enormous potential for its growth and expansion in India.  The Committee recommends that the
 Ministry should further explore the possibility for the development of Cruise Tourism at other beaches located in the eastern-coast in
 India.
(Para 107)
WILD LIFE TOURISM
 
The Committee notes with concern that India's great wildlife variety has not yet been developed as a tourist attraction.  The
 wildlife sanctuaries and national parks, which are the integral part of the Indian tourism product, has not been opened for facilitation of
 tourists.  However, the Committee appreciates the move of the Ministry for giving due prominence in the publicity and marketing
 campaigns to the lesser know States and the campaign focusing on India's competitive strength on the sphere of "Wellness and
 Spirituality" has been launched.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should co-ordinate the matter with the
 Ministry of Environment & Forests for better facilitation of tourists in the National Parks and wildlife sanctuaries. 
(Para 112)
CHARDHAM YATRA CIRCUIT
 
            The Committee appreciates the efforts taken by the Ministry of Tourism for developing Uttranchal as a most popular pilgrimage
 destination with better and upgraded facilities for tourists in Himalayan region.  The Committee recommends that the works sanctioned
 for development of Chardham Yatra Circuit should be completed in a time bound manner and the progress of work should be
 monitored for integrated development.  The Committee also desires that the Ministry of Tourism in co-ordination with the Ministry of
 Civil Aviation should start the helicopter services for Badrinath, Kedarnath and Hemkund Sahib so that the pilgrimage tourists could
 avail this facility for their travel with in short span of time.
(Para 116)
