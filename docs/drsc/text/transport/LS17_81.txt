The Ministry of Shipping is responsible for development; formulation and implementation of policies relating to Shipping,
 Shipbuilding, Major Ports, Inland Water Transport, Lighthouses & Lightships, Andaman and Lakshadweep Harbour works, Chartering of
 ships for Government owned/controlled cargo etc.
 
FUNCTIONS
2.         The main functions of the Ministry of Shipping relates to Development of Inland Water Transport, Maritime Shipping and
 Navigation, Development of Major Ports, Development of Lighthouses and Lightships, Shipbuilding and Chartering of Ships.

17.       The Committee notes with dismay that during the annual plan 2003-04, an allocation of Rs.1479.55 crore was approved for the Port
 Sector but the Ports could utilise only Rs.347.87 crore resulting into a heavy shortfull of Rs.1131.68 crore in the utilisation of budgetary
 allocations.  The Committee does not approve the reasons as detailed by the Ministry of Shipping for the shortfall.  The Committee fails to
 understand as to how the physical targets will be achieved with this low utilisation pattern.  In fact, the Committee feels that this raises
 doubts about the mismatch in the projections for physical and financial targets.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Shipping
 has full responsibility to ensure proper utilisation of the approved outlay.  The Committee feels that strict monitoring is crucial for timely
 completion of any project.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the monitoring agency should play rather proactive role and do
 away with the procedural hurdles for timely completion of the projects so that the budgeted allocation is utilised optimally.  The Committee
 also recommends the Ministry to keep informed about the progress in the matter.

INTERNATIONAL SHIP AND PORT FACILITY SECURITY
 
25.       The International Maritime Organization (IMO) has introduced new maritime security regulations called International Code for the
 Security of Ships and of Port Facility (ISPS Code) which was adopted as an amendment to 'Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS)' by the conference
 of Contracting Governments on 12/12/2002.  The ISPS Code embodies a number of functional requirements which include:
 
(i)         gathering and assessing information with respect to security threats and exchanging such information with appropriate Contracting
 Governments;
(ii)        requiring the maintenance of communication protocols for ships and port facilities;
(iii)       preventing unauthorized access to ships, port facilities and their restricted areas;
(iv)       preventing the introduction of unauthorized weapons, incendiary devices or explosives to ships or port facilities;
(v)        providing means for raising the alarm in reaction to security threats or security incidents;
(vi)       requiring ship and port facility security plans based upon security assessments; and
(vii)      requiring training, drills and exercises to ensure familiarity with security plans and procedures.
 
26.       The code was introduced as a fall out of 11 th September 2001 terrorist attack incident in the US, after which the need to tighten up
 security for all modes of transport, was realised.  Therefore, the 22 nd Session of the Assembly of the IMO in November, 2001 unanimously
 agreed to the development of new measures relating to the security of ships and of port facilities for adoption by a Conference of Contracting
 Governments to the SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea) 1974.  The ISPS Code provides for the international framework through which ship and
 port facilities can cooperate to detect and deter acts which threaten security in the maritime transport sector. The Code has become mandatory
 with effect from 1st July, 2004 in respect of the contracting parties to SOLAS 1974 including India.
 
27.       Giving the details of efforts made to implement the new security regulations in India the Ministry informed the Committee that the
 ISPS Code in India covers all Major Ports, Minor Ports catering to international trade, and Ships.  Under the Code if an ISPS compliant Ship
 enters a non-compliant Port, it will have problems in its next voyage which will affect international trade and business.  India is one among
 the first few countries to complete implementation of ISPS Code, ensuring that there will be no hindrance in Indian exports and imports.

·
  It is a scheme of presumptive taxation whereby the notional income arising from the operation of a ship is determined based

·  The national income is taxed at the normal corporate rate applicable for the year.

INDIAN SEAFARERS WORKING ON FOREIGN FLAGGED SHIPS
 
40.       Indian seafarers working on foreign flagged vessels enjoy tax free status, as they are in more advantageous position to comply with
 the requirements to become eligible to NRI status when they are employed on Indian ships.  Even when they do no comply with the
 requirements to become eligible for NRI status, they are not subject to TDS in India, because the foreign ship owners employing Indian
 seafarers are outside the jurisdiction of the Indian Tax authorities. 
41.       On a question of exempting the Indian seafarers from paying taxes so as to retain qualified and trained officers, the Ministry
 informed that as a measure of preventing the large-scale drift of qualified and experienced personnel, Sec.6 of I.T. Act was amended in
 1990 to provide for tax exemption in respect of income received by the Indian crew serving on Indian ships outside India for 182 or more
 days in a year. The provision was aimed specifically to help Indian shipping companies to retain the services of Indian crew employed on
 their ships.  However, this intent could not be achieved because of an interpretation of the relevant provision namely, Explanation (a) to
 Sub-Section (1)(c) of Sec.6 by CBDT Circular No.586 of 28-11-1990 that the period of the seafarers’ service when the ships are in Indian
 territorial waters is to be treated as period of service in India, in a year.  A communication dated May 7, 1996 received from CBDT clarified
 this point.  As Indian ships generally operate in international waters and also call frequently at Indian ports, a large number of the crew
 employed on Indian ships though trading outside India cannot comply with this eligibility criteria to have NRI status, resulting in denial of
 tax exemption benefits to most of Indian seafarers working on Indian ships.
 
42.       An expert committee under the chairmanship of Dr. Rakesh Mohan, the then Adviser to the Finance Minister was constituted in
 August, 2001. The committee considered this issue after taking into consideration various alternatives and recommended “In order to make
 employment on Indian ships attractive vis-à-vis foreign ships, it is essential that Indian nationals serving on board Indian flag ships be
 treated equally as those Indians employed on foreign ships. The Committee also recommended for insertion of appropriate provision in
 Sec.10 of the Income Tax Act and the alternative, the Board in exercise of its power under Sec.119 of the Act may issue a Circular that
 would put an end to the discriminatory consequences of the CBDT Circular of 28-11-1990, though such discrimination was unintended”.
 
43.       In line with the recommendation of the committee, following provision/clarification (as a sub-section inserted in Section 10 of the
 I.T. Act) is suggested for consideration of CBDT:
“In case of an Indian citizen who is a member of the crew of an Indian ship as defined in clause (18) of section 3 of the Merchant
 Shipping Act, 1958, any income chargeable under the head “Salaries” received by or due to any such individual being a non-resident as
 remuneration for services rendered in connection with his employment on such ship where his total stay in India does not exceed in the
 aggregate a period of 182 days in the previous year and where actual period/s of stay outside India of such individual while serving on such
 ship/s is determined as under :-          
“His stay in India may be determined from the embarkation date stamped by the Immigration authority on his passport or by the
 Master of vessel on the Articles of Agreement (Foreign Going), whichever is earlier, to the disembarkation date stamped on the passport or
 on CDC while signing-off from the ship, whichever is later.”
 
44.       The Ministry has been taking up the recommendation of the committee with the Ministry of Finance at various levels since 2002 and
 has suggested necessary amendments in the Income Tax Act so as to provide necessary relief to the deprived Indian seafarers.  Response
 from Ministry of Finance is still awaited in the matter.
 
45.       During the course of evidence, the Secretary deposed before the Committee that;
"the request of seamen to get exemption from Income tax deserves consideration.  This will encourage seafaring profession in India,

REPORT
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/81streport.htm[22-10-2018 12:02:02]
 and help to promote merchant navy, and help Indian owners and charterers to get good Indian crew.  Otherwise the Indian seafarer prefers
 to go on board the foreign-flag vessels.  Today he gets the income exemption while working on the Indian ships- only, and only if he
 becomes an NRI i.e. goes out of India for more than half the year.  It is understood that most of the Indian seamen on foreign-flag vessels do
 not pay any income tax in India and very rarely in the country whose flag their ships flies.  This is because most of the time they are out of
 India for more than half the year.  In any case they are on foreign ships which is equal to foreign territory and not subject to Indian law. 
 Thus in order to bring about an equitable basis, it is requested that the Hon'ble Committee may recommend for exemption of Income tax for
 seafarers."

       The Committee expresses its deep concern regarding continuing drift of personnels away from employment on Indian ship to foreign
 flagged ships.  The Committee notes that the drift is due to lack of uniform tax regime i.e. Indian officers on Indian Ships have to pay
 income tax whereas, Indian officers on foreign flagged vessels are enjoying tax free status.  The Committee feels that there is some lacunae
 in the CBDT circular dated 28.11.90.  The Committee, recommends, the Ministry to revisit the whole issue in order to make employment on
 Indian ships attractive vis-à-vis foreign ships, and if necessary, impress upon the Ministry of Finance for amendment in the Income Tax
 Act.

INLAND WATER TRANSPORT
 85.      Inland waterways comprises of rivers, creeks, canals, backwaters and lakes etc. which extend to about 14,500 kms length in the
 country out of which about 5,700 kms are navigable by mechanised vessels.  However, the potential of this important mode of transport has
 not been exploited so far to its desired level. Inland Water Transport (IWT) schemes mainly relate to development of waterways and
 augmentation of the traffic carrying capacity through expansion and modernization of I.W.T. vessels.  Whereas the Inland Waterways
 Authority of India (IWAI) is responsible for the development of National Waterways, responsibility for the development of State
 Waterways rests with the State Governments.  The Central Government have declared three Waterways, viz., Allahabad – Haldia Stretch
 (1620 kms.) Sadiya and Dhubri Stretch (891 kms.)  and  West Coast Canal from  Kollam to Kottapuram stretch along with  Udyogamandal
 and Champakara canals  (total 205 km) as National Waterways. Amongst the major initiatives, during the 10th Plan, it has been planned to
 construct Permanent Terminals at Gaighat, Patna, in N.W.- I and at Pandu, Assam, in NW-II. During the Financial Year 2004-05, a sum of
 Rs.145.57 crores (including Rs.47.5 crore from NE Pool) has been earmarked towards Plan Support to IWAI.
                       
86.       IWAI undertakes various schemes related to development of IWT infrastructure on these three National Waterways namely, Fairway

 National Transport Policy Committee (NTPC) had identified 10 waterways.  Out of these three waterways namely the Ganga-Bhagirathi-
Hooghly, the Brahmaputra and the West Coast Canal have already been declared as National Waterways.  The Ministry further informed
 that a view was earlier taken by the Ministry that no new waterways  would be declare as National Waterways until the existing National
 Waterways are made fully operational. The policy has since been reviewed by the Ministry. Accordingly, the following three proposals for
 declaration as National Waterways are being pursued:
 
(i)         Godavari-Krishna and the integrated canal system including Buckingham            Canal in Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu –

(v)        Writing off Govt. of India loan (plan and non-plan) together with interest accrued there on amounting to Rs. 425.98 crore as on 31st
 March 2000; and
 
(vi)       Roll back of age of retirement on superannuation to 58 years from 60 years.
101.     Besides, the cost of the revival package was worked out at Rs. 139.55 crore which was to be financed by budgetary support of Rs.
 76.55 crore, loan re-payable from sale proceeds of surplus land and assets of Rs. 39.67 crore and sale of surplus land and assets, of Rs.
 23.33 crore.  The Government have released Rs. 65.80 crore during the first year of revival i.e. 2001-02, Rs. 62.55 crore in the year 2002-03
 and Rs. 16.08 crore during the year 2003-04.
 
102.     Many components of the revival package (except the sale of surplus land and closure and sale of Rajabagan Dockyard) have been, by
 and large implemented.  However, physical and financial performance of the Corporation has not improved.  In view of this, Ministry is
 considering refinements in the revival package sanctioned earlier.
103.     The Committee notes that the Central Inland Water Transport Corporation is presently implementing four year restructuring schemes
 involving Government assistance of Rs.139 crore.  However, the Committee also note that there is a dispute over the time-frame of
 restructuring plan, the exact time of starting of the scheme and availability of funds. Given the fact that inspite of substantial investment for
 infrastructure building in Inland Water Transport sector and the insignificant participation of private sector in utilizing infrastructure for the
 restructuring the Central Inland Water Transport Corporation to a proper health is an urgent task.  Therefore, the Committee is of the view
 that the future of IWT operations including that of CIWTC holds immense possibilities for establishing a well-defined multimodal
 transportation network in the country and recommends that Government should consider a fresh financial restructuring of the company.

OBSERVATION/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
AT A GLANCE
 
ANYLYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2004-05
            The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction on the fact that there has been a heavy shortfall in the utilisation of funds as compared to
 approved outlay.  During 2003-04, out of the total outlay of Rs.2543.90 crore the Ministry could spent only Rs.1104.70 crore.  It indicates
 that while proposing its plan outlay, the Ministry of Shipping had not been realistic in its approach.  However, the Committee feels that the
 complete utilisation of approved outlay could have definitely helped in improving the schemes/projects undertaken by the Ministry of
 Shipping.  The Committee recommends that in future, the Ministry should have realistic planning, so that the approved outlay can be
 utilised fully.
(Para 9)

PORT SECTOR
            The Committee notes with dismay that during the annual plan 2003-04, an allocation of Rs.1479.55 crore was approved for the Port
 Sector but the Ports could utilise only Rs.347.87 crore resulting into a heavy shortfull of Rs.1131.68 crore in the utilisation of budgetary
 allocations.  The Committee does not approve the reasons as detailed by the Ministry of Shipping for the shortfall.  The Committee fails to
 understand as to how the physical targets will be achieved with this low utilisation pattern.  In fact, the Committee feels that this raises
 doubts about the mismatch in the projections for physical and financial targets.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Shipping
 has full responsibility to ensure proper utilisation of the approved outlay.  The Committee feels that strict monitoring is crucial for timely
 completion of any project.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the monitoring agency should play rather proactive role and do
 away with the procedural hurdles for timely completion of the projects so that the budgeted allocation is utilised optimally.  The Committee
 also recommends the Ministry to keep informed about the progress in the matter.
(Para 17)
CAPACITY AND TRAFFIC IN MAJOR PORT
         The Committee notes that the Ministry is taking capacity building major schemes for improving the capacity and productivity of all
 Major Ports.  The Committee appreciates the Ministry for adding capacity of 46.05 MTPA to Ports in the first two years of the 10 th Plan,
 making the aggregate cargo handling capacity of Port to 390 MTPA as against the total traffic of 344.53 MT.  The Committee observes that
 aggregate capacity of the Major Ports exceeds the traffic targets.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to initiate action for
 the modernization and capacity expansion of the port network across India's maritime zone so as to enable the Ports to utilise the additional
 capacity vis-à-vis traffic smoothly.
(Para 24)
INTERNATIONAL SHIP AND PORT FACILITY SECURITY
            
The Committee appreciates the Ministry for complying the ISPS code for all 12 Major Ports, 34 minor ports catering to international
 trade and 191 Indian Flagged ships in the country.  The Committee notes that the new maritime security regulations will provide international
 framework for ships and ports facilities besides detecting the deterring acts, which threaten security in the maritime transport sector.  The
 implementation of ISPS code, will also do away the hindrances in Indian export and imports.  The Committee, however, recommends that a
 task force be formed in the Ministry to observe strict compliance of international safety and security code/regulations.
(Para 30)
SAGAR MALA
            The Committee is happy to note that Sagar Mala programme has been initiated to cover all facets of maritime transport including Ports,
 Shipping and Inland Waterways with the aim to fully realise the potential of sea-borne trade and Inland Water Transport system.  The
 Committee feels that any delay in cabinet approval of this programme will adversely affect the modernization alongwith capacity expansion
 of ports network in the country.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that there is very little activity in the Inland Water Transport
 sub-sector.  This raises the question that how investment on infrastructure building will yield commensurate economically viable rate of
 returns.  The Committee was also disturbed to note that the IWAI is yet to develop the infrastructure for capacity building both in terms of
 human resources for proper technology appraisal, R&D and design inducement packages.  Therefore, the Committee concludes that this is a
 task, which should be urgently addressed.    The Committee, therefore, recommends that an early approval to launch Sagar Mala programme
 should be given and the Ministry should initiate action for setting-up of Sagar Mala Development Authority on the pattern of National
 Highways Authority of India.
(Para 36)
INTRODUCING OF TONNAGE TAX
            
The Committee notes that the Government has since introduced the tonnage tax in lieu of the corporate tax as per its
 recommendation.  The Committee hopes that with the introduction of tonnage tax and other fiscal incentives, the Indian Shipping
 companies will be able to acquire more tonnage.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that at present, the tonnage tax scheme is
 proposed for the vessels registered under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 only and vessels registered under the Indian Vessels Act, 1917,
 Indian Coastal Act 1838 and Dredgers have not been included for the purpose.  The Committee, therefor, recommends that in order to
 promote Inland Water Transport in the country, the Ministry should pursue the matter with Ministry of Finance for extending the benefit of
 tonnage tax to Dredgers and vessels registered under Indian Vessels/Indian Coastal Vessels Acts. 
(Para 39)
INDIAN SEAFARES WORKING ON FOREIGN FLAGGED SHIPS
           
The Committee expresses its deep concern regarding continuing drift of personnels away from employment on Indian ship to foreign
 flagged ships.  The Committee notes that the drift is due to lack of uniform tax regime i.e. Indian officers on Indian Ships have to pay
 income tax whereas, Indian officers on foreign flagged vessels are enjoying tax free status.  The Committee feels that there is some lacunae
 in the CBDT circular dated 28.11.90.  The Committee, recommends, the Ministry to revisit the whole issue in order to make employment on
 Indian ships attractive vis-à-vis foreign ships, and if necessary, impress upon the Ministry of Finance for amendment in the Income Tax
 Act.
(Para 46)

The Committee notes with concern that the share of Shipping Corporation of India for transportation of crude oil imports has been

(Para 57)
KOLKATA DOCK LABOUR BOARD
The Committee notes with anguish that the Kolkata Dock Labour Board has not yet revised the pension and liberalisation of post
 retirement benefits in terms of wage settlement and the pensioners have not been paid their pensionary and other post retirement dues for
 long.  The Committee notes that Kolkata Dock Labour Board, a body corporate, was constituted in terms of Section 5 (A) of the Dock
 Workers (Regulation of Employment) Act, 1948 in September 1952.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that the employees of
 the Board are not being treated as a Government employees so far as payment of pension and pensionary benefits to the employees of
 Kolkata Dock Labour Board is concerned, which is a matter of right of the Government employees.  The Committee also notes with
 constrained that Kolkata Dock Labour Board has also not been provided financial assistance out of the National Revival Fund. The
 Committee notes that a proposal for release of one time grant of Rs.42.96 crore to Kolkata Dock Labour Board was sent to the Ministry of
 Finance but the Ministry of Finance has not acceded to the request for release of grants to Kolkata Dock Labour Board.  The Committee is
 of the view that it is the sole liability of the Government to pay pension to the pensioners in time and it will be sheer discrimination on the
 part of the Government to withhold pension and post retirement benefits in the name of financial crisis that prevailed in Kolkata Dock
 Labour Board.  Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that no injustice should be meted to the pensioners of Kolkata Dock Labour
 Board in the payment of pension and pensionary benefits and the Ministry of Shipping should impress upon the Ministry of Finance for
 release of one time grant for the implementation the pensionary and other benefits to the pensioners.   In fact even Voluntary Retirement
 Scheme could be implemented to address this serious problem.  The Committee also recommends the Ministry to keep it informed about the
 progress in the matter regularly.
(Para 63)
            
INDIAN INSTITUTE OF MARITIME STUDIES (IIMS)
           
The Committee is not satisfied with replies of the Ministry regarding setting up of Indian Maritime University, terms and conditions
 of employees creation of Pension Fund and retention of Government accommodation etc. and convey its dismay over the delay in
 finalisation of terms and conditions of service of Indian Institute of Maritime Studies and confusion is still prevailing in this regard, this has
 certainly affect the morale of the employees.  The Committee is of the view that the creation of trust for the Indian Institute of Maritime
 Studies has resulted in ambiguity with respect to the management of funds of Indian Institute of Maritime Studies.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that Government must expedite their effort for formation of university for Indian Institute of Maritime Studies. In
 this connection, the Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in the 70th Report
of Demands for Grants 2003-04 of the Ministry of Shipping which is reproduce below:
 
"The Committee, however, recommends that the Ministry should go for establishing the University by an Act of Parliament so as to
 provide considerable operational freedom and a qualitative boost to the Maritime training in the country.  The Committee, however, notes
 with concern that the terms and conditions of services are being changed against the interest of the employees.   The Committee
 recommends that before taking any action with regard to the terms and conditions of the service of employees of erstwhile Maritime
 Institutes, the unions of the employees be consulted and it should be ensured that the terms and conditions of service of employees who
 have been transferred on deemed deputation to IIMS remain unchanged. Also, the interests of employees particularly relating to pension,
 CGHS facilities and retention of Government accommodation should be safeguarded."
(Para 69)
MISAPPROPRIATION OF SEAMEN'S PROVIDENT FUND
            The Committee is constrained to note that the matter of recouping the shortage to the tune of Rs.92.78 crore is pending for long time
 with the Ministry of Finance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Shipping should pursue the matter vigorously
 with Ministry of Finance for providing a grant to the extent of Rs.92.78 crore to recoup the shortage in the Seamen's Provident Fund.  More
 so, because the Fund was managed by the Government and the responsibility of irregularity in the management of that Fund cannot be
 borne by the seamen's who's life-long savings stands in uncertainty, if not, outrightly jeopardize.

 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should take steps to improve the performance of the shipyard besides impressing

INLAND WATER TRANSPORT
           
The Committee expresses its concern over the slow pace of development of Inland Water Transport sector in the country.  The
 Committee feels that the potential of this important mode of transport has not yet been exploited to its desired level.  The Committee is
 surprised to note that inspite of the efforts of the Government only Rs.292.74 crore was spent during the last 18 years for development of
 the existing three National Waterways.  The Committee disapproves the lackadaisical approach of the Ministry towards the development of
 this important mode of transport. The Committee however, notes that a policy frame-work for the Inland Water Transport system has been
 approved by the Government to mobilize the required resources by attracting private investments and forming joint ventures for developing
 waterways infrastructure and cargo handling facilities. The Committee is however, constrained to express its doubts over the participation
 of private sector unless the Government has focused approach for providing basic infrastructural facilities for Inland Water Transport.
 
            The Committee is constrained to note that cargo transportation by Inland Water Transport mode has not shown significant
 improvement on these three National  Waterways.  The Committee also expresses its displeasure over the fact that no comparative cost-
output evaluation for transportation of cargo through this alternate mode of transport has been carried out by the Ministry. The Committee is
 of the view that such evaluation would further establish the support of Inland Water Transport over other mode of transports. Therefore, the
 Committee recommends that a detailed study should be carried out by the Ministry in this regard. The Committee also recommends the
 Ministry to look into the possibility of formation of the taskforce to oversee the progress of the projects so that these could be completed in
 a time bound manner for development of Inland Water Transport in country.   The Committee feels that there is a need to strengthen R&D
 facilities particularly in the design of vessels, night navigation facilities, identification of economically viable routes and issues of mutual
 benefits with neighbouring countries like Bangladesh.  In this context, the Committee also reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in
 its 70th Report of Demands for Grants (2003-04) of the Ministry of Shipping, which is reproduce below;
"The Committee also does not approve the strategy of the Ministry to make investment for development and regulation of Inland
 Waterways without fixing the physical targets to be achieved vis-à-vis financial parameter. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
 Ministry while coming up with target of investment must have a realistic physical target for cargo transportation, year-wise for the entire
 10th Plan period and beyond."
            The Committee further recommends that the Ministry should approach the Ministry of Finance to enhance allocation by Rs.293 crore
 as requested by the Secretary, Ministry of Shipping for the remaining period of 10 th Plan so as to achieve the objectives of making the
 existing three National Waterways fully operational.
