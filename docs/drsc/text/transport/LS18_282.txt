RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY 
THE MINISTRY 
ANNUAL PLAN 2019-20 
Sharp reduction at RE 2019-20 stage  
Recommendation/Observation 
1. The Committee observes that the Ministry has been allocated Rs. 2547.00 crore at RE 
2019-20 stage as against Rs. 3042.35 crore at B.E. 2019 -20 which is a huge reduction of 
over 16%. The Committee observes that many of the projects/activities carried out by the 
Ministry of Culture, if postponed to a future date, will cause irreparable damage to the 
cultural herit age of our country. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry 
may ensure that the available funds are  utilized in an efficient manner, without rushing 
expenditure at the end of a Financial Year.  
      (Para 13) 
Reply of the Ministry 
1.1 Ministry ensured the utilization of available funds in efficient manner without 
expenditure rushing at year end. Ministry re -prioritized its programmes/ activities and 
accommodated them within the reduced allocation. 
 
ANNUAL PLAN (2020-21) 
Recommendation/Observation 
2. The Committee observes that while the Ministry had projected a demand of Rs. 
4323.61 crore for 2019-20, a demand of only Rs. 3857.50 crore has been projected for BE 
2020-21. The Committee may be apprised of the reasons for the substantial reduction in the 
projected requirement of the Ministry, even when there had been a reduction of almost Rs. 
500 crores at RE stage in 2019 -20. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may plead 
the cases of such schemes/projects for higher allocations at RE stage in 2020 -21 so that the 
projects are not stalled due to paucity of funds. 
  (Para 26) 
Reply of the Ministry 
2.1 The projection for 2019-20 was keeping in view the various commemorations such as 
Celebration of 150 th Birth Anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi, releasing various payments 
which remained due for the previous year’s commemorations such as payment to NBCC for 
construction of Pt. Deendayal Institute of Archaeology at Greater NOIDA, etc. The Ministry 
will certainly make efforts for higher allocation depending on the expenditure status at RE 
2020-21 so that the projects may not be stalled due to paucity of funds during the year. 
ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA 

3 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
3. The Committee is happy to note that ASI has received a 20% higher allocation at BE 
2020-21 stage compared to the Rs. 1036.41 crore allocation made at BE 2019 -20 and hopes

efficiently. However, taking into account that 3691 monuments come under the protection of 
ASI, even the increased allocation for protection and conservation appears very meagre. The

condition for generations to come. 
  (Para 31)

among its organizations which include attached /subordinate offices and autonomous bodies. 
ASI being a large organization and an attached office of the Ministry is generally allocated 
around one-third of the budget of the Ministry. For  2020-21, the allocation has been kept at 
1246.75 crore for ASI.

Recommendation/Observation 
7. The Committee desires that top priority should be given to excavation and

Reply of the Ministry 
7.1 The recommendation of Committee has been noted. 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
8. The Committee also urges that the field offices of ASI should pursue such cases of 
encroachment persistently with the District Authorities on urgent basis and chalk a way out 
in such cases. The Committee also underlines the need for adequate follow up of the cases 
filed in Court of law against the encroachers. 
  (Para 47) 
Reply of the Ministry 
8.1 For removal of encroachments, the Superintending Archaeologist in charge of the 
Circles has been vested with the powers of an Estate Officer to issue eviction notices/orders 
to the encroachers under Public Premises (Eviction of Unauthorized Occupants) Act,  1971. 
They are also delegated with the powers to issue notices under the provisions of Ancients 
Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958 and Rules 1959   followed by 
an order to the District Collector/Magistrate by Central Government to re move such 
encroachment under Section 19 of the Act. Assistance in containing and removing 
encroachments is also sought from the   respective State Government/police force. 
8.2 In most of the cases, defaulter seeks shelter in Court of Law against Notice or 
Demolition Order issued. Such cases are regularly monitored by Superintending 
Archaeologist of Circle Office with the help of Central Government Standing Counsels.

SUPPORT TO LIBRARIES 
Recommendation/Observation 
17. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Culture should make efforts to work 
seriously for modernization and networking of libraries, resource sharing, training of 
professional staff and strengthening of libraries at local level. 
  (Para 105) 
Reply of the Ministry 
17.1 This Ministry provides grants to four (4) Autonomous Body Libraries, viz. RRRLF, 
DPL, KBOPL and RRL under “Support to Libraries”.  
17.2 Besides, Under “Development of Libraries”, National Mission on Libraries (NML) 
scheme is being implemented, with its four (4) components, namely: 
a. National Virtual Library of India (NVLI),(now Indian Culture Portal) 

8 
 
b. Setting up of Model Libraries, 
c. Capacity Building and  
d. Quantitative & Qualitative Survey of Public Libraries in India 
17.3 RRRLF is the implementing agency for  the component (b), (c) and (d). The “Setting 
up of Model Libraries” component of NML envisages providing Financial Assistance to one 
State Central Library and one District Library in each State / UT as recommended by the 
State/ UT Authority & 6 Libraries under MOC. Under this component, provisions are there 
for modernization and networking of libraries.  
17.4 The “Capacity Building” component of NML envisages providing training to Library 
professionals to upgrade their professional skills as well as to apprise them with the latest 
technology. 
17.5 Apart from the above, the development of public libraries is a State subject as per the 
Seventh Schedule under Article 246 of Constitution of India. However, RRRLF provides 
some financial assistance only to var ious public libraries under various matching and non -
matching schemes and also provides professional skills as well to apprise them with the latest 
technology.

Recommendation/Observation

  (Para 146)

22.1 Ministry of Culture through the Artist Pension Section is ensuring timely disbursal of

22.2  For guiding the artists for getting artists pension as well as to know details  of other

10 
 
22.3 As per the guidelines of Artists Pension Scheme minimum of Rs. 500 from State/UT 
contribution is mandatory, thereby State s are also advised to guide the Artistes for getting 
Pension from the Central Government. 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
23. The Committee desires to know the reasons for not holding even a single meeting to 
process the applications in 2015 -16 and 2016 -17. The Committee feels that the Ministry 
cannot afford to keep artistes’ applications for pension, pending for years. The Ministry 
should ensure that regular meetings are held to update the list of b eneficiaries entitled for 
pension under the scheme of the Ministry. 
  (Para 148) 
Reply of the Ministry 
23.1 It has been decided then to carry out the physical verification of the existing pensioner 
to assess their authenticity as an Artist before holding the Expert Committee meeting in 
2015-16. The physical verification of pensioner was a lengthy exercise and he nce meeting 
could not be held in 2015 -16. However, all the proposals of the year 2015 -2016 and 2016-17 
were added to the meeting in the year 2017 -18 and recommended artistes were included in 
the list of beneficiaries. This Ministry is taking care of holdin g of Expert Committee Meeting

monitor any unauthorized construction in the prohibited/regulated area of a CPM.

monuments. The Committee strongly recommends that the NMA may come up with an 
effective plan of action to draft and finalize the HBLs for all monuments across the country. 
  (Para 161) 
Reply of the Ministry 
26.1 Section 20E of AMASRA Act prescribes the procedure for drafting Heritage Bye -
Laws for the CPMs/Sites. The Act envisages that 5 Regional Directors of ASI, declared as 
Competent Authority, shall prepare Heritage Bye -Laws (HBLs) in respect of each protected 
Monument and protected area in consultation with expert heritage bodies. 
26.2 Secondly, ASI is required to prepare the detailed site plan of each CPM/Site as 
prescribed in Section 20E (3). The site plan forms part of Schedule 1 of the Bye -Laws. The 
AMASRA Rules, 2011 prescribed time limit of 5 years for prepara tion of site plan of all the 
3691 monuments/sites. The time limit has now been extended by another 5 years. However, 
the site plans have still not been finalised for majority of the monuments. Regional Directors 
of ASI are regularly working on preparation of Heritage Bye -laws and sending it to NMA. 
NMA has to complete the formalities at their ends and then place it in the Parliament. 
26.3  The draft Heritage Bye -Laws are also required to be published through website 
inviting comments of public, which are re quired to be resolved before finalizing the Bye -
Laws, which is also time-consuming process. 
26.4  In view of the above NMA finds it very difficult to finalize the Heritage Bye -Laws at 
the required pace.

26.5 In addition to 5 HBLs already approved 44 draft  HBL covering 57 CPMs are under 
consideration. 
26.6 33 Draft Heritage Bye -Laws covering 38 CPMs have been sent back for clarification 
to Regional Directors 
Further recommendation/observation: 
26.7  The Committee understands that preparing the detailed site  plan and Heritage
