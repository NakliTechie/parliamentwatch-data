REPORT  
 
 
Culture plays an important and pivotal role in the development agenda of any nation. 
It represents a set of shared attitudes, values, go als and practices. Culture and creativity 
manifest themselves in almost all economic, social and other activities. India is a vast and 
diverse country with a rich cultural heritage that has been widely appreciated all over the 
world. 
 
2. The mandate of the Ministry of Culture revolves aro und preservation and 
conservation of our cultural heritage and promotion  of all forms of art and culture, both 
tangible and intangible. The functional spectrum of  this Ministry is wide, ranging from 
generating cultural awareness at grassroots level t o promoting cultural exchanges at 
international level. 
 
3. Broadly, the Ministry is working on protection, dev elopment and promotion of all 
types of Heritage and Culture namely, tangible Heri tage, Intangible Heritage and Knowledge 
Heritage. In addition, the Ministry also harbours t he responsibility of Gandhian Heritage and 
Commemoration of important historical events and centenaries of great personalities. 
 
4. Major programmes/activities relating to promotion, dissemination, preservation and 
conservation of the cultural heritage of the countr y are being implemented through a network 
of two attached offices, six subordinate offices an d 28 Autonomous organisations by the 
Ministry. It also implements schemes for promotion and dissemination of Art and Culture 
under umbrella(s) namely; Centenaries and Anniversa ries Schemes; Kala Sanskriti Vikas 
Yojana, National Mission on Cultural Mapping and Ro admap, Development of Museum, 
Development of Libraries and Archives and Global En gagement and International 
cooperation which also includes two Missions namely : National Mission on Libraries and 
National Gandhi Heritage site Mission. It has no Centrally Sponsored Schemes.

8. The Ministry has informed that against the Culture Ministry's projection for Rs 
3357.45 crores for the year 2022-23, an amount of R s. 3009.05 crores has been allocated 
which is about 89% of the projected demand. However , there is an increase of 11.95% over 
the allocation of Rs. 2687.99 crores at BE 2021-22.  The allocation of Rs. 3009.05 crore 

4  
comprises Rs. 532.55 crore for schemes and Rs. 2476 .50 crore for non-schemes. While 
intimating budgetary ceiling for 2022-23, MoF has s tipulated that 2.93% of the total 
allocation be kept under Capital heads, accordingly , the Ministry has kept Rs. 88.16 crore 
under Capital head. 
 
9. The Committee is concerned to note that the allocat ion to the Ministry of Rs 
3009.05 crore in BE 2022-23 is 11% lower than the p rojected demand at Rs 3357.45 
crores made by the Ministry. There is a shortfall o f Rs 348.40 crore in budgetary 
provisions for FY 2022-23 vis-à-vis the projected d emand of 3357.45 crore by the 
Ministry. The Committee also fails to understand th e reason for lower projection of Rs 
3357.45 crore in BE 2022-23 as compared to the proj ection of Rs 3843.68 crore in BE 
2021-22 by the Ministry. The Committee observes tha t Culture is integral to the 
development of the country because it promotes econ omic growth providing 
employment to a large section of population and spu rs other economic sectors thereby, 
contributing to the GDP of the country as also play s an important role in uniting the 
country. Further it is instrumental in sustenance o f our rich and diverse cultural 
heritage. Since the Ministry of Culture has the one rous responsibility of the 
preservation, development and promotion of the coun try's tangible and intangible 
heritage, the Committee is convinced that the BE allocation of 3009.05 crore for 2022-23 
is not commensurate with the fund requirements of t he Ministry. It  may  impact the 
vital activities of promotion and preservation of o ur cultural heritage. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should be p rovided with the projected 
financial outlay at RE 2022-23 stage so that the Mi nistry can achieve its targets/plans  
for preservation and conservation of our cultural h eritage and promotion of art and 
culture. 
 
10. In a reply to the Committee, the Ministry has state d that approved outlay for the 
Ministry for 2021-22 has been Rs. 2687.99 crores, r educed further to Rs. 2665.00 crores at 
RE stage. Against the approved outlay, the Ministry  has incurred an expenditure of Rs. 
1842.05 crores by 31 January 2022. Reasons for less  expenditure given by the Ministry are 
COVID pandemic and restrictions on tours and travels in the period. 
 
11. The Committee observes that the Ministry should ide ntify a core set of priorities 
so that the budgeted allocations are not divided wi th a very thin spread in numerous 

5  
areas and become less impactful. The Committee acco rdingly recommends the Ministry 
to review its priorities and concentrate on schemes /programmes which are absolutely 
essential so that scarce resources could be optimal ly utilized for achieving the desired 
results in focused areas. 
 
Actual expenditure in 2021-22: 
 
 
12. The Committee observes that the allocation of Rs 26 87.99 crore in B.E. 2021-22 
got slightly decreased in R.E. 2021-22 to Rs 2665.0 0 crore, the actual expenditure

13. The Archaeological Survey of India was set up in 1861 and is an attached office of the 
Ministry . Its main functions are preservation, con servation and development of protected 
monuments and sites including World Heritage Monume nts and antiquities, exploration and 
excavation of ancient sites, specialized study of i nscriptions and Indian architecture, 
maintenance of Archaeological site Museums, operati on of the Antiquities and Art Treasures 
Act, and research and Training in different areas o f Archaeology. It has 3693 monuments 
(including World Heritage Monuments) comprising pre -historic stone-age sites, temples, 
mosques, churches and forts. A National Mission for  Monuments and Antiquities has also 
been operationalised in ASI.

14. Out of the Ministry's proposed budget for 2022-23 , ASI has been allocated 35% of  
the total   outlay of   the   Ministry of Culture i. e. Rs. 1052.98 crore under Revenue and    Rs. 
27.36 Crores under Capital , a total of Rs. 1080.34 Crores 
 
 
15. The Committee is pleased to note that the budgetary  allocation of ASI has been 
increased from RE 2021-22 allocation of Rs. 915.43 crores to Rs. 1080.34 crores in BE 
2022-23. The Committee observes that India is a cou ntry with obvious constraints in 
resources. Hence in order to obtain the best possib le outcomes, it is imperative that 
allocation of resources is prioritized across compe ting ends and the available funds are 
deployed in a more efficient and productive manner.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Ministry should make efforts to  make optimum utilisation of 
funds available in an efficient manner so that cons ervation, development and 
maintenance of the protected monuments of ASI are c arried out and the best results are 
achieved. With the gradual removal of COVID-19 rest rictions more and more tourism 
activities are going to increase. ASI should make f resh endeavours to make use of this 
opportunity to generate more revenue through e-tick eting and other means available to 
it. 
 
16. The Committee notes that in the wake of the Coronav irus pandemic, ASI 
monuments and sites received lesser footfall in 201 9-20 and 2020-21. The Committee 
would like to observe that as the spread of Corona Virus is likely to see a decrease in 
2022-23, ASI should chalk out plans for acceleratin g ongoing programmes for 
conservation and preservation of monuments. The Com mittee hopes that 2022-23 
augurs well for ASI and also the tourists visiting the monuments. The Committee hopes 
that more and more tourists are likely to visit mon uments and this would help in 
generating more revenue during this year and touris ts with logistic support are 
facilitated. 
 
17. The Committee further recommends that since influx of tourists provides 
livelihood to significant sections of the populatio n, there is ample scope for growth and 
employment generation in this sector as Covid restr ictions are being relaxed. The 
Ministry should harness the huge potential of cultural heritage for this purpose.

VACANCIES IN VARIOUS ORGANIZATIONS OF THE MINISTRY  
 
34. Replying to a query whether any review has been done in the recent past regarding the 
personnel requirements of ASI for proper developmen t and preservation of centrally  
protected monuments, the Ministry in its written re ply has stated that a review has been done 
regarding the personnel requirement of ASI for prop er development and preservation of 
Centrally protected monuments. As a result, 758 pos ts have been created in 2021. Nearly 400 
of the newly created posts belong to the conservati on cadre responsible for upkeep/ 
preservation of Monuments. 
 
35. The Ministry in its written reply has furnished the  details of the vacancies and 
the steps taken to fill them in ASI (placed at Anne xure-I) and other organizations 
(Annexure-II) of the Ministry. The Committee feels that the vacancy details reflect a 
very pathetic state of affairs in all the organizat ions of the Ministry. In the Archaeology 
Cadre of ASI, against the total sanctioned strength  of 420, the vacant positions are 166, 
similarly in the Conservation Cadre, against the to tal strength of 918 there is a 
mammoth vacancy of 452 positions. The situation is not any better in other Cadres of 
ASI or other organisations of the Ministry. The Com mittee thus finds it difficult to 
understand how the Ministry can ensure the efficien cy of its organizations and manage 
to meet the present human resource needs of the res pective organisations, leave alone 
there being any scope for new initiatives by the or ganisations. The Committee strongly 
recommends that the Ministry may set up a foolproof mechanism in place to ensure that 
the staffing requirements of all its organizations are met at all times, so as to enable 
them to carry out their mandate in letter and spirit. 
 
36. The Committee is anguished to note the large scale vacancies in all the 
organizations of the Ministry for years together an d shocked to find that in some cadres 
the vacancies are more than 50% of the sanctioned s trength. It fails to understand how 
the Ministry will achieve the task of conserving th e culture and heritage of such a vast 
country with less than 50% of the sanctioned strength. 
 
37. The Committee notes with concern that a large numbe r of vacancies in  
important organizations like ASI, National Archives , Anthropological Survey of India 
etc.       are vacant for a considerable period of time. The Committee, therefore, strongly 

12
