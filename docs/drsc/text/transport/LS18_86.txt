 Transport. The Department of Shipping has been entrusted with the responsibility to formulate policies and programmes on these subjects and their implementation.

"Sir, you mentioned that our expenditure is too poor.  Budget Estimates, R.E. are too poor.  There are a lot of fluctuations.  Last time also, you had advised me about
 it.  I have personally taken two-three meetings. Our Financial Advisor was also there to see how we can increase the expenditure this year.  But I am sorry to say we
 have not been greatly successful.  Basically, the decision was not with us.  It was either with the Ministry of Defence or the Ministry of Environment.  But we have
 taken the advice.  We will try and either bring down the Budget requirement or increase the expenditure."
           
10.       The Committee expresses its concern over the failure of Department of Shipping to spend the approved outlay.  During the year 2004-05, out of the budgeted amount of Rs.2419.66
 crore the Department could spent only Rs.1400.99 crore which clearly indicates the poor fiscal management in the Department of Shipping.  The Committee observes that even while
 revising allocations, little consideration has been paid to the actual expenditure incurred in the past.  This shows that budgetary and revised estimates have always been made without
 taking into account the actual requirements as experienced in the previous years.

16.       The Committee is unhappy with the poor performance of Department of Shipping with regard to under utilization of approved outlays over the years for the port sector.  During the

CAPACITY AND TRAFFIC IN MAJOR PORT
17.       The Committee desires to know the progress for modernization and capacity expansion of the port network across India's Maritime Zone, the Department of Shipping in its written
 submission stated that modernization and capacity expansion of the ports in India is a part of the on going process of their development to cope with the demands of maritime trade. The
 Plans for development of major ports are included in the Five-Year Plans of the Government for the Port Sector.  During the Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002-07), an amount of Rs.4531.29
 crore has been allocated for development of major ports.  Out of this, Rs.450.00 crore are to be provided as budgetary support from the Government and Rs.4081.29 crore are to be raised
 by the concerned ports through their own internal and extra budgetary resources.  In addition, an investment of about Rs.11257.00 crore by the private sector is also envisaged in major
 ports during the Tenth Plan period.
 
18.       The focus during the Tenth Five-Year Plan period is on the major ports which inter-alia , lay emphasis on improving productivity through technological upgradation, provision of
 cost effective services and enhancement of service quality comparable to global levels. This is planned to be achieved through projects for enhancement of depths in the port channels and
 basins to enable the ports to receive large size vessels, upgradation/conversion of existing conventional berths to suit emerging requirements; construction of new berths, upgradation of
 cargo handling equipments and maintenance procedures; redesigning and upgradation of internal equipments and maintenance procedures; redesigning and upgradation of internal road
 network and circulation systems; upgradation of storage facilities and other port infrastructure coupled with commercial accounting practices, improved equipment and labour
 productivity, optimization of work force, training, optimal use of information technology, improvement in road and rail connectivity to ports, etc. 
           
19.       The aggregate capacity of major ports which was 343.95 million tones per annum (MTPA) at the beginning of the Tenth Plan period increased to 389.50 MTPA as on 31.3.2004. 
 The aggregate capacity is projected to reach 470.60 MTPA by the end of the Tenth Plan Period. The total capacity of all the Major Ports is anticipated to be 400 million tones per annum
 (MTPA), as on 31.03.2005 whereas the total traffic target during 2004-05 is 366.97 MT. Simultaneously, the National Maritime Development Programme, will include specific
 schemes/projects to be taken up over a ten year period is also under finalization.

21.    When enquired about the details of encouraging private sector participation and framing of guidelines for involvement of private sector for development of infrastructure services in
 the port sector, the Department of Shipping informed that the existing policy dispensation provides for private sector participation including foreign direct investment upto 100% in the
 port sector. The Government's endeavour is to provide a facilitative investor friendly environment with transparent procedures and decision making process for encouraging private sector
 participation in the sector.  The Government has, issued comprehensive guidelines for attracting private sector participation in major ports as also for formation of joint ventures by major
 ports for development of infrastructure services. The guidelines preferred route for private sector participation through open competitive bidding in which the bidder offering the highest
 percentage of revenue share out of the operation of the facility is selected.  Tariff Authority for Major ports (TAMP) which is an independent statutory authority fixes the tariffs to be
 charged from the port users by the private operators. These tariffs act as a ceiling and the private operators are free to charge below these ceilings.
 
22.    The major ports can also form joint ventures with publicly owned foreign ports for attracting new technology, introducing better managerial practices, etc.  In case it is considered
 that certain technology, expertise, managerial practices are necessary to be imported from a particular country, Government to Government arrangements for formation of a joint venture
 between a major port in India and a foreign port to be selected by the foreign Government can also be considered.  These joint ventures can be formed without following the tender route. 
 The form of collaboration will be on BOT basis. 
 
23.    In the reply to a query regarding achievement in attracting private sector participation in major ports, the Department of Shipping informed that at present, 37 private sector/captive
 port projects involving capacity addition of about 137 MTPA and an investment of about Rs.9756 crore are at various stage of evaluation and implementation. Out of these 13 Projects
 with capacity addition of about 47.40 MTPA involving an investment of about Rs.2597 crore have been operationalised.  However, the focus during the Annual Plan 2005-06 and Tenth
 Five Year Plan period will continue to be on the following:-
(i)                  Enhancing productivity of Major Ports through technology up gradation.
(ii)                Cost effective service.
(iii)               Improving service-quality
(iv)              Efficient management, institutional funding and attracting private investments.
 
24.    The Department of Shipping also informed that as part of on-going process for making the guidelines flexible and more investor friendly, the Government is in the process of
 reviewing the existing guidelines and revising them.
 
25.       During the course of evidence, the Secretary, deposed before the Committee that;
"Sir, I will now continue with what you mentioned earlier.  You were talking about private investment.  You said that it is not flowing in.  It is quite right.  In the port sector,
 any investment, whether it is foreign or private, is not taking place.  At the same time in JNPT two private terminal operators have come.  At Tuticorin, Vishakhapatnam
 private terminal operators have come in with their private investment.   I think we should have a very enabling policy.  We have talked to many other countries.  They are
 giving more liberal concessions on any individual item, which the investor brings up.  Suppose, he says that I will put in so much of investment on land, but your policy is
 for 30 years, then, nobody opens that matter.  Sir, there has been Rs.3800 crores investment in the private sector overall including everything."
 
26.       The Committee expresses its appreciation for achievement of the capacity of major ports which has either exceeded the target or likely to achieve target fixed for cargo handling of
 all commodities except container.  The containerization is the emerging global trend in the mode of cargo trade in which the country is lagging behind which is a matter of concern.
 
27.       The Committee feels that a proper environment with transparent procedures and decision making process can give boost to private sector participation in the port sector.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends the Department of Shipping to review the existing guidelines on Private Sector Participation in the port sector with emphasis on overcoming capital
 constraints, technology upgradation and value addition.

TONNAGE TAX
 
28.       The Government has announced introduction of Tonnage Tax from the financial year 2004-05 for the Shipping Industry with an option for the shipping companies to opt for either
 tonnage tax or corporate tax. This will facilitate investment in shipping sector. The silent features
of the tonnage tax regime are:-
Ø      The introduction of Tonnage tax will provide a level playing field to the Indian Shipping    Industry with its international counterparts.
 
Ø      This will be implemented from the current financial year. 
Ø      At least 20% of the book profits of the companies opting for tonnage tax scheme to be    transferred to a separate reserve to be utilized for acquisition of vessels within 8 years. This
 will help to add more tonnage to the Indian Shipping industry.
 
Ø      Provision regarding mandatory minimum training requirements for the companies opting for the proposed Tonnage Tax regime. 
 
Ø      The shipping companies are to opt either of Tonnage Tax or Corporation tax.
 
Ø      An option for tonnage tax has to be exercised by the company once. The Tonnage Tax regime would remain applicable for 10 years, for the companies opting for the same. 
 
29.       Dredgers have also been included in the Tonnage Tax regime from 2005-06. The Government has been making constant efforts for creating hospitable investment environment for
 the Shipping Industry.  Some of the efforts made/steps taken are as under:
Ø                              Prior to introduction of Tonnage tax, the Indian Shipping Industry were enjoying the benefits under Section 33AC of IT Act. Under Section 33AC shipping
 companies were allowed to create reserves upto twice the paid up capital and reserves including share premium account, which they were supposed to be utilized in next 8 years
 for acquisition of vessels. The amounts so transferred in the reserve were exempted from corporate tax;
 
Ø
                              Import of all types of ships has been included in the Open General Licence (OGL) thereby dispensing with licence requirement for import of various kind of
 ships, as per guidelines issued by the Ministry of Shipping on 15th June,2001 and 5th March,2002 ;
 
Ø                              100 percent Foreign Direct Investment  (FDI) is allowed in Shipping sector;
30.       Regarding extending the benefit of tonnage tax to the vessels registered under Indian Vessels Act, 1917 and Indian Coasting Vessels Act, 1838, the Department of Shipping in its
 written reply stated that while presenting the Finance bill for 2005-06, the Finance Minister has pronounced extension of tonnage tax regime for dredgers only.  However, for vessels
 registered under Indian Vessels Act 1917 and Indian Coastal Vessels Act, 1838 the benefit of tonnage tax has not been extended in this bill.  The issue is being taken up with Ministry of
 Finance for extending the benefits to these types of vessels also.
           
31.       The Committee notes that the Government has since introduced the tonnage tax regime to the dredgers also as per its recommendation.  The Committee feels that introduction of
 tonnage tax in lieu of corporate tax will not only facilitate investment in shipping sector but also provide a level playing field to the Indian Shipping Industry vis-à-vis its foreign
 counterparts and the shipping companies will be able to acquire more tonnage.  However, the Committee is unable to comprehend the reasons for excluding the vessels registered under
 the Indian Vessels Act 1917 and Indian coastal vessel Act, 1838.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the matter for extending the benefits of tonnage tax to the vessels registered
 under Indian Vessels Act 1917 and Indian Coastal Vessels Act, 1838 may be pursued further with the Ministry of Finance as this also vitally linked to the viability of the Inland Water

SETHUSAMUDRAM SHIP CHANNEL PROJECT
32.       India does not have a continuous navigable route around the peninsula within her own territorial waters due to presence of a reef called Adam's Bridge at Pamban near
 Rameshwaram.  Sethusamudram Ship Channel Project envisages cutting of a ship channel to connect the Gulf of Mannar to Bay of Bengal through Palk Bay so that most of the ships,
 depending on the draft required, moving between east and west coast of India could have a continuous navigable sea route around the Peninsula within India's own territorial waters.  This
 will save upto 424 nautical miles of travel and upto about 30 hours of sailing time for ships between east and west coast.    The project will serve the strategic and national security
 interests of the country since the channel can also be used by Naval and Coast Guard ships.  The project is also expected to spur overall economic development of coastal districts of
 Tamilnadu through development of infrastructure, ancillary industries etc. 
33.       To a specific query regarding adverse environmental effects of the proposed channel, the Department of Shipping in its written reply stated that a section of environmentalists have
 expressed their concerns about the potential adverse effects of the proposed channel on environment.  The main apprehensions expressed are:
(a)                Environmental Impact such as effect on marine flora and fauna, oil spillage, increased salinity in waterbed etc.
(b)               Economic Impact such as loss of livelihood of fishermen due to reduction in fish catches.

 received environment clearance from the Ministry of Environment & Forests.  The project will take about 180 weeks for completion from the date of receipt of approval of the competent

CRUISE SHIPPING
37.       Cruise shipping is a fast growing sector globally. As a departure from concept of cargo and passenger ships, cruise is like a moving township studded with all state of art facilities
 including all types of recreational activities. The idea is fast catching, because, journey on a cruise is not only pleasurable, but also almost free from sea travel health hazards. Experience
 gained in the Caribbean, Latin American and South-East Asian countries indicates that huge amount of foreign exchange can be earned by providing the right policy environment and
 infrastructure for the growth of cruise shipping. India with its vast and beautiful coastline, virgin forests and undisturbed idyllic islands, long historical and cultural tradition of
 architecture, theatre and performing arts can prove to be fabulous destination for cruise-tourists.  The global scenario for cruise shipping is picking up very fast, with an annual increment
 from 10-20% in the cruise ship fleet and passengers carried. Further, Indian coastline is unfortunately not witnessing the same growth pattern as mentioned.  Therefore, it is said that a
 strong policy intervention by the Government of India would be necessary for its stimulated growth. 
 
38.       To a specific question regarding efforts for growth of cruise shipping in India, the Department of Shipping in its written reply submitted that in order to formulate cruise shipping
 policy a High Power Steering Group under the Chairmanship of Minister of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways and Union Minister of State for Tourism (Independent Charge) as Co-
Chairperson has been set up on 29.9.2004.  The first meeting of this Group was held on 14
th December 2004 in which the draft Concept and Policy Paper for Cruise Shipping in India was
 discussed at length.  Various issues concerning this sector have been taken up and Working Groups have been formed to tackle all the issues.  Three Working Groups have already
 finalized their reports, which will come up before the Steering Group for providing finality to their recommendations.  Other Groups are also progressing well and it is hoped that very
 soon their recommendations will be compiled and given a final shape to the proposed policy.  The draft policy paper has also been deliberated by the Consultative Committee of this
 Ministry in its Meeting held on 20.12.2004.  A Workshop was also convened at Mumbai on 12.2.2005 which was attended by Members of the Consultative Committee, various
 stakeholders, hospitality industry, shipping lines, cruise lines, tour operators and shipping agents.  Once a policy is in place, besides providing a direction to the growth of cruise shipping,
 it will also remove the bottlenecks pertaining to the sector.
39.       The Committee desired to know the details of private sector participation for development of infrastructure and other facilities at various ports, the Department of Shipping in its
 written reply informed that working group comprising of the representatives of Department of Tourism, the major ports and the industry was constituted by Department of Shipping for
 identification of ports for development of cruise facilities.  The Group has recommended that in the first phase, 6 major ports of call by the cruise liners namely, Mumbai, Mormugao, New
 Mangalore, Cochin, Tuticorin and Chennai be selected for the development of infrastructure and other facilities so as to make India as an attractive cruise destination.  In the second phase,
 the major port of Kolkata and minor ports at Puri, Porbandar and Port Blair have been recommended for development of cruise facilities.  Further, if any major/minor port is able to attract
 a BOT operator to invest in infrastructure facilities, the port may develop such facility. The development of cruise tourism infrastructure is to be done through port resources, financial

 cruise tourism infrastructure, wherever viable.

INLAND WATER TRANSPORT
 92.      Inland Water Transport (IWT) is an efficient mode of transport from the point of view of energy consumption, cost of transportation of environment friendliness. IWAI executes
 various Schemes for development of the Inland Water Transport infrastructure namely fairway development, terminals, and navigational aids for National Waterways.  So far, following
 three waterways have been declared as national waterways:-
National Waterway No.1 (NW-1)
            Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hooghly river system from Allahabad to Haldia (1620 kms) - declared as national waterway in 1986.
National Waterway No.2 (NW-2)
            The Brahmaputra river from Sadiya to Dhubri (891 kms) - declared as national waterway in 1988.
National Waterway No.3 (NW-3)
            The west coast canal from Kollam to Kottapuram alongwith Champakara and Udyogmandal canais (205 kms) - declared as national waterway in 1993.
 
93.       To a querry regarding increasing the number of National Waterways, the Department of Shipping its written replies stated that the Department of Shipping is working in the
 direction of increasing number of National Waterways from the existing 3 to 6.  The proposals for declaring waterways as new national waterways are being considered, as under:-
(i)         Kakinada-Pondicherry canal along with rivers Godavari and Krishna
(ii)        The river Barak, and
(iii)       East Coast Canal along with river Brahmani and Mahanadi delta
 
94.       The Hydrographic survey and Techno-economic feasibility studies on all the above mentioned waterways have been completed. IWAI has also now taken up environmental study
 for the waterways at (i) and (iii) above.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
AT A GLANCE
 
ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2005-06
            The Committee expresses its concern over the failure of Department of Shipping to spend the approved outlay.  During the year 2004-05, out of the budgeted amount of Rs.2419.66
 crore the Department could spent only Rs.1400.99 crore which clearly indicates the poor fiscal management in the Department of Shipping.  The Committee observes that even while
 revising allocations, little consideration has been paid to the actual expenditure incurred in the past.  This shows that budgetary and revised estimates have always been made without
 taking into account the actual requirements as experienced in the previous years.
           
The Committee feels that Department of Shipping has not evolved effective system or planning to systematically anticipate, assess and project its actual requirements of funds
 otherwise the investments could have yielded commensurate returns.  The Committee recommends that in future, the Department of Shipping should be transparent and realistic while
 making projections of required funds so that resources may not remain unused and are allocated more judiciously.

PORT SECTOR
The Committee is unhappy with the poor performance of Department of Shipping with regard to under utilization of approved outlays over the years for the port sector.  During the
 Financial year 2004-05, an outlay of Rs.1066.55 crore was allocated for the port sector but the ports could utilize only Rs.440.51 crore. The Committee is dismayed by this repeated failure
 of Department of Shipping and is surprised as to why the Ministry asks for so much of allocation if it is not in position to spend the approved outlay. The Committee desires that the
 Department of Shipping should take all corrective measures based upon actual requirements for maximum utilization of budgeted amount during 2005-06.  In this regard, the Committee
 reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in 81st report of Demands for Grants (2004-05) of Ministry of Shipping which reads as under:-
"The Committee fails to understand as to how the physical targets will be achieved with this low utilization pattern.  In fact, the Committee feels that this raises doubts about
 the mismatch in the projections for physical and financial targets.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Shipping has full responsibility to ensure proper
 utilization of the approved outlay.  The Committee feels that strict monitoring is crucial for timely completion of any project.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that
 the monitoring agency should play rather proactive role and do away with the procedural hurdles for timely completion of the projects so that the budgeted allocation is
 utilized optimally.  The Committee also recommends the Ministry to keep informed about the progress in the matter."
            (Para 16)
CAPACITY AND TRAFFIC IN MAJOR PORT
            The Committee expresses its appreciation for achievement of the capacity of major ports which has either exceeded the target or likely to achieve target fixed for cargo handling of
 all commodities except container.  The containerization is the emerging global trend in the mode of cargo trade in which the country is lagging behind which is a matter of concern.
           
The Committee feels that a proper environment with transparent procedures and decision making process can give boost to private sector participation in the port sector.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends the Department of Shipping to review the existing guidelines on Private Sector Participation in the port sector with emphasis on overcoming capital
 constraints, technology upgradation and value addition.
(Paras 26 & 27)
TONNAGE TAX
            The Committee notes that the Government has since introduced the tonnage tax regime to the dredgers also as per its recommendation.  The Committee feels that introduction of
 tonnage tax in lieu of corporate tax will not only facilitate investment in shipping sector but also provide a level playing field to the Indian Shipping Industry vis-à-vis its foreign
 counterparts and the shipping companies will be able to acquire more tonnage.  However, the Committee is unable to comprehend the reasons for excluding the vessels registered under
 the Indian Vessels Act 1917 and Indian coastal vessel Act, 1838.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the matter for extending the benefits of tonnage tax to the vessels registered
 under Indian Vessels Act 1917 and Indian Coastal Vessels Act, 1838 may be pursued further with the Ministry of Finance as this also vitally linked to the viability of the Inland Water
 Transport sector.
(Para 31)
SETHUSAMUDRAM SHIP CHANNEL PROJECT
            The Committee notes the Sethusamudram Ship Channel Project is of great economic and strategic importance which will connect the Gulf of Mannar to Bay of Bengal so that the
 ship moving between East and West coast of India could have a continuous navigable sea route within their own territorial waters and the proposed channel save upto 424 nautical miles of
 travel and about 30 hours of sailing time for ships.  The Committee notes with satisfaction that Ministry of Environment & Forests has also given environmental clearance to the project
 and a special purpose vehicle has been incorporated to raise finance to create and operate navigable channel.  The Committee expects that the project once completed will serve the
 strategic and national security interests besides spuring the overall economic development of coastal districts of southern states.  The Committee however, recommends that while taking
 up the implementation of the project, the Department of Shipping must give due attention to the likely effect of natural calamities particularly one like as Tsunami which hit southern
 states during December 2004 and safety measures may be incorporated accordingly.
  (Para 36)
CRUISE SHIPPING
            The Committee is of the view that sea travel is becoming increasingly popular and there is enormous potential for its growth and expansion in India.  The Committee notes that six
 major ports- Mumbai, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Kochi, Tuticorin and Chennai have been identified for the development of infrastructure and other facilities with a view to make India
 an attractive cruise destination.  The Committee notes that a High Power Steering Group has been set up with a view to catalyze the growth and development of cruise shipping and to
 remove bottlenecks and a policy is being formulated in the regard. The Committee hopes that the group will submit its report expeditiously and the policy is framed at the earliest so that
 the benefit of opportunity available to cruise tourism sector is exploited.  The Committee recommends that pending finalization of policy, the Department of Shipping must take steps to
 attract investments from private sector with a view to create infrastructure and other facilities for development of cruise shipping especially in ports on the Eastern parts of the country
 including Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshdweep Islands having great potential in that area.
(Para 40)
CALCUTTA  DOCK LABOUR BOARD
            The Committee is distressed to note that the employees of the Calcutta Dock Labour Board are not being treated as Government employees so far as payment of pension and
 pensionary benefits are concerned, though, the Calcutta Dock Labour Board, a body corporate, was constituted in terms of section 5(A) of the Dock Workers (Regulation of Employment)
 Act, 1948 in September 1952.  The Committee expresses its concern that inspite of specific provisions under the Act, the rules and regulations concerning pension for employees of
 Calcutta Dock Labour Board have not been approved by Department of Expenditure and the employees of Calcutta Dock Labour Board are being made victims under the cover that
 Calcutta Dock Labour Board constitutes an autonomous body and therefore, denied of the benefits for long.

            The Committee is of the firm opinion that liabilities on account of payment of arrears of pensions and pensionary dues of Calcutta Dock Labour Board entirely lies on the
 Government and the Government cannot be absolved of its liability and withhold pension and post retirement benefits to the employees of Calcutta Dock Labour Board on one pretext or
 other.  The Committee, strongly recommends, the Department of Shipping to vigorously pursue the matter with Ministry of Finance for release of enhanced grant to Calcutta Dock Labour
 Board for meeting its pensionary liabilities and implement comprehensive restructuring of Calcutta Dock Labour Board for introduction of Voluntary Retirement Scheme.
 
            The Committee is of the view that problem for payment of pension and pensionary dues can be resolved once and for all, if the Government approves self-sustaining proposal for
 Calcutta Dock Labour Board's pensioners with provisions for an initial corpus and an appropriate policy and methodology for payment of pension worked out for the future.  The
 Committee, therefore reiterates, that the Government should build a corpus of pension fund for discharging the future pensionary liabilities of the Board, as the bulk of its employees will
 retire in another two years.
(Paras 49, 50 &51)
MISAPPROPRIATION OF SEAMEN'S PROVIDENT FUND
            The Committee is deeply concerned that the shortage to the tune of Rs.92.78 crore in Seamen's Provident Fund, which lost due to security scam, has not yet been recouped by the
 Department of Shipping.  The Committee is unable to comprehend the reasons for delay in recouping the fund, which is entirely managed by the Government and feels that any further
 delay may cause adverse impact in protection of interests of the seamen's whose life long savings stands jeopardized in the scam.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department
 of Shipping to approach the Ministry of Finance for grants-in-aid to recoup the shortage in the Seamen's Provident Fund.
 
            The Committee further recommends that in order to ensure that such happenings are not repeated in future, sufficient legislative and administrative safeguards have to be built in the
 system.  At the same time, those responsible for such scam should not be spared and suitable action including criminal action ought to be taken against them so that such actions provide
 deterrence to other as well.  The Committee would like to be apprised of the action in this regard.
(Paras 55 & 56)
INDIAN INSTITUTE OF MARITIME STUDIES (IIMS)
            The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department of Shipping is in the process of drafting Indian Maritime University Bill for the establishment of Indian Maritime
 University under an Act of Parliament and a token provision has been made by the Government for development of Indian Maritime  University. The Committee feels that the move will
 certainly give boost to quality education in the field of marine training.  However, the Committee is unhappy to note that no definite time limit has been fixed for establishment of Indian
 Maritime University and recommends, the Department of Shipping to fix definite time frame for the establishment of Indian Maritime University under a Central Act.  The Committee
 further hopes that while finalizing the location of the Indian Maritime University, existing infrastructure and assests have to be taken into cognizance. 
           
The Committee hopes that the issues pertaining to creation of pension fund and finalization of terms & condition for the absorption of employees of Indian Institute of Maritime
 Studies will be taken care of by the Department without any disadvantage to the employees in the draft bill itself and recommends the Department of Shipping to apprise the Committee of
 the development in this regard.
(Paras 62 & 63)
HINDUSTAN SHIPYARD LIMITED
            The Committee takes note of the fact that Hindustan Shipyard Limited is changing its marketing strategy by engaging itself in the utilization of installed capacity for increasing
 ship-repair turnover.  The vigorous marketing efforts are showing results  and Hindustan Shipyard Limited has been able to secure the hi-tech, hi-value repair orders for betterment of the
 financial health of the yard.  The Committee appreciates the decision of the Government for rehabilitation of Hindustan Shipyard Limited by way of financial restructuring for improving
 the performance of the yard and recommends for expeditious clearance of the proposal for the capital restructuring of the Hindustan Shipyard Limited.                                     
(Para 76)
HOOGHLY DOCK & PORT ENGINEERS LIMITED
            The Committee is happy to note that a rehabilitation-cum-revival package has been prepared for Hooghly Dock & Port Engineers Limited and a token provision has also been kept
 for the purpose as per its recommendation.  The Committee is of the opinion that Hooghly Dock & Port Engineers Limited with its infrastructure and a expertise can be a integral part for
 development and promotion of Inland Water Transport through NW-1 and NW-2 and contribute to the national economy as well. The Committee, therefore recommends the Government
 to grant clearance for the revival package of Hooghly Dock & Port Engineers Limited expeditiously.
(Para 82)
CENTRAL INLAND WATER TRANSPORT CORPORATION
The Committee notes with distress that the matter for disinvestments of Central Inland Water Transport Corporation Ltd has been referred to the Board for Reconstruction of Public
 Sector Enterprises.  The Committee feels that the Government has not made much efforts to improve the financial health of the company.  The Committee is gravely concern by the fact
 that inspite of repeated recommendation in the past the Government has not yet been able to come out with a complete blueprint for making full use of the existing assets of the company
 in ensuring productive potential by exploring all possibilities like merger with similar entities and integration with Inland Water Transport. The Committee is firmly of the view that the
 future of Inland Water Transport operations including that of Central Inland Water Transport Corporation Ltd holds immense possibilities for establishing a well-defined multimodal
 transportation network in the country and therefore recommends the Department of Shipping to take steps to improve the performance of the Central Inland Water Transport Corporation
 Ltd besides impressing upon the Ministry of Finance for capital restructuring with its existing infrastructure, capacity and manpower.
(Para 91)
INLAND WATER TRANSPORT

            The Committee is constrained to note that Inland Water Transport has been the most neglected sector and has not been developed with desired pace inspite of its economic potential
 and merely accounts for 0.15% of cargo movement in the country.  The Committee observes that though measures to boost the growth of cargo traffic have been put in place and
 department is committed to raise the share of Inland Water Transport to 2% over the next 8-20 years. The Committee however, notes that with a view to compete with the other developed
 modes of transport, the Department of Shipping is working on declaration and development of three more waterways as National Waterways and a task force to monitor the progress of
 Inland Water Transport projects has also been formed. The Committee, recommends the Department of Shipping to exploit the full potential of this efficient mode of transport, besides,
 addressing the issues of development of adequate infrastructure facilities namely fairways, terminal and navigational aids on the existing three NWs.
 
            The Committee also recommends the Government to improve the modal-mix of transport in the country and prepare a road map for integrated transport policy to include   Inland
 Water Transport, Coastal Shipping, Civil Aviation, Road Transport & Highways and Railways.
            The Committee is perturbed to note the practice of under utilization of budgeted amount by the Department of Shipping over the years.  During 2004-05, the Department of
 Shipping has projected Rs.133.50 crore plan outlays for Inland Water Transport sector, which was reduced to Rs.121.03 at R.E. stage, and ultimately the Department of Shipping has spent
 only Rs.80.00 crore during 2004-05.  The Committee observes that actual expenditure incurred has always been low compared to the budget estimates for Inland Water Transport sector
 including North-East Pool of fund.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department of Shipping to intensify its monitoring mechanism to ensure that the budgeted amounts
 earmarked at the B.E. stage are fully utilized during 2005-06.  At the same time the Committee desires that the Department of Shipping should review all the on-going schemes and if
 required, re-allocate the funds budgeted during the financial year.
 
            The Committee notes that a comprehensive Inland Water Transport policy has been announced by the Government to encourage investment from the Private-Public participation
 for accelerated development of Inland Water Transport sector.  The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Department of Shipping for creating transparent, hospitable and conducive
 environment for ensuring both the Private and Public sector participation in the development of Inland Water Transport sector.  The Committee expects that primarily public investment in
 Inland Water Transport sector will definitely help to create infrastructure facilities and augment Inland Water Transport fleet.  The Committee recommends that all pending Inland Water
 Transport specific projects should be cleared expeditiously.
           
The Committee notes with dismay that Research & Development Studies facilities especially in design of vessels and night navigation facilities and identification of economically
 viable routes for development of Inland Water Transport sector has not been given due consideration.  The Committee is of the view that Research & Development activities is the
 backbone for success of any sector and recommends the Department of Shipping to strengthen the Research & Development facilities for development of Inland Water Transport Sector in
 the country.
           
The Committee is happy to note that Department of Shipping has appointed a consultant for working out a realistic target for cargo transportation vis-à-vis targeted investments for
 Inland Water Transport promotion for the 10
th Plan and beyond as per its recommendation.  The Committee hopes that the study for linking the investment in Inland Water Transport
 sector with achievement of physical targets for cargo transportation will certainly enhance the prospects of Inland Water Transport and help to boost the growth of cargo traffic in Inland
 Water Transport sector. The Committee directs the Department of Shipping to apprise it about the progress in the matter.
(Paras 98, 99, 104, 106, 108 & 110)
