EPORT
 
     The Aircraft (Exemption from Taxes and Duties on Fuel and Lubricants) Bill, 2000 seeks to exempt from
 payment of all customs duties, local duties, taxes, etc. on Aviation Turbine Fuel (ATF) and other items uplifted by an
 aircraft belonging to a foreign airline operating international service to, from and through India.
2.       The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill states that bilateral air services agreements signed by the
 Government of India with other countries provide for, on reciprocal basis, either grant of outright exemption from
 payment of custom duties, local duties, taxes, etc., on Aviation Turbine Fuel (ATF) and other items uplifted by aircraft
 of one Contracting State from the territory of the other Contracting State or grant of most favoured nation treatment in
 these matters to the airlines of the Contracting States.
3.       The Bill has been introduced in order to fulfill the obligations arising out of the air services agreements and the
 Resolution of the International Civil Organization (ICAO).  The ICAO had adopted a resolution in December 1993,
 which, inter alia, provides that when an aircraft registered in one State or an aircraft leased or chartered by an operator
 of that State departs from an international airport of another State either for another customs territory of that latter State
 or for the territory of any other State, the fuel, lubricants and other consumable technical supplies taken on board for
 consumption during the flight shall be exempt from all customs and other duties.  Such duties and taxes shall include
 those levied by any taxing authority within a State, whether national or local.
4.       It is in pursuance of the fulfillment of the obligations arising out of the air services agreements and the resolution
 of the ICAO as detailed above, the Central Government was earlier paying subsidy to the State Governments for
 exempting the international airlines from levy of sales tax on ATF uplifted by them from India.  The Ministry of Civil
 Aviation in their background brief in respect of this Bill has pointed out that as a part of the general policy for reduction
 or elimination of various subsidies, the scheme was discontinued from the 1st December 1994 by the Central

 Government.
5.       The Ministry’s brief further states that following discontinuation of the subsidy by the Government,
 representations were received from foreign airlines stating that the levy of sales tax was contrary to the provisions of
 bilateral air agreement signed by the Government of India as well as the resolution adopted by the ICAO.  Keeping in
 mind the obligations arising out of the bilateral air services agreements and ICAO resolution, the present legislation has
 been proposed to be enacted under article 253 read with Entry 14 of List-I of the VII Schedule of the Constitution of
 India to exempt from all duties and taxes the fuel and lubricants uplifted by an aircraft registered in another State
 operating international services to, from and through India.
6.       The Ministry in its note has clarified that the new legislation will provide exemption from levy of sales tax on
 ATF and lubricants to foreign airlines only.  The exemption will not be available to national carriers as the legislation
 under article 253 can be resorted to only for the purpose of fulfilling obligations under international treaties. 
7.       In its meeting held on the 14th March 2001, the Committee had preliminary discussions on the provisions of the
 Bill.  It decided to obtain the suggestions/views of the domestic airlines operating in the country as also of the State
 Governments from where international flights are operating on the provisions of the Bill.
8.       In the light of the suggestions made by the domestic airlines and the State Governments concerned, the
 Committee heard the views of the representatives of the Ministry of Civil Aviation in its meeting held on the 7th
 August 2001.
VIEWS OF THE STATE GOVERNMENTS
9.       The Committee sought the views of the State Governments of Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Delhi, Karnataka,
 Maharashtra, Tamilnadu, West Bengal, Gujarat, Goa, Punjab and Kerala, the States which are likely to be affected by
 the provisions of the Bill.   Most of the State Governments have stated that they have no objection to the proposed Bill
 being passed by the Parliament of India.  However, they have added that the exemption of sales tax for the international
 airlines would result in substantial revenue loss to them and would have adverse effect on their finances.  The State
 Governments have, therefore, suggested that the loss as a result of this exemption may be made good by the
 Government of India by an annual grant equivalent to the loss of revenue suffered by the respective State Governments.
VIEWS OF THE DOMESTIC PRIVATE AIRLINES
10.     The Committee sought the views of the domestic private airlines operating in the country on the provisions of the
 Bill.  M/s Jet Airways and M/s Sahara Airlines have highlighted that the sales tax and other levies charged over and
 above the basic rate of fuel by the oil companies varies from 20 per cent to 30.55 per cent depending on the State
 Government.  These airlines have stated that this adds to the total cost of operations and have argued that this high levy
 of domestic duties is not at par with the matching revenue earning potential of the various sectors serviced by them.  
 The two airlines have, therefore, expressed hope that exemption given to the international carriers will be extended to
 the domestic airlines as well.
VIEWS OF THE MINISTRY OF PETROLEUM AND NATURAL GAS
11.     The Committee sought the views of the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas on the Bill with specific reference
 to the practice hitherto being followed of reimbursing the State Governments for the revenue loss which probably is
 met out of Oil Pool Fund. The Committee has been informed by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas that after
 December, 1994, while the oil companies are depositing the due sales tax amounts with the State Government
 authorities, they are unable to recover this amount from the foreign airlines.   Thus, it is the oil companies and not the
 State Governments which are suffering revenue loss on this account.   As on March 31, 2001, the cumulative
 outstanding of the oil companies on account of under-recoveries due to sales tax on sales of ATF to the international
 airlines had reached a staggering figure of about Rs.694 crores.  The Ministry have pointed out that the oil companies
 cannot go on incurring this loss particularly in the post-administered-price-mechanism period.  The outstanding dues
 have adversely affected the liquidity of oil companies and these companies have been, time and again, raising this issue
 with the Central Government requesting for an early reimbursement of the outstanding amount.   The Ministry of
 Petroleum and Natural Gas have emphasized that not only the outstanding amounts need to be quickly reimbursed by
 the Government to the oil companies but also the proposed legislation should be enacted at the earliest so as to contain
 the future under-recoveries on this account.
12.     The pricing of ATF has been decontrolled with effect from 1.4.2001, and hence the prices of ATF are market-

13.     The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas have further stated that in case the proposed legislation is not enacted,

 cost of sales tax in the sale prices of ATF.  Not only will the later alternative tantamount to a violation of the
 Agreement entered into by India with other countries in pursuance of the convention on international civil aviation but
 this option is also likely to result in reduction of sales tax revenues of the State Governments as the foreign airlines
 might then try to either avoid taking ATF from India or import this fuel and set up their own captive depots in India for
 re-fuelling purposes in which case also the States will not get any sales tax revenue.
VIEWS OF THE MINISTRY OF CIVIL AVIATION
14.     In the Committee’s meeting held on August 7, 2001, the Secretary, Civil Aviation made a presentation on the Bill
 before the Committee.  He stated that there was an international practice that no local tax, sales tax or other tax will be
 charged on the fuel that is uplifted from mutually contracting States.   Till 1st December 1994, the Central Government
 used to reimburse to the State Governments.  The Central Government did so to fulfil its obligations under the
 international agreement and bilateral treaties.   He added that the proposed legislation is under article 253 of the
 Constitution.  He added that once this Bill becomes an act, they would be able to resolve this problem which is causing
 them embarrassment because it was an international commitment.  It is in fulfillment of their international obligation.
15.     The Secretary, Civil Aviation, was specifically asked as to whether a discussion was held with the concerned
 State Governments before introducing the Bill.  The Secretary, Civil Aviation, did not give a direct reply to this specific
 query and inter-alia stated "…… there are certain obligations when two Sovereign States enter into an agreement.   One
 of the obligations, as in this case, is that the duties are not paid.  This is the fundamental point I am making …..".  He
 also observed that the State Governments were not at all entitled to this subsidy which they have been enjoying.   With
 regard to as to how long this practice of reimbursement by the Central Government to the State Governments had been
 going on, the Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation, stated that it might be around forty years.  The Committee had
 asked the Ministry of Civil Aviation as to whether there was any proposal to extend the benefit of sales tax exemption
 to Air India and other domestic airlines.  The Committee was informed that a separate proposal was being considered to
 exempt Indian carriers going abroad from exemption of sales tax by declaring upliftment of ATF as deemed exports. 
 However, this facility will not be available to domestic carriers operating within the country.
16.     The Committee also enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding the criterion/rate of sales tax being
 charged by various States on ATF and whether the criterion was the same for the domestic and international airlines.
 The Ministry of Civil Aviation informed the Committee that the criteria for charging sales tax on ATF by various States
 is a state matter.  The criteria being adopted by the states for sales tax on domestic and international airlines as well as
 flights is the same.  The sales tax being charged by the State Governments on ATF uplifted at various places by Air

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
17.     The Committee is of the view that the passage of this legislation is no doubt essential in order to fulfill the
 international obligations and bilateral treaties entered into with foreign countries.  However, at the same time, it feels
 that the State Governments have for almost forty years been provided with a facility, namely, reimbursement of sales
 tax by the Central Government and since December 1 1994, through the Oil Pool Fund.  The amount involved in each
 of the State concerned is fairly substantial.  Sudden stoppage of this source of income is bound to adversely affect their
 already precarious financial condition.   The Committee feels that the State Governments should have been consulted
 while enacting the legislation which directly concerns them, particularly in an important area such as financial matter. 
 The Committee recommends that the Central Government should continue to reimburse the State Governments of the
 loss of revenue which might result once this enactment is passed by the Parliament and comes into effect. 
18.     The Committee notes that a separate proposal is being considered by the Ministry of Civil Aviation to exempt
 domestic carriers going abroad from exemption of sales tax on ATF.  The Committee appreciates the proposal and
 hopes that the same would be implemented at the earliest.
19.     The Committee at its meeting held on October 29, 2001, took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill.   The
 Committee, having regard to the submission made by the representatives of the Ministry of Civil Aviation and
 documents made available to it on this subject, is of the opinion that there is a need to fulfill the international
 obligations and to achieve the objectives set out in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill.
20.     Clause II and III were adopted without any modification.
21.     The Committee notes that the proposed legislation is to exempt only the foreign carriers from the payment of
 customs and sales tax duty on ATF.  Since it does not apply to domestic carriers, the Committee recommends that the
 word 'foreign' may be added in the title of the Bill.  Therefore, the title of the Bill should be as under:
“The Foreign Aircraft (Exemption from Taxes and Duties on Fuel and Lubricants) Bill, 2001.”
CLAUSE – I, THE ENACTING FORMULA AND TITLE
22.     Clause 1, the Enacting Formula and the Title were adopted with some changes which are of consequential nature,
 namely, '2000' and 'Fifty-first' to be substituted by '2001' and 'Fifty-second' respectively.
23.     The Committee recommends that the Bill be passed taking into account the suggestions/recommendations as
 made in the Report.
