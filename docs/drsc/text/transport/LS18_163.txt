7.  The tables above do not reflect a very good per formance in achieving the financial and physical targets except a few. The 
Committee finds the reasons given for the savings are of routine nature. For example, one of the reasons for saving CRF is non-
submission of proposals and non-receipt of utilizat ion certificates by the State Governments. Reasons for these, if provided 
could have been more revealing to the Committee. Th e state-wise details also could have further provid ed a real picture in this 
regard. Saving of Rs.50 crores due to retendering o f works of development of National Highways and Sta tes roads in naxal 
affected areas which is a sensitive programme is a cause of concern. Efforts should have been made for  avoiding the problem 
necessitating re-tendering. Further an amount of Rs .20 crore meant for development of Vijaywada-Ranchi  National Highways 
could not be utilized because draft note for solici ting approval of CCI is still under process even af ter one year has passed. 
Another area of major concern is non-utilisation of Rs.600 crore under SARDP-NE due to slow progress o f work by BRO. The 
reasons for slow progress are not known. There is a  saving of Rs.15 crore in the projects of strategic  and Border Roads Project 
due to non-materialization of supply orders and con tract for procurement of Constructional stores and materials. Here also, 
reasons are not given. An amount of Rs.50 crores fo r POSCO could not be utilized due to non-receipt of  utilization certificates 
from the Government of Orissa. Obviously, due to non-utilization of funds the project must be languishing. An amount of Rs.20 
crore could not be utilized because Mughal Road Project is being funded directly by the Planning Commission. If it was so, why 
the allocation was provided in the Budget? What is the Status of project? Who is monitoring the projec t? What is the role of 
Ministry of Road Transport and Highways in this pro ject? The explanations given for saving by the Mini stry are not clear and 
convincing. In view of these, the Committee is of t he view that the Ministry should have been more pro active and taken 
necessary steps on time to ensure that these delays in execution of projects should have been avoided. 
 (Recommendation at para 10 of 155th Report)

Duburi - Brahmanipal-Harichandanpur- Naranpur Keonjhar Road (POSCO Project)  
12. Widening to 2 lane with paved shoulders alongwi th the provision of 4/6 lane CD works/bridges for Duburi - Brahmanipal 
- Harichandanpur- Naranpur Keonjhar Road (total length 91.18km) has been sanctioned by Ministry on 21st May 2007 under EI 
Scheme for an amount of Rs.302.09 crore. The work is being financed from Gross Budgetary Support. The Share of the Central 
Government in the project is limited to Rs.143.07 c rore and remaining Rs.159.02 crore is State Governm ent’s share. The work 
was commenced on 15.11.2007 with stipulated date of  completion as 14.11.2010. As on 28.02.2010, total Rs.62.22 crore have 
already been utilized against Rs.90.05 crore releases made from the Central share. The total expenditure in the job is reported to 
be Rs. 103.22 crore. Submission of updated Utilization Certificate (UC) from State Government of Orissa is still awaited. 
Mughal Road in J&K  
13. Allocation for Mughal Road for Rs.20 crore coul d not be utilized because it was decided in later part of the financial year 
that the funding for Mughal Road shall be done from  the Prime Minister’s Reconstruction Programme and the funds would be 
directly provided by the Planning Commisssion to st ate PWD under the State Plan. Earlier, this project  was envisaged to be 
taken up under Economic Importance Programme Scheme with 50% share of the centre and 50%share of the State Government. 
The Government of J & K has expressed its inability to bear 50% cost of the project since there is paucity of fund with the State 
Govt. In view of the above, it was decided that the  whole funding shall be done by the Planning Commis sion. The expenditure 
on the project is Rs.332.76 crores till March, 2010 . A High Level Committee under the chairmanship of DG (RD) & SS have 
been constituted with Representative from the Plann ing Commission, Secretary (R & B), Chief Engineer a nd Superintending 
Engineer of Ministry as Member-Secretary. Also the representative of Hindustan Construction Company, P rime Contractor of 
Mughal Road as Special Invitee. The role of the Min istry is to give the technical guidance for framing  of the project estimates 
and to monitor and review the progress of the work under the chairmanship of DG (RD) & SS.

matter has been taken up with Ministry of Defence a nd Director General (Border Roads) from time to tim e to improve the 
manpower and resources of BRO and to outsource the work for expeditious completion.

RECOMMENDATION /OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE  
16.  Another pattern the Committee noted is that ma ny road projects are delayed due to delay in obtain ing necessary 
clearances including environmental clearance. The d elays result in cost and time overruns and the non- achievement of the 
targets which ultimately impinges upon national gro wth and development. The Committee is of the view t hat it leaves an 
impression in the minds of public there being a nex us between the contractors and the authorities conc erned to delay the 
projects deliberately so that the project cost goes  up resulting in more profit. The Government of Ind ia should take effective 
steps to tackle such problems existing in the proce ss of award of the road projects including the gran t of environmental 
clearance. The Committee is aware about the steps t aken recently such as constitution of Task Force he aded by the Finance 
Minister and Inter- Ministerial Group, but feels th at process of environmental clearance needs to be f urther streamlined. The 
infrastructure projects including National Highways  are as important as protecting our national habita t. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry of Road Transport shou ld obtain the environmental clearance before finali zing or awarding the 
cost of project, not vice-versa. 
(Para 11 ibid)  
Reply of the Government:  
17.  The policy issue pertaining to grant of Enviro nmental and Forest Clearance is within the purview of the Ministry of 
Environment and Forests. Accordingly, the proposals for obtaining such clearances are prepared for the  NH Projects qualifying 
for Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) or Enviro nmental Management Plans (EMP), etc. The proposals for obtaining 
necessary clearances as per extant policy are formu lated at the stage of preparation of Project Report  itself by the consultants. 
As regards the possibility of nexus between middlem en, contractors and officers, it is not possible to  confirm or deny such 
suspicion. It is, however, stated that there is a f unctioning vigilance administration in both NHAI an d this Ministry and all 
complaints on the misconduct of officers are duly e xamined and processed in accordance with the instru ction and directives of 
CVC. 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE  
18.  The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry. The Committee feels that process of environmental clear ance for 
road projects should be streamlined so that Nationa l Highways projects do not take much time from conc eptualization 
to actual implementation since these projects requi re a massive investment. The Committee reiterates i ts 
recommendation that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways should obtain the environmental cleara nce before 
finalizing or awarding the cost of project, not vice-versa.  
POOR PERFORMANCE OF CONTRACTORS, NEXUS AND DELAYS  
RECOMMENDATION /OBSERV A TION OF THE COMMITTEE  
19.  The Committee is also aware of the existence o f such a nexus leading to corruption and delays not  only in this sector but 
in other sectors as well. A substantial portion of our developmental funds is being siphoned off, as a result of which, the desired 
benefits of our schemes and programmes are not reac hing upto the ground level. What worries the Commit tee most is the 
general sense of helplessness in fighting out this unholy nexus and the consequent alarmingly high “de livery leakages”. The 
Committee did not find any serious attempt made or proposed against such a nexus in the road sector. T he Committee would 
like to know the steps taken or proposed by the Ministry and NHAI in this regard. 
(Para 15 ibid ) 

20.  The Committee notes that apart from land acqui sition and various clearances, poor performance of contractors is another 
major reason for delay in road projects. Possibility of a nexus between middlemen, bad contractors and corrupt officials to delay 
the projects can not be ruled out. Ministry’s offic ials could not give any information about the proje cts delayed due to poor 
performance of contractors, in fact only one contra ctor had been blacklisted so far. The Committee rec ommends that the 
Ministry should compile the information regarding non-performing contractors and they should be black listed. 
(Para 17 ibid ) 
Reply of the Government:  
21. List of non-performing contractors under NHDP a re enclosed as Annexure-II.  The non-performing contractors are liable 
for penalty in the form of liquidated damages stipu lated in the civil work contract for delay in compl etion of the project, the 
reasons for which are attributable to the Contracto r. As per the policy of NHAI, the contractors put o n the non-performing” list 
shall not be pre-qualified for award of any future contract in NHAI until such time their performance improves in course of 
subsequent reviews. However, the recommendations of  the Committee are noted for further necessary acti on regarding review 
of policy in this context. 
22.  As regards the possibility of nexus between mi ddlemen, contractors and officers, it is not possible to confirm or deny such 
suspicion. It is, however, stated that there is a f unctioning vigilance administration in both NHAI an d this Ministry and all 
complaints on the misconduct of officers are duly e xamined and processed in accordance with the instru ction and directives of 
CVC. 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE  
23. The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry. The Ministry has pointed out that the mechanisms to fig ht 
corruption in road projects. The Committee, however, feels that it is due to the ineffectiveness of the existing mechanism 
the corruption is prevalent in road projects. The C ommittee, therefore, recommends that the existing m echanism to 
prevent corruption in road projects may be reviewed and more effective mechanisms may be put in place.

TOLL POLICY 
RECOMMENDATION /OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE  
110. The Committee feels that the toll policy in pe rpetuity is fundamentally wrong and it needs to be reviewed according to the 
changing situations. The Committee notes that there  are instances of collecting tolls from areas where  condition of the roads 
were so poor. There were also instances that 23 tol l plazas being operated in a stretch of 60 kms of N ational Highway - 62 in 
Meghalaya. The Committee, therefore, recommends tha t the Rules and Regulations regarding collection of  toll should be 
revisited and brought at par with the modern day re quirements of road users instead of keeping it as a n instmmet1tof unjust 
profiteering by unscrupulous elements. 
(Para 101 ibid ) 
Reply of the Government:  
111.  The collection of user fee and the expenditur e on the development and maintenance of National Hi ghways (NHs), 
permanent bridges, bypasses, tunnels, etc. for whic h user fee is being collected, is not on one-to-one  basis, by linking up with 
the recovery of capital investment on a particular section or structure of the NHs to the collection o f user fee on that section. 
The collection of user fee is ploughed back for dev elopment of other essential segments of NHs also so  as to supplement the 
available budgetary resources. The issue of explori ng sources of financing of NH projects is becoming more and more 
challenging with time from the point of view of providing better NH infrastructure with enhanced safety features. Therefore, the 
Committee is requested to consider and extend its s upport for the submissions of this Ministry to cont inue with the policy of 
tolling in perpetuity. 
112.  Further, it is pertinent to mention that in o rder to ensure an equitable application of user fee  rates, the Government while 
notifying the NH Fee Rules, 2008 had approved unifo rm rates of user fee for both the public-funded as well as public-private-
funded national highway projects which are uniforml y applicable for all national highways throughout t he country. The 

concessionaire in respect of PPP projects can colle ct and retain the toll (user fee) revenues only to the extent and for the 
duration prescribed in the concession agreement/con tract. The toll revenues for the period beyond the contract/concession 
period are the revenue stream of Government of Indi a. Hence, no undue benefit accrues to the concessio naire even with tolling 
in perpetuity. 
113.  In non BOT stretches, where tolling is carrie d out by agencies appointed by NHAI through DGR, th ere have been issues 
of corruption in the collection of toll. Subcontrac ting of tolling is difficult to establish, however,  the prolonged absence of the 
DGR sponsored ex-serviceman awarded tolling from th e toll plaza is an indication of subcontract of tol ling. In order to reduce 
corruption in tolling, NHAI has recently introduced  the system of tolling through OMT contract. Award of Tolling through 
open bid system is also being considered. 
114.  The existing provisions regarding levy of tol l as per the NH Fee (Determination of Rates and Col lection) Rules, 2008 
stipulate that any other toll plaza on the same sec tion of NH and in the same direction shall not be e stablished within a distance 
of 60 km provided that where the executing authorit y deems necessary, it may, for reasons to be record ed in writing, establish 
or allow the concessionaire to establish another to ll plaza within a distance of 60 km, provided furth er that a toll plaza may be 
established within a distance of 60 km from another  toll plaza if such toll plaza is for collection of  fee for a permanent bridge, 
bypass or tunnel. However, NH-62 in Meghalaya is mostly single lane and the road as such is not being tolled at present by the 
Ministry. Accordingly, there are no toll plazas on NH-62.
