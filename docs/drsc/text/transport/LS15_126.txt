R E P O R T
           
This Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Government on the observations/recommendations
 as contained in its One Hundred Nineteenth Report on Demands for Grants (2007-08) (Demand No. 92) of the Ministry of
 Tourism, which was presented to the Parliament on 14th May 2007.
 2.         Action Taken Replies have been received from the Government on                       5 th September, 2007 in respect of all
 the observations/recommendations contained in the Report.  These have been categorized as follows:-
 (i)        Recommendations/observations that have been accepted by the Government:
            Para Nos. 8, 11, 18, 22, 38, 48, 54, 57, 60, 68, 71 and  76                           Total - 12
 (ii)      Recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue in
            view of the Government replies:
            Para No. 63                                                                                                     Total - 1
(iii)
               Recommendations/observations in respect of which replies of the
            Government have not been accepted by the Committee:
             Para Nos. 14, 35 and 44                                                                                 Total - 3
(iv)              Recommendations/ observations in respect of which final replies of the
Government are still awaited:
            Para  Nos.  31, 33, 81, 90, 92 and 93                                                              Total – 6
 
 3.         The Committee desires that final replies in respect of the recommendations, which have been categorized as
 interim replies by the Committee should be furnished to it at the earliest.
 4.         On scrutiny of the said Action Taken Replies, the Committee has made observations on each item of the action
 taken/proposed to be taken by the Ministry of Tourism.  The recommendations of the Committee contained in the One
 Hundred Nineteenth Report, action taken thereon by the Government and recommendations/observations of the Committee
 on each item of action taken have been given in the succeeding paragraphs.

7.         The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism has placed its demand for more allocation of funds for the 11 th
 Five Year plan with the Planning Commission on the basis of 10% and 20% increase formula.  The Committee, however, is
 not satisfied with the reply of the Ministry.  The Committee feels that it seems the Ministry’s requirement of funds is
 minimal as they had opted for 10% and 20% increase.   The Ministry should have demanded more funds on the basis of
 their proposed requirement under various components and keeping in view the pattern of expenditure during the 10 th Five
 Year Plan justifying their demand for more allocation.   The Committee hopes that adequate budgetary allocation will be
 made to the Ministry of Tourism for the entire 11 th Plan period as per the projections for sustainable development of
 tourism products vis-à-vis strengthening of tourism infrastructure and hospitality sector in the country.
                       
(Recommendation at Para 11)
               8.      The Committee notes that during the year 2006-07, the Ministry of Finance imposed a cut of Rs.30 crore
 bringing it down from Rs.830 crore to Rs.800 crore at RE stage. The Committee is aware that the revised estimates
 are conveyed by the Ministry of Finance on the basis of the expenditure level upto 30
th September of the calendar
 year, and Ministry of Tourism was not able to utilize 50% of the budgetary allocation during 2006-07 till 30 th
 September, 2006. The Committee, therefore, desires the Ministry of Tourism to plan and incur their expenditure in
 a proportionate manner so that it is evenly spread throughout the year. The States/UTs may be continuously
 pursued to (i) send their proposals right at the beginning of the financial year, (ii) release funds to their Tourism
 Ministry or Department and furnish quarterly progress reports as well as utilization certificates of funds released
 in time by minimizing administrative and procedural delays in releasing funds.       
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
9.         The Ministry of Tourism plans to incur expenditure in a proportionate manner so that it is evenly spread throughout the year.
 For this the instructions of the Ministry of Finance for improvement in public expenditure have been circulated to all concerned for
 compliance and are being reviewed. The Ministry of Tourism has impressed upon all the States/UTs to send all their projects with
 requisite data and information well in time.  Also a system for online monitoring is being evolved. The status of the unspent
 balances lying with the States is regularly reviewed for every state and the states have been issued necessary directions to complete
 the projects and submit the pending Utilization Certificates/Completion Certificate expeditiously.

(Recommendation at Para 14)
               11.    The Committee is constrained to note that a large amount of unspent balances are lying with the States/UTs.
 Out of the releases made upto 31st March 2004, the unspent balances were Rs.184.37 crore as on 1st April 2005 and
 outstanding Utilization Certificates (UCs) were 397 which further went up to Rs.203.82 crore as on 31 st December
 2005 and outstanding UCs were 470. The cumulative unspent balance with States, for releases made upto 31 st
 March 2006, was Rs.542.09 crore. The Ministry’s contention that the amount shown as unspent is calculated on the
 basis of non-receipts of Utilization Certificates is untenable. The Committee feels that the Ministry has failed to
 gear-up the States/Union Territories and their machinery has not evolved effective system of planning and
 monitoring to do away with the procedural hurdles for timely completion of projects. Therefore, the Committee
 recommends the Ministry to impress upon the State/UT Governments to utilize all unspent balance lying with
 them on priority and furnish UCs so that the amount allocated for the entire duration of the 10
th Plan could be
 fully utilized. The Committee desires the Ministry to inform the Committee about the progress made in the matter.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
               12.    The Ministry of Tourism has impressed upon all the States/UTs to evolve a system of online monitoring with the
 help of NIC, and also to submit the interim reports for project implementation. The status of the unspent balances lying
 with them is constantly reviewed with   States/UTs and they have been issued necessary directions to complete the
 projects and also submit pending Utilization Certificates/Completion Certificate expeditiously. The status of pending
 UCs  is thus  being pursued vigorously.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
13.       The Committee is not satisfied with the reply of the Ministry.   The Committee fails to understand as to why the
 Ministry is not evolving a fool-proof system of monitoring and review mechanism system for timely completion of tourism
 infrastructure projects.  The Committee feels that mere issue of administrative instructions to the States/UT Governments
 will not help much for creation of tourism infrastructure in the country.  The Committee may like the Ministry to be more
 pro-active and tie-up with NIC and State/UT Governments to evolve a system of online monitoring of utilization certificates
 and status of project implementation on their own.  The Committee recommends that the progress of various tourism
 related projects be monitored in the Ministry and funds should be released to the concerned State/Union Territory for
 completion of the projects.   The States/UT Governments may be constantly pursued to utilize all unspent balances lying
 with them and furnish utilization certificates for the projects sanctioned during the 10
th Plan period.  The Committee also
 recommends the Ministry to evaluate the existing scheme in the light of the outstanding utilization certificates and
 cumulative unspent balances with the States/UT Governments so as to gain momentum for the future.

DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM INFRASTRUCTURE AND DESTINATION
(Recommendation at Para 31 )
20.       The Committee is constrained to note that during the 10
th Plan Period 1157 projects were sanctioned amounting to
 Rs.2012.91 crore and an amount of Rs.1558.25 crore was released. Out of the sanctioned projects only 441 projects or
 38.11% works could be completed till date and there still remains s shortfall of 716 projects. The Committee is not satisfied
 with the restricted progress of the Ministry in completion of tourism infrastructure development projects. The Committee
 fails to understand as to how the Ministry would achieve the physical and financial targets in the absence of quantifiable
 goals. The Ministry contended that the development of tourism infrastructure projects generally requires 36 months for
 completion. However, the fact is that these projects are lingering on for months together due to one reason or other. The
 Committee recommends the Ministry to prioritize the projects and impress upon the concerned State Governments for
 early completion of these projects sanctioned during the 10
th Plan Period so as to avoid time and cost overruns.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
21.       The Ministry of Tourism has sanctioned 1160 projects in the 10
th Five Year Plan, out of which 451 projects have been
 completed, 13 dropped and 696 are ongoing. The amount sanctioned for the projects is Rs.2011.68 crore and released amount is
 Rs.1562.00 crore. The State Govts. have been directed to complete the projects expeditiously and furnish UC/CCs for the same.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
22.       The Committee is perturbed over the slow progress of work in completion of tourism projects, which is visible that
 out of 1160 projects so sanctioned during 10
th Plan, only 451 projects could be completed and 696 are still in progress and a
 amount of Rs.1562 crore was also released.   The Committee feels that any delay in the completion of projects will have
 negative impact of growth both in terms of tourist arrivals as well as creation of infrastructure and other facilities.    The
 Committee recommends the Ministry to fix some quantifiable goals and prioritize projects in consultation with the
 concerned State/UT Governments for holistic and timely completion of the tourism projects.

HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
(Recommendation at Para 44)
33.       The Committee notes that the hospitality and service sector has been accorded priority with the growth and
 diversification of tourism activities.   The Committee further notes that there are five institutes of repute being run in the
 private sector apart from there are 125 institutes of hotel management in the country offering hospitality programmes in
 private sector.  The Committee observes that as a sizeable number of private IHMs have emerged in the country to offer
 courses in hotel management programme there is a need to provide an institutional and integrated course in development of
 the country’s human resources programme.  The Committee is pleased to note that although the guidelines for affiliation of
 these private institutes have been put in place spelling out the eligibility requirements for affiliation though these institutes
 are lacking in training and other infrastructural facilities.   The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to grant a
 statutory / deemed university status to NCHMCT for achieving excellence in the tourism sector and to standardize the
 quality of education and training through affiliation and academic supervision through an Act of Parliament.
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
34.       The role envisaged for the NCHMCT, that it should take into its fold all the Institutes of Hotel Management (IHM) in the
 country, has certain practical difficulties.   Statutory status to the NCHMCT to regulate hospitality education in the country will
 mean that the AICTE will be excluded from discharging these functions / duties.
35.       Upon such an eventuality, the NCHMCT will have to self-address the following question:   How will it deal with the
 prevailing mis-match between an IHM / NCHMCT’s degree and the degree of the AICTE as to their duration.   Presently, the
 IHM’s degree is awarded after a course of 3 years duration and that under the AICTE after 4 years duration.   The NCHMCT has
 been given the mandate to affiliate private Institutes of Hotel Management with a view to maintaining and regulating standards and
 quality of hospitality education in the country.  As a follow up, the NCHMCT received 23 applications and after a comprehensive
 and stringent scrutiny accorded affiliation to four institutes.  However, deemed university status to the NCHMCT, if granted, will
 not be consistent with the NCHMCT’s affiliating mandate.  As deemed university, the NCHMCT can not affiliate private Institutes
 of hotel management.

 private sector for efficient administration and successful operation of the quality of manpower.

NATIONAL TOURISM PROMOTION BOARD:
(Recommendation at Para 63)               
52.       The Committee notes that tourism is not single but involves multi-sectoral activities requiring close coordination with
 various agencies viz., the Ministries of Civil Aviation, Culture, Textiles, Environment and Forests, Railway, Home, Finance,
 Shipping & Road Transport and Highways and External Affairs, etc. The Government has brought the subject of tourism
 under the scope of the Committee on Infrastructure (COI) for initiating policy on creation of world class infrastructure in a
 time bound manner. The Committee is of the opinion that inspite of the efforts, the tourism is still in the appalling state of
 coordination. The Committee is not satisfied with the replies of the Ministry on the issue of the setting up of a National
 Tourism Promotion Board for better coordination with all agencies/stakeholders and expand its scope towards inter-
sectoral convergence of resources and policy integration and desires that the whole issue be re-examined and Committee be
 informed of the outcome thereof with reasons for conclusions. 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT
 53.      The recommendation of the Committee to set up a National Tourism Board has been re-examined in detail by the Ministry. 
 “The National Committee on Tourism” in its report submitted to the Government in 1988, taking into consideration the then
 prevailing circumstances, inter-alia, recommended the establishment of a “National Tourism Board”.  Since then, there has been a
 substantial change in the approach on the role of Government as provider of services to that of a facilitator and catalyst, particularly
 in sectors like Hospitality and Tourism.  It is true that tourism is a composite industry requiring coordinated efforts of several other
 Ministries and Departments and the State/UT Governments.  The implementation of tourism development projects and its
 promotion is primarily the responsibility of the State/U.T. Governments.  The Ministry is in full agreement with the observation of
 the Committee that inter-sectoral convergence of resources and policy integration is needed.   At present coordination is effected
 through established systems like inter-ministerial Committees constituted for specific purposes.  Besides the Committee on
 Secretaries chaired by the Cabinet Secretary takes up coordination and monitoring of issues at the highest level.   The Ministry of
 Tourism has also been included in the Committee of Infrastructure headed by the Hon’ble Prime Minister which enables better
 coordination on policy issues and monitoring for speedier implementation of major projects. 
 
54.       The earlier proposal to set up a ‘National Tourism Board’ as the principal arm of the Government for tourism sector with a
 Chairman at the level of Secretary to Government of India, with several full time members at the level of Additional Secretary
 assisted by three Additional Members at the level of Joint Secretary serviced by a number of technical officers and other staff

Action Taken Notes on the recommendations contained in the 119th report of the Department – related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport Tourism & Culture on Dema...
file:///D/responive/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/126th%20Report.htm[22-10-2018 12:28:26]
 subordinate manning a number of sub offices spread all over the country, is not in tune with the current thinking of a lean
 Government machinery.    Even if such a Board comes into existence, the Ministry of Tourism will have to exist, as the policy
 matters have to be essentially handled by the Ministry in the Government and not by a Board which is conceived as an executing
 field agency, outside the Ministry, vested with operational responsibilities. 
           
55.       Tourism related development programmes have to be essentially formulated and implemented by the State/U.T.
 Governments.  Even if a National Tourism Board is set up at the Centre, the State/U.T. Governments need not necessarily follow
 that pattern and they may continue to operate through their Departmental set up.  It may not be possible for the Central Government
 to direct the State/U.T. Governments to follow the central pattern, as tourism is not a subject matter which specifically appears in
 the Central list.  In the federal set up as in India, a Ministry rather than a Board at the Centre, would be a more effective mechanism
 to deal with a subject like tourism vis-à-vis the States/UTs.
 
56.       It may not be possible to draw a parallel between the Railway Board and the Tele-communication Board with the proposed
 National Tourism Board.   The Railway Board and Tele-communication Board provide specific services through their own agency
 throughout the country.  The proposed Tourism Board cannot be a service provider, as that of the Railway Board or Tele-
communication Board.  In the Tourism Sector, the institution at the Centre, whether it be a Ministry or a Board, is expected to be
 only a facilitator and catalyst, to promote tourism through the State/UT Governments and the private sector.  It cannot be the
 provider of services. 
 
57.       Matters like autonomy, operational flexibility, professionalism and better coordination can be addressed even in the existing
 framework without creating a separate Board.  The Directorate General of Tourism is the field organization under the Ministry of
 Tourism that can be further strengthened to meet with the emerging challenges and market demand. 
 
58.       The financial burden on a top heavy organisation can not be justified in the face of the changing role of the Government.  
 The Railway Board and the Telecommunication Board generate their own revenue whereas the Tourism Board would not be in a
 position to generate such revenues.  It will have to depend heavily on budgetary support.   If that is the case there does not appear to
 be any major advantage in setting up a National Tourism Board. Therefore, in the given circumstances when the Government is
 keen to contain administrative expenditure the proposal to create such a Board is considered to be neither essential nor desirable.  
 What is required is the capacity building of existing machinery and further empowerment through delegation of authority and
 moderanisation for better coordination and cost effective performance.
