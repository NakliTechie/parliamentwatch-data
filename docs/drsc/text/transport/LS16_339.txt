Introduction 
 The Indian Civil Aviation sector is one of the fastest growing sectors of the country as 
well as of the world today. In terms of domestic air passenger traffic, India is the third largest 
aviation market. The unprecedented growth of the Civil Aviation industry can be gauged from 
the fact that in the domestic routes the number of domestic passengers carried by scheduled 
domestic Indian Carriers has witnessed a growth of 48.9 percent in the year 2022, as compared to 
the previous year 2021 (up to 31 st December, 2021) and while in the International Routes during 
the period January to December , 2022 a total of 43.4 million passengers were carried by 
Scheduled India/ foreign carriers as against 17.2 million in the corresponding period in 2021, 
thereby witnessing a growth of 152.7%.   The number of aircrafts registered in the Civil Aviation 
sector is also likely to reach a figure of 1200 in the next few years from the current figure of 744.  
The freighter aircraft deployed by Indian airlines have increased from 7 (2018) to 28 during the 
last three years (2021). 
2.       The Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation highlighted the following vision and objectives of 
the Ministry:- 
 To c reate an environment where the a viation industry is sustainable; affordable; safe;  
internationally competitive; and connects to all parts of India and the World;  
 To expand airport capacity more than five times to handle one billion trips a year; 
 To e nhance the availability and affordability of regional air connectivity the Regional 
Connectivity Scheme – Ude Desh Ka Aam Naagrik (RCS-UDAN); and 
 To ensure that airport tar iffs, taxes on fuel, landing charges, passenger services, cargo and 
other charges are determined in an efficient, fair and transparent manner. 
Mandate of the Ministry 
3.       The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation of national policies and 
programmes for the development and regulation of the Civil Aviation sector in the country. It is 
responsible for the administr ation of the Aircraft Act, 1934;  Aircraft Rules, 1937 ; and various 
other legislations pertaining to the aviation sect or in the country. In addition to the primary 
functions of framing of policies, the Ministry provides guidance to the organizations in the 
implementation of policy guidelines ; monitors and evaluates their activities ; and also provides 
their interface with Parliament. It also supervises the implementation by the organizations of 
special programmes of the Government, particularly those intended for weaker sections of 
society.

was Rs. 3,532.13 /- crore. The Ministry of Finance reduced it to Rs. 3,113.36 /- crore, which

13. The Committee notes that the B.E. allocation for 2023 -24 is Rs. 3,113.36/ - crore, 
which is by far the lowest of all the B.E. allocation in the last five years. In fact, the B.E. 
allocation has been steadily decreasing from Rs. 6,602.86/- crore (2018-19), to Rs. 4,500.00/- 
crore (2019-20), to Rs. 3,797.71/ - crore (2020-21), to Rs. 3,224.67/ - crore (2021-22), barring 
the year 2022 -23 when the Ministry received a B.E. allocation of Rs. 10,667/ - crore on 
account of disinvestment of Air India ., and now the lowest at Rs. 3,113.36/ - crore for B.E. 
(2023-24). These decreasing allocations have been made despite the increasing trend of 
inflation in the country, which implies that the effective allocations are ever lower. Even 
during allocations at R.E. stage, the fund allocation remained below the projected demand 
of the Ministry, other than the year 2021 -22, though the Ministry  has shown a good 
capacity to absorb the funds allocated to it, with percentage expenditure as compared to 
R.E. allocations i.e., as high as 98.96% (2018 -19), 98.56% (2019-20), 99.07% (2020-21) and 
98.99% (2021 -22). For the year 2022 -23, the percentage exp enditure was 86.14% as on 
31.01.2023. The Ministry assured the Committee in its Demands for Grants meeting that 
full utilization of the R.E. allocation would be achieved.  
14. The Committee is of the view that the projected demand of the Ministry reflects the 
expenditure on targets and objecti ves it seeks to achieve in that  particular financial year. 
The Committee is aware that the Civil Aviation Industry had suffered a major setback on 
account of Covid -19 pandemic and needs full budgetary support from the Government at 
this critical juncture, where it has to recover its losses and come up to its earlier level in the 
first instance and then grow at an even faster rate. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry of Finance should favourably consider additional grant of funds to the Ministry at 
R.E. stage, to enable the Ministry to achieve its set targets and achieve the aim of the 
Indian Civil Aviation Industry.  
15. The Committee notes that a major part of the B.E. allocation in the financial yea rs 
2021-22, 2022-23 and 2023 -24 consists of the budgetary allocation towards  the 
disinvestment of Air India, which effectively did not create any asset for the Civil Aviation 
Sector in the long run. The Committee hopes that the trend would be reversed with the 
completion process of the disinvestment of Air India and more focus would be laid towards 
asset creation in the Civil Aviation Sector.

Regional Connectivity Scheme – Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik (RCS-UDAN)  
Introduction and Vision 
16. Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA) launched Regional Connectivity Scheme (RCS) – 
Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik (UDAN) on 21st October, 2016. RCS-UDAN is a flagship programme 
of MoCA. The National Civil Aviation Policy (NCAP), 2016 envisages Regional Air 
Connectivity under Regional Connectivity Scheme (RCS).  
17. The primary objecti ve of RCS -UDAN is to facilitate / stimulate regional air connectivity 
and by making it affordable to  masses through measures like : (i) Concessions by Centra l 
Government; State Governments / Union Territories (UTs) ; and Airport Operators to reduce the 
cost of airline operations on regional routes / other support measures and (ii ) Viability Gap 
Funding (VGF) to meet the gap, if any, between the cost of airline operations and expected 
revenues on such routes.  
18. Through his presentation, the Secretary deposed before the Committee that the Ministry 
plans to expand UDAN to 1,000 routes and 100 airports . He further enumerated the following 
particulars of the Scheme:- 
 in four rounds of bidding, 1152 valid routes awarded, covering more than 32 States/UTs;

Surge in Air Fares and Rationalisation 
65. The Committee also took up the issue of affordability of air tickets for the common man 
and enquired into the reason for removal of upper price cap on airfares. The Committee pointed 
out that by doing so, no extra revenue is being generated for the Governm ent or the airport 
operator and only the airline operators benefit at the cost of the passengers, who have to pay for 
the surge in flight tickets.  
66. The Committee during its deliberations expressed concerns about the high fares 
charged by the private ai rlines in the domestic sector. The Committee also pointed out the 

Page 27 of 108 
 
wrong information published by the private airlines on their websites, regarding the 
number of seats left in the flight and the prices of the tickets. The level of misinformation 
can be gaug ed from the fact that even after the last tickets have been sold, the same 
number of seats show on the website, as indicated before the tickets sale. This indicates that 
airline operators are misguiding the public and forcing passengers to pay more. In vie w of 
the above, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should formulate appropriate 
guidelines regarding rationalization of fares and publishing the correct information on the 
website of the airlines.  
67. The Committee  also pointed that 'Predatory Pri cing' is being restored to by the 
domestic airlines sector. A particular airline may sell its air tickets at such a low level, that 
other competitors cannot compete and are forced to exit the market. A company that does 
this will see initial losses, but eventually it benefits by driving competitions out of the 
market and raising its prices again. The Committee desired to know whether the DGCA 
had at an y point of time, intervened to check the fares of air tickets. The Committee also 
expressed concern at the fact that in the domestic sector, private airlines are charging 
different fares for same sector, route and same direction of flights. This is specifically so for 
the NE Region and Hilly areas including Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh , where the prices 
of domestic sector tickets are, sometimes even more than the i nternational airline sector  
prices.  
68. The Ministry apprised the Committee that after repeal of the Air Corporation s Act, 1953 
in March , 1994, the Indian domestic aviation market has been deregulated. Consequently, 
airlines are free to induct capacity with any aircraft type for selecting whatever markets and 
network they wish to service and operat e with compliance of Route Dispersal Guidelines 
(RDGs) issued by Government across the country. Thus, it is up to the airlines to provide air 
services to/from any airport in country depending on their operational and commercial 
viabilities. 
69. After deregulation of airline sector, the airfare is market driven and is neither established 
nor regulated by the Government. Air ticket prices generally vary depending upon the market 
forces. Airline pricing runs in multiple levels {buckets or Reservation Book ing Designator 
(RBDs)} which are in line with the practice being followed globally. Due to dynamic fare 
pricing, the tickets bought in advance are much cheaper than those purchased near the date of 
travel. The airlines are free to fix reasonable tariffs un der the provisions of sub -rule (1) of Rule 
135 of the Aircraft Rules, 1937, having regard to all relevant factors, including cost of operation, 
characteristics of services, reasonable profit and the generally prevailing tariff.  Directorate 
General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has a Tariff Monitoring Unit that monitors airfares on certain 
routes on monthly basis to ensure that the airlines do not charge airfares outside a range declared 
by them. The airlines are compliant to the Sub Rule (2) of Rule 135 of the A ircraft Rules, 1937, 
as long as fare charged by them is in line with fare displayed on their website. 

Page 28 of 108 
 
70. The Director General, Directorate General of Civil Aviation stated that the Ministry does 
not regulate the fares, however, in compliance with the Airc raft Act, 1934, the Ministry 
exercised regulation of fares during the Covid pandemic, which was later withdrawn. The 
Secretary also apprised the Committee that as per international norms, fare buckets are created 
by the airlines and the Ministry only makes them publish their fares on the website. 
71. The Committee takes note of the fact that after the repeal of the Air Corporations 
Act, 1953, the airfare is market driven and depends on market fares, and is neither 
established nor regulated by the Government. It notes the DGCA ’s comments that the 
airfares were regulated for a fixed period during the COVID pandemic in compliance with 
Aircraft Act, 1934 and the regulation was withdrawn as the COVID pandemic abated and 
that airlines are free to fix reasonable tariffs under the Aircraft Rules, 1937, with regard to 
cost of operation, services, reasonable profit and generally prevailing tariff. 
72. The Committee is fully aware that the Indian domestic Aviation sector is growing in 
an open market and acknow ledges that the private airline operators should be given a free 
hand to decide the air fares as they are governed by competition and depends on market 
fares. It also notes that there is a provision in the Aircraft Rules, 1937, which inter-alia has 
a provision for fixing the air fares after considering ‘reasonable profit and generally 
prevailing tariff’ amongst other factors. The Committee observes that presently the surge 
in air fares does not reflect any sort of mechanism on part of the Minist ry to control the 
fares, based on existing Aircraft Rules, 1937, as during surge in air fares the prices go 
beyond the acceptable or justifiable limits of 'reasonable profit and generally prevailing 
tariff'.  
73. The Committee notes that while on one hand Government plans to make air 
transport affordable for the common man and the air capacity is being increased, on the 
other hand there is no commensurate expansion of the capacity to  bring in aircrafts. This 
creates lack of airline tickets considering the h igh demand and leads to increase in  the 
prices. The Committee recommends that there should be a mechanism with the Ministry 
such as capping of upper and lower prices to stop the practice of 'Predatory Pricing' or the 
sudden surge in prices. The Committee is of the firm opinion that a perfect balance has to 
be maintained between the commercial interest of the private airlines and the interest of 
the passengers so as to enable the private airlines to grow and at the same time the interest 
of passengers should also be kept in mind, so that they are not fleeced in the garb of 
commercialization. The Committee feels that even though p rice buckets are being created 
as per international aviation norms, a close watch has to be maintai ned by the DGCA &  
the Ministry and a mechanism should be devised to monitor the websites of various airlines 
to prevent them from misguiding passengers.  And, in case the private airlines do not 
publish the correct information regarding fares, they should be penalized for it. The 
Committee observes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation has a responsibility to the travelling 
public and the nation at large to ensur e that predatory pricing mechanism are not adopted 

Page 29 of 108 
 
by the airlines under the cloak of free market economy. The Committee recommends the 
Ministry should formulate a pricing mechanism for air fares to ensure that passengers are 
not charged exorbitant prices.  The Committee also recommends that DGCA may be 
empowered to regulate tariffs based on the data generated by their monitoring Department 
or otherwise. Further, the passengers may also be  given an option to approach the said 
Department with their grievances.

 The Directorate General of Civil Aviation is the principal regulatory body in the field of 
civil aviation. It is not only responsible for regulation of air transport services to/ from/ 
within India, formulation and enforcement of civil air regulations, air safety and 
airworthiness standards, but also co -ordinates all regulatory functions with International 
Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). 
 Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau: 
 Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) was established in 2012, as an 
independent organization from DGCA for investigation of accidents and serious 
incidents. 
 Bureau of Civil Aviation Security: 
 The Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS) is an attached off ice of the Ministry of 
Civil Aviation. The aim of BCAS is to safeguard civil aviation operations against 
operations against acts of unlawful interference. The Bureau is responsible for laying 
down the standards for security in respect of civil flights oper ating to/from India and 
ensuring their compliance through regular inspections and security audits.

Use of GEO Augmented Navigation (GAGAN)  
191. GAGAN is the acronym for Global Positioning System (GPS) Aided Geostationary Earth 
Orbit (GEO) Augmented Navigation, which has been jointly developed by the Indian Space 
Research Organisation (ISRO) and the Airports Authority of India (AAI). The GAGAN uses a 
system of ground stations to provide necessary augmentations to the GPS St andard Positioning 
Service (SPS) navigation signal. A network of precisely surveyed ground reference stations i.e., 
Indian Reference Stations (INRES) is strategically positioned across the country to collect GPS 
satellite data. Using this information, the master control centre i.e., Indian Master Control Centre 
(INMCC) generates messages to correct any signal errors. These correction messages are then 
uplinked through Indian  Land Uplink Station (INLUS) and broadcast through communication 
satellites (Geostat ionary) to receivers onboard aircraft , using the same frequency as GPS.   
192. The GAGAN is designed to provide the additional accuracy, availability, and integrity 
that may be necessary to enable users to rely on GPS, for all phases of flight, from enroute  
through approach for all qualified airports within the GAGAN service volume. GAGAN will 
also provide the capability for increased accuracy in position reporting, allowing for more 
uniform and high -quality Air Traffic Management (ATM). In addition, GAGAN w ill provide 
benefits beyond aviation to all modes of transportation, including maritime, highways, and 
railroads. 
193. The Ministry has informed the Committee that National Civil Aviation Policy (NCAP), 
2016 prescribes that new aircrafts being registered i n India from 1 st January, 2019 (subsequently 
revised to 1 st July, 20 21) will mandatorily have GAGAN -enabled GPS receivers onboard. 
However, exemption from GAGAN mandate has been given to Boeing -787 aircraft till 
31.12.2025 owing to major design changes to be made in the aircraft and subsequent approval of 
the State of Design i.e., Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). Moreover, five aircrafts of Air

Surge in Air Fares and Rationalisation 
The Committee during its deliberations expressed concerns about the high fares charged 
by the private airlines in the domestic sector. The Committee also pointed out the wrong 
information published by the private airlines on their websites, regarding the n umber of 
seats left in the flight and the prices of the tickets. The level of misinformation can be 
gauged from the fact that even after the last tickets have been sold, the same number of 
seats show on the website, as indicated before the tickets sale. Th is indicates that airline 
operators are misguiding the public and forcing passengers to pay more. In view of the 
above, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should formulate appropriate 
guidelines regarding rationalization of fares and publishing the  correct information on the 
website of the airlines.  
(Para 66) 
The Committee also pointed that 'Predatory Pricing' is being restored to by the domestic 
airlines sector. A particular airline may sell its air tickets at such a low level, that other 
competitors cannot compete and are forced to exit the market. A company that does this 
will see initial losses, but eventually it benefits by driving competitions out of the market 
and raising its prices again. The Committee desired to know whether the DGCA had at any 
point of time, intervened to check the fares of air tickets. The Committee also expressed 
concern at the fact that in the domestic sector, private airlines are charging different fares 
for same sector, route and same direction of flights. This is spec ifically so for the NE 
Region and Hilly areas including Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, where the prices of 
domestic sector tickets are, sometimes even more than the international airline sector 
prices.  
(Para 67) 
The Committee takes note of the fact that afte r the repeal of the Air Corporations Act, 
1953, the airfare is market driven and depends on market fares, and is neither established 
nor regulated by the Government. It notes the DGCA’s comments that the airfares were 

Page 80 of 108 
 
regulated for a fixed period during th e COVID pandemic in compliance with Aircraft Act, 
1934 and the regulation was withdrawn as the COVID pandemic abated and that airlines 
are free to fix reasonable tariffs under the Aircraft Rules, 1937, with regard to cost of 
operation, services, reasonable profit and generally prevailing tariff. 
(Para 71) 
The Committee is fully aware that the Indian domestic Aviation sector is growing in an 
open market and acknowledges that the private airline operators should be given a free 
hand to decide the air fares as they are governed by competition and depends on ma rket 
fares. It also notes that there is a provision in the Aircraft Rules, 1937, which inter-alia has 
a provision for fixing the air fares after considering ‘reasonable profit and generally 
prevailing tariff’ amongst other factors. The Committee observes t hat presently the surge 
in air fares does not reflect any sort of mechanism on part of the Ministry to control the 
fares, based on existing Aircraft Rules, 1937, as during surge in air fares the prices go 
beyond the acceptable or justifiable limits of 'rea sonable profit and generally prevailing 
tariff'.  
(Para 72) 
The Committee notes that while on one hand Government plans to make air transport 
affordable for the common man and the air capacity is being increased, on the other hand 
there is no commensurate expansion of the capacity to bring in aircrafts. This creates lack 
of airline tickets considering the high demand and leads to increase in the prices. The 
Committee recommends that there should be a mechanism with the Ministry such as 
capping of upper and lower prices to stop the practice of 'Predatory Pricing' or the sudden 
surge in prices. The Committee is of the firm opinion that a perfect balance has to be 
maintained between the commercial interest of the private airlines and the interest of the 
passengers so as to enable the private airlines to grow and at the same time the interest of 
passengers should also be kept in mind, so that they are not fleeced in the garb of 
commercialization. The Committee feels that even though price buckets are being create d 
as per international aviation norms, a close watch has to be maintained by the DGCA & 
the Ministry and a mechanism should be devised to monitor the websites of various airlines 
to prevent them from misguiding passengers. And, in case the private airlines  do not 
publish the correct information regarding fares, they should be penalized for it. The 
Committee observes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation has a responsibility to the travelling 
public and the nation at large to ensure that predatory pricing mech anism are not adopted 
by the airlines under the cloak of free market economy. The Committee recommends the 
Ministry should formulate a pricing mechanism for air fares to ensure that passengers are 
not charged exorbitant prices. The Committee also recommend s that DGCA may be 
empowered to regulate tariffs based on the data generated by their monitoring Department 
or otherwise. Further, the passengers may also be given an option to approach the said 
Department with their grievances.

objective to provide Grants-in-Aid to Institutions in  the Civil Aviation Sector for 
Promotion/Development of Aero Sports, the projected demand was of Rs. 1.00/ - crore 
however, only Rs. 0.01 crore has been allocated in B.E. 2023 -24. The Committee would like 
to know the reasons for this token provision. The Co mmittee underlines the need for 
bringing in the latest technology; equipment; and infrastructure, to promote air sports in 
the country. The Committee is aware that the International Olympics Committee (IOC) is 
considering the inclusion of air sports in IOC -approved sporting events. In this regard, the 
Committee recommends the Ministry to popularize air sports and take concentrated steps 
in consultation with State Governments; the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports; and the 
Ministry of Tourism, along with the Air Sports Federation of India.  
(Para 178) 
The Committee notes that with the formulation of the National Aero Sports Policy (NASP), 
2022, a great fillip is required towards the Aero Sports sector in the country. However, the 
Committee feels that the Aer o Club of India (ACI) which was the nodal organization and 
the National Aero Sports control authority designated under the Fédération Aéronautique 
Internationale (FAI), the equivalent of the International Olympics Committee, has not been

India.

The Committee also notes that the FAI has given recognition to the Aero Club of India 
(ACI), an autonomous body under the Ministry of Civil Aviation, for conduct ing, 
certifying, sponsoring and authenticating Aero Sports bodies in India. Hence, the 
Committee feels that the Policy may be fine -tuned to incorporate a provision to have the 
Aero club of India as the Nodal Aero Sports development centre for promotion of Aero 
Sports which it has been handling prior to and post-independence as well. 
(Para 180) 
The Committee would like to note that under the NASP, 2022, the Ministry is in the process 
of establishing the Air Sports Federation of India (ASFI), which shall be a n autonomous 
body under the Ministry of Civil Aviation, to represent India in the FAI and other global 
platforms related to Air Sports. However, the FAI recognizes the Aero Club of India as the 
longstanding autonomous body of the Ministry of Civil Aviation  which is audited by the

body of the FAI, as the National Aero Sports control authority in India. The creation of the 
new ASFI under the NASP, 2022 may not have the s ame legitimacy and as it may hamper 
the growth and development of Aero Sports in the country at the national and 
international level.

Trans-Shipment Hubs  
The Committee is of the opinion that given India's strategic geographical location between 
Europe and Southeast Asia, it has the potential to become a leading air cargo trans -
shipment hub. The National Civil Aviation Policy, 2016 also envisages un -tapping this 
trans-shipment opportunity and also proposes setting up of Free Trade and Warehousing 
Zones to facilitate trans -shipment cargo. The Committee notes that after the SEZ Act, 
2005, only 8 FTWZs have been set up and out of these, 4 are non -operational. Th e 
Committee notes the low number of FTWZs set up since the Act was enacted in 2005 and 
desires to know the reasons for the obstacles and the steps taken by the Ministry to remove 
the bottlenecks.  
(Para 185)

Issues of Cyber Safety and Security  
The Committee opines that the aviation sector is highly vulnerable to cyber security threats 
as most major airlines have reporte d incidents of cyber-attacks, putting air operations and 
passenger data at huge risk, in the past few years. The Committee is aware that recently 
several flights in the United States of America were grounded or delayed due to a possible 
cyber attack that i ncapacitated the airlines websites. The Committee notes the issuance of 
necessary circular/order for countering cyber attacks and protection of sensitive data in the 
Aviation Sector in India. The Committee appreciates Ministry’s move to seek Reports from 
Airport/Aircraft Operators on preventive measures taken to protect the Civil Aviation 
Sector from potential cyber security threats. The Committee recommends that the Ministry 
may expedite the replies from the concerned stakeholders and would like to be info rmed of 
the summary of all the cyber security measures taken by the Airport/ Aircraft Operators.   
(Para 189) 
The Committee also notes that in the last 5 years, 13 incidents of cyber -attacks have been 
reported with AAI. The Committee would like to know the  details of these attacks and the 
action taken by the Ministry, thereof. The Committee recommends the Ministry 
orchestrate a robust and comprehensive mechanism to counter cyber attacks/ terrorism. 
The Committee notes that AAI has utilized Rs. 51.80/ - crore for the implementation of 
Cyber Security in civil aviation from 2019 -2022. The Committee feels that the Ministry 
should make concentrated efforts to overcome the challenges viz., adoption of a 
multidisciplinary approach; data protection; and lack of skill ed manpower. The 
Committee further recommends that for proper monitoring of the challenges with respect 
the cyber security, the Ministry may explore the possibility of including a separate budget 
head and enhancing the budgetary allocations.  
(Para 190)

registered Indian aircraft, in consultation with concerned stakeholders & expert agencies.

difficult airstrips/runways to ensure smooth and safe take-offs and landing. The Committee

these 21 airports, 10 have already been operatio nalized. Remaining 11 airports are being 
developed in partnership with respective State Governments. The Ministry is carrying out a 
series of reform measures to promote the Unmanned Aircraft System (Drones) in the country . 
Drones are  extensively being used  for the property survey in rural areas under the Swamitva 
Yojana. An amount of ₹ 33/- crores has been earmarked for F.Y. 2023 -24 under the Production 
Linked Incentive Scheme  and Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan  to incentivize manufacturing of 
Drones and Drone components in India respectively.  
7.         The Secretary briefed the Committee about the various digital initiatives viz., e-GCA; e-
BCAS and Digi Yatra to ensure efficient and effective processes ing. He stated that there is a 
need for 1000 pilots per yea r. To meet the  present requirements, a liberalized Flying Training 
Organization (FTO) Policy has been adopted to facilitate the growth of FTOs.  The Ministry has 
actively taken up the issue of reduction in Value Added Tax (VAT) on Aviation Turbine Fuel 
(ATF) with all the States and Union Territories . 18 States/UTs have already reduced VAT on 
ATF. 
8.         Regarding the disinvestment of Air India,  Secretary informed that  the process of 
disinvestment of its four subsidiaries viz. Alliance Air Aviation Ltd.; AI Airport Services Ltd.; 
AI Engineering Services Ltd.; and Hotel Corporation of India Ltd are under process.  The 
Chairman, then enquired into the justification for the Enterprise Valuation Method adopted for 
disinvestment of Air India and the time it will  take to clear the entire liability. Secretary 
responded that the Government had taken over a liability of Rs 62,365/ - crores, and got a cash 
component of Rs . 2,700/- crores. The Chairman also enquired into the details of the amount 
which has been invested by the Government in Air India since it was nationalized in 1953 till the 
date of its disinvestment. 
9.       Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) facilitated the 
disinvestment of Air India in which Enterprise Value (EV) based bidd ing process was adopted. 
The highest bidder i.e., M/s Talace Private Limited, estimated the EV at ₹ 18,000/- crores. Out 
of this the Ministry received cash component of 15% i.e., ₹ 2,700/- crores. The bidder took over 
the balance as debt as prescribed in the bidding process. Also, 68 assets out of the total 211 non -
core assets of the Air India are in the process of being disposed off. The Committee decided that 
the views of the Ministry and Air India Assets Holding Limited on the disinvestment of Air 
India, may be heard at a later date.  
10.       The Chairman also took up the issue of affordability of air tickets for the common man . 
He enquired about the reason for removal of price cap on airfares. He pointed out that by doing 
so, no extra revenue is being generated for the Government or the airport operator . Only the

Secretary clarified that as per international norms, fare buckets are created by the airlines .

11.       The Secretary further informed that the long pending issue of opera tionalization of a 
fourth runway at the Indira Gandhi International Airport, Delhi will be resolved by September,
