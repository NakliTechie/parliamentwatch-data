Report
The tourism has been a major social phenomenon of the societies all along.  It is motivated by the natural urge of every
 human being for new experience, adventure, education and entertainment.  Tourism is a multi sectoral activity characterized by
 multiple services provided by a range of suppliers.  It is largest service industry in the country.  Its importance lies in being an
 instrument for economic development and employment generation.   The tourism has been pivotal in social progress as well as
 an important vehicle of widening socio-economic and cultural contacts throughout human history.  A wide array of interests-
entertainment, sports, religion, culture,  adventure, education, health and business- drives tourism through increased contact.   It
 helps to promote cultural cohesion and social bonding and creates a durable social fabric.  With the advancement of transport,
 communication and improvement in general economic well being the demand for tourism has increased manifold.   The Five-
Year Plan perspective initiatives required for the development of tourism synergize with the development efforts needed to
 accomplish a number of other goals like employment generation, advancement of women and disadvantaged groups.  The
 Government plays a crucial role in making provision for tourism infrastructure and skill development. Such initiatives
 catalyses private enterprises to come forward and invest in this sector.  The share of India in international tourist arrival
 continues to increase from 2004 onwards.  But the share is still low at 0.55% in 2007.  The foreign tourist arrival rose from
 3.46 million in 2004 to an estimated 5 million in 2007.  Further more, foreign exchange earnings from tourism in India rose
 substantially from US$3 billion in 2002 to an estimated US$ 11.96 billion in 2007.  The share of India in world’s tourism
 receipts scaled from 0.65 per cent in 2002 to 1.21 percent in 2006. 
2.         The Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and programmes and for the
 coordination of activities of various Central Government agencies, State Governments/Union Territories and the Private Sector
 for the development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Ministry of Tourism plays a crucial role in coordinating and
 supplementing the efforts of the State/Union Territories Governments, catalyzing private investment, strengthening
 promotional and marketing efforts and in providing trained manpower resources. The function of the Ministry of Tourism
 mainly consists of the following:-
·
        Framing of policy matters on development of tourism
·        Grant of Central Financial Assistance to States/UTs for Development of Tourism Infrastructure
·        Encouragement of Public Private participation in tourism infrastructure development
·        Promotion and marketing of Tourism Products within the country and abroad
·        Providing incentives in the accommodation sector
·        Regulation of tourism industry
·        Investment facilitation
·        Human Resource Development and Training
·        Research, analysis, monitoring and evaluation
·        International Cooperation of promotion and development of India Tourism
3.         The administrative head of the Ministry is the Secretary (Tourism).  The Secretary also acts as the Director General
 (DG) Tourism. The office of the Director General of Tourism {now merged with the office of Secretary (Tourism)} provides
 executive directions for the implementation of various policies and programmes.  Directorate General of Tourism has a field
 formation of 20 offices within the country and 13 offices abroad and one sub-ordinate office/project i.e. Indian Institute  of
 Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM)/Gulmarg Winter Sports Project (GWSP). The activities of IISM/GWSP have been revived
 and various ski and other courses are conducted in the J&K valley.  In addition to the Headquarters Organization at New Delhi,
 the Directorate General of Tourism has 20 India Tourism Offices including 5 Regional Offices, situated at New Delhi,
 Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata , Guwahati and other offices are located at Agra, Bhubaneswar, Aurangabad, Bangalore, Kochi,
 Hyderabad, Imphal, Jaipur, Khajuraho, Naharlagun, Panaji (Goa), Patna, Port Blair, Shillong and Varanasi.  There are 13 India
 Tourism Offices Overseas, which include 5 Regional Offices at, New York, Frankfurt, Dubai, Sydney and Tokyo.  The other
 India tourism Offices are located at Los Angeles, Toronto, London, Paris, Amsterdam, Milan, Johannesburg and Singapore.
 The overseas offices are primarily responsible for tourism promotion and marketing in their respective areas and the field
 offices in the country are responsible for providing information service to tourists and to monitor the progress of field projects.

8.         When asked about the projected demand of the Ministry of Tourism for 11th Five Year Plan and the allocation made for
 the entire Plan, the Ministry of Tourism in their replies submitted that the projected demand for eleventh Plan of the Ministry
 of Tourism was   Rs.5405.58 crore (10% basis) and Rs. 6569.61 crore (20% basis) The Planning Commission has allocated
 Rs.5156.00 crore. The requirement of additional funds could be considered at the time of mid term appraisal of the 11 th Plan.

 Commission has allocated Rs.1000.00 crore.  The major activities/schemes of the Ministry of Tourism is for Tourist
 infrastructure augmentation, Development of Human Resource for hospitality and travel trade, Promotion and Publicity,
 Creating budget accommodation etc. for which Rs. 1000 crore have been allocated in B.E. 2008-09 for Ministry of Tourism. 
 All the schemes have been continued except the equity contribution to ITDC.
9.         The Committee observes that the Ministry of Tourism projected a requirement of Rs.5405.58 crore (10% basis) and Rs.
 6569.61 crore (20% basis) for the entire 11
th Plan against this requirement, the Planning Commission approved an allocation
 of Rs.5156.00 crore for carrying out major activities of the Ministry viz. Augmentation of Tourist infrastructure, Development
 of Human Resource for hospitality and travel trade, Promotion and Publicity, Creating budget accommodation etc.   The
 Committee also notes that in the Annual Plan 2008-09 the Ministry has projected an outlay of Rs.1062.85 crore against which
 an amount of  Rs.1000.00 crore was allocated.  The Committee deplores the tendency of Planning Commission and Ministry of
 Finance for imposing cuts on the amounts demanded by the Ministry.  This mismatch between projected amount and
 sanctioned outlay may result in widening further the gap between the tourism potential and development of tourism synergies
 for achieving the goals.   The Committee feels that in order to attain the National growth targets in the 11 th Plan Period, huge
 planned funding is required.  Thus, the Government spending for the Ministry of Tourism will have to be significantly stepped-
up from the present level to meet the investment targets.   The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry of
 Tourism should be provided with Rs.6569.61 crore (20% basis) by the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance for
 the 11 th Plan Period as per their original demand.    The Committee also recommends the Ministry of Tourism to take
 appropriate caution and remedial action to absorb in time the allocation made to them.

14.       The Committee notes that during 2007-2008, the plan outlay of Rs. 996.62 crore of Ministry of Tourism was not
 subjected to any cut at RE stage.  However, the Committee notes that during the year 2007-08, out of the budgeted allocation
 of Rs. 996.62 crore, the Ministry  could spent Rs. 658.55 crore upto December, 2007 which clearly indicates the poor fiscal
 management in the Ministry  of Tourism. The Committee observes that the major portion of the budgetary outlay has been
 utilized during the last quarter of the financial year.   The Committee disapproves the tendency of spending funds desperately
 towards the end of the financial year especially, in the months of February and March.  The Committee expects that the
 maximum amount of plan outlay would be utilized suitably and substantially during the first two quarters of the financial year
 so that the Ministry will be in a position to demand for higher allocations of the funds at RE stage for implementation of
 various projects/schemes of augmentation of tourists infrastructure and promotion and publicity of tourism in the country.  The
 Committee therefore, recommends the Ministry to plan and incur their expenditure uniformly so that funds are utilized
 appropriately   throughout the year and the Ministry should shun the practice of   incurring expenditure indiscriminately
 towards the last quarter of the financial year.  This way no alibi will be provided to the Ministry of Finance to reduce the
 allocation at RE Stage.   The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should do away with procedural hurdles and
 maintain close liaison for the purpose of review, monitoring, implementation and coordination of projects/ schemes to achieve
 the envisaged targets.
 AUGMENTATION OF TOURISM INFRASTRUCTURE
15.       The Ministry of Tourism extends Central Financial Assistance to the State Governments/ Union Territory
 Administrations for Product/ Infrastructure Development in Destinations and Circuits for tourism projects. The projects are
 identified and prioritized in consultation with the State Governments/ UT Administrations for improving the existing as also
 for developing new tourism products. 100% Central Assistance is given under the scheme for identified projects for
 development of major destinations/circuits to world standard and also for rural tourism infrastructure development.   The
 Ministry provides Financial Assistance of Rs. 5 crore for the development of tourist destinations and Rs. 8 crore for the
 development of tourist circuits to States/ UTs. The upper limit of financial assistance has been increased to Rs. 25 crore and
 Rs. 50 crore for the development of identified major tourist destinations and identified major tourist circuits, respectively to
 world class infrastructure.
16.       On being asked about the utilization of increased outlay during 2008-2009,  the Ministry of Tourism informed that
 augmentation of  infrastructure is a thrust area for Ministry of Tourism. Hence, higher allocation of  Rs. 472.00 crore has been
 made in B.E. 2008-09 for tourist infrastructure with a view to take up more development works at important tourist
 destinations and circuits. State Governments/Union Territory Administrations have been requested to send proposals for
 prioritization of projects during 2008-09. Projects for sanction will be selected from the proposals received from them.   During
 the year 2007-08, the Ministry has sanctioned 165 new projects and utilized Rs. 460.00 crore for the upgradation  of
 infrastructure facilities at important tourist destinations .
17.       The Ministry of Tourism has identified  destinations/circuits as mega projects, out of which projects for Agra, Varanasi-
Sarnath, Bodhgaya-Rajgir-Nalanda, Delhi, Kurukshetra and Hyderabad (Charminar area) have been sanctioned so far. This is
 an on-going process and is based on the national/international importance of destination/circuit, number of visitors, potential
 for growth, etc.Tourism  being a cross-cutting sector, building synergies with other ministries and sectors will be critical in
 achieving efficiency and speed in the development process. The effort is on to synergies with the Jawaharlal Nehru National
 Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM) of the Ministry of Urban Development, which is being implemented in 63 selected cities
 in the country for the integrated development of infrastructure services, urban renewal, basic services to the urban port, asset
 management and urban governance. Convergence with the JNNURM will ensure that there is no duplication and would
 maximize the impact of investments on infrastructure.
18.       An important high level forum to achieve consensus and co-ordination among various infrastructure related ministries,
 the Committee on Infrastructure, Chaired by the Prime Minister, includes Ministry of Tourism. The Committee is expected to
 play an important role in the Ministry’s endeavour  to secure co-ordination with other ministries in the implementation of its
 policies and programmes.An undertaking by the beneficiary State Governments/Union Territories provides for the
 management and maintenance of the tourism assets created with the assistance from the Central Government.

22.       The Committee notes that one of the reasons for underutilization of allocation is unspent balances with the States and
 also non-submission of utilization certificates by most of the States on time.  The Committee observes that most of the time the
 Central Government delay release of funds to the State or releases it at the fag end of the financial year and expects the States
 to utilize it in a very short time.  The Committee criticize this practice as obviously the States are not in a position to spend the
 entire amount in such a short period and therefore, have unspent balance lying with them.   The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the release of the funds to the States should be made on time so that they could utilize the amount rationally
 under proper monitoring of expenditure.   The Committee also desires that there should be proper coordination with the States
 Government, so as to obtain utilization certificates and to safeguard that money released is spent only on approved
 infrastructure development projects.
23.       The Committee notes that the Ministry of Tourism is making efforts to develop quality tourism infrastructure at major
 tourist destinations and circuits for developing tourist products and providing better services to both domestic and international
 tourists.  The Committee observes that tourism is one of the economic sectors in the country that has the potential to grow at
 high rate and can ensure consequential development of the infrastructure of the destinations and circuits.  The Committee feels
 that the infrastructure development holds the key to India’s sustained of growth in the tourism sector,  therefore, tourist sites
 and destinations needs to be carefully selected on the basis of their potential to provide all infrastructural  facilities  required by
 the tourists within such destinations and circuits.   The Committee is of the opinion that, if, India has to exploit its vast inherent
 potential  in tourism, then it must provide exclusive quality tourism product and destinations comparable with other Asian
 countries like Singapore, Malaysia, Maldives, Thailand and China. As tourism cannot develop independent of other
 infrastructure, therefore, a lot of information dissemination is required in addition to hospitality and the scope and reach of the
 domestic tourism should also be broadened through development of these competitive destinations and circuits.   The
 Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to develop quality tourism at identified tourist circuits and destinations with
 focus on mega destinations and circuits that match international standards and satisfy the domestic and international tourists.  
 The aggressive marketing and publicity of these destinations and circuits should also be done to generate a niche market for
 Indian tourism products.   The Committee also urges the Ministry that while selecting a destination or a circuit they should
 attach priority for harmonious growth and development of all the Regions and States. 
24.       The Committee also recommends that in order to maximize the impact of investment on tourism infrastructure, the
 Ministry should make efforts for convergence of these mega destinations and circuits and projects with Jawahar Lal Nehru
 National Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM) for integrated development of infrastructure services, urban renewal, asset
 management and urban governance. 
RURAL TOURISM

      To showcase rural life, art, culture and heritage at rural locations in villages and to benefit the local community
 economically and socially as well as to enable interaction between tourists and local population for a mutually enriching

MULTIPLICITY OF TAXES
84.       The Committee in its various Reports on Demands for Grants of Ministry of Tourism has dealt with the matter of
 Multiplicity of Taxes and recommended the Government for ensuring uniformity and rationalization of multiple taxes which
 has adverse impact on the tourism sector.  The Ministry of Tourism in their Action Taken Notes informed the Committee that
 the issue had been followed up with the Member Secretary, Empowered Committee of Finance Ministers, that the proposal of
 Ministry of Tourism regarding rationalization of taxes on tourism sector be taken up during the meeting of Empowered
 Committee.  In addition, this matter would again be taken up with respective State Government.   The Ministry of Tourism is
 aware that multiplicity of taxes in the tourism sector as well as the variation of quantum of taxes levied by different States and
 Union Territories does affect the growth of tourism in the country. Some of the major taxes which are having an adverse affect
 in growth of tourism are the luxury tax on hotel, VAT on food and beverages and on Airlines Turbine Fuel (ATF).  In addition,
 there is huge variation on taxes on tourist transport vehicles in the States.The Ministry of Tourism has forwarded a detailed
 proposal on the rationalization of the above taxes to the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance and has also taken up this
 issue with the Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers.
85.       In order to rationalize the tax on tourist transport and to facilitate smooth movement of inter-state travel, the Ministry of
 Tourism has mooted a proposal regarding introduction of Zonal Permits for a minimum of 3 States.  It has been suggested that
 the States may agree to the Tourist Transport by Operators depositing a composite tax per State, per quarter, per bus.  The tax
 component to a particular State could then be deposited in separate designated bank accounts of each State so as to ensure
 there is no loss of revenue to each of the State in the zone.  The second suggestion which has been mooted to complement the
 above recommendation is introduction of a separate Tourists Vehicles (T.V.) series as registration number for Government of
 India approved Tourist Transport Operators.  This will enable easy identification of tourist transport vehicles at border crossing
 and distinguish them from State carriages. The first proposal was forwarded to the Department of Revenue to be taken up with
 the Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers.  The second proposal has been taken up with the Ministry of Road
 Transport and State Governments.  A decision in this regard is awaited from the respective quarters.
86.       The Committee is constrained to notes that luxury tax on hotel, VAT on food and beverages and on Aviation Turbine
 Fuel (ATF) and higher rate of taxes on tourist transport vehicles in the States have adverse effect on the growth of tourism.  
 The Committee, however, is perturbed to note that even after repeated recommendations of the Committee,  the Government
 has not been able to do much to rationalize the tax regime, which is ailing the tourism sector at large.  The Ministry of Tourism
 has forwarded two proposals for consideration of Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers and Department of Road
 Transport and Highways respectively, in this regard and a decision is awaited.   The Committee strongly criticizes the
 lackadaisical approach of the Government in the matter which has adversely affected the growth of tourism and hospitality
 sector in the country.   The Committee reiterates that the Government must provide a level playing field to the tourism and
 hospitality sector including tourist transport by attempting to rationalize taxes or eliminate the disparity in tax treatment as
 levied by the different States and Union Territories.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to pursue vigorously the matter
 of rationalization of taxes with the Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers till the matter is favourably resolved. 
 The Committee also recommends the Government to set up an Empowered Group for rationalization of roads and transport
 taxes on tourist vehicles.

BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS AND ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2008-09.
            The Committee observes that the Ministry of Tourism projected a requirement of Rs.5405.58 crore  (10% basis)
 and Rs. 6569.61 crore (20% basis) for the entire 11
th Plan against this requirement, the Planning Commission approved
 an allocation of Rs.5156.00 crore  for carrying out major activities of the Ministry viz. Augmentation of Tourist
 infrastructure, Development of Human Resource for hospitality and travel trade, Promotion and Publicity, Creating
 budget accommodation etc.  The Committee also notes that in the Annual Plan 2008-09 the Ministry has projected an
 outlay of Rs.1062.85 crore  against which an amount of  Rs.1000.00 crore was allocated.  The Committee deplores the
 tendency of Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance for imposing cuts on the amounts demanded by the
 Ministry.  This mismatch between projected amount and sanctioned outlay may result in widening further the gap
 between the tourism potential and development of tourism synergies for achieving the goals.   The Committee feels that
 in order to attain the National growth targets in the 11
th Plan Period, huge planned funding is required.  Thus, the
 Government spending for the Ministry of Tourism will have to be significantly stepped-up from the present level to
 meet the investment targets.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should be
 provided with Rs.6569.61 crore (20% basis) by the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance for the 11
th Plan
 Period as per their original proposal.   The Committee also recommends the Ministry of Tourism to take appropriate
 caution and remedial action to absorb in time the allocation made to them.
(Para 9)
            The Committee notes that during 2007-2008, the plan outlay of Rs. 996.62 crore  of Ministry of Tourism was not
 subjected to any cut at RE stage.  However, the Committee notes that during the year 2007-08, out of the budgeted
 allocation of Rs. 996.62 crore , the Ministry  could spent Rs. 658.55 crore upto December, 2007 which clearly indicates
 the poor fiscal management in the Ministry  of Tourism. The Committee observes that the major portion of the
 budgetary outlay has been utilized during the last quarter of the financial year.   The Committee disapproves the
 tendency of spending funds desperately towards the end of the financial year especially, in the months of February and
 March.  The Committee expects that the maximum amount of plan outlay would be utilized suitably and substantially
 during the first two quarters of the financial year so that the Ministry will be in a position to demand for higher
 allocations of the funds at RE stage for implementation of various projects/schemes of augmentation of tourists
 infrastructure and promotion and publicity of tourism in the country.  The Committee therefore, recommends the
 Ministry to plan and incur their expenditure uniformly so that funds are utilized appropriately   throughout the year
 and the Ministry should shun the practice of   incurring expenditure discriminately towards the last quarter of the
 financial year.  This way no alibi will be provided to the Ministry of Finance to reduce the allocation at RE Stage.   The
 Committee also recommends that the Ministry should do away with procedural hurdles and maintain close liaison for
 the purpose of review, monitoring, implementation and coordination of projects/ schemes to achieve the envisaged
 targets.

            The Committee notes there are a number of destinations and circuits have huge tourist potential.  The
 Committee is deeply concerned over the poor road connectivity of these tourism destinations and circuits including
 cultural heritage sites.  It observes that many of tourist destinations are away from the National Highways and lack
 road connectivity of desired standard.  However, the Committee notes that an Inter-Ministerial Group of Secretaries is
 looking after the road connectivity projects to identify the road links to tourist destinations, assess their present

 Highways.  The Ministry of Tourism has provided a list of 11 roads to the group for improvement and upgradation

 improvement and upgradation of States roads, is the responsibility of States Government.  Therefore, the Ministry of
 Tourism should impress upon the respective States Government/U.T’s to improve the condition of such roads.  The

 roads under Central Road Fund Scheme.

 the view that domestic tourism cannot be promoted unless air, rail and road links across the country and especially the
 places of tourist interest are improved to desired level.   The Committee recommends that tourist destinations in the
