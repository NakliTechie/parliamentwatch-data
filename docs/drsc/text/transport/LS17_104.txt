REPORT
            The Department of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and programmes and for the
 coordination of activities of various Central Government agencies, State Governments/Union Territories and the Private
 Sector for the development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Department of Tourism plays a crucial role in
 coordinating and supplementing the efforts of the State/Union Territories Governments, catalyzing private investment,
 strengthening promotional and marketing efforts and in providing trained manpower resources. The functions of the
 Department inter alia include - All Policy Matters; Planning; Co-ordination with other Ministries, Departments, State/UT
 Governments. Regulation of Standards Guidelines Infrastructure & Product Development; Research, Analysis, Monitoring
 and Evaluation;  International Co-operation and External Assistance Controlling the functions of attached offices viz,
 Directorate General of Tourism; Inspection quality control and infrastructure development, Human Resource Development,
 publicity and marketing.
 
2.         The administrative head of the Department is the Secretary (Tourism).  The Secretary also acts as the Director
 General (DG) Tourism. The office of the Director General of Tourism {now merged with the office of Secretary (Tourism)}
 provides executive directions for the implementation of various policies and programmes.  Directorate General of Tourism
 has a field formation of 20 offices within the country and 13 offices abroad and one sub-ordinate office/project i.e. Indian
 Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM)/Gulmarg Winter Sports Project . The activities of IISM/GWSP have now
 been revived and various ski and other courses are conducted in the J&K valley.  In addition to the Headquarters
 Organisation at New Delhi, the Directorate General of Tourism has 20 Indiatourism Offices in India (including 5 Regional
 Offices), situated at New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Agra, Bhubaneswar, Aurangabad, Bangalore, Cochin,

APPROACH IN THE 10th FIVE YEAR PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2006-07.
 
4.         The Tenth Plan approach towards Tourism signifies a distinct shift from the approach adopted in earlier plans. The
 10 th Plan acknowledges the traditionally recognised advantages of developing tourism for the promotion of national
 integration, international understanding and the earning of foreign exchange besides recognizing vast employment
 generating potential of Tourism and furthering the socio-economic objectives of the Plan. The State Governments would
 use Tourism as a means for achieving their socio-economic objectives and catalyze the private sector to enhance investment
 in tourism.  It seeks to enhance the employment potential within the tourism sector as well as to foster economic integration
 through developing linkages with other sectors. It also seeks to provide legislative and regulatory support for sustainable
 tourism and protect the interests of both the industry as well as the consumer. It further seeks to involve the rural sector in
 the promotion of all rich areas of tourism viz., rural, heritage, adventure, medical and eco-tourism.  It emphasizes on the
 development of competitive high quality products and destinations and for creation of world class infrastructure.  It also
 seeks to remove the barrier to growth of tourism and resolve contradictions in policy to achieve inter-sectoral convergence
 of activities that helps to foster the growth of tourism making India a competitive favoured destination.

6.         By the end of December, 2005, an expenditure of Rs. 552.71 crore was incurred  for infrastructure development and
 other promotional programmes against the outlay of Rs. 786 crore, which was approximately 70.3 per cent of the Annual
 Plan allocation for the year 2005-06.  The Committee wanted to know the reasons for this low utilization of funds upto
 December 2005 and how the Department of Tourism will utilise the remaining 30 per cent outlay during the financial year
 2005-06. The Department of Tourism informed the Committee that the Department extends central financial assistance to
 States/U.T.s for implementation of the projects prioritized in consultation with them.  During the year 2005-06, some of the
 projects were not forthcoming from the States/UTs concerned well in time, for financial assistance to the Department of
 Tourism, causing low utilization of funds upto December 2005.  For other promotional programmes like International and
 Domestic campaigns the funds could not be utilized due to late receipt of bills because the payment to various agencies is
 made on reimbursement basis once the campaign has been carried out as per media plan.  As regards utilization of the
 balance 30 per cent of the outlay in the remaining three months of the financial year, the Department of Tourism informed
 that the low utilization of funds upto December 2005 was recouped by spending Rs.772 crore out of the Budget provision of
 Rs.786 crore by the end of the financial year 2005-06.

7.         As regards proposal to set up a Task Force to oversee that the allocation be utilized uniformly throughout the year
 instead of spending indiscriminately during the last fag of the financial year, the Department of Tourism submitted that in
 order to ensure uniform spending of funds, especially for implementation of tourism infrastructure projects, a monitoring

 in this regard.
8.         The Committee notes that the plan allocation of Rs. 786 crore of Department of Tourism was not subjected to any
 reduction at RE stage during 2005-06.  However, the Committee notes that during the year 2005-06, out of the budgeted
 allocation of Rs. 786 crore, the Department could spend Rs. 552.71 crore upto December, 2005 and the same was recouped 
 to Rs. 772 crore by the end of the financial year 2005-06,  which clearly indicates the poor fiscal management in the
 Department of Tourism.  The Committee disapproves the tendency of spending funds desperately towards the end of the
 financial year especially, in the months of February and March.  The Committee expects that the maximum amount of plan
 outlay would be utilized suitably and substantially during the first five months of the financial year so that the Department
 will be in a position to make demand for more allocations of funds at RE stage for implementation of various
 projects/schemes of development of tourism infrastructure and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Committee
 therefore, recommends that the Department should evolve some mechanism of uniform utilization pattern throughout the
 year so as to avoid incurring expenditure at the fag end of the financial year.

 the Committee that :

12.       Taking into account the Actual Expenditure of 2002-03 to 2004-05 and Anticipated Expenditure of 2005-06 and BE
 of 2006-07, the Department of Tourism has been allocated approximate 91 per cent of the total outlay  of Rs. 2900 crore for
 the 10
th Five Year Plan.  The Committee notes that the outlay for the annual plan 2006-07 is Rs. 830 crore which is short by
 Rs. 259 crore of the total budgeted outlay of Rs. 2900 crore for the 10 th Plan.  The Committee is of the view that the plan
 outlay is insufficient to explore the tourism potential available in the country and the Department of Tourism has not been
 allocated the budgeted outlay judiciously. The Committee feels that the financial requirements of the Department of
 Tourism is much more and expects that the plan outlays of the Department would be increased in the coming years.  The
 Committee expects that Department of Tourism will also be able to utilize the enhanced allocation for effective
 implementation of the schemes/programmes for development and promotion of tourism and also for creating world class
 and dependable tourism product and infrastructure required for sustained and structured tourism growth.  The Committee
 recommends that while revising allocations, due consideration should be given to the actual expenditure incurred in the
 past.  This way, the Department of Tourism will be in a position to make demand for more allocation of funds at RE
 stage.

16.       As regards, hurdles and prevention of delays in the implementation of projects under the  scheme, the Department of
 Tourism informed that Department of Tourism assists State Governments for creation of infrastructure and other
 promotional projects by releasing Central Funds  under the various schemes.  The reasons, which sometimes lead to delay in
 timely implementation of the projects earlier were non-availability of land, free from all encumbrances, lack of technical
 expertise and resources etc.  These issues relating to delay in completion of the projects are now being sorted out by making
 available the services of HUDCO, ITDC for project preparation and CPWD for project implementation and making the land
 availability certificate mandatory with the project proposal and also releasing of 80 per cent of the project cost as first
 installment.  The issue of project implementation is also addressed on priority with the concerned Ministries, where the
 other Ministries/Departments like Urban Development, Culture, Environment and Forests are involved for various
 clearances. The Tourism infrastructure projects sanctioned by Department of Tourism in favour of various State/U.Ts. are
 normally completed within a time period of 12-36 months.

18.       The Committee is happy to note that Department of Tourism has been able to spend the entire amount available for
 development of infrastructure. The Committee is also happy to note that the Department of Tourism has finally decided to
 concentrate on major destinations and major circuits as was being stressed by the Committee for long.  The Committee
 agrees with the Department that amount of Rs. 8 crore per circuit and Rs.5 crore per destination is insufficient  and
 recommends that the amount should be increased to Rs. 50 crore as suggested by the Department of Tourism.  The
 Committee notes that the Department of Tourism has modified the pattern of funding to enable all States for improving the
 existing products and developing new tourism products of world class standard.   Now 100 per cent assistance is provided to
 State Governments/UTs for creation of infrastructure and other promotional projects  and the State Governments have to
 provide mainly land and external infrastructure.  The Committee is of the view that if India has to realise its enormous
 potential in tourism, then it must provide exclusive world class tourism products and destinations to compete successfully
 with other Asian tourism markets and the scope and reach of domestic tourism should also be broadened through the
 development of competitive destinations.  The Committee also observes that arrival of foreign tourists is only 3.46 million,
 which is much less, in comparison to other countries in the world.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department
 of Tourism to develop competitive destinations that match international standards in terms of price, quality and satisfy the
 national and international travelers. Aggressive marketing efforts and publicity should be given to these destinations in
 order to create a niche market for Indian tourism products as well as to attract more tourists to these destinations.  The
 critical infrastrucutral gaps can be bridged by creating a tourism development fund for augmenting larger flow of funds for
 development of tourism infrastructure.   The Committee also recommends the Department of Tourism to create awareness
 about the environment impact of tourism with regard to the carrying capacity of tourist destinations.

 sites, Buddhist sites and major destinations in the country. As intimated by Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport &
 Highway, most of the World Heritage sites have already been connected with the National Highways and further
 connectivity with the State roads are to be done by the respective States. Department of Tourism has approached the
 concerned States for sending their proposals for improvement of such roads through Central Road Fund Scheme. Some of
 the States have sent their proposals, which have been forwarded to Ministry of Road Transport and Highways for
 improvement of roads from their “Central Road Fund Scheme”.

28.       The concept of Rural Tourism has been developed within the product/infrastructure development of destinations and

 crore allocated in BE 2005-06.  However, no separate allocation has been made during 2006-07 for Rural Tourism Projects. 
 The Department of Tourism  propose to implement a minimum of 50 identified Rural Tourism Sites during 2006-07
 through central financial assistance.  A maximum of  Rs. 50.00 lakh is sanctioned for each project under the scheme. 
29.       Tourism growth potential can be harnessed as a strategy for Rural Development. The development of a strong
 platform around the concept of Rural tourism is definitely useful for a country like India, where almost 74 per cent of the
 population resides in its 7 million villages. Across the world, the trends of industrialization and development have had an
 urban centric approach. Alongside, the stresses of urban lifestyles have led to a "counter-urbanization" syndrome. This has
 led to growing interest in the rural areas. At the same time this trend of urbanization has led to falling income levels, lesser
 job opportunities in the rural areas leading to an urbanization syndrome in the rural areas. Rural Tourism is one of the few
 activities which can provide a solution to these problems. Besides, there are other factors which are shifting the trend
 towards rural tourism like increasing levels of awareness, growing interest in heritage and culture and improved
 accessibility, and environmental consciousness. In the developed countries, this has resulted in a new style of tourism of
 visiting village settings to experience and live a relaxed and healthy lifestyle. This concept has taken the shape of a formal
 kind of Rural Tourism. Under this Scheme, thrust is to promote village tourism as the primary tourism product to spread
 tourism and its socio-economic benefits to rural and its new geographic regions. Key geographic regions are identified for
 development and promotion of Rural Tourism. The implementation is done through a Convergence Committee headed by
 the District Collector. Activities like improving the environment, hygiene, infrastructure etc. are assisted. Apart from
 providing financial assistance, the focus is to tap the resources available under different schemes of Ministry of Rural
 Development, State Governments and other concerned  Ministries/Departments of the Government of India.
30.       A concept of rural tourism has been developed within the old scheme of Product Infrastructure and Destination

 tourism potential. The software component of the scheme is provided through the UNDP Endogenous Tourism Projects.
 The hardware components are funded by the Department of Tourism.  The training, capacity building, publicity etc. are
 funded by UNDP.  The Department of Tourism in partnership with UNDP has also launched the Endogenous Tourism

42.       Tourism being an employment oriented Sector, it is estimated that the hotel and catering sector provide more than
 50% of the total employment generated by tourism industry.  Being a labour oriented industry, directly in contact with the
 country's tourists, skills required by personnel engaged in this sector are different from those required by personnel engaged
 in other sectors.  Training Program in the field of Hotel Management, Catering and Nutrition were first initiated by
 Government of India in the year 1962. In order to harness the resources and also to provide a central thrust to the program,
 Department of Tourism also established the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology (NCHMCT)
 in the year 1984.  The Memorandum of Association of the National Council for Hotel Management broadly provides the
 following mandate:
 
a)
                  To advise the Government on coordinated development of hotel management and Catering education.
b)                  To collect, collate and implement international development in the area of human resource development for
 the hospitality sector.
c)                  To affiliate institutes and prescribe courses of study and instructions leading to examinations conducted by
 it.
d)                  To standardize courses and infrastructure requirements for institutes imparting education and training in
 hospitality management.
e)                  To prescribe educational and other qualifications, experience, etc. for members of staff in the affiliated
 institutes and organize faculty development programs.
f)                    To conduct examinations and award Certificates and Diplomas.
 
43.       As a result of vast expansion and modernization of the country’s hospitality industry, hotel management and catering
 education programs gained tremendous popularity and the profile of students seeking admission to these courses also
 underwent a transformation.  As a result, courses offered by the National Council are of international standard which have
 been recognized by the hospitality industry.  At present, there are 24 Institutes of Hotel Management and 7 Food Craft
 Institutes following the course curriculum of National Council.  In addition, training imparted at Indian Navy’s INS Hamla
 for Catering Courses for Food Production and F&B Service is also regulated by National Council for Hotel Management
 through Special Examination.

UNIFORMITY AND RATIONALIZATION OF TAXES ON TRANSPORT AND HOSPITALITY SECTOR
 
76.       India's competitiveness as a tourist destination is affected by the high and varied taxation both at Central and State
 level.  The accommodation and transport taxes tend to be very heavy in various states policy in others is extremely harsh
 while excise.  The high taxation on accommodation, differential rates of taxes in various states on tourist vehicles, entry tax
 and parking charges at each state/destination push-up the cost of tourist transportation.  As regards, uniformity and
 rationalization of taxes on transport and hospitality sector to a reasonable extent, the Department of Tourism informed the
 Committee that the issue of high incidence of taxes and its adverse impact on the growth of tourism in States has been of
 immense concern to the Department of Tourism. The Department is aware that to encourage free flow of tourist traffic and
 to make  packages competitive in the domestic and foreign markets, there is a need for uniformity in the levy of taxes by
 State Governments. Multiple rates and high taxes result in malpractices and poor collection as well as a disincentive for
 higher tourist traffic. This matter has been taken up at the Chief Ministers level with all States. A proposal has also been
 mooted amongst the Northern States to consider evolving a Zonal Tourist Permit in the Northern region which envisages
 payment of the taxes in the home state and the share of each state getting deposited in a separate designated bank account.
 This model also suggests displaying of the appropriate labels with tourism logos on the vehicles for identification at border
 points. In case this model is accepted by the Northern States and proves to be a success, the same could be replicated by
 other zones. The Department of Tourism has also requested the State Governments to reduce luxury tax on hotel
 accommodation and sales tax / VAT on food and beverages. Any saving for the tourist on these counts would be an
 incentive to them to spend correspondingly higher amounts on other purchases.

94.       Health and medical tourism is perceived as one of the fastest growing segments in marketing 'Destination India'
 today.  While this area has so far been relatively unexplored, the Department of Tourism, Government o India and also the
 various state tourism boards and even the private sector consisting of travel agents, tour operators, hotel companies and
 other accommodation providers are all eying health and medical tourism as a segment with tremendous potential for future
 growth.  Our country will become a major medical hub as its healthcare industry is both competitive on cost and quality.
            
95.       Our country is famous for its traditional and natural medicines.  Indian system of medicine and nature cure has been
 adopted all over the world.  The Health Care Sector in our country has many strengths to offer the world.  It is emerging as
 one of the countries offering cost effective world-class medical facilities and has already started attracting patients from all
 over the world.  It is known to promote positive health, natural beauty and long life.  Ayurveda, Yoga, Panchkarma,
 Rejuvenation etc. are some of the traditional Health Care System.

 country, the Department of Tourism submitted that the following steps have been taken by the Department of Tourism to
 promote health tourism in the country and abroad:
(a)
    A Task Force, headed by Secretary (Health) with Senior Officials of Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Ministry of
 Tourism and experts in the medical field has been constituted to assess the opportunities and to recommend action to be
 taken by the Government and private industry for promoting India as a health destination.

 London.

 tourism in the country.

111.     The North Eastern part of India is endowed with diverse tourist attractions and each one of these states hold immense
 tourist potential.  The attractions are scattered over the entire region and largely located in remote areas within highly
 fragile environments.  The Department of Tourism attaches great importance to the development of tourist infrastructure in
 this region.  It has also launched a nation wide special campaign under the brand entity ‘Paradise Unexplored’ to promote
 the North Eastern States.   However, though product development is taking place, inflow of traffic to these States is being

 proximity to the international borders, law and order situation in the state, preservation of the environment and tribal
 culture, etc.  Hence foreign nationals entering these areas require a permit from the competent authority while the domestic
 tourist visiting these areas require the Inner-line Permit.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
APPROACH IN THE 10th FIVE YEAR PLAN AND ANALYSIS OF THE ANNUAL PLAN 2006-07.
 
            The Committee notes that the plan allocation of Rs. 786 crore of Department of Tourism was not subjected to any
 reduction at RE stage during 2005-06.  However, the Committee notes that during the year 2005-06, out of the budgeted
 allocation of Rs. 786 crore, the Department could spend Rs. 552.71 crore upto December, 2005 and the same was recouped 
 to Rs. 772 crore by the end of the financial year 2005-06,  which clearly indicates the poor fiscal management in the
 Department of Tourism.  The Committee disapproves the tendency of spending funds desperately towards the end of the
 financial year especially, in the months of February and March.  The Committee expects that the maximum amount of plan
 outlay would be utilized suitably and substantially during the first five months of the financial year so that the Department
 will be in a position to make demand for more allocations of funds at RE stage for implementation of various
 projects/schemes of development of tourism infrastructure and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Committee
 therefore, recommends that the Department should evolve some mechanism of uniform utilization pattern throughout the
 year so as to avoid incurring expenditure at the fag end of the financial year.
             Taking into account the Actual Expenditure of 2002-03 to 2004-05 and Anticipated Expenditure of 2005-06 and BE
 of 2006-07, the Department of Tourism has been allocated approximate 91 per cent of the total outlay  of Rs. 2900 crore for

PRODUCT/ INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT OF DESTINATIONS AND CIRCUITS
 
(a)        MAJOR DESTINATIONS AND CIRCUITS DEVELOPMENT
            The Committee is happy to note that Department of Tourism has been able to spend the entire amount available for
 development of infrastructure. The Committee is also happy to note that the Department of Tourism has finally decided to
 concentrate on major destinations and major circuits as was being stressed by the Committee for long.  The Committee
 agrees with the Department that amount of Rs. 8 crore per circuit and Rs.5 crore per destination is insufficient  and
 recommends that the amount should be increased to Rs. 50 crore as suggested by the Department of Tourism.  The
 Committee notes that the Department of Tourism has modified the pattern of funding to enable all States for improving the
 existing products and developing new tourism products of world class standard.   Now 100 per cent assistance is provided to
 State Governments/UTs for creation of infrastructure and other promotional projects  and the State Governments have to
 provide mainly land and external infrastructure.  The Committee is of the view that if India has to realise its enormous
 potential in tourism, then it must provide exclusive world class tourism products and destinations to compete successfully
 with other Asian tourism markets and the scope and reach of domestic tourism should also be broadened through the
 development of competitive destinations.  The Committee also observes that arrival of foreign tourists is only 3.46 million,
 which is much less, in comparison to other countries in the world.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department
 of Tourism to develop competitive destinations that match international standards in terms of price, quality and satisfy the
 national and international travelers. Aggressive marketing efforts and publicity should be given to these destinations in
 order to create a niche market for Indian tourism products as well as to attract more tourists to these destinations.  The
 critical infrastrucutral gaps can be bridged by creating a tourism development fund for augmenting larger flow of funds for
 development of tourism infrastructure.   The Committee also recommends the Department of Tourism to create awareness
 about the environment impact of tourism with regard to the carrying capacity of tourist destinations.
            The Committee notes that the major constraint in the expansion of international tourist traffic to India is non-
availability of adequate infrastructure including accessibility to tourist destination and cultural/heritage sites.  The
 Committee is deeply concerned over the poor road connectivity of tourist destinations and circuits and cultural heritage
 sites.  The Committee notes that Department of Tourism is also seized with the matter and appreciates the efforts taken by
 Department of Tourism for improvement of better road connectivity to these sites.  The Committee observes that many of
 the tourist destinations are away from the National Highways and lack road connectivity of desired standard. The
 Committee recommends that Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways must be impressed about the proper road
 connectivity of all important destinations and circuits.  However, preference may be given to World Heritage Sites  and
 Buddhist Circuits where most of the tourists in general and foreign tourists in particular visit every year.  In this regard the
 Committee agrees to the suggestions of the Department of Tourism for making available fund from Central Roads Fund.

 Department of Tourism during the 8
th, 9th and 10th Five Year Plans are still incomplete.  During 8 th and 9 th Plans, 2526
 projects were sanctioned and only 1761 projects were got completed and balance 765 projects remained incomplete for one
 reason or the others.  The progress of work with regard to the 10 th Plan period is also not very satisfactory.  Out of the 895
 projects sanctioned only 276 projects were got completed and there still remain a shortfall of 619 projects.  The Committee
 is apprehensive that with this slow pace of work the Department of Tourism will not be able to achieve the physical and
 financial targets.    The Committee is of the view that Department of Tourism has failed to activate the State Government
 machinery which have resulted in such a huge mismatch in terms of projections and achievements.  The Committee feels
 that if these projects were not completed in  a definite time frame, then, the entire tax-payers money will go waste.  In this
 regard, the active participation and support of the state Government is essential for tourism to bloom and spread its
 numerous benefits among the host population. The Committee recommends the Department of Tourism to prioritize the
 projects and seek active support of the State Governments/UTs for the completion of the remaining tourism projects so as to

(B)              RURAL TOURISM
            The Committee notes that the concept of rural tourism is the new emerging area under product/infrastructure and
 destination development scheme and aim to promote village tourism to spread tourism and its social-economic benefits to
 rural and its remote geographic regions.   The Committee feels that rural tourism needs to be given impetus by earmarking
 funds for projects on the basis of a model plan for each State for the purpose and these villages should be taken up for
 priority development and linked with the restoration of heritage properties in the area.  The Committee is of the opinion that
 this would provide boost to local performing arts and help conserve the local culture apart from preventing rural-urban
 migration. The Committee recommends that local people in the rural areas should be encouraged to participate in tourism
 related projects in order to provide employment opportunity and to increase the income level in the rural areas.  The
 Committee also recommends that proper training should be imparted on improvement of environment, hygiene and
 sanitation, etc. as coordinated effort to develop rural tourism infrastructure.(Para 32)
 
ASSISTANCE FOR LARGE REVENUE GENERATING PROJECTS
 
            The Committee notes that the Department of Tourism is providing support in the form of grant to Large Revenue
 Generating Projects through the financial institutions and tourism finance corporations. It also notes that 10  projects like
 Tourist Trains, Cruise Vessels, Cruise Terminals, Convention Centres, Golf Courses etc. have been sanctioned by the
 Department of Tourism under the scheme. The Committee expects that these projects would be competed within the
 stipulated time frame though no target therefore have been communicated to the Committee.  The Committee is constrained
 to note that only an amount of Rs. 9.28 crore could be spent by the end of December, 2005.  The Committee hopes that the
 Department will be able to utilize the amount of Rs. 30 crore allocated for the current year.  However, the Committee feels
 that the allocation is meager  when grants-in-aid for one project itself can go up to 50 crore.  The Committee feels that there
 is a need for higher level of participation by the private sector in all the stages of the tourism development as the growth in
 tourism sector can only be achieved by major flow of investments coming in from private sector and public investments can
 act as a catalyst. The Committee recommends that private sector investment should be enhanced by removing the
 impediments to the growth of tourism and expediting policy support.  The proposal for inclusion of special trains like
 “Palace on Wheels” and “Deccan Odyssey” should also be considered in collaboration with the concerned State
 Governments for improving the connectivity and urban infrastructure in Buddhist sites. The Committee also recommends
 that while identifying the projects under the Public Private participation, the strategy for removing regional imbalances
 should also be taken care of. (Para 37)
CAPACITY BUILDING FOR SERVICE PROVIDERS
            The Committee notes with dismay that there is a heavy shortfall in the utilization of funds allocated under the head
 “Capacity Building for Service Providers” and the Department of Tourism has not furnished any reasons for under
 utilization of funds.  It appears to the Committee that while revising allocation, little consideration has been paid to the
 actual expenditure incurred in the past and expects the Department of Tourism to utilize the funds more judiciously during
 2006-07.  The Committee notes that the Capacity Building for Service Providers Scheme provides training through one to
 five day modules to persons who are working in unorganised sectors and who come into contact with a large number of
 tourists like those working in small hotels, dhabas, ticketing agents, travel agencies, police personnel etc. The Committee
 desires the Department of Tourism to prepare broad framework for the countrywide training programme to develop Human
 Resource and place them with important  groups of service providers engaged  in the tourism industry. (Para 41)

 which these institutes have varying standards and are lacking in training and other infrastructure facilities.  The Committee
 recommends that the guidelines for affiliation of these privately managed institutes be notified at the earliest for achieving
 excellence in the tourism sector and the Department of Tourism should grant a statutory /deemed university status to
 NCHMT for better regulation, management, monitoring and coordination of public and private sectors in the hospitality
 education.
(Paras 50 and 51)
DOMESTIC PROMOTION AND PUBLICITY INCLUDING HOSPITALITY
            The Committee is unhappy with the poor performance of Department of Tourism with regard to low utilization of
 approved outlays for the domestic tourism and publicity.  Out of the total allocation of Rs. 230 crore for the 10
th Plan, the
 Department of Tourism could spend Rs. 71.88 crore upto December, 2005 leaving thereby a huge shortfall in the utilisation
 pattern.  The Committee is of the view that the Department of Tourism  has full responsibility to ensure  proper utilisation
 of the approved outlay and recommends that the Department of Tourism should take all corrective measures based upon the
 actual requirements for maximum utilisation of budgeted amount during 2006-07.
            The Committee notes that due to increase in income level and emergence of the dynamic urban middle class, the
 potential of domestic tourism has grown substantially.  In the country for every international tourist there are 80 domestic
 tourists but surprisingly there is no precise estimate of the domestic tourist traffic in the country.  During the year 2004, an
 estimated 367 million domestic tourists traveled the country showing a  positive growth rate of about 19% as compared to
 309 million in the year 2003.  The Committee is of the view that the domestic tourism is the foundation of a viable and
 sustainable tourism industry  and much of the growth of global tourism has been generated by the domestic tourism.  The
 Committee observes that the potential for domestic tourism is massive and required focused approach in all arena especially
 on creation of tourism infrastructure and promoting amenities and facilities including marketing support for the budget
 tourists segment.  The Committee recommends the Department of Tourism to evolve some mechanism for precise
 estimation of the domestic tourist traffic in the country and provide strong support to tourism sector for creation of large
 infrastructure besides launching mass tourism awareness campaign and publicity material especially focussing the newly
 created tourists destinations and circuits. This is imperative in view of the fact that domestic tourists have started looking at
 and exploring their own country with renewed interests.
 
            The Committee also recommends that as the variety of fairs and festivals are a powerful tools for showcasing the
 Indian culture for the tourists and generates income and employment and these events are viewed with interest and curiosity
 by domestic and foreign tourists, the Department of Tourism must  support and lay special attention on domestic publicity
 and marketing of these products. (Paras 55, 57 and 58)
 
OVERSEAS PROMOTION AND PUBLICITY INCLUDING MARKETING DEVELOPMENT ASSISTANCE
            The Committee notes that though allocation for overseas promotion and publicity has been enhanced considerably
 during the year 2005-06, still there is a significant gap between the expenditure of the Government of India and the world
 average expenditure.  The Committee opined that promotion and marketing is an important component of tourism
 development and needs to be undertaken along with the consumer profiles and product characteristics.  The Department of
 Tourism through its 13 Indiatourism overseas offices, endeavor to position India as a preferred tourism and business
 destination and to increase India's share of the global tourism market.  The Committee recommends that Department of
 Tourism should develop and implement cost effective marketing strategies based on market research and analysis in each of
 the tourist generating countries to position India as a global brand and reinforce 'Incredible India' campaign as a unique and
 preferred tourism destinations.
 
          The Committee notes that international tourism is still relatively concentrated in the industrialized countries of
 Europe, the America and Asia and the Pacific.  However, with rising levels of disposable income, many emerging
 economies have shown fast growth particularly in Central Europe,  North-East and South East Asia and middle East.   The
 Committee is of the view that with the enhancement of budget of Indiatourism offices overseas, aggressive promotional
 activities should be carried out in emerging markets like China, Korea, Taiwan etc.  The Committee recommends that

NATIONAL TOURISM PROMOTION BOARD
            The Committee notes that Department of Tourism being the nodal agency for development of tourism in the country
 requires close coordination and interaction with various Ministries/Departments, State Governments and private sector
 players for integration of policies and to mobilize the State Governments and private sector for development of unique and
 competitive tourism products and destinations.  The Committee feels that the Department of Tourism has tended to
 concentrate largely on its role as a promoter of tourism and generator of foreign exchange earnings and paying less
 attentions to inter sectoral policy coordination.  The Committee observes that though the Government have established
 several forums/mechanism for inter-institutional coordination and the Department of Tourism is also seized with matter of
 setting up of National Tourism Promotion Board, the tourism is still at the appalling state of coordination.  The Committee
 desires the Department of Tourism to redefine and expand its role and work toward inter-sectoral convergence and policy
 integration.  This could be achieved through creation of National Tourism Promotion Board for better coordination with all
 agencies/Stakeholders.  In this regard the Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in 95

 would amalgamate the various aspects/departments is a herculean task.  But the establishment of an autonomous
 institution like this is all the more important for this precise reason.  As regards the synergising/coordination of the
 various departments and administrative nitty-gritty is concerned, studies should be made on the basis of model
 countries who have already formed Tourism Promotion Board.  Further, the Committee recommends that
 participation of the private sector should be elicited by taking up the matter at the forums like Chamber of
 Commerce & Industry, FICCI, Tourist Associations, etc. '(Para 75)
 
UNIFORMITY AND RATIONALIZATION OF TAXES ON TRANSPORT AND HOSPITALITY SECTOR
            The Committee notes with serious concern that a multiplicity of taxes exist in the tourism sector and the quantum of
 taxes also varies from place to place.  The Committee feel that high and multitude of central and state taxes create hurdles in
 packaging of tourism product and makes the product expensive as well.  The Committee feels that there is a need for
 introduction of new incentives to increase tourism expenditure, volume and promotion of investment in the industry.  The
 Committee desires that attempt should be made to reduce the heavy and multiple taxes that reduce the competitiveness of
 the our tourism product and recommends that taxes like expenditure tax, service tax, customs duty and taxation of Aviation
 Turbine Fuel (ATF) and luxury tax at State level should be rationalized and reduced for end customer to make the
 destination competitive and some uniformity in taxes on tourist transport should be adopted to facilitate free flow of tourist
 traffic across the state borders.(Para 78)
SHORTAGE OF HOTEL ACCOMMODATION
            The Committee is concerned with the shortage of hotel accommodation in all categories particularly the budget
 hotels which does not auger well at time when more and more foreign tourists are coming to India particularly
 Commonwealth Games is scheduled to be held in New Delhi in 2010.   The Committee notes that the Department of
 Tourism is augmenting the hotel room capacity commensurate with the growth in the tourism sector. The Department of
 Tourism has taken up the matter for creation of infrastructure with the State Governments and other land owning agencies
 like Ministry of Civil Aviation, Railway and DDA for creation of land banks, allocation of hotel sites on revenue sharing
 basis and provide additional FSI/FAR and a capital subsidy scheme as an incentive for accommodation infrastructure in
 budget hotel is also on an offing.  The Committee recommends the Department of Tourism to take step to ensure that more
 budget hotels are set up by making sites available on central Government land in order to maintain country's
 competitiveness as a tourist destination and facilitate identification of hotel sites and their allocation by State Governments
 to the deserving entrepreneurs with facilities for speedy single window clearance through various Government agencies.
 (Para 83)

 hotels with 103973 rooms in the country till the end of December, 2005 and classification of 50 number of hotels with 5125
 room is pending with the Department.  The Committee feels the hotel accommodation remain a vital area of concern and
 pendency in the classification of hotels is not desirable.  The Committee recommends the Department of Tourism to speed
 up the process of classification/reclassification of hotels at an early date.   The Committee also recommends that India will

CRUISE TOURISM
            The Committee notes that the Government has constituted a high powered steering group to formulate cruise

 Committee also notes that five major ports Mumbai, Marmagao, New Manglaore, Kochi and Tuticorin have been identified

 ports on the East coast in the country including Andaman & Nicobar Islands for development of major cruise destinations,
 which also have exotic appeal.
            (Para 93)
MEDICAL TOURISM
            The Committee notes that medical tourism is new emerging area with a high potential for growth.  The Committee
 observes that the health tourism is not season specific and the average length of stay of a person coming as health tourist is
 generally long depending upon the medical procedure.  The Committee notes that the Government has introduced medical
 visa to facilitate foreign tourists coming to India for medical treatment.  The Committee hopes that low cost of medical
 treatment, high level of expertise and varied system of medicines of India are factors that can lead to a boost in health
 tourism in the country.  (Para 97)
 
BEACH TOURISM
                        The Committee notes that the concept of Beach Tourism is fast emerging and growing in the country.  The
 Committee observes that for development of beach tourism, the Department of Tourism has extended the financial support
 to a number of sites on the west coast of India and a study for an integrated sustainable development of coastal tourism in
 the states of West Bengal, Orissa and Andhra Pradesh has already been undertaken and a comprehensive coastal tourism
 policy for these states is under consideration.  The Committee feels that development of beach tourism along the coastal
 lines of the country will certainly give impetus to the cruise tourism and recommends the Department of Tourism to identify
 and develop more beaches both on the West and East coast in the country with active participation by private sector and due
 publicity should be given for developing these beaches as a major tourists attraction.(Para 103)
        
ADVENTURE TOURISM
            The Committee notes that geographical diversity of our country provides opportunities for out door and adventure
 sports activities for all tastes and interests and every level of experience.  The Committee also notes that major adventure
 tourism activities range from trekking and skiing in the Himalayas, river rafting along the Ganges, water sports in Goa, trout
 fishing in Himachal Pradesh and U.P., heli skiing in Himachal Pradesh and wind surfing, scuba diving and yatching in the
 Andaman  and Lakshadweep Islands.  The Committee feels that there is need for regulation  and promotion  in the country
 of adventure tourism products. The Committee therefore, recommends the Department of Tourism to better regulate and
 promote the adventure tourism asserts in the country especially the mountain based and water sports adventure activities
 amongst the youth and introduce the minimum standards of safety and conservation for tour operators. (Para 110)
 
DEVELOPMENT OF TOURISM IN NORTH-EASTERN REGION
            The Committee notes the efforts taken by the Department of Tourism for promotion of tourism in the North-Eastern
 States.  It also notes that a nation wide special campaign 'paradise unexplored' has also been launched for promoting and
 marketing of various tourism products in the North Eastern region.  However, the Committee is constrained to note  that
 inspite of the concerted efforts of Department of Tourism  the flow of tourist traffic to these regions are marginal and is
 being largely hampered by Restricted Area Permit, Protected Area Permit and Inner Line Permit and lack of suitable
 infrastructure and other related facilities and amenities in various tourists places in the region.  The Committee observes that
 inspite of incurring a lot of expenditure for development of tourism in North-East region no perceptible improvement is
 seen in the ground situation and Department of Tourism has to put lot of efforts to fully explore the tourism potential in the
 Region.  Further, the revenue earned from tourism are not estimated by the States.  The Committee, therefore, recommends
 the Department of Tourism to prioritize the projects in consultation with the State Government for upgradation and creation
 of infrastructure and destinations in the Region besides addressing the major issues for the growth and development of
 tourism in the Region like mandatory permit system, law and order situation and lack of private investment etc.  This way,
 the Department of Tourism will be able to bring the North Eastern Region in the mainstream of national development
 agenda.
