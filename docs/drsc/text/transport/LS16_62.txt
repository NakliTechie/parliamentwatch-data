RE
PORT
           
            The Major Port Trust Act, 1963 provides for constitution of Board of Trustees of all the major ports in the
 country and vests the administration, control and management of these ports and for matters connected therewith in
 such authorities.  11 major ports are governed under this Act.
 
2.         The Major Port Trusts (Amendment) Bill, 2001 seeks to enable corporatisation of the existing major ports in the
 country.  Corporatisation of major ports would involve abolition of the present system of trustees by de-notification of
 major port from the purview of Major Port Trusts Act, 1963, and after sorting out several related aspects convert the
 Port Trusts into a company under the Companies Act, 1956.  At present, the major ports in the country function under
 the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963. 
 
3.         The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill inter-alia states that the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963, needs to
 be amended to provide for vesting undertaking of the major ports in successor companies; define scope of transfer of
 assets and liabilities, protect the rights of successor companies in relation to the licenses, etc., granted to the erstwhile
 Major Port Trusts, vest the land and waterfront in the Central Government, provide consideration for transfer of assets
 and liabilities; protect the guarantees enjoyed by the port trusts and lay down the terms and conditions of leasing the
 land and waterfront to the companies.  Certain consequential amendments are required to the Indian Port Act, 1908.

4.         The Ministry of Shipping *cited the following reasons for corporatisation of major ports and introduction of the
 Bill: - 
 
REASONS FOR CORPORATISATION
 
4.1       11 major ports in India are autonomous bodies under the Ministry set up under Major Port Trusts Act, 1963. 
 These ports have made constant endeavour to improve the conditions and facilities provided by them.  In the fiercely
 competitive world of today, ports face new challenges and can no longer afford to be complacent.   A structural change
 is therefore required for improvement of functioning of the ports. 
 
4.2       The Ministry further felt that the provisions of the Act do not allow operation of services by the ports on
 commercial lines as approval of the Central Government is required in a majority of decisions. A number of measures
 taken by the Central Government in the recent past have resulted in improving the operational efficiency at major ports
 to some extent. However, under the restrictive ambit of the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963 the major ports are unable to
 operate in market-oriented situations and obtain flexibility of commercial operations. Even after delegating more
 powers and making some amendments to the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963, from time to time, the basic objective of
 offering efficient and satisfactory services to the port users has not been achieved fully.   A structural reorganization of
 Major Port Trusts is called for to: (i) provide the necessary independence of authority and decision-making; (ii)
 coagulate investment; (iii) execute projects speedily; and (iv) manage operations with professional finesse.
 Accordingly, the required structural re-organization of the major ports is sought to be achieved through their
 corporatisation.

4.3       A profit oriented commercial organization operating in a competitive environment will strive to minimize costs
 and improve services. These are necessary measures if the organization is to retain and expand its operations while
 earning a satisfactory return on the investment, without compromising on the goal to improve efficiency and thereby,
 reap the benefits of lower cost and improved services for importers and exporters. The other advantages expected from
 commercial activities to be carried out by corporate bodies are operational freedom and flexibility enjoyed by the
 management in taking commercial and investment decisions and their quick response to the market conditions or the
 need of the trade. At present, as Major Port Trusts all that they are required is to prepare annual statement of income
 and expenditure, which merely shows operating surplus. They do not maintain accounts on commercial lines; nor do
 they maintain profit and loss accounts. Corporatisation of major ports would make profit and loss statements mandatory
 and will lead to establishment of commercial accounting practices, creation of profit centers and drawing up of balance
 sheets.  The Ministry feels that this would enable correct valuation of the assets of the ports in the long term, corporate
 bodies would eventually contribute revenue to the Government in the form of corporate tax. Further, formation of
 corporate bodies will increasingly compel their management to tap capital market to raise funds like any other
 corporate entity. This will also make it possible for the Government to measure their performance in terms of the return
 on investment and eventually enable divestment of Government holding.
 
4.4       The Ministry has further stated that certain consequential amendments were required to the Indian Ports Act,
 1908. The Indian Ports Act, 1908, was applicable to major ports. However, certain sections of this Act such as Sections
 6(1)(j), 6(1)(jja), 6(1)(kk), 33(1), 33(3), 34,35,46 and 47 were not made applicable to the major ports. The corporatised
 major ports would function out of the ambit of MPT Act, 1963. Therefore, these provisions of Indian Ports Act, 1908,
 have to be made applicable to the corporatised ports to enable them to fix rates for use of moorings, any boat, hawser,
 piers, jetties, landing places, wharves, quays, warehouses, sheds etc., levy of port dues and variation thereof, charge for
 pilotage and other services etc. In other words, making these provisions of Indian Ports Act, 1908, applicable to
 corporatised major ports would help them in carrying out their day-to-day business.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
 
7.         The Para 2 of the Statement of Objects and Reasons explains the necessity of the introduction and objectives of
 the Major Port Trusts (Amendment) Bill, 2001.  Para 2 reads as under:
 
“Under the restricted ambit of Major Port Trusts Act, 1963, major ports are unable to operate in market-oriented
 situations and ensure flexibility of commercial operations.  It is, therefore, necessary to change the work culture
 at the major ports through their structural reorganisation to provide the necessary independence of authority and
 decision-making; encourage private investment; execute projects speedily; and manage operations with
 professional finesse.  The required structural reorganisation of major ports is sought to be achieved through their
 corporatisation”.
 
8.            Throwing further light on the necessity of introduction of Bill, the Secretary, Ministry of Shipping in his
 deposition before the Committee in the meeting held on the 22
nd January 2002, explained the objectives of Bill as
 under:
 
“The point I am trying to stress is, today, there is a need for being very flexible, for commercial-minded, and so
 on.  Under the corporate structure, ports will no longer be under the Indian Port Trusts Act.  There will be a
 Companies Act.  There will be a company wholly owned by the Government.  The companies will be more
 flexible; they will be more economically viable and more commercially attuned to the needs of the country.  We
 are trying to move away from the Indian Port Trusts Act.  We are going to the Companies Act.  As a result, we

 feel that the net worth of the shareholders of the ports will be increased, and the shareholders of the ports are the
 people of India.  You may ask if this cannot be achieved under the Major Port Trusts Act.  That is a good
 question.  The point is, under the Act, there are various restrictive provisions that require the port to come to the
 Government to look into those small matters.  It would change, perhaps, the character of the Port Trust Act.  It
 is possible easier to achieve under the Companies Act”.
 
“….The most significant point, and my last point, is, under the present dispensation, ports do not have a
 commercial system of accounting.  They do not have a profit and loss account; they do not have a balance
 sheet.  As a result, we have a system under which it is difficult to assess their performance, and it is difficult for
 them to assess the financial market.  Today, if they want money, they use their own sources.  The ports should
 be able to access the financial market.  This they can do if they are reorganized under the Companies Act.  They
 are required to be kept within the financial discipline.  They must have a balance sheet; they must have a profit
 and loss account, and we know exactly where they stand.
 
Under this background, in order to make ports more autonomous, more functional, economically more viable
 and better able to cope with the new commercial environment under which they find themselves to bring about a
 situation in which major ports can be corporatised and can become port companies under the Companies
 Act….”
 
9.         The Committee notes that the major objective of the Bill is to give more autonomy and flexibility to the existing
 major ports by corporatising and converting them into companies under the Companies Act, 1956.  The Committee,
 however, is of the view that these objectives could have been achieved by introducing some changes in the present
 Major Port Trust Act, 1963, and there was no need for corporatisation of ports which was proposed to be achieved by
 the Major Port Trusts (Amendment) Bill, 2001. Almost all the major ports right from the beginning have been earning
 profits that too working without any budgetary support or grant from Government of India. 
 
10.       The Committee has also strong objection to the term “to change work culture” appearing in the Para 2 of the
 Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill as it does not reflect good upon Indian working ethos.  The Committee is
 of the view that the term “improve the functioning” could have been used instead.            
 
VIEWS OF THE EMPLOYEES UNIONS/FEDERATION OF DIFFERENT MAJOR PORTS
 
11.       The Committee visited Mumbai, Goa, Chennai and Visakhapatnam on 9

 views of the different port unions/federations of Mumbai Port Trust, Mormugao Port, Chennai Port Trust and
 Vishakhapatnam Port Trust as well as representatives of managements of these ports.  The Committee, in its meetings
 held at Delhi on 20

 Unions/ Federation of different major port trusts on the Bill i.e. All India Port and Dock Workers’ Federation; Indian
 National Port and Dock Workers’ Federation; All India Port and Dock Workers’ Federation (Workers); Water
 Transport Workers’ Federation of India; Port, Dock & Waterfront Workers’ Federation of India. The representatives of
 different port unions/ federations opposed the Bill mainly on the following grounds:-
(i)     There is no justification and necessity for amending the Major Port Trusts Act.  It is a unique legislation. 
 In this legislation, various interests are directly represented. The Major Ports are performing very
 well despite reduction in the number of employees.  The workers have worked hard over the rated
 capacity determined by the Government.  Private sector is also represented on the boards of major
 ports.  
(ii)    National security will be at stake, if foreign companies will be allowed to gain control over the ports. 
 After corporatisation of ports, there will be no control to restrict the movement of ships of any
 country including that of the enemy country.  
(iii)
  The proposed amendment stipulates that the service of the port employees would be transferred to the
 new company that would be formed.  Under the new provisions of the proposed bill, the new
 company will be at liberty to terminate the services whenever they would like to do so.

VIEWS OF REPRESENTATIVES OF THE MANAGEMENTS OF THE DIFFERENT MAJOR PORTS
 
12.       The Port administration supported the Bill mainly on the following grounds:-
 
(i)                 Under the Major Port Trusts Act, major ports are corporate bodies having perpetual succession.  The
 transfer of the Port Trust into the regime of Companies Act after corporatisation will radically change
 the overall system and procedures, rules & regulations under which major ports operate today.  Though
 major ports work as a commercial enterprise, they do not have that functional autonomy or operational
 freedom, which is required for a commercial entity in a globally competitive environment. 
 Corporatisation will bring flexibility and autonomy for that purpose.  In the present set up, port cannot
 apply differential tariffs.  This adversely affects bigger importers/exporters who do not get the incentives
 under the corporatised set-up.  The Boards are likely to become professional bodies.  Upon
 corporatisation, there will be Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association.  This would give
 an option to change the Trust to Company.  This would certainly help the port to improve its
 performance and ensure more accountability and more customer-orientation etc.
(ii)
                The proposed amendment to the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963 will give more powers to the Board. 
 Generally, Major Port Trusts function as a controlled mechanism.  The Companies Act gives total
 freedom to run the affairs of a company in a commercial way.
(iii)              The company will enjoy total monopoly.  It will not be necessary to go to Tariff Authority for Major
 Ports (TAMP).  At present, ports are not allowed to operate differential tariffs.  After corporatisation,
 this impediment will be removed.  Even the minor ports set up in Gujarat are enjoying such freedom. 
 They work in corporatised manner.  Ports must function according to the prevailing economic situation,
 which will ultimately create market demand.

SECTION 111C(1)
 
20.       Section 111 C contains five subsections.  Subsection 1 of the Section 111C provides that the Board of Trustees
 of a major port shall be dissolved and its entire capital will be vested in the successor company. 
 
21.       After dissolution of the Board of Trustees of the major ports, as envisaged in the Subsection 1 of Section 111C,
 with regard to composition of Board of the successor company, the Committee desired to know whether there will be
 any representation of various interests in the Board of Successor Company, as at present in all the Boards of major
 ports under the Major Port Trust Act, 1963 i.e. all the concerned such as representation of workers, shippers, State
 Government concerned, customs and excise etc. are represented.    The Secretary, Ministry of Shipping, in his
 deposition before the Committee in its meeting held on the 21

 stated as under:
 
“……The shareholders will have a right to name the persons on the Board of Directors, but apart from that, it is
 the policy of the Government to involved labour also in the management process.  The hon. Members
 apprehension is whether the approach, attitude or composition of the Board; the representation of different
 interests like shippers, workers etc. will still continue or not.  I want to say that it has not changed at all.  The
 Government will only try to make some changes while constituting the Board.  I have already said very clearly
 that the policy of the Government is that labour should be involved in the management and most Government
 companies do have labour on the Board…”
 
22.       The Committee is unable to understand that when the Government of India have the policy of associating labour
 in the management, then why this intention of the Government has not been reflected in the Bill itself. The Committee
 is of the view that there must be a specific provision in the present Bill ensuring the representation of labour of the port
 and the State Government where the port is located in the Boards of management of the successor companies. 
 Therefore, the Committee recommends that a provision should be included in this subsection, which may be as under:
 
“Provided that labour employed in the port and the government of the state where the port is located be
 represented in the management of the successor company as prescribed.”

SECTION 111C (5)
 
29.       Sub-section5 of the section 111C is as under:
“Every employee serving under the Board of Trustees of the major port specified in the notification

30.       While considering the provisions of the sub-section 5, the Committee took note of the apprehensions expressed
 by different employees’ unions/federations with regard to the likely effect on the terms and conditions of services of the
 employees after the ports are corporatised and converted into the successor companies.
           
31.       The representatives of the employees unions drew the attention of the Committee towards the schedule of
 employees under Section 23 of Major Port Trusts Act, 1963 which is as under:
 
Schedule of Board’s staff – A Board shall, from time to time, prepare and sanction a Schedule of the
 employees of the Board whom it deems necessary and proper to maintain for the purposes of this Act
 and such Schedule and shall indicate therein the designations and grade of employees and the salaries,
 fees and allowances which are proposed to be paid to them. 
           
32.       As per the schedule, the Board shall, from time to time, prepare and sanction a schedule of the employees of the
 Board.  The representatives of the Employees’ Unions/Federations of the major ports were of the view  that if the
 power available under the schedule is transferred to the successor company, the company may take benefit thereof and
 terminate the services of employees after one year, which may not constitute a change in the terms and conditions of
 service and for that the permission of the Central Government will not be required as provided in the proviso to this
 sub-section. 
 
33.       The Committee invited the attention of Secretary, Ministry of Shipping to the apprehensions expressed by the
 representatives of employees’ unions/federations and sought clarification in this regard.   In reply, during his evidence

st January 2003, the Secretary inter-alia stated as under:
 
“…Coming to section 23, I would like to say that the purpose of this section is that the Port shall, from time to
 time, prepare a list.  But it does not mean that the Port can arbitrarily reduce the number of employees.  The
 intention of this section is merely to allow the Port to determine what kind of function different employees will
 perform.  The Port is given freedom to re-designate the people.  Every port does not use the excuse of trying to

 34.       The Committee is not satisfied with the provisions contained in sub-section 5 of Section 111C.  The
 Committee feels that the apprehension expressed by the representatives of employees’ unions/federations of the

 redundant and the interest of the employees will not be protected.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
 the term “unless and until his employment in the successor company is terminated or until his tenure,

2.         The Committee considered the Major Port Trusts (Amendment) Bill, 2001 clause-by-clause.  The representatives
 of the Ministry of Law and Justice, Legislative Department also clarified some points raised by the Members.  After
 some discussion, the Members suggested amendments to Sections 111C(1), 111C(5) and 111F(1) of Clause 2 of the

 Bill. 
2.1       As regards Section 111C(1) of Clause 2 of the Bill, the Committee recommended that enabling provision should
 be incorporated in the Bill itself so that employees of the ports as well as the State Government where the port is
 situated could be represented in the Board of Management of the successor company.
2.2            Considering the job security and other financial interests of the employees of the ports after their
 corporatisation, the Committee also suggested amendments to Section 111C(5) of Clause 2 of the Bill.  The Committee
 was of the view that the words “solely or mainly” and the terms “unless and until his employment in the successor
 company is terminated or until his tenure, remuneration or terms and conditions of service are duly altered by the
 successor company” and “provided that the tenure, remuneration and terms and conditions of service of any such
 employee shall not be altered to his disadvantage without the previous sanction of the Central Government”, were
 unnecessary and could be used against the interests of the employees by successor company and, therefore, be deleted
 from the said Section.
2.3       As regards Section 111F(1), Committee was of the view that manner of valuation of assets should be fixed by
 the Government before the entire capital of the ‘Board of Trustees’ are transferred to a ‘successor company’.  The
 provision regarding valuation of assets, therefore, should be inserted in the Bill either through a Schedule framed by the
 Government or as prescribed by rules from time to time.
3.
                  Clause 3 and Clause 4 of the Bill were adopted without any change. 
3.1       The Committee was also of the view that after corporatisation, if there are any structural changes, that should be
 subject to the approval of Parliament.
4.         Clause 1, the Enacting Formula and the Title were adopted with some changes of consequential nature. 
5.         The Committee objected to the words “change the work culture” used in the Statement of Objects and Reasons
 of the Bill since it reflected the entire work ethos in Public Sector Organisations in poor light.  Therefore, the
 Committee was of the view that words “improving the functioning of ports” could have been used instead.
