INTRODUCTION
I, the Chairman, Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism
and Culture, having been authorised by the Committee, do hereby present on its behalf this One
Hundred Seventy Second Report on ‘Development of Tourism in the North-East Region’.
2. India’s North-East has the potential to become an attractive tourist destination because of its
unique geographical distribution and peculiar flora and fauna. However, this tourism potential is not
properly developed and exploited. The Committee, in continuation to its special focus on promotion
of Tourism in India, took up the State of Jammu and Kashmir in the first phase with its l40
th Report
and now the North-Eastern Region as a second step. Accordingly, the Committee undertook a study
visit to Guwahati, Aizawl, Shillong and Agartala from 9
th to 15 th February, 2011. Regarding other States
of the region, the Committee interacted with the State Government and other agencies connected with
the tourism sector at Delhi.
3. The Committee emphasized on the tourism infrastructure, trained man-power, development and
maintenances of national highways in the Region; increasing air connectivity and expansion/modernization
of airports and protection and preservation of ancient monuments for the enhancement of tourist flow
to the Region. The Committee was apprised of the difficulties that came in the way of promotion of
tourism in the Region such as restriction of entry of tourist, poor infrastructure, lack of transport
facility, poor security and road-rail-air network etc. The Committee, therefore, decided to present a
report focusing mainly on these aspects.
4. The Committee expresses its gratitude to the officers of the Ministry of Tourism, Ministry of
Culture, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, Ministry of Civil Aviation, Ministry of Shipping,
Ministry of DONER, Airports Authority of India, Air India, National Highways Authority of India,
Archaeological Survey of India, Inland Waterway Authority of India, North Eastern Council and
Officers of State Government Departments of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Nagaland, Manipur, Meghalaya,
Mizoram, Sikkim and Tripura, Tour-Travel and Hotelier Association of these States and others for their
cooperation in furnishing the required feedback and information in connection with the examination of
the subject and also for making valuable suggestions for improving the tourism scenario in the State.
5. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its meeting held on the 11
th August, 2011.

REPORT
North Eastern region of the country has a vast tourism potential in the form of wildlife
sanctuaries, adventure tourism assets, diverse cultural heritage, multifaceted fairs and festivals,
world famous Buddhist Monasteries, etc. that remain largely unexplored. They are very rich in bio-
diversity and some of these bio-resources are unique in the sense that they are endemic. The region
is proud of with ancient treasures and archaeological wealth. The potential of the region has been
repeated often, especially its great variety. The niches that the North East can offer range from
attractions in tribal lifestyle, heritage, tea garden, golf, monasteries at the one end and the more
commonly offered eco-tourism, adventure and wildlife on the other end. North East India is the
starting point of South East Asia. There are natural and cultural affinities between the region and
South East Asia, China, Bangladesh and Myanmar. 96 per cent of the region is bound by
international borders. India’s “Look East Policy” holds potential for the region to break out of its
landlocked borders and engage in cultural, economic and tourism related interactions with its
international neighbours. With the right investment and policies, North Eastern Region has all the
potential to emerge as “Paradise Unexplored.
2. To look into the aspects relating to Tourism in North East, the Committee decided to take
up the issues relating to development of tourism in the North-East Region in detail for consideration
and report. The Committee conducted on-the-spot study and visited Guwahati (Assam), Aizwal
(Mizoram), Shillong (Meghalaya) and Agartala (Tripura) during the month of February, 2011. The
Committee had detailed interaction with the transport operators, hoteliers and other such
stakeholders in the private sector as well as with the Ministries of Tourism, Civil Aviation, RTH,
Culture, Shipping, DoNER and organizations like ASI,  NHAI, Airports Authority of India, BRO, Air
India, IWAI and the State departments of Assam, Mizoram, Meghalaya and Tripura in connection
with the development of tourism there. The Committee during its meeting in Delhi interacted with
the representatives of the State Governments of Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim
and various other Government agencies.
3. The Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for promotion of tourism. The State
Governments of the region, Ministry of DONER and the North Eastern Council, Shillong are
partners.
4. During the deliberations, the Committee was informed that Ministry of Tourism has drawn
up some perspective tourism plan for each of the North Eastern States for 20 years. These plans
have been sent to the respective State Governments for development of tourism in their States.
Projects are prioritized in consultation with the concerned State Governments. Based on the
decision taken in prioritization meetings, the State Governments approach Ministry of Tourism with
detailed proposals. Ministry of Tourism sanctions such projects after due scrutiny, under the
existing guidelines.
5. The Committee was informed that DoNER coordinates with the Ministry of Tourism and
all the line Ministries for promotion and development of the various sectors in the North East.
There is North Eastern Council Regional Planning body which is coordinating with various
Ministries for the development of various activities particularly regarding tourism. NEC along with
the Ministry of Tourism had sponsored a project to the TCS. With this study, all the regional
planning for the tourism of the States has been integrated. Plan for development of various tourist
circuits have been framed. The Ministry of DONER also has a Vision 2020 document. On the basis
of this, certain regional tourist circuits have been formed which include monuments, historical
places, explorations and adventure tourism.

Incentive for setting up Hotel Industries
17. The Committee was informed that Hotel industries are entitled for capital investment subsidy
to the tune of 30% for Hotels (Not below 2 Star Category) under the North East Industrial
Investment Promotion Policy (NEIIPP), 2007. Five year tax holiday is available for creation of
accommodation in the districts close to World Heritage Sites, provided they are constructed and
start functioning between the period from 1
st April 2008 to 31 st March 2013.
18. Further the Committee was further informed that the Ministry of Tourism, at present, only
gives financial assistance for accommodation in North-East and Jammu and Kashmir. In the rest
of the country, this sector has been left open to the private sector. Capacity is being built. Over
a period of time, i.e. in the last 4-5 years, the accommodation is coming up, the local capacity
is being built. The Ministry of Commerce and Industry also gives incentives for construction of
hotels above two star category in the North-East. The subsidy on capital investment and some tax
benefit is also provided.
19. Rural Tourism: Rural Tourism Scheme is the 10
th Plan initiative of the Ministry of
Tourism. It aims at improving the rural economy by promoting traditional rural art, craft textile,
culture etc. based livelihood through tourism process. Ministry grants Central Financial Assistance
(CFA) for creating infrastructure and for human resource development in  rural areas for developing
sustainable tourism in the form of rural tourism projects. However, this opportunity has remained
largely unexplored by the State Governments. During the course of evidence the Committee was
informed by representative of Arunachal Pradesh that:
“...We are trying to promote the villages especially in remote areas where we are not able
to provide Government accommodation or hotel accommodation for that matter. There are

7
three or four districts which are quite remote. There we are trying to promote home stays.
Although we are not giving any financial aid for home stays, we are bringing them down;
we are showing and explaining to them how they can go about home stays. We are also
helping them arrange cultural shows and revive certain art forms, song forms and dance
forms — that is done by the Cultural Department — and for that we bring them down and
take them out. They are paid a nominal amount, but it is sort of a beginning of an incentive.
A decision has been taken that all assets, as far as accommodation is concerned, would be
leased out to private entrepreneurs. We have leased out 11 assets of ours so far and there
are another 11 in the pipeline, which we hope to lease out this year. This is in the form
of either wayside amenities, cafeterias or accommodation.”

Relaxation of norms for recognition  of service providers in the North Eastern States
48. To meet the need to increase registration coverage for service providers, the Ministry of
Tourism recently liberalized its guidelines. For the North-Eastern region the relaxation is even more.
For instance, the requirement of qualified staff has been relaxed from the existing two qualified
staff to one with the other being owner who would be treated as a qualified staff. Secondly, the
requirement of office space has also been reduced from the existing 250 Sq. ft. to 100 Sq. ft. in
hilly areas. North-Eastern Region has been considered as hilly area. Thirdly, the chartered
Accountant’s Certificate regarding turn over of the firm in Rupee earnings would be treated as
Foreign Exchange earnings, provided there is documentary evidence that such earnings are from
inbound tour operations only.

Special Accelerated Road Development Programme in the North-Eastern Region (SARDP-NE):
73. The Committee was informed that the Ministry has formulated a special programme for
accelerated road development in region covering 9740 km length of National Highways, State roads
and GS roads. This programme is called as ‘Special Accelerated Road Development Programme in
North-East’ (SARDP-NE).
• To connect all State capital with improved/upgraded National Highways.
• To provide connectivity to all the 88 District Headquarters of the NE Region. Since 49
District Headquarters are already connected or are being connected under phase ‘A’ of
SARDP-NE and Arunachal Pradesh Package of Roads and Highways, the remaining 39

18
District Headquarters, are proposed to be connected to 2-lane National Highways
through improved State roads under Phase ‘B’ SARDP-NE.
• To improve connectivity to the neighboring countries.
• To provide road connectivity to backward and remote area of NE region to boost
socio-economic development.
• To improve some of the important roads of strategic importance.
74. The programme has been divided into 3 parts (i) Phase ‘A’, (ii) Arunachal Pradesh Package
of Roads and Highways and (iii) Phase ‘B’. Phase ‘A’ of the programme, will cover 4099 km, out
of which 2041 km length is National Highways and 2058 km length is of State roads and GS
roads. The estimated cost is Rs.21,769 crore. The target for completion of roads under this phase
is by the year 2013-14. The Arunachal Pradesh Package of Roads and Highways was approved for
implementation by the Cabinet on 09.01.2009. The Package covers 2319 km, out of which 1472
km length is National Highways and 847 km length is State road, GS roads and Strategic roads.
The estimated cost is Rs.11,703 crore. The target for completion of roads under this Phase is by
the year 2014-15.
75. Phase ‘B’ comprises 35 roads with an aggregate length of 3723 km. Approval to undertake
DPR preparation has been given by the Cabinet. The estimated cost is about Rs.17,000 crore.
Implementation of Phase ‘B’ will be taken up during the Twelfth Five Year Plan as per the
suggestion of the Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS
100. North-Eastern Region consists of 8 States viz. Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur,
Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura. This region is full of bewildering
cultural diversity, magical beauty in terms of nature, wild life, biodiversity, etc. Each State
is, in fact, a travellers’ paradise with immense tourism potential which unfortunately
remains unutilized. It has been, therefore, rightly termed as ‘Paradise Unexplored’. The
Committee has, however, noted that NE States are far behind as compared to other States
in terms of tourist arrivals, both domestic and foreign. State of Assam is shade better
than other States in this region leaving lot of scope for improvement in attracting
tourists.
101. The Committee notes with satisfaction that there was no dearth of funds for this
purpose. Union Ministry of Tourism has released amount ranging from 15% to 19% of the
total budget of the Ministry which is well above the mandatory provision of 10% for the
NE States. The Committee, however, has noted that funds for Centrally sponsored Schemes/
State Plans have not been released uniformally amongst all the NE States. The Committee
found that no funds were released under those schemes for Manipur, Meghalaya and
Mizoram during the year 2010-11. For the State of Tripura an amount of Rs.1.19 crore was
released for Centrally sponsored scheme during this period. The Committee recommends
that Ministry of Tourism should ensure that proper proposals, UCs, etc. are received from
the States in time so that funds are released to them without much delay.
102. The Committee noted that a number of projects have been taken up for developing
tourist circuit and other such projects. However, the Committee finds that some of those
projects were taken up long back and are yet to be completed. Union Ministry of Tourism
should be in touch with the State Governments to help them get the projects completed on
priority basis.
103. The Committee during its visit found that Neer Mahal in Tripura could be developed
as an attractive tourist spot as it holds immense potential. However, the water levels in the
lake is very low, particularly during winter just which is the tourist season. It is learnt that
Government of Tripura has taken some action for desilting of Rudra Sagar lake. The
Committee is, however, of the opinion that efforts of State Government alone may not be
sufficient. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Tourism should
extend financial help to develop the lake as one of the tourist destination. This effort will
also be able to create employment to a large number of people inhabiting the area. Tourist
guest houses, house boats, water sports, adventure sports and rowing facilities may be added
to attract tourists. Union Government may also prepare a comprehensive master plan to
develop Neer Mahal, in consultation with the State Government.
104. The Committee has observed that most of the states are not geared up for the
conventional tourism in respect of accommodation - affordable hotels and guest houses.
They do not have good and affordable accommodation, both in terms of numbers as well as
quality. This was also evident from field trips to these States. There is no Five Star Hotel
in NER till date. The Committee notes that Government of India has launched the scheme
for capital subsidy of 30% for hotels (not below 2-star category) with effect from 1st April,
2008 for a period of five years. However, this scheme has not been successful in this region
in achieving the desired results. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry
should impress upon the State Governments to offer new initiatives for the creation of
quality accommodation to the visiting tourists. Central assistance may be extended for
achieving this goal.

classification of cities in the NER and they demanded that the cities in the North-Eastern

could be considered for holding business conventions by corporates and PSUs. Ministry of
Tourism should take up the matter with concerned Ministry and inform the Committee.

development of various tourism activities like hotels, transportation etc. may be extended
to the North-East area. Tax holidays, as in the case of other industries, may be provided
to the hoteliers of the North-Eastern States. The existing high luxury taxes and pollution
charges may be revisited with a view to making these affordable to attract tourists from all
categories.

unexploited. The Committee is of the view that the scheme for development of rural
tourism prepared by the Ministry of Tourism has not been able to attract much proposals
from NE States. The Committee finds that only Government of Nagaland has submitted
some proposal for development of rural tourism. The Committee recommends that Ministry
of Tourism should consult the NE States and if necessary modify the schemes so that the
proposals are received from those States and funds earmarked therefor are utilized.
108. The Committee is of the view that there is need to promote helicopter operations in
NER to provide the connectivity to inaccessible areas having tourism potential. The

heliports with a view to promote heli tourism there. The Committee wants the Ministry of

a spurt in helicopter accidents in the NE region. This may certainly discourage the tourists.

would like to draw attention to the recommendations contained in its 169

“The Committee recommends that the entire gamut of helicopter operations in India
may be reviewed and adequate/appropriate steps may be taken to ensure safer
helicopter travel. The Committee recommends that guidelines/regulations for take
off/landing of helicopters during low visibility should not only be strengthened but
enforced strictly.” (Para 39)

destination and the facilities accordingly may be planned. However, the ecological fragility

114. Promotion of the North Eastern Region as major tourist destinations would depend
on the air connectivity to these places. Air connectivity assumes great importance in these
areas because of poor rail/road connectivity. These areas remain cut-off from rest of the
country during monsoon season especially because of the peculiar hilly terrain and climatic
conditions. During the deliberations the Committee was informed by the stakeholders in
Guwahati, Aizawal, Shillong and Agartala that the flights in this region get cancelled
frequently and 532 flights were cancelled in 2010 due to either adverse weather conditions,
air force operations or lack of adequate landing facility at the airports in NE States. They
emphasized the need for improvement in air connectivity to the North-Eastern Region and
regular direct flights to Delhi.
115. The Committee finds that landing of aircrafts in the comparatively small airfields is
the major constraint for air traffic in the region during the winter and monsoon seasons.
The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take steps to ensure
that night landing facilities and difficult weather landing equipments are needed to be
installed at the airports in NE regions to avoid frequent flight diversions and cancellations.

(AAI) and Air Force, regular commercial flights are cancelled or diverted/delayed leading to
avoidable inconvenience to the passengers and loss of revenue. The Committee is of the
considered view that under normal circumstances the regular commercial flights should not
be disturbed due to Air Force sorties, unless it is unavoidable to do so.
120. The Committee found that many airport projects were being carried out in this
region. While appreciating the development works being carried out by the Airports
Authority of India, it desires that delayed projects at various airports of the North-Eastern
States be expedited and other ones completed within the scheduled time frame. The
Committee also reiterates the need for early transfer of the Lengpui airport of Mizoram to
the Airports Authority of India so that comprehensive airport development can be carried
out.
121. The Committee finds that delay in the environmental clearance has been one of the
major reasons for the delay in starting as well as completion of airport projects. For some
of the projects, environmental clearance is still awaited. The Committee recommends that
the Ministry of Civil Aviation should take up the matter with the Ministry of Environment
and Forest to expedite the necessary clearances.
122. The Committee has observed that the North eastern region has early sunrise and
sunset. About two hours early sun rise has an impact on the biological clock of the people
in the region and if this early hours be utilized for early operations of aircrafts to other

The Committee recommends that Airports Authority of India may take steps to ensure that
Agartala airport may be provided with night halt facilities so that early morning flights
could start operation within and out of the Region. The Committee, therefore, also
recommends that Guwahati and Agartala airports should be developed as hub by providing
hanger for parking of Aircrafts which is a pre-eminent requirement for promoting intra-
region connectivity. The airline operators then may be instructed to park their aircrafts at
Guwahati and Agartala so that they can start early morning operations utilizing the early
sunlight conditions.

report on tourism promotion in Jammu and Kashmir, regarding the shortage of ATR pilots
with Air India. Due to same constraints, the North Eastern region is demanding for ATR

124. The Committee finds that one of the major impediments in development of North

127. The Committee found that the condition of roads in all the NE States was pitiable.
A number of National Highway projects were running far behind the schedule, delays being

submissions. The Committee is of the view that development of roads in the North Eastern
region is necessary not only for promotion of tourism in the region but also is of strategic
importance for the nation.
128. The Committee appreciates the problem encountered in the construction of National
Highways in North Eastern region particularly on account of climatic conditions, topography,
hilly terrain including shortage of manpower. Forced stoppage of work due to climate
conditions naturally results not only delay in completion but also in under-utilization of
funds. Unfortunately, this in turn leads to reduction in plan outlay by the Ministry of
Finance and Planning Commission. The Committee, therefore, emphasizes the need for
providing special guidelines in respect of North Eastern region in keeping with these
peculiar problems and conditions to ensure that funds are available in time for the work
relating to National Highways in the region.
129. The Committee feels that some kind of special procedures may be devised for the
region. Funds may be put in non-lapsable category as is being done for other such areas
of the country. Cost-norms of the project also need to be made state specific. For land

acquisition, environmental and other clearances etc. single-window facility should be put in

to the people working on these projects.
130. The Committee feels concerned to see the poor maintenance of roads and fails to
understand to have the kind of roads/National Highways in hilly terrain that got damaged
almost every year due to vagaries of the adverse climatic conditions. Why can’t we have
concrete roads in place of bitumen roads in this highly rain-fed area? The Committee was
given to understand that the bitumen roads are more durable/suitable in such difficult
terrain because these roads are capable of adjusting themselves in case of small land slides,
etc. whereas the concrete roads tend to get damaged and its repair will be difficult.
131. The Committee also has noticed during the deliberations that the utilization of funds
were done in a haste manner in order to meet the budgetary stipulations which ultimately
compromises the quality and the standard procedure of constructions. Ultimately, the whole
exercise becomes futile.
132. The Committee desired that involving the local bodies in the planning for the
development of National-Highways passing through the towns and villages with a view to
avoid the damages to the National Highways by having proper drainage system, should be
considered.
133. The Committee is also of the view that prescribing the fixed period of 5 years for

Committee, therefore, feels that the repair guidelines also need to be modified for this
region to provide that whenever there is damage to the NH, the repair work should be
taken up immediately. As regards the damage to the National Highways passing through the
towns and villages due to poor drainage, the Committee, recommends that the agencies
engaged in planning and development of NH should consult the local bodies to know the
patches of the roads where the National Highways get generally damaged due to poor
drainage, etc.

(NH31A) is the lifeline of Sikkim that connects it to the rest of the country through West

activity from Gangtok to Nathula and some intermediate stretch between Sevoke and

4 laning of NH-44 that exposes its lack of seriousness in developing an important national

136. The Committee has received complaints about adulteration of fuel in most part of

ensure that adulteration of petroleum products are stopped.
137. Because of poor road conditions, the accidents are very frequent. Adequate numbers

138. The Committee is also of the view that due to increased requirement of transport

The Committee further recommends that maintenance of coaches and provisions for
cleanliness of coaches and good quality food to the passengers in order to facilitates the
tourists, particularly in Tripura should be given priority.

143. The Committee noted that Majuli Island, a tourist destination, is under threat due

144. The Committee had seen some of the important places of tourist attraction in some
of the States. The facilities provided at various such places/sites leave much to be desired,
including their accessibility. The Committee, however, is not satisfied with the facilities and
pace of work at many of these sites. The Committee also noticed that out of the 79
monuments, only 10 places have been provided with tourist amenities, that also at the bare
minimum in many places. The role of the ASI needs to be increased in this area which does
not have a visible presence there.
145. The Committee is of the view that the Pilak and Boxanagar monuments need more
excavation works and recommends that ASI may take the necessary actions during the
current year itself, as the monuments are getting damaged. The Committee also desires
that the Committee may be informed about the preservation status of Unakoti monuments
which is the world’s largest rock cuttings. ASI should examine the damages to rock carvings
and necessary steps need to be taken to stop further damages and its repair works. The
Committee also recommends that rope way connection may be provided between
Bhubaneswari temple and Matamari in Tripura.
