A. Scheme for Modernization and Reforms through Technology in 
Public Distribution System (SMART-PDS) 
 
Recommendation (Sl. No. 5, Para No. 3.40) 
 
1.5  The Committee in their original report observed/recommended as follows:- 
 
“The Committee note that the during 2025 -26, the actual utilization of the 
funds under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for Modernization and 
Reforms through Technology in Public Distribution System (SMART -
PDS) has been at     Rs. 23.18 crore against Rs 51.00 crore at RE 2025 -
26. The reason for decrease in budget allocation during 2025 -26 at RE 
stage, the Department informed that Scheme for Modernization and 
Reforms through Technology in Public Distribution System (SMART -
PDS)” has to be onboarded on SNA SPARSH platform w.e.f. 01.07.2025. 
Due to delay in onboarding and in submission of financial proposal by 
States/UTs, funds could not be released. The Committee understand that 
the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for Modernization and Reforms through 
Technology in Public Distribution System (SMART -PDS) has been 
introduced to strengthen transparency, efficiency and accountability in the 
Public Distribution System through technology -driven reforms. The 
extension of the scheme for the period 2026 –2031 under the umbrella 
programme Scheme for Assistance in Ration Transport and Handling -
Income with Automation in PDS (SARTHAK -PDS) to strengthen backend 
interoperability, enhance data analytics, upgrade infrastructure and 
improve grievance redressal mechanisms is under consideration. 
 
The Committee emphasize that SMART -PDS is an important 
initiative aimed at strengthening transparency, efficiency and 
accountability in the Public Distribution System through technology -driven 
reforms. The Committee, therefore, recommend the Department to 
expedite the full -scale implementation of SMART -PDS across all States 
and Union Territories by strengthening coordination with State 

3 
 
Governments, ensuring timely onboarding on digital financial platforms, 
and simplifying procedures for submission of financial proposals and 
release of funds. The Committee further note that the extension of the 
scheme for the period 2026 –2031 under the umbrella programme 
SARTHAK-PDS is under consideration and urge the Government to 
expedite its finalization while incorporating measures to enhance backend 
interoperability, data analytics, infrastructure upgradation and grievance 
redressal mechanisms to ensure more efficient delivery of PDS services.” 
 
1.6  The Ministry in its action taken reply has stated as under:- 
 
“The Department of Food and Public Distribution acknowledges the 
observations and recommendations of the Hon’ble Standing Committee 
regarding expediting the full -scale implementation of the Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme “SMART-PDS”. 
 
The development and implementation of the SMART -PDS platform 
has been entrusted to the National Informatics Centre (NIC), which has 
made significant progress in developing all core modules of the system 
within the prescribed timelines. These modules include Ration Card 
Management System (RCMS), Supply Chain Management and 
Allocation, Distribution and Procurement. 
 
As per the current status of implementation, the RCMS module has 
been deployed in 16 States/UTs, while the Supply Chain and Allocation 
module, as well as Distribution module, have been implemented across 
22 and 30 States/UTs respectively. 
 
It is submitted that while the core application has been developed 
centrally, the implementation across States/UTs requires State -specific 
configuration, data migration, customization and integration with existing 
systems, which has a bearing on the pace of rollout. The Department has 
been actively coordinating with States/UTs and NIC to ensure timely 
onboarding, including facilitation of financial approvals, handholding 
support and regular review meetings. 
 
Further, with respect to the observations regarding delays in fund 
utilization, it is submitted that onboarding on SNA SPARSH platform and 
submission of financial proposals by States/UTs are key prerequisites for 
fund release. The Department has taken necessary steps to streamline 
these processes and is continuously engaging with States/UTs to 
expedite compliance. 
 
The Department is committed to ensuring full -scale implementation 
of SMART - PDS across all States/UTs in a time -bound manner, in line 
with the recommendations of the Committee. 
 
Further, the extension of the SMART -PDS scheme for the period 
2026–2031 under the umbrella SARTHAK -PDS scheme has been 
approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA). The 

4 
 
scheme aims to further strengthen backend interoperability, enhance data 
analytics capabilities, upgrade infrastructure and improve grievance 
redressal mechanisms, thereby ensuring more efficient and transparent 
delivery of PDS services.” 
 
 
1.7 The Committee note from the action taken reply that the core 
modules of SMART-PDS, namely Ration Card Management System (RCMS), 
Supply Chain Management and Allocation, Distribution and Procurement 
have been developed by NIC and implementation has progressed in 
different States/UTs. The Committee also note that extension of SMART -
PDS under the umbrella SARTHAK -PDS scheme for the period 2026 -2031 
has been approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs.  
 
The Committee while reiterating their earlier recommendation  desire 
the Department to ensure that the remaining State -specific configuration, 
data migration, integration with existing systems, onboarding on SNA -
SPARSH platform and release of funds are completed in a time -bound 
manner. The Committee may be apprised about the State/UT-wise 
implementation status, funds released/utilised and timelines for full rollout  
of SMART-PDS across the Country. 
 
 
B. Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY) 
 
Recommendation (Sl. No.  6, Para No. 3.49) 
 
1.8 The Committee in their original report observed/recommended as follows:- 
 
“The Committee note that the Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana 
(PMGKAY) has emerged as a major food security intervention for the 
poor and vulnerable sections of the country by providing free foodgrains 
to beneficiaries covered under the National Food Security Act, 2013 
(NFSA). The Committee also take note that the scheme involves a 
substantial financial commitment, with ₹2,05,250 crore allocated in BE 
2024–25, ₹2,03,000 crore in BE 2025 –26 and ₹2,27,429 crore in BE 
2026–27, reflecting the Government’s continued emphasis on food 
security. The Committee observe that the scheme currently covers about 
80.56 crore beneficiaries against the intended coverage of 81.35 crore 
persons, indicating that around 0.79 crore eligible persons are still yet to 
be identified and included under the scheme. The Committee are of the 
view that the continued reliance on 2011 Census data for determining 

5 
 
NFSA coverage may lead to exclusion of newly eligible and vulnerable 
households due to population growth, migration and socio -economic 
changes. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Government, in 
coordination with the State/UT Governments, undertake a comprehensive 
and time -bound exercise to update the beneficiary database, intensify 
identification of the remaining 0.79 crore eligible persons and periodically 
revise the coverage limits in line with updated population estimates.” 
 
1.9 The Ministry in its action taken reply has stated as under:- 
 
“The Central Government has issued advisories to all the States/Union 
Territories from time to time to identify all eligible and poor persons/ 
households including vulnerable Sections of the Society for inclusion 
under the NFSA. States undertake updation of their beneficiary database 
so that ineligible ration cards get deleted and better targeting of rightful 
beneficiaries is ensured. Thus, deletion of ineligible beneficiaries and 
addition of eligible beneficiaries under the Act is a continuous process.” 
 
1.10 The Committee had recommended a comprehensive and time -bound 
exercise to update the beneficiary database, intensify identification of the 
remaining 0.79 crore eligible persons and periodically revise the coverage 
limits in line with updated population estimates. The reply of the 
Department is general in nature and merely states that advisories have 
been issued and addition/deletion of beneficiaries is a continuous process. 
The reply does not indicate any time -bound exercise, State/UT -wise 
roadmap or any proposal for revision of coverage limits in line with updated 
population estimates. The Committee, therefore, reiterate their original 
recommendation and desire the Department to undertake a comprehensive, 
time-bound and verifiable exercise in coordination with States/UTs and 
apprise the Committee of concrete steps taken in this regard. 
 
C.  Dues and Liabilities of Food Corporation of India (FCI) 
Recommendation (Sl. No.  7, Para No. 3.53)   
1.11 The Committee in their original report observed/recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee observe that FCI supplies foodgrains to various 
Ministries/Departments for implementation of welfare schemes, including 
the Sampoorna Grameen Rozgar Yojana (SGRY) and the Mid -Day Meal 
Scheme, on a payment basis and that delays in reimbursement by the 
concerned Ministries lead to accumulation of outstanding dues for FCI. The 
Committee note that in the case of the Ministry of Human Resource 
Development (now the Ministry of Education), outstanding dues under the 

6 
 
Mid-Day Meal Scheme persist ₹205.93 crore as on 31.12.2025 
(provisional) despite periodic payments.  
 
The Committee recommend that the Department should evolve a time -
bound payment framework for all Ministries and Departments receiving 
foodgrains from FCI under welfare schemes, including mandatory timelines 
for reimbursement and periodic reconciliation of accounts. The Committee 
emphasize the importance of pursuing this matter at the highest levels, 
engaging with the concerned Ministry directly to resolve the matter in time 
bound manner.” 
 
1.12  The Ministry in its action taken reply has stated as under:- 
 
 “As per Gol guidelines, after the issue of food grains under the MDM 
Scheme, the FCI raises monthly bills on the Revenue District 
Administration/MDM Office within 10 days of the following month after the 
lifting of stocks. The District Administration is required to make payment 
within 20 days of receipt of the bill from FCI.” 
 
1.13     The Committee had recommended evolution of a time -bound 
payment framework for all Ministries/Departments receiving foodgrains 
from FCI under welfare schemes, including mandatory timelines for 
reimbursement and periodic reconciliation of accounts. The Department, in 
its reply, has only referred to the existing guidelines regarding raising of 
monthly bills and payment within 20 days by the District Administration. 
The reply does not specify the reason for  the outstanding dues of              
Rs. 205.93 crore under the Mid -Day Meal Scheme as on 31.12.2025 
(provisional), nor does it indicate any higher -level institutional mechanism 
for reconciliation and liquidation of pending dues. The Committee, 
therefore, reiterate their recommendation and desire the Department to 
evolve a robust inter -Ministerial payment and reconciliation framework and 
furnish the status of outstanding dues and prompt action to clear the same.

Recommendation (Sl. No.1, Para No. 2.19) 
 
      The Committee note that total Budget allocated to the Department of 
Food & Public Distribution is Rs. 285047.20 crore for the year 2026 -27 which 
includes Rs. 235024.58 crore for Revenue section and Rs. 50022.62 crore under 
Capital section. Budget Estimates (BE) for 2026 -27 have been kept almost at the 
level of Revised Estimates (RE) 2025-26 i.e. Rs 285047.65 Cr. As informed, large 
share of this budget support is for PMGKAY. The Committee further note that, 
Actual Expenditure (AE) under the scheme ‘Food subsidy to FCI under PMGKAY’ 
for the year 2025 -26 has been Rs 1,20,712.00 Cr till 17.02.2026 against RE 
(2025-26) of Rs. 1,53,834.55 i.e. 78.47%. The reason furnished is that the funds 
have been released upto Feb 2026 as allocated in BE 25-26. Funds amount to Rs 
8,368.90 crore is under submission for release. Remaining funds will be utilized in 
March 26 on receipt of MoF approval.  
 In this regard, the Committee observe that the near status -quo allocation 
in BE for the year 2026-27 in comparison of RE 2025-26 indicates fiscal continuity 
while the actual expenditure yet to match the allocation under RE 2025 -26. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend the Department of Food and Public 
Distribution to ensure realistic budgeting based on actual expenditure trends by 
adopting improved forecasting and expenditure planning while preparing future 
budget estimates. The Committee also underscores the importance of robust 
oversight mechanisms and continuous review processes to ensure effective 
implementation and sustained improvement across all schemes and projects. 
 
Reply of the Government 
2.2 For FY 2025 -26, actual expenditure occurred under Grant No. 15 is Rs. 
284398.88 crore against the BE of Rs. 261406.37 crore and RE of Rs. 285047.65 
crore. Hence, expenditure during the FY is 108.80% of BE and 99.77% of RE.  
  Since funds to the tune of RE 2025 -26 (99.77%) are utilized during the FY 
2025- 26, the status -quo allocation in BE 2026 -27 w.r.t. RE 2025 -26 indicating 
fiscal continuity (as observed by the committee) is maintained. 
  Further, in order to ensure realistic budgeting, all measures as defined by 
Ministry of Finance are followed. Also, during a financial year, expenditure 
monitoring is done by holding Senior Officers’ meeting. Recommendations of the 
committee are already being complied. However, lapses, if any, will be duly taken 
care of.

State Procurement Portals with the Central Food Grains Procurement Portal

within 48 hours. The Committee observe that despite expansion of DCP, 9 and 
6 States are still depend on the Centralized (Non -DCP) system for Paddy/rice

and wheat respectively. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Government intensify efforts to expand decentralized procurement to more 
States and regions to widen MSP coverage to farmers in order to fulfil its 
objective.

2.16 STORAGE OF FOODGRAINS AND AUGMENTATION OF 
STORAGE CAPACITY:      
FCI continuously assesses and monitors the storage capacity and based 
on the storage gap assessment, storage capacities are created/hired. The 
requirement of Storage capacity in FCI depends upon the level of procurement, 
requirement of buffer norms and PDS operations for Rice and Wheat 
mainly.  Storage gap is assessed in the procuring States based on the highest 
stock levels in the last three years and in the consuming States on the basis of 
4 months (6 months in case of NE regions) requirement of National Food 
Security Act (NFSA) and Other Welfare Scheme (OWS) for creation / 
construction of new godowns.  
The land acquisition for own godown is tedious task. Further, 
augmentation of storage capacity involving private entrepreneurs not only 
expedite the construction process but also reduce long term liability as the 
option to de-hire the surplus capacity in lean period exist. 
FCI hires storage capacities from CWC/ SWC/ State Agencies/ Private 
Agencies wherever required while ensuring optimum utilization of existing 
capacities. As on 01.03.2026, the Storage capacity of 483.70 LMT is available 
with Food Corporation of India and 463.40 LMT is available with State 
agencies for Central Pool stock of Foodgrains. As a result, a total covered 
capacity 947.10 LMT is available for storage of Central Pool stock of 
Foodgrains.

i. Private Entrepreneurs Guarantee (PEG) Scheme-2008/09:         
       To address Storage needs due to increasing procurement, a scheme to 
create godowns was initiated in 2008. The godowns under this scheme are 
constructed by mobilizing private investment. FCI gives 10 years guarantee on 
the godowns constructed under this Scheme at identified locations to private 
parties and 9 years for CWC & SWC. The first offer is given to CWC/SWC for

needs of the area and existing storage capacity. In Consuming States,

2.21  The Committee appreciate the initiative taken by the Food Corporation of 
India through the development of the FCI Depot Darpan Portal as a digital 
mechanism for systematic monitoring and evaluation of depot infrastructure 
and operational efficiency. The portal, with its star -rating system, structured 
self-assessment framework, and multi -layered verification process, has the 
potential to significantly enhance transparency, accountability, and 
performance benchmarking across FCI-owned and hired depots. However, the 
Committee note that the portal presently does not include provisions for 
predictive analysis, which could further strengthen planning and preventive 
maintenance of storage infrastructure. The Committee, therefore, recommend 
that the Department further strengthen the Depot Darpan Portal by 
incorporating predictive analytics and data -driven decision support systems to 
identify infrastructure deficiencies and forecast maintenance requirements. The 
star-rating results generated through the portal should be directly linked with 
time-bound action plans for infrastructure upgradation and allocation of

maintenance funds so that depots graded below acceptable standards are 
improved expeditiously.  
 
Reply of the Government 
 
2.22 The Analytics part of Depot Darpan Portal is being handled with the 
Business Analytics tool which generates reports on operational efficiency 
parameters. The portal provides multiple reports for identification of 
deficiencies and performance gaps. 
  
Further, noncompliance with infrastructure grading parameters is linked 
with predefined triggers and timelines, ensuring time -bound action for 
infrastructure upgradation and maintenance of substandard depots. 
  
The Committee has rightly emphasized the need for integrating predictive 
analytics and data -driven decision support systems to improve planning, 
maintenance and monitoring of storage infrastructure. At present, the Depot 
Darpan Portal primarily functions as an assessment and monitoring tool; 
however, its upgradation into an AI -enabled platform would enable proactive 
identification of infrastructure deficiencies and facilitate timely interventions. 
   
In this regard, it is observed that implementation of AI -based features 
would require availability of standardized and reliable historical data, 
stabilization of the existing system and necessary technical support for 
development and integration of such modules. Further, appropriate safeguards 
would also be required to ensure transparency and reliability of AI -driven 
outputs.

Recommendation (Sl. No. 14, Para No. 7.16) 
 
2.27    The Committee note that domestic production of edible oils remains 
insufficient to meet national demand, resulting in heavy reliance on imports of 
Crude Palm Oil, Refined Palm Oil, Crude Soybean Oil and Crude Sunflower Oil 
which accounted for 56–57% of total availability in recent years (2019-20 to 2023-
24). The gap between demand and supply of edible oils is met through imports. 
On the matter of controlling continuous rise in edible oil prices over the past year, 
they have been apprised that Central Government had reduced the Basic 
Customs Duty on crude edible oils (crude soybean oil, crude palm oil and crude 
sunflower oil) from 20% to 10% with effect from 30th May 2025. While 
acknowledging this step, the Committee are of the view that the continued 
demand–supply gap and price volatility in cooking oils require sustained policy 
attention.  
  The Committee, therefore recommend the Department to adopt a 
coordinated approach for enhancing domestic production of oilseeds, implement 
prudent import -export policies and strengthening market stabilization 
mechanisms. Such measures would help reduce import dependence, achieve 
self-reliance in edible oils, protect consumer interests and ensure remunerative 
returns for farmers. 
 

28 
 
Reply of the Government 
 
2.28 The management of edible oils in the country is undertaken through a 
coordinated, multi -pronged strategy. This includes continuous assessment of 
domestic demand and availability from indigenous sources, with any demand -
supply gap addressed through calibrated imports to maintain price stability. This 
Department also closely monitors domestic and international price trends and 
recommends appropriate policy interventions as required. 
In line with the Committee's recommendations, the Department will 
continue to strengthen efforts towards enhancing domestic oilseed production, 
adopting prudent trade policies, and reinforcing market stabilisation mechanisms 
to reduce import dependence, safeguard consumer interests and ensure 
remunerative returns to farmers.
