REPORT
This Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the Ministry of Personnel,
Public Grievances and Pensions on the Recommendations/Observations of the Department-related
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice in its Ninety-fifth
Report on the Demands for Grants (2018-19) of the Ministry, which was presented to the Rajya Sabha and
laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 14th March, 2018.
2. The Action Taken Replies (ATRs) were received from the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances
and Pensions in respect of the recommendations contained in the above mentioned Report on 14th June,
2018. These replies have been categorized as follows:
Chapter – I : The Recommendations/Observations which have been accepted by the Ministry, i.e.
in respect of paras 3.24, 3.25, 3.26, 3.48, 3.49, 3.50, 3.74, 3.75, 3.80, 3.102 and
3.103 of the Report. The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry has accepted
those recommendations.
Chapter – II : The Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue
in view of the replies given by the Ministry, i.e. in respect of paras 2.57, 2.58, 3.12,
3.33, 3.34, 3.35, 3.60, 3.61, 3.62 and 3.63 of the Report. The Committee is satisfied
with the explanations advanced by the Ministry and does not wish to pursue those as
of now.
Chapter – III : The Recommendations/Observations in respect of which Committee is not satisfied
with the replies given by the Ministry, i.e. with regard to paras 3.3, 3.5, 3.10, 3.22,
3.23  and 3.78 of the Report. The Committee does not accept the reasons given for not
accepting its recommendations of the Committee and reiterates the same.
3. The Committee desires that the Department should furnish pointed and detailed Action Taken Notes in
respect of all the further Recommendations/Observations made by the Committee in this report.
4. The details of the ATR have been discussed in the respective Chapters in the succeeding pages.

DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING (DoPT)
Staff Selection Commission (SSC)
Recommendation/Observation
1.0 As per the information provided by the Commission, the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) has had to
cancel nine of its examinations, fully or partially, in past five years. Most recently, the SSC had to recommend
for a CBI enquiry into allegations pertaining to the conduct of Combined Graduate Level (Tier-II)
Examination 2017. Such cancellation of exams is a complete waste of efforts and resources of both the
aspirants and the Government and also dims the image of the Commission in the eyes of general public. The
job aspirants lose their precious time and effort that they have put in preparing for those exams. On the
other hand much of time and money of the Commission is lost and the user Departments have to wait more
for getting shortlisted candidates for appointment. The Committee feels that the sanctity of examination
process must be maintained and it is equally important that the trust of public in general and of examinees
in particular, in the examination process is maintained at any cost. Therefore, the Committee recommends
that learning from the past experiences, the Commission should spare no efforts in making the process of
examination free from all types of malpractices and technical glitches. As the examinations have gone
online and the IT infrastructure for conduct of examinations are hired from private institutions/labs and
technical service providers, these entities should be closely supervised by the Commission and adequate
number of Central Government officers should be deployed at examination centres in invigilation/supervisory
capacity. In addition to this, regular audit of the hardware and software should be carried out by government
agencies for making the examination foolproof. As the SSC has been entrusted with the responsibility for
conducting Common Eligibility Test (CET) from next year , the Commission has got its work cut out to
conduct the exam in a fair and efficient manner, free from all malpractices. (Para 3.24)
Action Taken Reply
1.1 Seven of the nine examinations cancelled in the last five years were conducted in the Optical Mark
Reader (OMR) mode which was highly vulnerable to leakages and other examination related malpractices.
To effectively counter this situation, the Commission changed the modality of examination and adopted the
Computer Based Mode of examinations w.e.f. June, 2016. The transition from the OMR based mode to the
Computer Based Mode was swift and comprehensive wherein presently all objective multiple choice question
papers of the Commission are conducted in the computer based mode.  From June 2016, till date, the
Commission has conducted 29 examinations for approx. 3.14 crore candidates in the Computer Based
Mode.
1.2 The Computer Based Mode, has certain distinct advantages in so much as:
(a) this automated mode of examination is more robust, reliable and efficient.
(b) human intervention is minimized thereby reducing the vulnerability of the examination to leakages
and exam related malpractices.
(c) there is greater flexibility and higher confidentiality in administering the question papers.
2

3
(d) greater accuracy and faster processing of results.
(e) better data management contributing to more effective analysis and report generation.
1.3 With the adoption of Computer Based Mode, the Examination cycle for a single tier examination has
also come down perceptibly to 3-5 months while larger examinations conducted by the Commission in 3-4
Tiers / Stages, are completed within 10-12 months.
1.4 This modality of examination is less vulnerable to malpractices but was not suited to certain vested
interests i.e. Coaching Centres, etc. who recently sponsored an agitation against the Commission, making
false and reckless allegations regarding leakages of one  question papers of CGLE (Tier-II) 2017 and
reckless allegations of corruption which were totally misplaced and unfounded.
1.5 The Commission has repeatedly made its position clear in the public domain that there was no leakage
of any question paper of CGLE (Tier-II) 2017 nor was there cancellation of any paper in the said examination.
The allegations regarding corruption / scam are malafide without any substantive/ conclusive evidence.
Some snapshots were reportedly taken by some mischievous candidates during the examination which is an
exam related malpractice i.e. (cheating) which would be strictly dealt with in accordance with the law and
rules of the Commission.
1.6 Confident of its position of integrity and efficiency in the conduct of its examinations, the
Commission suo motu  recommended to Government that a CBI enquiry may be conducted so as to
effectively counter these reckless allegations of leakages and corruption and also to investigate the role
of certain vested interests who sponsored, ( i.e. initiated, nurtured and escalated) the agitation with the
malafide intent of discrediting the Commission and disrupting and dismantling the computer based
mode of examination  so as to revert  to the OMR based mode which is more expedient to their
commercial interests. The Government acceded to the request of the Commission and ordered a CBI
enquiry in the said matter. Accordingly, the CBI has registered a Preliminary Enquiry in the said matter
which is currently under investigation.
1.7 The Commission fully endorses the view of the Committee that the sanctity of the examinations are to
be maintained at all cost and therefore the Commission exercises due diligence and takes comprehensive
precautions to ensure the smooth conduct of its examinations. A List of the salient actions taken in this
regard is recorded at Annexure-I.
1.8 To facilitate this process, the Commission has drafted and put into place a well formulated and
comprehensive Standard Operating Procedure, for the conduct of Computer Based Examinations, which is
used as a “master plan” and is meticulously adhered to by the Commission and the Service Provider to
ensure the seamless conduct of its examinations.  Under this dispensation, the Commission exercises effective
superintendence over the conduct of its examination process.
1.9 Furthermore, at the operational level, it is also pertinent to mention that various  examinations of the
Commission, at all venues, are closely monitored from the Commission Headquarters ( i.e. the centralised
Control Room) and  the Nine Regional / Sub-Regional Offices with the Service Provider, on a real time basis
on telegram and through video conferencing. Simultaneously, the Regional Offices / Sub- Regional Offices
also exercise effective control over the Service Provider at the field level.
1.10 Y et, because most of the mandated examinations conducted by the Commission have lakhs of candidates,
(some of which even exceed 60 lakh) are conducted over a long duration of time extending beyond one

4
month, stray cases of malpractices and technical glitches sometime do occur. However, the Commission
takes immediate corrective action in such situations.
1.11 In terms of IT infrastructure for the conduct of computer based examinations, it would be relevant to
mention that availability of high quality, well equipped computer labs is a scarce resource. Yet while selecting
venues, each venue is selected by the Service Provider in conformity with a carefully formulated check-list,
which clearly prescribes the specific parameters / facilities which are required to be made available at the
venues. The Service Provider and the Commission, conduct independent venue audits to ensure the selection
of proper venues to the extent possible.
1.12 Examinations in the Computer Based Mode are held over long durations, exceeding one month and
mostly on week days. Sufficient numbers of Government officers/ officials as recommended by the Committee
are not readily available. Moreover, shortage of staff in the Staff Selection Commission also imposes a
constraint on the deployment of Commission officers/ officials at each and every examination venue.  However,
despite these manpower constraints, the Commission deploys its own staff to the optimum extent possible
and has also  been expanding its panel of Inspecting Officers by sourcing them from retired officers /
officials of the Central / State Governments and PSUs.
1.13 The regular audit of hardware and software by specialist Government agencies is a critical activity
and an ongoing process. The Commission, which is in the process of finalising a fresh contract with the
Service Provider would give due emphasis to this requirement in its future contracts.
1.14 The Commission is conscious of the fact that the CET examination is to be conducted in a fair and
efficient manner free from all malpractices and technical glitches. As the contract of the Commission with
the existing service provider are to end soon, the Commission with the prior approval of the Government in
DoPT has engaged the services of National Institute of Smart Government (NISG) to formulate the Request
for Proposal so as to select a panel of competent vendors to execute future computer based examinations
of the Commission including CET. The fresh contract to be signed with the panel of Service Provider
indentified would carry more stringent provisions to ensure the smooth conduct of the examinations by the
Commission.
Further Observation
1.15 The Committee takes note of the reply and appreciates steps taken by the Staff Selection
Commission to make the examination free from all types of malpractices.
Recommendation/Observation
2.0 The Common Eligibility Test (CET) proposed to be conducted from next year is an ambitious endeavour
of the Commission and in the long term will benefit both the Government and the job aspirants. It will
reduce the number of examinations conducted by various agencies of the Government and will save significant
amount of money spent on conducting these examinations by different recruiting agencies. Conducting
screening test (i.e. first stage) of any examination is very difficult as the number of applicants are huge
compared to the actual number of seats slated to be filled. By using scores of the CET, the work of other
recruiting agencies of conducting subsequent stages will become easier. In future, the CET scores may be
further used by State Governments, if they so desire and even private companies may use it for short listing
candidates for employing them. (Para 3.25)

5
Action Taken
2.1 The CET, at all three levels viz. Matriculation, Higher Secondary (10+2), Graduation Level is estimated
to attract about 5 crore candidates, which would make it the largest recruitment examination in the world.
It is also likely to benefit both the Government and job aspirants.
2.2 Under this dispensation of examination, while the burden of other recruiting agencies is likely to come
down significantly, after CET, as their function would be restricted only to conducting Tier-II examinations.
However, on the contrary, the Staff Selection Commission’s workload and responsibilities would increase
manifold, in so much as, the Commission would not only be conducting CET (at all three levels), as the first
stage of examinations for all other recruiting agencies but would also have to conduct its regular Tier-II
mandated examinations in consonance with the calendar of examinations.
2.3 There is, therefore, an imperative requirement to fortify the manpower and infrastructural support to
the Commission with functional and financial autonomy so that it can discharge its functions seamlessly and
efficiently.
2.4 In the process, because of the enormous increase in workload, the Commission would not be in a
position to conduct any non-mandated examinations in future.
2.5 As observed by the Committee, it is anticipated that CET scores in the future would also be used by
the State Governments and also by the Private Sector for employing shortlisted candidates.  This would also
reduce the inconvenience to candidates of having to contact with number of agencies for taking multiple
recruitment examinations and also give them an opportunity to improve their scores.
Further Observation
2.6 The Committee takes note of the reply and hope that the Commission accomplishes the task
of holding the Common Eligibility Test, next year on time. The CET will go a long way in streamlining
the multiple examinations conducted by the Government.
Recommendation/Observation
3.0 The Committee notes that initially the Government will be conducting the CET only in English and
Hindi medium. The Committee feels that conducting this examination in only two languages will be
disadvantageous to a large chunk of job seekers who are not proficient in these two languages. The Committee
is of the view that since CET will be an objective type, multiple-choice questions based examination,
conducting the same in other Eighth Schedule languages should not be a difficult task to achieve and thus
recommends that the Government should strive to hold CET in all Eighth Schedule languages in a phased
manner . (Para 3.26)
Action Taken
3.1 The Commission in accordance with its mandate makes recruitment to Group ‘B’ Non-Gazetted,
Group ‘C’ Non-Technical and Group ‘B’ Gazetted Posts of Assistant Accounts Officer and Assistant Audit
Officer in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department. In this process, the Commission is tasked with the
responsibility of filling up of vacancies in the Offices of the Central Government located in different parts of
the country.
3.2 The official work in the Central Government Offices, across the country is mainly transacted
in English and Hindi and therefore in the overall administrative interest, it becomes necessary that

6
candidates appointed to the Government of India have a fair knowledge of English and / or Hindi.  In
the given context, the Commission initially proposes to conduct the CETs in the bilingual i.e.  English
and Hindi.
3.3 Since the CET examinations with approximately 5 crore candidates is to be taken up as a fresh
initiative, to be held for the first time, it would be necessary to ensure that the said examination, in the
bilingual mode, stabilizes  in the initial few years.  Thereafter, at a subsequent stage, after due analysis by an
Expert Committee, the possibility of conducting CET in all languages of the Eight Schedule, can be decided
upon.
Further Observation
3.4 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.
Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM)
Recommendation/Observation
4.0 The ISTM is a very old institute established way back in the year 1948 and it caters to the training and
development needs of the employees of the Central Secretariat, viz. Central Secretariat Service, Central
Secretariat Stenographic Services, Central Secretariat Clerical Services, etc. who form the bedrock of the
Central Government. However, in spite of being such an old institution and performing such an important
role, the Institute has not grown much, especially when it is compared to the institutions like LBSNAA. This
is perhaps due to the fact that Secretariat services, especially at the lower and middle level are not given
their due weightage despite the fact that they play crucial role right from the policy formulation to the
review stage. They serve as a permanent bureaucratic set up and thus form a coherent institutional memory.
In view of this, the Committee strongly recommends that the ISTM should be developed into an institute of
excellence and necessary resources in terms of funds and human resource should be provided to it to achieve
its strategic plans of next five years.  (Para 3.48)
Action Taken
4.1 The observations of the Committee have been noted for compliance. The requirement of resources
both in terms of funds as well as human resource will be provided to them on the basis of proposals from
ISTM in consultation with Ministry of Finance, by giving adequate justifications, so as to achieve the
strategic plans of ISTM.
Further Observation
4.2 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.
Recommendation/Observation
5.0 The institute is functioning on a depleted strength, as one-third of its sanctioned posts of both, faculty
and staff are remaining vacant. Also, in addition to their training job, faculty members are diverted to
manage day-to-day activities of the Institute. In this background, the Committee recommends that the ISTM
should be provided with necessary faculty and staff for effectively delivering on its mandate. Further, for
performing administrative, financial and other managerial activities, separate posts of US/DS should be
encadred for the Institute. (Para 3.49)

7
Action Taken
5.1 The observations of the Committee has been noted for compliance and all out efforts are made to fill
up the vacant posts of ISTM by taking quick and prompt action on the proposals.  Time-bound action is
being taken to circulate the posts and call the selection committee meetings wherever required and also the
proposals to fill up the posts are being sent to UPSC wherever the consultation with UPSC is mandatory in

Further Observation
5.2 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.
Recommendation/Observation
6.0 It is also recommended that the Institute should continually revise and upgrade its courses as per the
changing needs of the Central Secretariat. The training modules devised by the Institute should focus on the
practical aspects of the secretariat management and should not be too academic. The Institute should also
take a lead in developing and grooming Training Academies of States/UTs and organisations not falling
under the Government of India.                                                                                                (Para 3.50)
Action Taken
6.1 The ISTM continuously strives to improve the quality of training being imparted by reviewing and
upgrading its courses, contents/ session plans/ reading materials as per the training needs of not only of the
Central Secretariat, but also the needs of States/ UTs/ Autonomous bodies and PSUs. The institute has
recently developed training modules on the latest happenings and areas like GST, GEM, E-Office and orientation
program for retiring officials. The Department of Personnel and Training advices and guides the ISTM to
ensure that it continuously revises and upgrades its courses as per the changing needs of the Central
Secretariat.
6.2 Further, to ensure attitudinal and behavioural changes, the institute has introduced two training modules,
namely, Ethics and Values and Basic Leadership skills during 2015-16. The course directors are required to
review and update the session plans/ reading materials before the commencement of each training program
as per the need of the hour.
6.3 To build capacity of the trainers of the training institutions of States/ Union Territories UTs, the ISTM
organizes trainer’s development courses, like, Direct Training Skills (DTS), Design of Training (DoT),
Evaluation of Training (EoT), Training Needs Analysis (TNA) and Management of Training (MoT) regularly.
This institute also conducts capacity building programs, specifically for North Eastern Region States and
sends its peripatetic teams to impart trainings to the officials of States/ UTs regularly.
Further Observation
6.4 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEV ANCES
Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System

8
Recommendation/Observation
7.0 Dealing with public grievances effectively is one of the core components of Good Governance. The
CPGRAMS has evolved into an effective platform for redressal of public grievances related to the Central
Government. Grievances received through the Public Grievance Portal have increased from 2.7 lakhs in
2014 to more than 14 lakhs in 2017. This phenomenal increase may be attributed to the increasing awareness
in public about this portal and also to the fact that people at large are getting the desired relief. However,
large part of the population is still not aware about existence of such a portal, particularly those who are
living in smaller towns, rural and far-flung areas. The Committee, therefore, recommends that CPGRAMS
should be publicized in mass media in vernacular languages also, so that common people become aware of
availability of such avenue for redressal of their grievances against the Government Departments/
organizations. It is further hoped that 
` 50 lakhs proposed to be allocated to the Department for advertising
of CPGRAMS efficiently and fully utilised in the FY 2018-19. (Para 3.74)
Action Taken
7.1 The proposal for advertising of CPGRAMS in the National Newspapers has been forwarded to DAVP,
publication of which would enable common people become aware of availability of such avenue for redressal
of their grievances against the Government Departments/ organisations. Advertisement of CPGRAMS in
vernacular languages also is proposed to be taken up with the DA VP.
Further Observation
7.2 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.
Recommendation/Observation
8.0 Many Government Departments, Organizations, PSUs, PSBs, State Governments/UTs are also having
online facilities/portals for addressing Citizen Grievances and above all there is a Directorate of Public
Grievances (DPG) in the Cabinet Secretariat, Government of India, which is functioning as an appellate
body investigating selected grievances of selected Ministries/Departments of Government of India. The
Committee suggests that the DARPG , which is the nodal Department for dealing with citizen grievances
at Central level, should provide web-links (URLs) of all the online facilities/portals of Government
Departments/PSUs/PSBs/State Governments/UTs meant for addressing public grievances  pertaining to
their respective organisations on its PG Portal and to develop the portal into a single platform at pan
India level dealing with all sorts of public grievances pertaining to the Governments (both Centre and
States) and their agencies. (Para 3.75)
Action Taken
8.1 The DARPG has written to all States/UTs for duly linking or integrating the public grievance portal of
the State Governments with CPGRAMS. CPGRAM is already a pan- India platform as citizens can lodge
their grievance on it from anywhere and at anytime. Web-links (URLs) of all the online facilities/portals of
Government Departments are already available with the respective Ministries/Departments including online
facilities for lodging of public grievances.
Further Observation
8.2 The Committee takes note of the reply of the Ministry.

DEPARTMENT OF PENSIONS AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE
Pension Adalats
Recommendation/Observation
10.0 The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare is a small but very important Department having
the mandate to serve and facilitate employees who have retired after rendering their services to the Government.
After retirement because of increasing age the pensioners are encircled by many problems like failing health,
loneliness and many other problems. So it is the duty of the Departments from where a pensioner has retired
and DPPW in particular to ensure that all benefits and entitlements of the pensioners are timely paid and
they are not made to run around the government agencies like their P&AOs, Pension Accounting offices,
Banks and Tribunals to receive their rightful dues on time. The Committee would like to suggest the Government
that this year’ s Good Governance Day which falls on 25th December may be dedicated to our pensioners in
order to minimize the Pensioners woes. Programmes and events like Pension Adalats should be held throughout
the country in order to reach out to the pensioners and redress their problems.
(Para 3.102)
Action Taken
10.1 In view of the recommendations made at para-3.102, DoPPW will organize ‘Pension Adalat’ at grand
level. For this, the event of Pension Adalat will be advertised through print media on a wider scale to increase
the participation in event.
10.2 Beside this, Pension Adalat will also be held in various parts of the country at regular intervals in order

ANNEXURE – I
Action taken by the Staff Selection Commission to prevent malpractices
during examinations and Addressing the technical glitches.
 (A) The Commission has been taking all possible measures to prevent malpractices during the examinations.
These include:
 (i) careful selection of examination venues,
 (ii) use of CCTV cameras for surveillance in the actual conduct of the computer based examinations,
 (iii) bio-metric registration of the candidates,
 (iv) random seating arrangements of the candidates linked to bio-metric registration,
 (v) isolation of suspected group of candidates for better and focused vigilance,
 (vi) high level of invigilation and inspection - including deployment of  officers as Inspecting Officers
and surprise inspection by use of flying squads,
 (vii) thorough frisking of candidates with metal detectors,
 (viii) three levels of security involving private security agency at each  and every venue of examination,
State Police in providing conducive  environment for conduct of examination, and specific
deployment of  State Police in selected regions. Deployment of Para-military Forces at  hyper-
sensitive venues,
 (ix) deployment of Quick Response Teams by service provider in all nine Regions/Sub-regions of
the Commission,
 (x) issue of Notification regarding prohibited items during the  examination and strict implementation
of the same,
 (xi) Strict document verification,
 (xii) ensuring proper training of invigilators, Inspecting Officers (IOs) and other examination
functionaries,
 (xiii) strict action against the candidates caught involved in malpractices during the examination like
lodging of FIRs, cancellation of candidature and debarment from future examinations.
The introduction of computer based mode of examinations and bio-metric registration of candidates
has led to high level of confidentiality in administering questions and effectively checking the cases of
impersonation.
(B) The Commission started conducting its examinations in the Computer Based Mode (CBM) with effect
from June, 2016.  During the initial period, some teething problems like slow speed of server, hanging
of system, LAN related issues etc. were faced during the conduct of examinations.  However, with
the passage of time, the Commission succeeded in stabilizing its systems for the conduct of examinations
in the CBM.  Now a days, only a small number of such issues are reported and most of these are
effectively handled with the minimum inconvenience to the candidates.  In all such cases, it is ensured
that the candidates do not suffer due to these glitches and the affected candidates are provided complete
35

36
duration of examination time.  In worst cases, if it is not possible to continue/finish the examination in
a particular session, the affected candidates are allowed to appear in the following sessions/days of
the examination.
Measures taken by SSC to counter technical glitches are as follows:-
(i) All the shortlisted venues are audited before final selection.
(ii) A full-fledged mock test at its full capacity in all the venues, is conducted prior to the actual
conduct of the examination.
(iii) Commission also ensures that there are enough IT personnel/Test administrators available at the
venues to counter any kind of technical glitches experienced by the candidate.
(iv) Candidates can access examination application through secure browser with Firewall Policy to
allow connection only between  exam server and candidate’s PC.
(v) Access to examination server is only available during specific timings of examination.
(vi) Question Papers are downloaded in encrypted form only 5 to 30 minutes prior to commencement
of the examination.
(vii) Question Papers in the examination server can be activated through activation password only.
(viii) Complete log of candidates’ activates is recorded during the conduct of examination.
(ix) Automatic removal of candidates’ details from the browser session after the examination.
(x) Candidate cannot access any other programme during the examination.
(xi) The Commission appointed vendor has implemented some additional innovative measures for a
secured examination, which are as follows:-
(a) Auto seat allocation (randomized) on biometric registration.

ANNEXURE – II
Extract from the Report of the Expert Group constituted by the
Government in 2014 for reviewing the examination system in the staff
Selection Commission on granting it statutory status.
The Expert Group constituted by the Government in 2014 for reviewing the examination system in the
Staff Selection Commission has inter alia  recommended according Statutory status to the Commission,
which in turn would contribute to greater functional autonomy and faster decision making and efficiency in
the overall performance and delivery of results by the Commission in the recruitment process.
The recommendation of the Expert Group, have been submitted to the Government in DoP&T for
consideration.  The same are reproduced as under:-
“The SSC was set up in November, 1975 with the objective of taking over the work of holding
examinations for recruitment to lower categories of Central Government posts and services from the
UPSC, which functions in terms of Articles 315 to 323 of the constitution of India.  Over a period of
time the work entrusted to the SSC has grown to many times that of the parent body viz. UPSC.
However the formal status and resources provided to the SSC has proved far short of the requirement.
The range and complexity of the various posts and services for which the SSC is now conducting
examinations far exceeds anything handled by the parent body but without even a fraction of the
resources and statutory/functional autonomy of the UPSC.  The SSC has the status of an attached
office of the Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms in Government of India and is
entirely dependent and under the control of DOP&T.  There is no reason why the Constitutional
protection and human and financial resources provided to the UPSC and State Public Services
Commissions should not be provided to the SSC which performs essentially the same or similar functions.
As a matter of fact the bulk of the work done by State PSCs is quite similar in scope and content as the
SSC and there appears to be no justifications for placing the SSC on a completely different footing.
There is also a clear need for sharper role definition and drawing of boundaries for this organization
- it cannot continue to be treated as a “carry-all” organization catering to all manner of users and
staff categories. There is a need for a close structural study culminating in a proper organizational
design for the SSC - it cannot continue to grow in an adhoc manner .  The Expert Group strongly
recommends the following restructuring/strengthening measures for the SSC:-
(i) The Commission, being an offshoot, in a sense, of the UPSC (having  taken on some functions
originally entrusted under the Constitution to the  UPSC) needs to be accorded a statutory status
which would confer upon  it meaningful autonomy-both functional and financial.  Its present
status  as an Attached/Subordinate office of the Department of Personnel and  Training leaves it
struggling constantly to achieve its goals and give the  exponential rate of growth of the candidature
handled by it and the  complexity and variety of tasks entrusted to it, the time may be fast
approaching when it is left unable to cope.
(ii) The unwarranted burden of handling recruitment to non-mandated cadres  like constables/
riflemen in the Central Armed Police Forces, or any  post/service for which education qualification
is less than 10+2 etc., has  to be immediately taken off the back of the SSC which had never
been  intended or designed to take on such tasks.  The Expert Group sees no  reason why such
recruitment to entirely operative and one-dimensional positions cannot be organized by the
concerned department/Organisations themselves as they had been doing in the past. The
37

38
Department of  Personnel needs to urgently undertake a thorough review of the work  entrusted
to the SSC and work out a plan for relieving this overburdened  body of its multitude of mandated
and non-mandated tasks.
(iii) All necessary financial physical and human resources should be made available to it as indicated
by the growth in its work dimensions and complexity.
(iv) The rank, status and Terms and Conditions of Service of its Chairman and Member should be
raised appropriately to the level of Secretary and  Additional Secretary respectively in view of
the proposed statutory role and status of the Commission.  The strength of the Commission also
needs to be raised and the Membership needs to be made more representative with inclusion of
persons with technical background and the uniformed services.  The Chairman and Members
should have tenure of five years subject to an upper age limit of 65 years.
(v) The Commission in its new avtar should be mandated to review its own  functioning and
performance by means of an outside expert body at  regular intervals, say, every 5-7 years and
take steps aimed at validation, updation and revitalization as a result of such reviews.
(vi) The Commission today functions in the manner of a typical Government Office with the
conventional pattern of submission of files through the  hierarchy upto the Member and then
Chairman. The Group recommends  that with the upgradation of the Commission to a statutory
level, its function should be modeled on the pattern of other such bodies like UPSC.
Further, Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public, Grievances,
Law and Justice have in para 49 of its 83rd Report, recommended as under:-
“The Committee supports the recommendation of the Expert Group. The DoP&T may pursue
the matter and ensure that statutory backing is provided to the SSC. The Committee further

ensure that they are implemented as far as practical.”
