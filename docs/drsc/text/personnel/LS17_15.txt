DELIMITATION COMMISSION 
6.1 On a specific query raised about the progress of delimitation work, the Ministry submitted the 
following written reply:- 
 “The Delimitation Commission was constituted in July, 2002. It became functional in 
November/December, 2002. The Commission - in terms of the Constitutional provision - 
was to delimit the constituencies on the basis of 1991 census figures. By June, 2003 the 
Commission had completed almost 60 per cent of its work. The totality of the Commission’s 
work (all the Parliamentary constituencies and th e Assembly constituencies in all the states 
in the country) would have been completed by November/December, 2003. But it could not 
be done because the Parliament amended the Constitution by the Constitution (Eighty-
Seventh Amendment) Act, 2003, which provides that the delimitation work shall be done by 
the Commission on the basis of the 2001 census and not the 1991 census figures. As a 
consequence, the work done by the Commission on the basis of the 1991 census became 
non-est by the operation of law. The Commission, however, started preliminary work on the 
basis of the provisional 2001 census figures, which were available. But the preliminary work 
could not be taken to the logical extent becau se; even the provisiona l 2001 census figures 
regarding the schedule castes and schedule tribes were not available. 
 The 2001 Census figures for most of the States were published on December 31, 2003. 
Thereafter the Commission took-up the work of collecting data and tabulating the same. 
While the process was going on, the Lok Sabha was dissolved and fresh elections were 
declared. The Commission has five Lok Sabha  Members from each State as its associate 
members. Where the number of MPs in a Stat e is less than five, all of them become 
Commission’s members. With the dissolution of the Lok Sabha, the associate members 
ceased to be members by operation of law. Even otherwise during the elections, the 
fieldwork by the Commission was almost stopped. After the constitution of the Fourteenth 
Lok Sabha, the Commission requested Hon’ble Speaker to nominate associate members to 
the Delimitation Commission from each State.  After receipt of the nominations, the 
Commission restarted the delimitation work which is progressing on a tight schedule. 
  The delimitation of the Lok Sabha and assembly constituencies in all the States is a 
time consuming work. Under the scheme of the Act associate members are to be associated 
with the delimitation work from the very beginning. As a consequence there have to be more 
than one meeting with the associate memb ers from each State. Thereafter, the draft 
delimitation proposals are published in newspapers and suggestions and views are invited 
from the public. Finally, the public meetings are held by the Commission in all the States 
and the draft finalized, after that. 

 Delimitation work in all the States in the country is at different stages. Four States viz; 
Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal and the Union Territory of Pondicherry are 
scheduled to go to polls in May, 2006. Out of these, Final Delimitation Order has been 
issued in respect of the State of Kerala, West Bengal and Union Territory of Pondicherry and 
published in the State and Central Gazette on 31.3.2005, 31st May, 2005, 31st March, 2005 
and 15th February, 2006 respectively. For Assam and Tamil Nadu the Commission has 
decided that further discussion relating to the above mentioned two States would be held 
after the Legislative Assembly election to th e States is over and new set of Associate 
Members (five MLAs) are appointed by the Speaker of the new Legislative Assembly. 
 It may be added that under Article 82 of the Constitution, the readjustment of 
constituencies for the House of the People ‘shall take effect from such date as the President 
may, by order, specify’. Similarly under Article 170 (3) of the Constitution, the readjustment 
of constituencies for the State Legislative Assemblies ‘shall take effect from such date as the 
President may, by order, specify’. The question of elections to be held on the basis of the 
new delimitation exercise will be dependent on the date determined by the President of 
India.” 
6.2 The Committee reiterates its earlie r recommendations, put forth in its 6
th report on 
Demand for Grants (20 05-2006), that the Delimitation Commission should complete the 
delimitation exercise in the Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies  in all the States within a 
specific timeframe. The Committee is anguis hed that inspite of its recommendations for

to go to polls in April - May, 2006, the Commission  could complete the exercise in the States of

the constituencies of the House of People, which shall take effect from the date to be specified by

NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA) 
7.6 Budgetary allocation (2006-07) for NALSA has been  made as Rs. 10 crore which is the same as 
in the previous year was made. 
7.7 National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) has been constituted under section 3 of the Legal 
Services Authorities Act, 1987 in the year 1995 to  provide free and competent legal services to the 
weaker sections of the society with the objective to  ensure that opportunities for securing justice are 
not denied to any citizen for the reason of economic or other disabilities. To be eligible for seeking 
legal aid under the NALSA, the annual income limit fixed by the Central Government for the cases 
before the Supreme Court is Rs. 50,000 at present. Out of twenty eight States Legal Services 
Authorities, and 7 Union Territories, 18 State Legal Services Authorities and 3 Union Territories have 
raised the income ceiling limit to Rs. 50,000 per annum to provide free legal aid under Legal Services 
Authorities Act, 1987. This has resulted in an increase of number of beneficiaries. During the period 
from the 1st April 2005 to the 31st December 2005, more than 5.70 lakh persons have been benefited 
through legal aid services. Out of them, 54811 persons belong to the Scheduled Castes, 15716 to the 
Scheduled Tribes, 51577 persons were women and 6582 were children. 

7.8 The Committee is satisfied that its reco mmendation (6th Repo rt on the Demand for 
Grants 2005-06), that more and more States and UT s should raise the income-ceiling limit, has 
resulted in 18 States and 3 UTs raising the limit to the prescribed level. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should  speed up the entire process so that income-
ceiling limit throughout the eountry remains uniform and la rger number of persons can get 
benefit under the scheme. The Committee also fe els that the counsels engaged for the poor 
under the legal aid programme are paid meager ly; and as a result thereof good and reputed 
lawyers do not come forward to take up the case s. Even Senior Advocates do not take up such 
cases. As a result the poor liti gants feel that legal aid being pr ovided to them is mere eyewash. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the fee structure for the legal aid counsel should be 
reasonably enhanced so that the experienced  and competent lawyers are attracted and 
motivated to take up / accept the cases of the legal aid beneficiaries with full enthusiasm 
Moreover, the fee structure should be made uniform throughout the country.

7.11 The Committee notes that NALSA has la unched a laudable mission for legal 
empowerment of all the sections of the society, ye t, certain grey areas need to be addressed by 
the Ministry. 0ne such problem rel ates to lack of proper planning. Moreover, non-utilisation of 
grants by the States Legal Serv ices Authorities is another area  of concern. The Committee feels 
that NALSA has not prepared a consolidated sc heme, covering all its schemes and programmes 
for formulating a Centrally Sponsored Scheme due to which the target set was not achieved. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that NALSA  should submit as early as possible a 
perspective plan covering all it s schemes to the Ministry at th e earliest, for consideration. The 
Ministry should effectively moni tor not only utilisation of the f unds but also implementation of 
the schemes and programmes of NALSA. Th e Committee, therefore, recommends that 
constraints of funds should not come in the way of successful implementation of the scheme. The 
Ministry should, therefore, allo cate additional funds to he lp operationalis e the various 
programmes to be undertaken by NALSA at RE st age. The Committee also feels that the actual 
benefit of this scheme is not gaining access to po or litigants. The programme is confined to high 
profile areas or capital cities only. In this direction, the Committee acknowledges the historic 
Maha Lok Adalat organi sed by Hon’ble Justice A.P.Shah, Chief Justice,  High Court, Madras 
and Patron-in chief, Tamil Nadu State Legal Serv ices Authority on 15/04/2006 who along with 
Justice P.D.Dinakaran and others at High Cour t Legal Services Committee, Madurai Bench of 
Madras High Court, Madurai made  quick disposal of all the case s at the Madurai Bench of the 
High Court, by bringing all the Judges of High Court and Subordinate Courts as well as 

litigants at one place. The Committee feels such proactive steps would go a long way in disposing 
off the pending cases with in a prescribed time frame. 
7.12 Lok Adalat is an important constituent of NALSA. Permanent Lok Adalats under section 19 of 
the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 have been established in almost all the States However, 
Permanent Lok Adalats for Public Utility Services under section 22B of the Legal Services 
Authorities Amendment Act, 2002 have been established only in Eight States viz. Assam, Bihar, 
Haryana, Jharkhand, Punjab, Rajasthan, Sikkim and U.T. of Chandigarh. The State Governments of 
Kerala and Delhi have also approved the proposal of their Legal Services Authorities for 
establishment of Permanent Lok Adalat  u/s 22B of the Act and establishment of Permanent Lok 
Adalats in these States is under process. So far as Jammu and Kashmir is concerned, amendment to 
the Act is required. During the period from 1st April, 2005 to 31st December, 2005, 38,261 Lok 
Adalats were organized. These Lok Adalats sett led 9,60,202 cases. The remaining State Legal 
Services Authorities have informed that their pr oposals for establishment of Permanent Lok Adalats 
are pending with the State Governments. The State Authorities have been impressed upon to get their 
proposal cleared from their respective State Government. The NALSA has also taken up the matter 
with the State Governments to expedite the matter but the progress in this matter is slow due to 
paucity of funds.

REVISION OF ELECTORAL ROLLS
 
7.30 The electoral rolls are revised by the Election Commission every year. All persons who attain  
18 years of age or above are eligible for inclusion of their names in the electoral rolls and can apply 
for the same. Budget Estimates for 2006-07 for meeting expenses on revision of electoral rolls are 
covered under sub head 00.103. 
7.31 The Committee has been apprised by the Ministry through their action taken reply that keeping 
in view the elections to the Legislative Assemblie s of Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and 
Pondicherry 
due in 2006, the electoral rolls for these States/UT have been revised w.r.t. 01.01.2006 as the 
qualifying date. In Assam final publication was made on 10.02.2006. As on that date about 1.74 crore 
electors were 
enrolled in the State. The electoral rolls in Kera la (except for Assembly constituencies falling in 
Kasargod and Palghat districts) were finally published on 27.02.2006 and that of Assembly 

constituencies falling in Kasargod and Palghat districts were published on 04.03.2006. About 2.12 
crore electors stood enrolled in the State. The electo ral rolls of West Bengal were finally published on 
22.02.2006 and as on that date about 4.89 crore electors stood included in the rolls. The electoral rolls 
of Tamil Nadu were finally published on 7.03.2006 and as on that date about 4.64 crore electors stood 
included in the rolls. In Pondicherry the electoral rolls were finally published on 24.02.2006 and 
25.02.2006 and approximately 6.5 lakh electors were enrolled. 
 In all the four States of Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and UT of Pondicherry, the 
electoral rolls have been computerized and are avai lable on the website of respective Chief Electoral 
Officer and also on the website of the Commission. In the State of Kerala and UT of Pondicherry, the 
electoral rolls are with photographs. For the first time the General Elections to the Legislative 
Assemblies of Kerala and UT of Pondicherry have the facilities of having electoral rolls with 
photographs of electors for poll purpose. In the state of West Bengal, electoral rolls in respect of 46 
assembly constituencies have been prepared/printed with photos of electors. The electoral rolls with 
photographs while on one hand helps establish identity of voters; on the other it eliminated the 
chances of impersonation. 
7.32 During the meetings of the Committee some Members expressed concern about the revision of 
electoral rolls in different Parliamentary as well as the Assemblies Constituencies. It was observed 
that there had been numerous complaints of the voters all over the country about irregularities 
committed in preparation of these rolls. The Committee feels that such irregularity impedes the 
successful implementation of the schemes and should be avoided. The Committee is happy to note 
that the process of revision of the electoral rolls in Assam, West Bengal, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, UT of 
Pondicherry are completed and computerized. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier 
recommendation (para 7.32 of 6th Report) that to overcome such shortcomings, the Election 
Commission should consider establishing its offices  for this purpose at the district levels and 
district level officer would be made accountable for revision of the rolls. The Committee further 
recommends that the process of revision of the electoral rolls must be completed within a 
specific time frame.

SIMPLIFICATION OF LEGISLATIVE LANGUAGE 
7.41 The Committee notes that the process of simplific ation of laws is the need of the day. The 
Committee observes that legisl ative language is often quite  technical, intricate and 
incomprehensible for the common man for whom la ws are made. It feels that, laws should be 
drafted in national or regional languages to convey th e purpose and intent of framing laws and 
all central laws should be translated into Hindi  in order to make the provisions known to the 
masses. Even a common law country like En gland has started simp lifying its laws. The 
Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendation that  the Ministry should explore 
ways and means to simplify laws so as to be understood even by the people having no specialized 
knowledge of legal formulations. In other words, Legislative Department should act as a catalyst 
for launching a movement for plai n language drafting of laws and instruments of subordinate 
legislation.

DELINKING OF DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE FROM THE MINISTRY OF HOME AND 
CREATION OF A SEPARATE DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE WITH A FULL TIME 
SECRETARY OF JUSTICE 
8.5 The Committee has been recommending persistently that the Department of Justice should be 
separated from the Home Ministry and provided with a full fledged Secretary of its own. This long 
standing recommendation of the Committee can be traced in its 2nd Report on the Demand for Grants 
of the Ministry of Law and Justice (2004-05) under the heading “Delinking of Budget of Department 
of Justice.” wherein the Committee had observed  “the Committee on Home Affairs has been 
reiterating its view since 1995, either to make the Department of Justice an independent Department 
or to merge its budget with the Ministry of Home Affairs.” 
8.6 The Committee was informed that the Government of India had taken a conscious decision in 
1971 to continue administrative association of the Department of Justice with the Home Ministry. The 
position 
emerging as of now seems to be that the Department of Justice is a full- fledged separate Department 
headed by a Secretary who has to be the Home Secr etary. The position held by the Ministry has been 
that the mere fact that the Home Secretary holds ad ditional charge of the Department of Justice, does 
not affect the status of the Department as an independent full-fledged Department. 
8.7. The Committee on Home Affairs also in its 86th Report had made detailed observations that, 
“the Committee has been persistently recommending to set right the anomaly relating to the budget 
presentation 
and administrative control over Department of Justice since 1995 through its 17th Report (1995), 31st 
and 34th Reports (1996), 39th Report (1997), 45th  Report (1998), 56th Report (1999), 61st Report 
(2000), 69th and 72nd Report (2001) to either make it an independent Department or merge the budget 
with the Ministry of Home Affair.” While making final recommendations the Committee on Home 
Affairs had observed that it finds it difficult to understand the advantages for the Government in 
keeping the anomaly intact. The need and justification for making the Department of Justice a full-
fledged Department with a Secretary of its own was thus explained by the Committee on the 5th April, 
2006 as under:- 
“What is the rationale behind attaching the Justice Department to the Home Ministry? 
Today, the Home Ministry is the busiest Ministry in the entire set up of the Government. 
Problems have grown beyond our expectations. New issues have cropped up. Today, the 
very security of the country is at stake. Naxalism has developed beyond our imagination and 
scale. Today, our hands are full with problems and it is no exaggeration if it is said that 
howsoever devotional we may be, we will not be able to give full attention to the Department 
of Justice, which also deserves much more gr eater attention today than at any time in the 

past. It is beyond doubt when we say that the time has come that we should think of 
separation of Justice Department from Home and create a separate full-fledged Department 
of Justice, with a full-fledged Secretary devoted fully to this Department. It is unfair to the 
Home Secretary to burden the Justice Department when their contribution can only be in 
improving governance in various States and making sure that court dealings are done. It is 
earnestly suggested that the Government should take a policy decision on this issue as there 
is a valid reason for this.” 
8.8 From the above observations, the Committ ee finds it reasonable to deduce that the 
rationale behind keeping the Department of Ju stice under the Administrative control of the 
Home Secretary might has been logical at that ti me but the situation has gone sea change since 
then. Keeping in view the fact th at the department of Justice has been assigned a distinct task 
under the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Committee 
understands and notes that the Department of Ju stice has been performing sensitive functions 
pertaining to judiciary and the administration of justice. Seen in this context and also of the fact 
that there are about 3 crore cases pending in different courts thus shaking the faith of the people 
in the judicial system, it would be more appropri ate if the Department is  accorded a distinct 
identity including strong administ rative structure of its own. It  is in this context that the 
Committee is of the considered opinion that for the sake of administrative efficiency and 
exigency, the Department is separated from the Home Ministry and provided with a full-fledged 
Secretary. It this context the Committee again reit erates and recommends that it is a high time 
that this long standing recomm endation is implemented in le tter and spirit with immediate 
effect.

THE JUDICIARY IN INDIA : AN OVERVIEW 
Judiciary - From Supreme Court to Subordinate Courts 
8.41 The Preamble to the Constitution of India adopted in the Constituent Assembly on 26 
November, 1949, provides to resolve to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic 
Republic and to secure to all its citizens justice-social, economic and political. 
8.42 The concept relating to “separation of power s between the Legislature, the Executive and the 
Judiciary’ as well as the fundamental concept of an independent judiciary are elevated to the level of 
basic tructure of the Constitution. To achieve the purpose of imparting justice to the common man, the 
Supreme Court of India as an Apex Court, and the 21 High Courts as well as Subordinate Courts have 
been established. 
8.43 The Constitution initially provided for a Chief Justice of India and not more than 7 Judges in the 
Supreme Court. However, with the passage of time, and keeping in view the quantum of work in the 
Supreme Court, the number of judges in the Supreme Court has been raised to 26. 
8.44 Appointment of judges in the Supreme Court of India is made by the President under article 124 
of the Constitution, after consultation with Supreme Court/ High Court Judges, except the 
appointment of Chief Justice of India himself. 
8.45 Article 214 of the Constitution provides that there shall be a High Court for each State. 
However, article 231 also provides that Parliament may by law establish a common High Court for 
two or more States, or for two or more States and a Union Territory. In keeping with these provisions, 
there were only 18 High Courts all over the countr y till November, 2000, number of which rose to 21 
on the creation of 3 new States. 
8.46 Appointment of Permanent Judges and Additional Judges of High Courts are made by the 
President under article 217 and article 224 of the Constitution, respectively, after consultation with 
State constitutional uthorities/Chief Justice of the High Court and after consultation with the Chief 
Justice of India. 
8.47. Under article 235 of the Constitution of Indi a, the administrative control over the members of 
Subordinate Judicial Service vests with the concerned High Court. Further, in exercise of powers 
conferred under proviso to article 309 read with articles 233 of the Constitution, the State Government 
frames rules and regulations in consultation with the High Court. The members of the State Judicial 
Service are governed by these rules and regula tions. Therefore, the service conditions, including 
appointment, promotion etc., of judicial officers of  the District/Subordinate Courts are governed by 
the respective State Governments. It has been observed that at any given point of time, 25-30 percent 
of the vacancies in the Subordinate Courts remain unfilled. 
8.48 The Department of Justice, Union Ministry of Law and Justice which deals with appointment of 
judges and their conditions of services as well as various courts in the country, has taken some major 
initiatives to address the problem of delay in cour ts and huge pendency of cases. The initiatives are 
both for seeking long-term solutions as well as for dealing with the immediate problem of pendency. 
8.49 The Indian judiciary comprises of nearly 15,0 00 courts situated in approximately 2,500 court 
complexes throughout the country. The total pend ency of cases in these Courts as on the 31st 
December, 2005 was 2,91,21,117; the individual break up being - Supreme Court- 33,635; High 
Courts- 35,31,774 and the District and Subordinate Courts- 2,55,55,704. 

8.50 Judiciary is always held responsible for mounting arrears of Court cases. But, it doesn’t control 
the resources of funds and has no power to create additional Courts, appoint adequate Court staff and 
augment the infrastructure required for the Courts. Ideally, judiciary should have autonomy with 
regard to the creation of posts, allocation of budget and incurring of expenditure. However, Financial 
Autonomy at to the High Courts always remains a distant dream. 
8.51 Stagnation in courts, particularly lower judiciary, where more than 22 million cases are pending, 
and high transaction cost due to such delay, ultimately lead to denial the justice to the poor and 
disadvantaged. This is the core concern, which warrants judicial reform. Presently the reform projects 
are being monitored by two national-level Steering Committees headed by sitting-Judges of the 
Supreme Court of India. In every state, where the project is being implemented, there is a Project 
Monitoring Committee (PMC) headed by a sitting judge of the High Court of that state. 
8.52 The objective of judicial reform is to enhance judicial productivity both qualitatively and 
quantitatively by eliminating arrears and making the justice delivery system affordable, accessible, 
cost effective, transparent and accountable. Due to la ck of scientific data about judicial process in the 
form of real time judicial statistics performance au dit of the Judiciary as an institution and individual 
judges is difficult. This onstitutes a significant stumbling block for addressing the problem of judicial 
arrears. Currently real time data about cases can be made available through information technology 
tools and this is being currently addressed by the Government. 
8.53 There is a need for measures to rationalize an d standardize the infrastructure of the courts. The 
need of proper facilities, particularly at mufassils or  at taluka level in backward areas is imminent as 
there, most of the courts function from dingy, dilapidated and outdated structures with poor hygienic 
conditions even for judicial officers, not to mention Members of the Bar or litigants and witnesses. 
The Indian State in the 21st century, flush with economic boom, must meet the modern standards in 
courts’ infrastructure the world over and provid e ‘Plan’ funds in ratio commensurate to the 
requirements. 
8.54 The e-Committee, set up by the Union Government to advice on technological communication 
and management related changes, has submitted its National Policy and Action Plan for 
implementation of nformation and Communication Tech nology (ICT) in the Indian Judiciary at a cost 
of 854 crores in three phases spreading over a period of 5 years. 
8.55 Based on the National Policy and Action Plan, a revised proposal for computerisation of the 
Indian Judiciary has been prepared for the cons ideration of the Expenditure Finance Committee and 
submitted for the approval in December, 2005. 
8.56 There was an overwhelming realization in the judicial sector in favour of devising a National 
Policy and Action Plan with appropriate spread and phasing to implement Information and 
Communication Technology (ICT) in courts across the country and their web-based interlinking.  
8.57 Based on the inputs received from persons having expertise in diverse domains relevant for 
change management in India Judiciary, the E-Committee framed the national Policy and Action Plan 
for mplementation of Information and Communication Technology in the Indian Judiciary. 
8.58 The Department of Justice, Union Ministry of Law and Justice, Government of India has taken 
up the computerization of the 29 Capital City Courts in technical cooperation with the National 
Informatics 
Centre. The computerization project of Capital City Co urts in States and Union Territories is a central 
scheme with 100 per cent funding from the Central Government. The scope of the Capital City Courts 
Computerisation is to develop, deliver, install and implement automated Decision-making and 
Decision support Systems in all the 900 courts located  at capitals States and Union Territories. It will 
help the judicial administration in streamlining its routine activities, reduce the pendency of case by 
implementing appropriate automated process, and pr ovide easy and transparen t information access to 
litigant public. 

 The objectives of the Scheme are :— 
 (i) To help the judicial administration of the District and Subordinate Courts of States 
and Union Territories in streamlining their day to day activities,  
 (ii) To assist the judicial administra tion in reducing the pendency of cases. 
 (iii) To provide transparency of information to the litigants,  
 (iv) To enhance access of judges to legal and judicial databases. 
8.59 The plan scheme of the Government - Ce ntrally Sponsored Scheme for Development of 
Infrastructure in Judiciary includes construction of  court buildings and residential accommodation for 
Judges/Judicial Officers, covering High Courts and subordinate Courts. The Central Government’s 
share is restricted to the funds made available by  the Planning Commission and the expenditure under 
the Scheme is hared by the Central and State Governments on 50:50 basis. 
8.60 The Union Ministry of Law and Justice also proposes to provide for the establishment of Gram 
Nyayalayas at the Panchayats at intermediate level. The Gram Nyayalayas will have both criminal and 
civil jurisdiction. The Gram Nyayalayas will also have jurisdiction to try all original suits of civil 
nature as the lowest courts. This will address th e problem of access to justice to the women and rural 
masses who have no means of seeking redressal through the existing judicial process. 
8.61 The Supreme Court in the Salem Bar Association Vs Union of India has given certain directions 
to the Central and State Governments with respect to providing funds for the creation of more courts 
and anpower for the Judiciary and also to assess the impact of legislation upon the courts. The 
directions have been circulated to the States and Departments and the Ministry of Law and Justice is 
in the process of finalizing its views. 
Role of Registry of Supreme Court 
8.62 The Committee, in its meeting held on 4th April, 2006 observed as under:- 
"........ The Supreme Court Regist ry can call for the data keep ing in view th e subordinate 
courts also. We need not depend upon the pre ss reports, where there is a comment that the 
lower courts are not having even a manual typewriter. There is no need for agitation. These 
types of reports need not come. This is the work of the Ministry of Law. But we can have 
firsthand information from the Supreme Court Registry regarding the subordinate judiciary 
in this matter. It can make a mega plan and present it to the Committee. We will study it and 
submit in our eport so that the Parliament and the Government can be asked to deal with that 
subject. That is why we want everything to be sorted out here within the four walls of this 
Parliamentary system instead of having everything being commented upon by the press and 
media. That is the roblem we are facing. That is  why we want to thrash out the points within 
the four walls so that we can think about it an d we can have some arrangement for it. In the 
same way, the Chief Justices, the Law Ministers and others say about the pending litigations, 
accumulation of cases and huge a rrears of cases. All these things can be made up in a better 
way as a report and if it comes from the Supreme Court Registry, the Committee and the 
Parliament can consider it. That will have the authenticity and that can be taken into 
consideration by the Parliament. That can be looked into and discussed.........". 
8.63 In view of the facts mentioned in the fo regoing paragraphs, the Committee is of the 
opinion that there should be so me body/agency which would appr ise the concerned authorities 
of the needs of the subordinate j udiciary, which are being neglected on some pretext or other at 
the macro level. The Committee expresses the desire that Government should evolve a 
mechanism for having an overall view of the needs/hindrances of the judicieary by inviting an in 
house deliberation. 

8.64 The Committee is seized of the fact that Chie f Justices Conferences ar e convened periodically 
by the Chief Justice of India. Heads of Judiciar y in the States meet and deliberate under the 
Chairmanship of the Chief Justice of India, in th e presence of two senior most Judges of the Apex 
Court and take policy decisions on the matters which are vital to and which materially affect the 
functioning of Judicial dministration. 
8.65 In that backdrop the Committee recommends th at a Conference of all Registries of the 
High Courts and Administrative  Heads of Subordinate/Lower co urts should be convened at 
least once in a year under the overa ll supervision of the Registry of the S.C. The authorities can 
ventilate the problems faced by them and narrate  the bottlenecks, while participating in such 
fora. This would ensure that the benefits would percolate down to the lower Courts also. In 
order to achieve this object ive, the Committee recommends that the Supreme Court Registry 
can call for the data, keeping in view the needs of the subordinate Courts also. 
Role of Ministry of Law and Justice 
8.66 The Committee further recommends that the De partment of Justice, Ministry of Law and 
Justice can provide necessary infrastructure and funds for convening such conferences annually 
on the line of seminars of Collectors/District Magistrates being organize d by the Ministry of 
Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions. Wh en such efforts are made, the Ministry can 
consider the needs of the lower judiciary in a better and effective manner and place them before 
the Committee for consideration. The Committee is confident that these discussions would 
certainly prove to be a milestone for the Departme nt of Justice for formulating policy decisions 
in the matter of Judicial Reforms. 
JUDICIAL REFORMS 
8.67 After introduction of economic reforms and globalisation and revolutionary technological 
innovations, new challenges and complexities have cropped up before Indian Judicial System 
imposing enormous urden upon the courts. As the so ciety has become more aware, the law, the image 
of the court system and administration of justice suffer a loss of  public confidence. In this context, it 
has become all the more important that the grey areas confronting Indian Judiciary need prompt 
attention. 
8.68 The Committee feels that it is time to consid er application of the principles and techniques 
of modern management in solving some prom inent problems like law’s inordinate delays, 
arrears of cases and increased workflow in the court system. This requires scientific study of the 
problems. For this, the judiciary is required to develop skills to manage its resources, setting 
targets and evolving concepts like case management and time  management. Simultaneously 
there is need to increase output. 
8.69 The Committee has been informed that the Judges in the United Kingdom and the United States 
of America has applied some innovative methods. These methods have been successful in certain 
areas. For xample, Annual Judicial Statistics Report, published by the Chief Justices in the United 
Kingdom and the United States every year, help to enable the public to understand the performance of 
the judiciary. imilarly, in the United States, every Bill is accompanied by Judicial Impact Statement 
describing the likely effect of the legislation upon the workload of the courts. The Committee also 
notes that the Electronic udicial Resource Management is gaining importance and it has been found 
the most effective tool for judicial  reforms. In Indian context, no new legislation is required as the 
Information Technology Act 2000 has already incorporated the requisite provisions. 
8.70 The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlie r (Para 8.48, 6th Report) view that the above 
measures would have significant impact on ref orming the Indian Judi cial System and the 
Ministry should, explore the possibility of introducing these methods. 

ALL INDIA JUDICIAL SERVICE 
8.71 During the discussion a point was raised regarding setting up of All India Judicial Service. In a 
written reply, the Committee was informed that the 1st, 8th and 11th Law Commissions had inter-alia 
recommended the formation of All India Judicial  Service. The Supreme Court of India also 
recommended vide its judgments delivered on 13th November, 1991 in W.P. No. 1022 of 1989 and 
24th August, 1993 in Review Petition No. 249/92 in the matter of All India Judges Association for 
setting up of an All India Judicial Service. 
 The Ministry has also stated that as the creati on of an All India Service requires cooperation of 
the State Governments and the High Courts, their views were sought earlier. Most of the States did not 
support creation of All India Judicial Service. The National Commission to Review the Working of 
the Constitution submitted its report on this matter stating that after examining the responses received 
from the public and after detailed deliberations, the Commission decided that the formation of All 
India Judicial Service would not be a better alternat ive to the present system. In the light of these 
observations of the National Commission, the matter of formation of All India Judicial Service could 
not be proceeded with. The Government has further stated that as the matter of creation of an All India 
Service is gaining momentum, the proposal for setting up of an All India Judicial Service is proposed 
to be re-examined on merits after ascertaining views of the all concerned including consultations with 
Hon’ble Chief Justice of India and the State Governments. 
8.72 In view of the position explained by the Ministry the Committee recommends that all out 
efforts, including the amendment in Constitution, if necessary, may be taken up expeditiously to 
set up All India Judicial Service. The Committ ee presumes that the consultations with the 
Ministry of Finance, Mi nistry of personnel, Public Grievan ces and Pensions and also the UPSC 
would be necessary in the matter.

action for changing the names of the Madras, Calcutta and Bombay High Courts in line with the 
official names of these metropolitan cities. The Committee feels that this is an administrative 
matter and must be changed as early as poss ible as recommended by the Committee in its 
previous report.
