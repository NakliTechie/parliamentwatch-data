CHAPTER – I 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
MINISTRY 
1.0 The Ministry of Law and Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by 
the British Parliament. The key mandate of the Ministry includes providing policy support in 
administration of justice; furnishing legal advice to the Government of India and defending 
Government of India in various Courts and Tribunals and drafting Supreme and Subordinate 
legislations for Government of India, etc. 
1.1 The Ministry comprises the following three Departments: 
(i) Legislative Department; 
(ii) Department of Legal Affairs; and 
(iii) Department of Justice. 
 
 
1.2 The Ministry is presently under the overall charge of the Law and Justice Minister 
assisted by a Minister of State. Each of the three departments is administratively headed by 
an independent Secretary. 
1.3 This report pertains to the Department of Justice.

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
 
Functions of the Department 
 
1.4 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, functions of the 
department are as under: 
 
i. Appointment, resignation and removal of the Chief Justice of India and Judges 
of the Supreme Court of India; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of 
absence (including leave allowance), pensions and travelling allowances; 
ii. Appointment, resignation and removal etc. of Chief Justi ce and Judges of 
High Courts in States; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of absence 
(including leave allowances), pensions and travelling allowances; 
iii. Appointment of Judicial Commissioners and Judicial Officers in Union 
Territories; 
iv. Constitution a nd organization (excluding jurisdiction and powers) of the 
Supreme Court (but including contempt of such Court) and the fees taken 
therein; 
v. Constitution and organization of the High Courts and the Courts of Judicial 
Commissioners except provisions as to officers and servants of these courts; 
vi. Administration of justice and constitution and organization of courts in the 
Union Territories and fees taken in such courts; 
vii. Courts fees and Stamp duties in the Union Territories; 
viii. Creation of All India Judicial Service; 
ix. Conditions of service of District Judges and other Members of Higher Judicial 
Service of Union Territories; 
x. Extension of the Jurisdiction of a High Court to a Union Territory or exclusion 
of a Union Territory from the Jurisdiction of a High Court; 
xi. Legal Aid to the poor; 
xii. Administration of Justice; and 
xiii. Access to Justice, Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms. 
 
1.5 The Department of Justice is the administrative Department for the following 
institutions/autonomous bodies/policy initiatives: 
a) National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):- The National Legal Services 
Authority has been constituted under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. The 
Chief Justice of India (CJI) is the patron-in-chief and the second senior-most judge of 
the Supreme Court is the Executive Chairman of the Authority. NALSA monitors and 
evaluates implementation of legal aid programmes, and lays down policies, principles, 
guidelines and frames effective and economical schemes for the State Legal Services 
Authorities (SLSA) to implement the legal services programmes throughout the 
country. The State Legal Services Authorities, District Legal Services Authorities 
(DLSA), Taluk Legal Services Committees (TLSC), etc. provide free legal services to 
underprivileged litigants and organize Lok Adalats for speedy resolution of disputes. 

 
11 
The prime objective of NALSA is speedy disposal of cases and reducing the burden 
of the judiciary. 
b) Family Courts:- The Family Courts Act, 1984 was enacted with a view to providing 
for establishment of Family Courts by the State Governments in consultation with the 
High Courts to promote conciliation and secure speedy settlement of disputes relating 
to marriage, family affairs and related matters. In accordance with the provisions of 
Section 3 of the Act, the State Government has to mandatorily establish Family Court 
for every area in the State comprising a city or a town population of which exceeds 
ten lakh. In other areas of the States, the Family Courts may be set up if the State 
Governments deem it necessary. 
c) Special Courts for trial of crim inal cases involving elected MPs /MLAs:- In 
compliance to Hon’ble Supreme Court Judgment in Ashwini Kumar Vs UoI  (WP (C) 
699 of 2016)10 Special courts (02 Special Courts in Delhi, and 01 each in the State of 
Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, 
Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal) are functional for expeditious trial and disposal of 
criminal cases involving elected MPs/MLAs. 
d) Fast Track Special Courts: - In pursuance of the  Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 
2018, and direction of Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suo Motu Writ (Criminal) No. 1/2019 
dated 25th July, 2019, Union of India started a Centrally Sponsored Scheme in October 
2019 for setting up of 1023 Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs) including 389 
exclusive POCSO (e-POCSO) courts across the country for expeditious trial and disposal 
of pending cases related to rape and Protection of Children from Sexual Offences 
(POCSO) Act, 2012.  
e) National Judicial Academy (NJA):- The National Judicial Academy (NJA), Bhopal 
is an autonomous body established in 1993 under the Societies Registration Act, 
1860. This independent body functions with its office at the Supreme Court of India 
and its campus at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh. This is an Apex body which imparts 
judicial training to Judges/Judicial Officers of the country and provides facilities for 
training of min isterial officers working in the Supreme Court, study of court 
management and administration of justice in the State/Union Territories, organizing 
conferences, seminars, lectures and research in matters relating to court management 
and administration. The core objectives of the said Academy have been to foster 
development of national judiciary in the country and strengthen administration of 
justice, judicial education, research and policy formulation. 
f) National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms: The Mission was set up 
with twin objectives of increasing judicial access by reducing delays and arrears in the 
system and enhancing accountability at all levels through structural changes and 
setting performance standards and facilitating enhancement of capacities for 
achieving such performance standards. The schemes/initiatives under the Mission are 
Action Research and Studies on Judicial Reforms, Access to Justice for all, E-Courts 
Mission mode, Infrastructure development of Subordinate Judiciary, judicial 
performance evaluation, etc. 
 

 
12 
g) Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for Development of Infrastructure Facilities 
for the Judiciary: The government has been implementing a Centrally Sponsored 
Scheme (CSS) for development of infrastructure facilities for judiciary since 1993-94. 
The scheme covers construction of court buildings and residential accommodations 
for Judicial Officers/Judges of District and Subordinate Courts. The fund sharing 
pattern of the Scheme has been revised to 60:40 (Centre: State) and 90:10 (Centre: 
State) for the eight North-Eastern and three Himalayan States from the year 2015-
2016. In November, 2017, the Union Cabinet approved continuation of the Scheme up 
to 31st March, 2020 with an outlay of Rs 3,320 crore to be implemented in mission 
mode through the National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms. The 
Scheme has been extended till the current Financial Year, and an Expenditure Finance 
Committee Memorandum has been sent to the Ministry of Finance for extension of 
the Scheme with a financial outlay of Rs. 8640 crore for five years (2021-22 to 2025-
26). 
h) Computerization of District and Subordinate Courts: E-Courts Mission Mode 
Project (MMP) Phases – I and II: - This Project is being implemented by the 
Department under the guidance of e-Committee, Supreme Court of India primarily for 
universal computerization of all District and Subordinate Court complexes. Initiated 
in 2010, the Phase-I of the Project concluded at a total cost of Rs 639.14 crore. The 
Phase II of the Project with an outlay of Rs 1670 crore, commenced in August, 2015 
and was to be concluded in Financial Year 2019-20. However, an outlay of Rs. 250 
crore was sought under BE of FY (2020-21). The scheme includes components such 
as computerization of Courts, Wide Area Network (WAN) connectivity, Cloud 
connectivity in all Court complexes, use of solar energy in 5% of Court complexes, 
computerization of the Public Prosecutors’ Office, video-conferencing facility for 
jails, enhancement of ICT infrastructure of courts, digitization of old case records, 
computerization of judicial libraries, upgradation of application software, SMS based 
services, Touch Screen Kiosks, etc. 
i) Establishment of Gram Nyayalayas under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 :- The 
Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 has been enacted by the Parliament to provide for the 
establishment of the Gram Nyayalayas at the grass root level for the purpose of 
providing speedy and inexpensive access to justice to the citizens at their door steps. 
The Central Government meets fifty percent of the recurring expenses incurred on the 
setting up of these courts in the first three years, subject to a ceiling of Rs. 3.20 lakh 
per Gram Nyayalaya per annum. 
j) Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice In India (DISHA):- 
Department of Justice has created a new scheme Designing Innovative Solutions and 
Holistic Access to Justice  - DISHA for a period of five year 2021-2026 . To prioritize 
citizen-centric justice delivery system by integrating access to justice with 
technology, DISHA has the objective : -  
 To strengthen pre-litigation mechanism through Tele-Law; 
 To develop an effective dispensation of pro bono legal services through 
Nyaya Bandhu programme;  

 
13 
 To facilitate disposal of pending cases in the courts through cadre of retired 
Judges called Nyaya Mitra; and 
 To empower citizens through pan India Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness 
programme .  
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 
1.6 The Supreme Court of India is at the apex of a pyramidal, unified and integrated 
judicial system. Though the political system of the country is federal in nature, the judicial 
system is unitary. The Supreme Court came into existence on 28th January, 1950 and it 
comprises the Chief Justice of India and 33 Judges. The strength of the apex court was 
enhanced over the years from 8 in 1950 to 11 in 1956, 14 in 1960, 18 in 1977, 26 in 1986, 31 
in 2009 and 34 in 2019, with the increase of case load on it. The Constitution of India 
provides for the Supreme Court to be a court of record, guardian of liberties & rights and also 
as the final arbiter in most disputes. It is an appellate court, can review its own judgement and 
in its advisory capacity may answer references by the President on any question of law of 
public importance. The Supreme Court is designated as the custodian of fundamental rights 
and the 'rule of law'. It has power of judicial review and the law declared by the Supreme 
Court is binding on all courts. 
1.7 The administrative wing of the Supreme Court is called 'Registry'. The Registry is 
divided into two wings - (i) Administrative Wing; and (ii) Judicial Wing. These wings are 
further divided into various Divisions, Branches, Sections and Cells. The Chief Justice of 
India is the Administrative head of the Supreme Court. The highest Administrative Officer of 
the Supreme Court is the Secretary General, the post manned by a judicial officer of the rank 
of a District & Sessions Judge and is of the rank of Secretary to the Government of India. The 
Secretary General is assisted by Registrars, Officers on Special Duty, Additional Registrars, 
Deputy Registrars, officers of other lower ranks and subordinate staff. 
1.8 The Department of Justice is the nodal Department for the Supreme Court of India. 
There is a separate demand, Demand No. 67 pertaining to the Supreme Court of India, which 
is entirely charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be submitted to the vote 
of Parliament. However, Article 113 of the Constitution provides for those estimates to be 
discussed in either House of Parliament.

Vacancies in the Department of Justice 
 
3.0 The Department of Justice was separated from the Ministry of Home Affairs and set 
up as an independent Department under the Ministry of Law and Justice and was made 
operational w.e.f. 01.01.2010.  In the beginning it was decided that the manpower to be 
provided to the Department of Justice will be held on the common cadre with the Ministry of 
Home Affairs. In this joint cadre, all posts are first allotted to Home Affairs and thereafter as 
per the assessment of need  of the Department of Justice they allot the staff.   Under this 
arrangement, in the Department of Justice as against the sanctioned strength of 101, only 57 
officials are working resulting in vacancies to the tune of 44%.   
 
3.1 The Department of Justice h as apprised this Committee that at different times, the 
matter of posting sufficient strength of officials in the Department of Justice has been taken 
up with the Ministry of Home Affairs and other cadre controlling departments like DoPT.   
However, there c ontinues to be an acute shortage  of manpower in the Department. After 
becoming an independent Department, the matter of sanctioning additional posts was taken 
up with the Ministry of Finance and additional posts have also  been sanctioned by the said 
Ministry. However, these posts continued to remain vacant as adequate staff has not been 
posted by the Ministry of Home Affairs. This manpower shortage is having an impact on 
functioning of the Department, which is inter alia performing important functions like justice 
delivery and access to justice.  
 
3.2 The Committee notes that even after 12 years of its separation from the Ministry 
of Home Affairs  and becoming an independent Department , the Department of Justice 
is still dependent on the MHA for all its man power requirements. The vacancies in the 
Department are particularly staggering at the middle and operating level of SO, ASO 
(50% vacancy), UDC (100% vacancy), PA (100% vacancy) and Steno (100% vacancy).  
It is surprising that the DoPT is posting officers o f the rank of US and above directly to 
the Department, but other officials are posted by the MHA. There is hardly any 
justification for this type of cumbersome arrangement being followed in the case of the 
Department of Justice, where it is being given a s tep motherly treatment in this matter. 
The Department of Justice has been mandated with a significant role in appointment of 
judges, access to justice and justice delivery. Accordingly, it should be provided with 
sufficient resources and manpower to fulfil  its mandate. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Department of Justice should be given full autonomy i n case of 
personnel management and a ll cadre controlling authorities , including DoPT, should 
post all encadred posts directly to the Department of Justice. The Department of Justice 
is directed to bring this recommendation of the Committee to the notice of DoPT and 
MHA.

Designing Innovative Solutions and Holistic Access to Justice (DISHA) 
3.16 The Department of Justice has curated a unified pan-India Scheme namely ‘Designing 
Innovative Solutions on Holistic Access to Justice in India’ (DISHA) for the period 2021 -
2026 that endeavours to provide comprehensive, integrated, technology -based citizen-centric 
solutions on Access to Justic e. The Scheme consolidates and merges various programmes 
that include Tele -Law, Nyaya Bandhu, Nyaya Mitra and Legal Literacy & Awareness being 
implemented by the Department of Justice.  The Scheme was introduced in 2021 with a total 
outlay of Rs 250 crore for a period of 2021-2026. The approach of DISHA includes: 
(i) Strengthening of Pre -litigation advice and consultation at the grassroots – This 
approach is being implemented through leveraging indigenous technological tools and 
platforms that have presence in th e village or at gram panchayat level. This approach 
is being carried forward through DoJ’s programme called Tele -Law: Reaching the 
Unreached, where in people are empowered to seek pre –litigation and advice and 
consultation through Panel Lawyers via tele/v ideo conferencing facilities available at 
the Common Service Centres situated in the villages or through Mobile App. NALSA 
has presence only till the Taluk level. 
(ii) Pan India Dispensation framework for Pro Bono Delivery of Legal Services - This 
approach is be ing executed through Nyaya Bandhu (Pro Bono) Legal Services 
Programme where DoJ has four pronged strategies for unlocking the Pro bono 
potential in the country. This includes creating a database of pro bono lawyers, 
facilitating direct connect between the beneficiary and Pro Bono Lawyer though 
mobile / web based application and following up through an online monitoring 
mechanism; embedding culture of pro bono in Law Schools through Pro Bono Club 
Scheme and engaging with Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) an d Law firms 
including NALSA to build a consensus for a comprehensive policy on Pro Bono in 
the country.

CHAPTER - III - POLICIES, PROGRAMMES AND SCHEMES 
 
Vacancies in the Department of Justice 
 
1. The Committee notes that even after 12 years of its separation from the Ministry 
of Home Affairs and becoming an independent Department, the Department of Justice 
is still dependent on the MHA for all its man power requirements. The vacancie s in the 
Department are particularly staggering at the middle and operating level of SO, ASO 
(50% vacancy), UDC (100% vacancy), PA (100% vacancy) and Steno (100% vacancy).  
It is surprising that the DoPT is posting officers of the rank of US and above dire ctly to 
the Department, but other officials are posted by the MHA. There is hardly any 
justification for this type of cumbersome arrangement being followed in the case of the 
Department of Justice, where it is being given a step motherly treatment in this matter. 
The Department of Justice has been mandated with a significant role in appointment of 
judges, access to justice and justice delivery. Accordingly, it should be provided with 
sufficient resources and manpower to fulfil its mandate. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Department of Justice should be given full autonomy i n case of 
personnel management and a ll cadre controlling authorities , including Do PT, should 
post all encadred posts directly to the Department of Justice. The Department of Justice 
is directed to bring this recommendation of the Committee to the notice of DoPT and 
MHA.  
(Para 3.2) 
Vacancy of Judges and Pendency of Cases in Higher Judiciary 
 
2. The Committee observes that the judicial system of the country is plagued with 
perennial problems of vacancy and pendency, especially at the High Courts level and 
there is no end in sight for these problems. Both the problems are interwoven in a way 
that vaca ncies have a natural fall out on pendency of cases. The figures regarding 
vacancies and pendency are staggering. One has to acknowledge that the existing 
process is not working and it overwhelms us completely, it needs to be re-engineered.  
(Para 3.8) 
3. The Committee fails to understand that even if the date of vacancy of a 
particular post becomes known the day a Judge assumes charge, then why such a huge 
delay in first sending recommendations by the collegium and then its processing by the 
Government. The Committee has also observed a recent phenomenon that because of 
delays in appointments, many lawyers have started saying no to such appointments. No 
respectable lawyer is prepared to wait ad infinitum and thus there have been spate of 

 
42 
refusals by them. Th e Committee in its 87 th Report on inordinate delay in filling up of 
vacancies in the Supreme Court and High Courts have observed/recommended the 
following: 
".... The Committee expresses its deep sense of disappointment and anguish that 
the timelines laid i n the Second Judges Case and the MoP are not being adhered 
to and are violated with impunity which is leading to extraordinary and 
unacceptable delay in filling up of the vacancies. The Committee recommends that 
an institutional mechanism should be evolved , so that retirement of a particular 
Judge and appointment against the resultant vacancy is simultaneously completed. 
Such a mechanism will be in the interest of the judicial administration and its 
efficacy. For this to happen the timelines for completion of various stages of 
appointment process in all Constitutional Courts should not only be firmly laid in 
the Memorandum of Procedure but also needs to be scrupulously adhered to by all 
Constitutional authorities." 
(Para 3.9) 
4. Accordingly, the Committee reiterates its recommendation as referred above and 
also recommends that the entire process of appointment of Judges, particularly at the 
High Courts, should be relooked into. A workable and efficient process, including a 
proactive timeline should be drawn a nd scrupulously adhered to, by synergising the 
efforts of the Judiciary and the Government.  
(Para 3.10) 
Gram Nyayalayas 
5. The Gram Nyayalayas were envisaged to make justice delivery more accessible 
and affordable for the people at the grassroots.  However, even after more than 12 years 
of coming into force the Gram Nyayalayas are yet to take off in the country. Only 15 
states have notified them and about half of those are yet to be operationalized. This is in 
spite of the fact that financial assistance is being given to States, by the Centre, to 
operationalize them. The apathy of States and the challenges enumerated above by the 
Department has m ade this institution almost defunct. The Committee therefore, 
recommends the Department to ponder seriously over continuation of this scheme 
anymore. The funds under this may be diverted to some other new scheme(s) or any 
existing scheme, which is working well.  
(Para 3.15)

2. At the outset, the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee 
and the officials of the Department of Justice, NALSA, Supreme Court of India 
and National Judicial Academy attending the meeting on Demands for Grants 
(2022-23). He then a sked them to give a brief overview of the functioning and 
budgetary allocations made to them. 
3. The Secretary, Department of Justice in his opening remarks informed the 
Committee about the functioning of the Department and a presentation was 
made highlighting the issues related to the budgetary allocations, projections to 
Ministry of Finance, functioning and recent initiatives/ achievements of the 
Department. He apprised the committee of the status of infrastructural 
development of judiciary and vacancy positions of judges in higher judiciary. 
4. Thereafter, the Member -Secretary, NALSA made a presentation before 
the Committee informing about the organization and its budgetary allocations, 
NALSA mobile app, Lok Adalats and other initiatives taken by the NALS A in 
the preceding year. 

 
49 
5. After that, the Registrar, Supreme Court of India apprised the Committee 
about their budgetary allocations, disposal of cases by the Supreme Court and 
about various IT services being provided by the Supreme Court. 
6. Thereafter, the Director, National Judicial Academy, informed the 
Committee about the training programmes conducted by them during 2021 -22, 
including training of foreign trainees and the budgetary allocations made to the 
Academy. 
7. After the presentations, some quer ies were raised by the Chairman and 
Members relating to budgetary allocation of the Departments/Organization's 
actions, time-line of live streaming of cases, break -up and pendency of cases in 
courts, Lok -Adalats and Fast -track Courts, delay in appointment of judges, 
infrastructural development of courts, data on judges of reserved categories, 
backlog of vacancies in Courts and ITAT, judicial accountability, operationality 
of Gram Nyayalayas and Nyaya Mitras, roadmap to establishmend of legal aid 
clinics, confinement of virtual courts and e -Sewa Kendras to some  States only, 
revision of fees of  panel lawyers of State Legal Services Authority. The 
Chairman of the Committee asked them to furnish the detailed note/replies in 
writing within three working days. 
(Witnesses then withdrew) 
8. Thereafter, the Chairman welcomed the Secretary, Department of Legal 
Affairs, President, Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Director, Indian Law 
Institute and Chairman, Bar Council of India and other officers related to these 
Departments. 
9. The Secretary, Department of Legal Affairs made a presentation before 
the Committee giving a brief description about the Department, highlighting the 
work done in the preceding financial year. Thereafter, the President, ITAT, 
informed about its functioning, composition of  ITAT and recent appointment of 
the Members. Then, the Chairman, Bar Council of India apprised about the lack 
of social security for lawyers, poor  infrastructure and lack of funds for Bar 

 
50 
Council of India and State Bar Councils.  After that, the Director, Indian Law 
Institute, informed about the functioning of the Institute and requested the 
Committee  for recommending the Government for granting Institute of 
National Importance status to ILI. The Director, ILI also requested for 
enhancement of grants. 
10. After that, some queries were raised by the Members relating to backlog 
of vacancies in Department of Legal Affairs, vacancies in ITAT Branches and 
backlog of cases on ITAT, poor infrastructural facilities in legal institutes and  
stringent check on law schools, welfare of advocates and their insurance, lack of 
library and grant from Government to the Bar Council of India, , queries 
regarding the establishment of regional benches of Supreme Court, delay in 
establishment of International Arbitration Centre at New Delhi, non-functioning 
of 22 nd Law Commission, appointment of notaries. Replies to some of the 
queries were given by the respective officers. The Chairman of the Committee 
asked them to furnish the detailed note/replies in w riting within three working 
days. 
(Witnesses then withdrew) 
11.  Thereafter, the Chairman welcomed the Secretary, Legislative Department 
and Senior Deputy Election Commissioner, Election Commission of India and 
other officers present in the meeting. 
12. Thereafter, the Secretary, Legislative Department made a presentation 
before the Committee giving brief description about the Department, 
highlighting the work done by Department in previous years and the budgetary 
demands for the FY (2022 -23). Thereafter, t he Senior Deputy Election 
Commissioner, Election Commission of India made presentation before the 
Committee and apprised about their functions, budgetary allocations, 
projections to Ministry of Finance and recent initiatives taken by them. 
13. After the pr esentations, some queries were raised by the Members 
relating to electoral reforms, amendment in Tenth Schedule, acceptability of

Electronic Voting Machines, errors in voter's list, lack of manpower during 
elections, non -following up of the electoral offen ces registered during the 
elections. Replies to some of the queries were given by the respective officers. 
The Chairman of the Committee asked them to furnish the detailed note/replies 
in writing within three working days. The Chairman, then, thanked all t he  
members and witnesses of the Committee. 
(Witnesses then withdrew) 
14. Verbatim record of meeting of the Committee was kept. 
15. The meeting adjourned at 6.10 P.M.
