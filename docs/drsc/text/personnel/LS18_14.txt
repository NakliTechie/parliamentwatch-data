FOR GRANTS OF THE MINISTRY
1.0 The Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions is the coordinating agency of
the Central Government in personnel related matters especially in respect of the issues concerning
recruitment, training, career development, staff welfare as well as post-retirement dispensation. The
Ministry is also concerned with promotion of responsive, people-oriented, modern administration.
The Ministry comprises three Departments, namely:—
(i) The Department of Personnel and Training;
(ii) The Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare; and
(iii) The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.
1.1 The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister assisted by a Minister of
State. All the three Departments of the Ministry function under the charge of Secretary who is
presently assisted by four Additional Secretaries, three Joint Secretaries and other supporting
officers and staff. The Ministry is divided into seven Divisions, each of which is headed by an
officer of the level of Joint Secretary or above. The Divisions are:
(i) Establishment Officer;
(ii) Services and Vigilance;
(iii) Establishment;
(iv) Administrative Tribunal and Administration;
(v) Training;
(vi) Pension and Central Services; and
(vii) Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.
1.2 The Ministry has under its administrative control four attached offices, namely:
(i) Staff Selection Commission (SSC);
(ii) Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM);
(iii) Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie; and
(iv) Central Bureau of Investigation.
1.3 The other offices under the administrative control of the Ministry are:
(i) Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), a constitutional body;
(ii) Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), a statutory body;
(iii) Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), a statutory quasi-judicial body; and
(iv) Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a body set up by a resolution of the
Central Government.

DEMAND NO. 76 (UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION)
1.16 The Union Public Service Commission is a Constitutional body, created under Article 315
of the Constitution. The Commission’s charter of duties is given in Articles 320 and 321 of the
Constitution. In brief, it is the duty of the Commission to conduct examinations for appointment
to services of the Union and to advise Government on all matters relating to framing of recruitment
rules, method of recruitment and the principles to be followed in making promotions and transfers
from one service to another. The Commission is also required to be consulted in respect of
disciplinary matters affecting a civil servant including appeals and memoranda filed by individual
Government servants.
1.17 Article 322 of the Constitution envisages, inter-alia, that the expenses of the UPSC
including salaries, allowances and pensions payable to members and staff of the Commission shall
be ‘charged’ on the Consolidated Fund of India.

CHAPTER–II
MAJOR INITIATIVES AND SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY
Right to Information Act, 2005
2.0 The Secretary, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions while making
presentation on the Demand for Grants (2006-07) of the Ministry, deposed before the Committee
that according to their latest reports, 20 State Governments had already implemented the Right to
Information Act.
2.1 As per the Annual Report of the Ministry (2005-06), seven workshops were conducted at
the Institute of Secretariat Training and Management on Right to Information Act for Public
Information Officers and Assistant Public Information Officers nominated from various Ministries/
Departments to the Government of India.
2.2 The Committee appreciates the setting up of the Central Information Commission.
The Committee also appreciates the initiatives taken by the Ministry in this regard. The
Committee recommends that the Ministry should ensure that all Ministries/Departments/
Organisations do implement the provisions of the Right to Information Act, 2005 in its true
letter and spirit. The Committee also recommends that a mechanism should be put in place
to monitor and evaluate its status of implementation. Right to Information Act is a
milestone in the efforts made to promote transparency and accountability in the working of
public authorities. Therefore, the Ministries should persuade/motivate all the Ministries/
Departments/Organisations to implement pr ovisions of the Act. At the same time, the
Government should also ensure that the provisions of the Act are not misused, failing which
the very purpose of the Act will be defeated.
Training Policy and Programmes
3.0 In the era of globalisation, with the advent of latest information technology methods,
enriching of human resources has become an imperative need. Training and capacity building have
emerged as an important prerequisite for an efficient administration.
3.1 On the Cadre Training Plan, the Annual Report of the Ministry states that a Committee
of Senior Officers was set up in February, 2001 to devise a Comprehensive Training Plan for
CSS Officers and that its Report was submitted in February, 2005 making far reaching
recommendations.
3.2 The Ministry, in its reply on the issue, inter alia, stated that the recommendations were
examined in details by the Department and were accepted by the Government with certain
modifications. It was also stated that the Training Division of the Department and the ISTM had
been directed to provide necessary training to the officers at various levels in the CSS and that
the training programmes are likely to start in the current financial year.
3.3 The Committee is pleased to note that some initiatives have been taken in the
direction of implementation of its recommendations. The Committee recommends that the
training programmes should be started without further delay. The Committee hopes that its
recommendations would be implemented and pursued in order to ensure career growth and
development of CSS officers.

Welfare of pensioners
4.0 As per the Annual Report of the Ministry (2005-06), the Ministry/Departments are having
a variety of mechanisms for redressal of pensioners’ grievances, which are not uniform and
compatible with each other. The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ welfare has, accordingly,
taken up a proposal for putting in place a “Pensioners Portal” which would adopt a uniform system
by harnessing IT resources.
4.1 The Committee is pleased to note that a good proposal has been initiated for the
guidance and welfare of the pensioners. The Committee recommends that the Ministry
should ensure that the proposal is implemented in a meaningful, effective and responsive
manner.
Information and Facilitation Counters
5.0 It has been stated in the Annual Report of the Ministry that computerised Information and
Facilitation Counters (IFCs) have been set up to provide information and assistance to the public
regarding the programmes and procedures of the concerned organisations as well as
acknowledgment and status report of the individual applications and grievances. Evaluation studies
of these IFCs are undertaken at random basis for suggesting further improvement in their
functioning.
5.1 The Secretary, Ministry of Personnel deposed before the Committee as follows:
“...Information for citizens is an important aspect of democratic functioning of the
Government and, Sir, in order to achieve this, we have been instrumental in getting
Information and Facilitation Counters set up in different Central Government Ministries and
Departments. And, so far, till the end of last year, 106 such facilitation counters were set
up in various Ministries in the Government of India...’’
5.2 The Committee appreciates this initiative of the Ministry and is of the opinion that
the suggestions made after completing the evaluation studies of these Information and
Facilitation Counters should be implemented in totality and should not remain confined to
paper alone.
Civil Services Reforms
6.0 As per the Annual Report of the Ministry, the UPSC on the 19th July, 2000 had constituted
the Yoginder Kumar Alagh Committee to review the existing scheme of the Civil Services
Examination. The said Committee has since submitted its report and its recommendations are under
Government’s examination.

10
6.1 The reply of the Ministry regarding the action taken so far on implementation of the
recommendations of the Alagh Committee is as follows:—
“(i) The salient points/recommendations of the Committee inter alia pertain to the eligibility
parameters of candidates appearing for Civil Services, schemes of Preliminary and
Main Examinations, Personality Test, allocation of Services and post-induction issues
of training and management of services.
(ii) The Government has set up an Administrative Reforms Commission to prepare a
detailed blue print for revamping the public administration System. The Commission,
while finalizing its recommendations, shall take into account the recommendations
made in the past by various Committees including the Alagh Committee.”
6.2 The Committee takes a serious note that the reply is not in consonance with the
Committee’s observation as contained in its 5th Report (page No. 43, para 3.41) that the
appointment of the Second Administrative Reforms Commission should not come in the way
of implementation of the recommendations made by various Committees in the past. The
Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier considered view that the recommendations of
the Alagh Committee should be examined and implemented expeditiously; otherwise it will
be a callous wastage of public money, time and energy of the Committee which had worked,
hard to make recommendations as per its mandate.
Citizen’s Charter
7.0 As per the Annual Report of the Ministry, the Citizen’s Charter initiative was started by
Government of India in the year 1996-97. In the Chief Ministers’ conference held in May 1997,
it was decided that Central Government and State Governments would formulate Citizen’s Charters
starting with those organisations which have a large public interface.
7.1 As on 30th November 2005, 30 Ministries/Departments have brought out 111 Citizen’s
Charters and 24 State Governments/UT Administrations have brought out 656 Citizen’s Charters.
7.2 The Ministry has furnished the following reply regarding the present status of its
implementation:—
“(i) The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, being the nodal
Department for the Citizen’s Charter initiative is constantly pursuing with Central
Government Ministries/Departments/Organizations and State Government/UT
Administrations regarding formulation and implementation of the Citizen’s Charters. The
Central Government Ministries/Departments and State Governments/UT Administrations
are regularly impressed upon to formulate Citizen’s Charters in case of Departments/
Organizations where no Citizen’s Charter has been formulated so far. A Core Group
under the Chairmanship of the Secretary, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and
Pensions with the Secretary, Department of Consumer Affairs; the Principal Information
Officer and the Director General of National Informatics Centre (NIC) as Members and
the Additional Secretary, Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances
(DARPG) as Member-Secretary is functioning in the Government of India to examine
and review the Citizen’s Charters of Central Government Ministries/Departments/
Organizations. Till date, 111 Charters of Central Government Ministries/Departments/
Organisations have been formulated and reviewed. Similarly 656 Charters of State

RECRUITMENT AGENCIES
17.0 The Union Public Service Commission and the Staff Selection Commission are the two
designated recruitment agencies of the Central Government. The UPSC makes recruitment for
Group A services/posts and Group ‘B’ gazetted posts in Ministries/Departments etc. SSC makes
recruitment for non-gazetted posts in Ministries/Departments and their attached and subordinate
offices, carrying the pay scale of Rs. 6500-10,500 and non-technical Group ‘C’ posts. While the
UPSC is a Constitutional body set up under Article 315 of the Constitution, the SSC has been set
up by a Resolution of the Government and has the status of an attached office of the Department
of Personnel and Training.

18.3 There is no doubt about the Constitutional status of the UPSC. Under Article 323 of the
Constitution, the UPSC presents its Annual Reports to the President as to the work done by the
Commission, who in turn causes a copy thereof to be laid before each House of the Parliament.
Such reports are also debated in the Parliament from time to time. On the 22nd of December, 1954
the Rajya Sabha discussed the Annual Reports of the UPSC for the years 1951-52 and 1952-53.
18.4 Again on the 16th March, 1956 the House discussed the Annual Reports of the UPSC for
the years 1953-54 and 1954-55. The then Deputy Chairman had ruled that the discussions on the
motion would be restricted to criticism of the Government for any acts or omissions in the
exercise of their functions under the Constitution in relation to the UPSC and all criticisms of the
actions of the Commission or of its members will have to be avoided.
18.5 It is pertinent to note that the Committee on Subordinate Legislation of the Rajya Sabha had
examined the Union Public Service Commission (Exemption from Consultation) Regulations, 1958.
The Estimates Committee of the Lok Sabha examined the working of the UPSC in 1967-68. In the
introduction chapter of the 47th Report of that Committee on the subject, as well as in the Minutes
of its meetings held on 24th and 25th October, 1967, it has been stated that the Committee had
taken oral evidence of the then Chairman as well as the Secretary, UPSC.
18.6 Further, the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, while
drafting its 20th Report on the Working of the UPSC, heard the representatives of the UPSC on
the subject, in its sitting held on the 11th February, 1994. In its sitting held on the 26th May, 1994,

20
the Committee further discussed the subject and sought clarifications from the representatives of
the UPSC. Further the Committee, in its sitting held on 19th July, 1994 sought, clarifications from
the representatives of the UPSC. The Committee on Home Affairs in its 20th Report had inter alia
observed that “the UPSC has been impressing upon the Government to treat the Commission as
a Ministry for financial purposes and to delegate the financial powers accordingly. In other words,
it favours the financial autonomy as enjoyed by other Constitutional bodies, namely, the Election
Commission and the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India...The Committee has also been
informed by UPSC that in this regard UPSC is treated as an Attached/Subordinate Office of the
Department of Personnel and Training in the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions
resulting in a situation when the UPSC is sometimes constrained to take decisions which are not
compatible with its Constitutional position.”
18.7 The Committee on Home Affairs had, therefore, made the following recommendation: “The
Committee finds no reason whatsoever for discrimination of the UPSC with other Constitutional
bodies, namely the Election Commission and the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India in
financial matters and feels that in this respect UPSC should enjoy the same status as other
Constitutional bodies.”
18.8 Regarding monitoring agency for UPSC, the Committee on Home Affairs had observed as
under: “A suggestion was mooted that there should be an independent body to critically examine,
scrutinize, monitor and supervise the functioning of the UPSC and to deal with all complaints and
representations against it as the present arrangement authorizing the UPSC to examine its own
failures may lead to inefficiency and miscarriage of Justice. The Committee having considered all
aspects of the suggestions found it to be impracticable. The UPSC is a Constitutional body and
in the scheme of the Constitution relating to UPSC there is no provision for an independent body
to examine and monitor its functioning. However, under Article 323, the Commission is required
to present annually to the President a Report as to the ‘Work done by it and the President causes
a copy of the Report to be laid before each House of Parliament, thus providing ample scope for
thorough discussion of the Commission’s functioning by Parliament. The Parliament through the
discussion can also make suitable observations.”
18.9 Taking into account the facts mentioned in the preceding paragraphs, the Committee
notes that by granting Constitutional status to certain bodies/organisations, the Constitution
of India has not deprived the Parliamentary body of examining/scrutinizing its working/
functioning within the permissible “limits particularly when such examination is in the
larger interest of that Constitutional body. The Committee desires to convey its view that
it regards and sanctifies the status of UPSC as a Constitutional body, but at the same time
it can hardly satisfy itself with this contention as is being interpreted in their own manner.
18.10 The Committee notes that the refusal of UPSC for sending representative of its Secretariat
to appear before the Committee amounts to shirking of its accountability to public through
Parliament. Both the Houses of Parliament are represented in the Committee which is known as a
mini Parliament. Had they attended the meeting of the Committee, they would have been able to
narrate/justify the Demands for Grants and activities described in the Annual Report submitted to
the Parliament for discussion.
18.11 The Committee is not at all convinced with the contention of the UPSC Secretariat
regarding its accountability to the Parliament. The Committee urges that the Annual
Reports of the UPSC are placed before both the Houses of Parliament for discussions and
takes cognizance of the fact that the Minister as an executive head of Government, on
behalf of the UPSC, answers the Questions in both the Houses. In view of the foregoing,
the Committee is of the view that as per the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business
in the Rajya Sabha, the Committee is well within its powers and jurisdiction to examine the

if any, of the Committee in respect of the activities as mentioned in their Annual Report.
The Committee is, therefore, unable to examine the Demands for Grants of UPSC due to
the wilful avoidance of the Secretariat of UPSC, which avoidance ipso facto is against public
probity and contravenes Constitutional norms.
18.12 This Committee finally observes that the Demands for Grants of a particular financial
year of a Constitutional body can be examined in a better and effective manner if
representatives of the Secretariat of that Body depose before the Committee to throw light
on the areas/operations which may need more finances. Ultimately, scrutiny of Demands for
Grants in the presence of the representatives of UPSC Secretariat would help it in the long
run to make an assessment and evaluation of its fund starved plans/polices/programmes.
18.13 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should deliberate on the
issue with all sincerity at an appropriate level and take corrective measures to restore its
accountability towards people through Parliament.
STAFF SELECTION COMMISSION
19.0 As per Article 320 of the Constitution, the examinations for recruitment to all the posts and
services of the Union Government are to be conducted by the UPSC. As per recommendations of
the Estimates Committee (1967-68) of the Lok Sabha regarding setting up a separate Service
Selection Commission for taking over from UPSC, examination for recruitment to lower category
of posts, the Government made an interim measure by creating an examination wing under ISTM.
Finally on 26th September, 1977 a separate Commission SSC came into being. Later on, pursuant
to the recommendation of Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs
(20th Report) wherein the Government was requested to identify more areas where consultation
with UPSC could be dispensed with so as to decrease the work load of UPSC, certain
modifications were made in the UPSC (Exemptions from Consultation) Regulations, 1958 so as to
transfer some more areas from UPSC to SSC.
19.1 The S taff Selection Commission has been entrusted with the duty to make recruitment to
Group ‘C’ (non-technical) and Group ‘B’ (non-gazetted-both technical and non-technical) posts in
various Ministries/Departments of Government of India, its attached and subordinate offices except
those for which recruitment is made by the Railway Recruitment Boards and Industrial
Establishments.
19.2 The Staff Selection Commission has nine Regional/Sub-Regional Offices in different parts
of the country. These Regional/Sub-Regional Offices are responsible for smooth and peaceful
conduct of the examinations. They provide administrative support to the Headquarters for
performing various activities involved in the smooth conduct of examinations, like receipt of
applications from the candidates, processing of these applications, data entry into computer and
then issue of Admit Cards (ACs) to the candidates for the examination, booking of venues at the
various centres in consultation with the District Authorities, sending of examination material (other
than centrally dispatched Test Papers) to the Centre Supervisors, appointment of invigilators and
Inspecting Officers at various examination centres. Further, Regional Offices are also responsible
for holding the interviews/Physical Endurance Test (PETs), wherever required, either at the
Regional Headquarters or at other major cities within the respective regions.
19.3 The Regional Offices are also responsible to contact the various Government of India
offices located in the States of their jurisdiction and collect the vacancies from these departments.
These vacancies are collected and intimated by the Regional Offices to the SSC Hqrs.

allocate the candidates to the user departments as per their requirement intimated to the Regional
Offices earlier. The policy decisions taken by the SSC Hqrs, which govern the recruitment/selection
are implemented by the Regional Offices in totality.

CENTRAL BUREAU OF INVESTIGATION
20.0 The CBI came into existence in the year 1941 with a rather limited role and responsibility
to investigate offences of bribery and corruption connected with transactions relating to War and
Supply Department, Government of India. Over the last 64 years, the organisation has evolved
from an anti-corruption agency to a multi-faceted, multi-disciplinary Central police-law enforcement
agency with capability, credibility and legal mandate to investigate and prosecute offences anywhere
in India. At present, CBI has the following Divisions:
(1) Anti Corruption Division;
(2) Economic Offences Division;
(3) Special Crimes Division;
(4) Directorate of Prosecution;
(5) Administration Division;
(6) Policy and Coordination Division; and
(7) Central Forensic Science Laboratory.
20.1 The CBI derives the power to investigate from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act,
1946 (DSPE). As per section 2 of the Act, DSPE has jurisdiction to investigate offences in the
Union Territories only. However, the jurisdiction can be extended by the Central Government to
other areas including Railway areas and States under section 5(1) of the Act, provided the State
Governments have accorded consent under section 6 of the Act.
20.2 During the discussion on Demands for Grants (2006-07) of the Ministry of Personnel,
Public Grievances and Pensions; the Director, CBI made a brief presentation before the Committee.
He narrated the mission and functions of CBI and the problems existing in the organisation.

been stated that a consolidated Plan Document for modernization of CBI is awaited from the CBI.
Their proposal is likely to include:
(i) Purchase of latest gadgetry like laptops, cellular phones, digital video cameras.
(ii) Purchase of vehicles.
(iii) Construction of 2nd phase of CBI Academy.
(iv) Replacement of existing distributed database architecture with central database.
20.4 The Committee is pleased to note that the recommendation as contained in its 5th
Report regarding the modernization of CBI has been seriously considered. The Committee
hopes that the consolidated plan document for modernization of CBI, which is awaited,
would be prepared and implemented at the earliest. The Committee commended the working
of CBI but were also of the opinion that much more needs to be done regarding
modernization in terms of investigation, interrogation and training.

(a) Providing essential commodities of daily needs to the customers at reasonable prices,
representing assured standards of quality and value for money.

CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION
22.0 The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) was established in 1964, as an apex body for
exercising general superintendence and control over vigilance administration, through the
Government of India Resolution of 11.2.1964. The main mandate of the Commission was based
on the report of the Committee on Prevention of Corruption, popularly known as the Santhanam
Committee. The establishment of the Commission was considered essential for evolving and
applying common standards in deciding cases involving lack of probity and integrity in
administration.
22.1 The Resolution empowered the CVC to undertake inquiry into any transaction in which a
public servant is suspected or alleged to have acted for an improper purpose or in a corrupt
manner irrespective of his or her status. Through subsequent ordinances and legislations, the
Government has added to the functions and powers of the Commission. Subsequent to the
directions of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the judgement in the Writ Petition filed in public interest,
by Shri Vineet Narain and others in Jain Hawala Case, the Government promulgated an Ordinance
in 1998. The Ordinance of 1998 conferred statutory status to the CVC and the powers to exercise
superintendence over functioning of the Delhi Special Police Establishment, and also to review the
progress of the investigations perta ining to alleged offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act,
1988 conducted by them.
22.2 In 1998, the Government introduced the CVC Bill in the Lok Sabha in order to replace the
Ordinance, though it was not successful. The Bill was re-introduced in 1999 and remained with
the Parliament till September, 2003, when it became an Act after being duly passed in both the
Houses of Parliament and with the President’s assent. The provisions of the Act include inquiries
into offences alleged to have been committed by certain categories of public servants of the Central
Government; corporations established by or under any central Act; Government companies;
societies; and local authorities owned or controlled by the Central Government; and for matters
connected therewith or incidental thereto. To give effect to the provisions of the Act of 2003, the
Commission exercises all powers and functions entrusted to it under the Government of India
Resolution No.24/7/64-A VD dated 11.2.1964, which are not inconsistent with this Act.
22.3 Three years have passed since the enactment of CVC Act of 2003, and prevention of
corruption continues to be the prime concern of the country. Recently, in response to a Public

28
Interest Litigation which was filed in the Supreme Court on account of the murder of Satyendra
Dubey, the Supreme Court directed the Government to designate a suitable machinery to act on
complaints from “whistle blowers” till such time as a suitable legislation was enacted to that effect.
The Central Government while implementing the directive of the Supreme Court, has reposed its
faith on the CVC. Through a Resolution dated 21st April, 2004 the Government has designated the
Central Vigilance Commission as the agency to act on complaints from “whistle blowers” till such
time as the Parliament passes a law on the subject. According to the Government Resolution on
“Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informer”, 2004 the Commission has been entrusted
with the additional responsibility of keeping the identity of the complainant secret and the power
to take action against complainants making motivated or vexatious complaints. There is no
restriction on jurisdiction of the Commission in the said Resolution.
22.4 As per the reply of the Ministry, the recommendations of the CVC to make the vigilance
set up more effective in the Central PSUs and Government Departments by appointment of full time
CVOs in Departments/Ministries is under consideration of this Department. Views/comments of
various Ministries/Departments have since been obtained and the implications are being examined.
22.5 The Committee recommends that the recommendations of the CVC regarding better
vigilance mechanism should be implemented without further delay.
22.6 The Committee has taken a serious view that no reply has been furnished by the
Ministry on its query on the status of implementation of the suggestions of CVC regarding
unauthorised constructions in Delhi. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
suggestions should be examined and the outcome thereof be informed to the Committee,
along with the action Government proposes to take in the matter.
