INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, 
Public Grievances, Law and Justice, having been authorized by the Committee to submit the Report 
on its behalf, do hereby present this One Hundred Sixty Third Report on Demands for Grants 2026-
27 pertaining to the Legislative Department (Ministry of Law and Justice). 
2. In accordance with the constitutional requirement under Article 113, the estimated 
expenditure of Ministries/Departments of Government of India projected under various Demands 
for Grants for the upcoming financial year need to be voted by Parliament. As a sequel thereto, 
Demands for Grants of the relevant Ministries/Departments stand referred to concerned 
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee to make a close scrutiny thereof under Rule 
270 of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council of States. While making scrutiny 
of Demands for Grants, the Committee has made an appraisal of performance, programmes, 
policies of the Legislative Department vis-à-vis expenditure made out of Consolidated Fund of 
India in the current Financial Year. 
3. In the Report, scrutiny of Demand Nos. 65 and 66 pertaining to the Legislative Department 
and the Election Commission of India, for the Financial Year 2026-27 has been made by the 
Committee. 
4. During the course of examination of the above Demands, the Committee heard the views of 
the Secretary, Legislative Department (Ministry of Law and Justice), Sr. Deputy Election 
Commissioner, Election Commission of India (ECI) along with senior Officers of the Department 
and ECI in its sittings held on the 19th February, 2026.  
5. The Committee, while making its observations/recommendations, has mainly relied upon 
the following:-  
(i) Presentations made by the Secretary, Legislative Department;  
(ii) Presentations made by senior Officers of the Election Commission of India;  
(iii) Detailed Demands for Grants of the Legislative Department and the Election 
Commission of India (ECI) for the Financial Year 2026-27;  
(iv) Detailed Explanatory Notes on the Demands for Grants 2026-27 received from the 
Legislative Department and Election Commission of India (ECI);  
(v) Annual Report of the Ministry of Law and Justice for the Year 2025-26;  
(vi) Written replies furnished by the Legislative Department and the Election 

iii  
Commission of India (ECI) to the Questionnaires sent by the Committee Secretariat; 
(vii) Written clarifications to the points/issues raised by Members in the meeting of the 
Committee. 
6. The Report is based on facts, figures and submissions (both oral and written) tendered by 
the Officers of the Legislative Department (Ministry of Law and Justice) and the Election 
Commission of India (ECI) to the Committee. 
7. The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its sitting held on the 12th March, 
2026 and presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 16th March, 2026.

1.9 Legislative Department does not have any statutory or autonomous body under its control.  
It has two other wings under it, namely, the Official Languages Wing (OLW) and Vidhi Sahitya 
Prakashan (VSP), which are responsible for propagation of Laws and Legal literatures in Hindi and 
other Official Languages. 
(i) Official Languages Wing is a successor organisation of the Official Languages 
(Legislative) Commission under the Legislative Department and is responsible for 
preparing and publishing standard legal terminology and for translation into Hindi of 
all the Bills to be introduced in Parliament, as well as all Central Acts, Ordinances, 
and subordinate legislation as required under the Official Languages Act, 1963. The 
Official Languages Wing is also responsible for arranging translation of the Central 
Acts, Ordinances, etc., into the Official Languages specified in the Eighth Schedule 
to the Constitution as required under the Authoritative Texts (Central Laws) Act, 
1973. The Official Languages Wing also provides grants-in-aid to various registered 
voluntary organisations engaged in the promotion and propagation of Hindi and 
other regional languages and to organisations directly engaged in the publication of 
legal literature and the propagation of Hindi and other languages in the field of law. 
Functions of Official Languages Wing:- 
a) Preparation of authoritative texts in Hindi of all legislations prepared by the 
Legislative Department; 
b) Preparation of Hindi texts of all statutory Notifications under Section 3(3) of 
the Official Languages Act, 1963;  
c) Work relating to the Coordination Committee of Hindi Speaking States for 
ensuring effective coordination among committees such as the Hindi 
Salahkar Samiti and the Official Languages Implementation Committee for 
the evolution of uniform legal phraseology and model standard clauses in 
Hindi and their publication thereof; 
d) Publication of diglot editions of Central Acts and their regional language 
versions (with legislative history) and popularisation thereof; 
e) Preparation and publication of a standard legal glossary of words and phrases  
in English, Hindi and Regional Languages. 
f) During the year 2025, Hindi version on 35 Central Acts have been published 
in the Official Gazette as Authoritative Texts.

 (ii) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan is a wing under the Legislative Department, Ministry of 
Law and Justice. It was established in the year 1968 with the objective of providing 
Hindi text of the judgments delivered in English by the Supreme Court and various 
High Courts. Initially, it published only the judgments of the Supreme Court in 
Hindi. Subsequently, it also started translating judgments of various High Courts into 
Hindi. These publications are widely sought after in the Hindi-speaking States. The 
three publications, namely, Uchchatam Niyayalai Niranai Patrika, Uchcha Niyayalai 
Civil Niranai Patrika and Uchcha Niyayalai Dandik Niranai Patrika have been in 
publication since 1968, 1978 and 1979, respectively.  This Wing is also responsible 
for sale of official diglot edition of Central Acts, Constitution of India, Election 
Manual, Legal Glossary etc. 
Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan Functions:- 
a) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan (VSP) aims to provide Hindi text of the judgments 
in English passed by Hon’ble Supreme Court of India and various High 
Courts; 
b) Publication of High standard text books in Hindi on various subjects in the 
field of law; 
c)  To award various prizes for the best publications in Hindi in the field of law; 
d)  Sale of Hindi publications of the Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan and diglot editions 
Central acts, Constitution of India, Legal Glossary, Hindi Law Text Books 
and Election Manual; and 
e)  Promotion and propagation of Hindi in the legal field by holding 
conferences, training programmes, seminars and book exhibitions all over 
India  
(iii) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan publishes three Law Journals in Hindi: 
a. Uchchtam Nyayalaya Nirnaya Patrika (since 1968) ; 
b. Uchcha Nyayalay Civil Nirnaya Patrika (since 1978); and 
c. Uchcha Nyayalay Dandik Nirnaya Patrika (since 1979). 
1.10 The organisational set-up of the Legislative Department includes officers at the level of 
Secretary, Additional Secretary, Joint Secretary and Legislative Counsel, Additional Legislative 
Counsel, Deputy Legislative Counsel and Assistant Legislative Counsel, along with other support 

7  
staff. Work relating to legislative drafting in the case of principal legislation and the scrutiny and 
vetting of subordinate legislation has been distributed among various Groups. Each Group is headed 
by a Joint Secretary and Legislative Counsel or an Additional Secretary, as the case may be, who in 
turn are assisted by a number of Legislative Counsels at different levels. The Secretary of the 
Legislative Department acts as the Chief Parliamentary Counsel and the Additional Secretary is in 
charge of all subordinate legislation. 
 
1.11 Legislation is one of the major instruments of articulating the policy of the Government. In 
this context, the Legislative Department plays an important role to secure the policy objectives 
which the Government may wish to achieve through legislation. 
(i) Legislative Department not only performs functions as the primary Department for 
drafting the legislation initiated by the administrative Ministries and Departments but 
also initiates legislative proposals in respect of the matters with which it is 
administratively concerned. 
(ii) Legislative Department drafts the Finance Bill to give effect to the financial proposals 
of the Central Government every year.  This exercise is undertaken in the Legislative 
Department on the budget proposals being brought before it by the Ministry of 
Finance.   
(iii) For the purpose of convenience, the various subjects on which Bills are drafted in the 
Legislative Department at the behest of administrative Ministries/ Departments can be 
broadly categorised as under: - 
(a) Constitutional amendments; 
(b) Economic and corporate laws; 
(c) Civil Procedure and other social welfare legislation; 
(d) Criminal laws; 
(e) Repeal of obsolete laws; and 
(f) Miscellaneous laws. 
1.12 Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research (ILDR) was established in the Legislative 
Department, Ministry of Law and Justice in January, 1989 and it is an ISO 9001:2005 certified 
institute, to scale up the capabilities and availability of trained Legislative Counsel to deal with 
legislative proposals and to impart theoretical, clinical, practical knowledge as well as off-campus 
training in Legislative Drafting and Research to officers of Central Government Departments, State 
Government/Union Territories, Public Institutions and Government Bodies.

ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA 
1.13 The Legislative Department is the administrative Department for the Election Commission 
of India, an autonomous constitutional authority. Election Commission of India (ECI), since its 
establishment in 1950, has been instrumental in ensuring free, fair and transparent elections in 
India. Articles 324 to 329 of the Constitution of India define the functions, responsibilities, structure 
and powers of the ECI. These Articles mandate the Commission to conduct elections to the Lower 
and Upper Houses of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies and to the high offices of the 
President and Vice-President of India at regular intervals. 
(i) As per Article 324 of the Constitution of India, “The superintendence, direction and 
control of the preparation of the electoral rolls for, and the conduct of, all elections to 
Parliament and to the Legislature of every State and of elections to the offices of 
President and Vice-President held under this Constitution shall be vested in a 
Commission (referred to in this Constitution as the Election Commission)”.  
(ii) Election Commission currently consists of Chief Election Commissioner and two 
Election Commissioners. 
(iii) The President appoints Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners. 
They have tenure of six years, or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.  
 
1.14 The salary and other conditions of service of the Chief Election Commissioner of India and 
other Election Commissioners are governed by the “Chief Election Commissioner and Other 
Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023”. 
According to this Act, the salary, allowances and other conditions of service of the Chief Election 
Commissioner and other Election Commissioners shall be the same as those admissible to a Judge 
of the Supreme Court. The Commission has a separate Secretariat at New Delhi. Different divisions 
in the Commission are headed by Deputy Election Commissioners and Director Generals, who are 
officers of the rank of Additional Secretary/Joint Secretary to the Government of India. They are 
generally appointed on deputation with the Commission. 
1.15 The functions of the Election Commission under the Constitution, the Representation of the 
People Act, 1950 (43 of 1950) and this Act, or under the rules made thereunder may, subject to such 
general or special directions, if any, as may be given by the Election Commission in this behalf, be 
performed also by a Deputy Election Commissioner or by the Secretary to the Election 
Commission—Section 19A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. 
 

9  
1.16 It registers political parties and allots symbols to them and ensures inner party democracy in 
their functioning by insisting organizational elections at periodic intervals. Political parties 
registered with the Commission are granted recognition at the State and National levels on the basis 
of their poll performance at general elections according to criteria specified by it. It implements 
Model Code of Conduct (MCC) for smooth conduct of free and fair elections. It also observes 25th 
January as National Voters' Day to encourage young electors to take part in election process.  
1.17 The electoral expenses on account of conduct of elections to the House of the People and the 
State Legislative Assembly are borne by the Central Government and the concerned State 
Government, and in case of simultaneous elections, the expenditure is to be shared on 50:50 basis 
between Central and the concerned State Government. The initial expenditure is borne by the 
respective State Governments, and on submission of the audited report, the Central Government’s 
share is reimbursed through the Legislative Department.  
1.18 The Election Commission has its independent Secretariat for the work relating to the smooth 
conduct of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, and there is an exclusive demand 
(Demand No. 66), i.e. voted expenditure under which budgetary allocation is sought for running the 
activities of Election Commission of India. 
1.19 The Commission has purchased land from DDA at Sector-9, Dwarka, New Delhi for 
construction of additional office building for Election Commission. The budget earmarked by the 
Ministry of Finance under Capital Section includes nominal provision for carrying out pre-
construction activities for additional office building of ECI. Since, during next financial year 
nominal funds (₹1 lakh) have been earmarked.  Proposal for additional funds would be moved to 
the Ministry of Finance at appropriate time as soon as detailed construction plan is received from 
NBCC.

Repealing of Obsolete and Redundant Laws  
2.29 The Department in its written submission has stated that 1725 obsolete and redundant laws 
have been repealed by Government of India through various Repealing and Amending Acts, 
Finance and Taxation laws since 2014. This has reduced compliance burden on citizens and ensured 
ease of doing business and ease of living for citizens.  The Parliament had enacted the Repealing 
and Amending Act, 2025 (37 of 2025) to repeal certain enactments and to amend certain other 
enactments. Vide Second Schedule of the said Act, it has amended the Code of Civil Procedure, 
1908 (5 of 1908) and the General Clauses Act, 1897 (10 of 1897); and omitted section 213 of the 
Indian Succession Act, 1925 (39 of 1925) and consequential amendments are, inter alia, made in 
section 3 and 370 of the Act. 
2.30 The amendments in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and the General Clauses Act, 1897 
are made to meet the challenges faced in the light of the merger of Registered Post services with 
Speed Post.  The amendments in the Indian Succession Act, 1925 removed the discriminatory 
linkage of mandatory probate on the basis of religion of the testator and depending on geographical 
location of the property. The amendment in the Indian Succession Act, 1925 will reduce 
unnecessary court intervention, promotes uniform and fair applicability of succession laws across 
the country and is a major step towards ease of living.   Thus, by omitting section 213 of the Indian 
Succession Act, 1925 in its entirety, the Government has transformed probate from a mandatory 
requirement into an optional measure. 
2.31 The Committee appreciates the steps taken so far in this regard and desires that the 
Department may make further efforts to repeal obsolete and redundant laws through a 
regular and time-bound review mechanism and evolve a systematic framework for periodic 
identification of outdated statutory provisions in consultation with stakeholders. The 
Committee further desires that post-legislative impact assessments of major repealing and 
amending exercises be undertaken at regular interval to assess their effectiveness in reducing 
litigation, compliance burden and administrative delays. 
2.32 The Committee, during deliberations, noted concerns regarding extensive and time-bound 
legislative workload handled by the Department, including scrutiny of a large number of Bills, 
Cabinet Notes and subordinate legislations. In response, the Department informed the Committee 
that legislative drafting is undertaken from the executive perspective and is subsequently enriched 
through Parliamentary scrutiny, which provides a broader and 360-degree perspective. The 
Department further submitted that both principal and subordinate legislations undergo due scrutiny 

20  
and that the Legislative Department functions under considerable time-bound pressures, particularly 
during peak legislative sessions. 
2.33 The Committee appreciates the sustained efforts of the Department in managing a heavy 
legislative workload within strict timelines. The Committee also acknowledges the role of 
Parliament in strengthening legislative outputs through deliberation and value addition. 
2.34 The Committee is of considered view that the increasing volume and complexity of 
legislative work necessitates continuous strengthening of institutional capacity. The 
Committee desires that the Legislative Department undertake a comprehensive assessment of 
its workload, staffing pattern and skill requirements, particularly during peak legislative 
periods, and evolve structured internal review and quality control mechanisms. The 
Committee further desires that a clear roadmap be formulated for strengthening drafting and 
scrutiny processes so as to enhance drafting precision, ensure consistency between principal 
and subordinate legislation, and minimize avoidable litigation arising from drafting or 
interpretational ambiguities.

of 313 posts in Group ‘B’ (Gazetted and Non-Gazetted), 48 posts remain vacant. Further, out of the 
373 sanctioned posts in Group ‘C’, as many as 167 posts are vacant. The Committee also notes that 
13 posts out of 16 sanctioned posts of Canteen Staff are vacant. Overall, 264 posts remain vacant 
out of the total sanctioned strength of 847 posts, indicating a vacancy level of about 31%.  
3.9 The Committee is of the view that such persistent vacancies may adversely affect 
administrative efficiency and service delivery. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Commission should expedite the recruitment process in a time-bound manner to fill up 
the vacant posts and ensure optimal utilization of human resources so that the functioning of 
the Commission is not hampered due to manpower shortages. 
3.10 The Committee notes the decision of the Election Commission of India, as conveyed vide 
Press Note dated 24.06.2025, to undertake a Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of Electoral Rolls in 
the State of Bihar, aimed at ensuring inclusion of eligible electors, deletion of ineligible names and 
enhancing transparency in the electoral process. The Committee takes note of the proposed house-

25  
to-house verification by Booth Level Officers and the uploading of relevant documents on ECINET 
with restricted access, as well as the involvement of political parties through Booth Level Agents. 
3.11 The Committee is of the view that the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of Electoral Rolls 
should be carried out in a fair, transparent and inclusive manner, ensuring that no genuine elector is 
excluded and that electors are not subjected to undue hardship during the revision process. The 
Committee emphasises the need for adequate safeguards for vulnerable sections, including senior 
citizens, persons with disabilities, economically weaker sections and migrant populations.  
3.12 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Election Commission of India should 
put in place robust monitoring mechanisms at all levels to ensure uniform implementation of 
the revision process, while also ensuring strict adherence to data privacy and security 
protocols in respect of documents uploaded on the Election Commission Integrated Network 
(ECINET). These measures, in the view of the Committee, would help enhance transparency 
and strengthen public confidence in the integrity of the electoral rolls.  
3.13 The Committee observes that voters in remote, rural, tribal and hilly areas face significant 
practical difficulties in accessing polling stations, with electors in certain cases required to travel 
long distances, sometimes up to 10–15 kilometres. The Committee further notes that strict 
application of population-based norms for the location of polling stations in such difficult and 
sparsely populated areas may result in avoidable hardship to voters and could adversely impact 
voter participation, besides posing operational challenges for the election machinery. 
3.14 The Committee recommends that the Election Commission of India may adopt a 
flexible and area-specific approach in the location and rationalisation of polling stations in 
remote, rural, tribal and hilly regions, while broadly adhering to the prescribed norms. The 
Commission may consider relaxing the norms in genuinely difficult terrains, taking into 
account geographical constraints, connectivity and voter convenience. The Committee further 
recommends that periodic accessibility assessments may be undertaken to identify hardship-
prone areas so that no elector is effectively deprived of the opportunity to vote due to distance 
or terrain-related barriers. 
3.15 The Committee observes that concerns were raised regarding certain practical challenges 
faced by voters at polling stations. These include difficulties in reading the details displayed on 
Electronic Voting Machines due to inadequate lighting and enclosure-related darkness, uneven 
visual prominence of election symbols on EVMs, gaps in voter slip distribution leading to 

26  
confusion and disputes at polling booths. The Committee also notes the need for greater 
transparency and accuracy in dissemination of voter turnout data to avoid ambiguity and confusion. 
 
3.16 The Committee recommends that the Election Commission of India may examine the 
feasibility of introducing suitable technical and procedural measures to enhance voter 
convenience, including improved lighting or backlit display for EVMs, ensuring visual parity 
among election symbols, strengthening and verifying voter slip distribution mechanisms, and 
rationalising the establishment of polling stations so that no village is left without reasonable 
access to a polling booth. The Committee further recommends that the Commission may put 
in place a transparent and real-time system for dissemination of voter turnout data, with a 
view to minimising disputes and enhancing public confidence in the electoral process. 
3.17 The Committee, during deliberations, discussed certain systemic and operational aspects of 
the electoral process relating to the Election Commission of India, including the continued reliance 
on indelible ink despite increasing digitisation, transparency in the manufacture of Electronic 
Voting Machines (EVMs) and Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trails (VVPATs), difficulties faced by 
migrant workers in exercising their franchise. The Committee note that these issues, if not 
adequately addressed, may affect voter confidence and inclusiveness in the electoral process. 
3.18 The Committee, recommends that the Election Commission of India may consider 
adopting appropriate technological measures to strengthen the principle of “one person, one 
vote,” enhance transparency in the functioning of EVMs and VVPATs, facilitate the 
participation of migrant workers in the electoral process, and improve polling station 
infrastructure, so as to enhance voter confidence and accessibility.
