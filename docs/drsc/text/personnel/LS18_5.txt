CHAPTER-I 
OVERALL  ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS OF THE MINISTRY 
1.0 The Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions is the coordinating agency of the 
Central Government in personnel matters specially issues concerning recruitment, training, career 
development, staff welfare as well as the process of responsive, people-oriented, modern 
administration. The Ministry comprises three Departments, namely:- 
  (i) Department of Personnel and Training; 
  (ii) Department of Pensions  and Pensioners’ Welfare; and 
  (iii) Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances 
1.1 All the three Departments of the Ministry have  been functioning under the charge of Secretary 
(Personnel) who is presently assist ed by four Additional Secretaries,  three Joint Secretaries and other 
supporting officers and staff. The Ministry is divided into seven Divisions, each of which is headed by 
an officer of the level of Joint Secretary or above. The Divisions are: 
  (i) Establishment Officer; 
  (ii) Services and Vigilance; 
  (iii) Establishment; 
  (iv) Administrative tribunal and Administration; 
  (v) Training; 
  (vi) Pension and Central Services; and 
  (vii) Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. 
1.2 The Ministry has under its administrative control four attached offices, namely: 
  (i) Staff Selection Commission (SSC); 
  (ii) Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM); 
  (iii) Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA); and 
  (iv) Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). 
1.3 The other offices under the administrative control of the Ministry are: 
  (i) Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), a constitutional body; 
  (ii) Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), a statutory body; 
  (iii) Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), a statutory quasi-judicial body; and 
  (iv) Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a body set up by a resolution of the 
Government.

1.12. The Budgetary provisions make it clear that the major share of funds has been allocated for Non 
Plan provisions. Whereas funds to the tune of Rs. 75 crore has been provided for Plan provisions, 
Non-Plan has got a share of Rs. 229 crore, i.e., more than three times. When the Ministry was asked 
the reasons for this difference between  Plan and Non-Plan provisions, the Ministry stated that it is the 
coordinating agency of the Central Government in personnel matters, especially on the issues

the Ministries/Departments in these areas. Towards this end, its role is essentially regulatory and 
policy making rather than that of an actual implementing agency. Even then the Ministry had 
formulated several Plan Schemes which are being implemented. In fact, the Ministry had sought a 
total Plan allocation of Rs. 120.33 crore for 2005-06, but the Planning Commission had allocated only 
Rs. 75 crore. Besides, out of the Non-Plan allocation, nearly 55.12 per cent is meant for payment of 
salaries to officers and staff of the Ministry and its attached offices and another 15.15 per cent goes to 
office and travel expenses. 10.42 per cent of the budget is earmarked for payment of professional 
services and another 3.93 per cent is for giving loan to State Governments for payment of House 
Building Advance to All India Service Officers. Balance 15.38 per cent is on account of other heads.

DEMAND NO. 77 (UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION) 
1.23 The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional body, created under Article 315 of the 
Constitution. The Commission’s charter of duties is given in Articles 320 and 321 of the Constitution. 
In brief, it is the duty of the Commission to conduct examinations for appointment to services of the 

Union and to advise Government on all matter relating to framing of recruitment rules, method of 
recruitment and the principle to be followed in making promotions and transfers from one service to 
another. The Commission is also required to be consulted in respect of disciplinary matters affecting a 
civil servant including appeals and memoranda filed by individual Government servants. 
1.24 Article 322 of the Constitution envisages, inter-alia, that the expenses of the UPSC including 
salaries, allowances and pensions payable to members and staff of the Commission shall be ‘charged’ 
on the Consolidated Fund of India. A statement indicating the actual 2003-04 and BE and RE 2004-05

CHAPTER-II  
FUNCTIONING OF CENTRAL BUREAU OF INVESTIGATION (CBI) 
2.0 During the discussion on Demands for Grants (2005-06) of the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances and Pensions, the Director, CBI made a brief presentation before the Committee 
explaining the various issues that have arisen in the context of the functioning of the organisation and 
the need to resolve those issues expeditiously. 
2.1 The CBI came into being in the year 1941 with rather limited role and responsibility to 
investigate offences of bribery and corruption connected with transactions relating to War and Supply 
Department, Government of India. Over the last 64 years, the organisation has evolved from an anti-
corruption agency to a multi-faceted, multi-disciplinary central police - law enforcement agency with 
capability, credibility and legal mandate to investigate and prosecute offences anywhere in India. At 
present, CBI has the following Divisions: 
  (1) Anti Corruption Division; 
  (2) Economic Offences Division; 
  (3) Special Crimes Division; 
  (4) Directorate of Prosecution; 
  (5) Administration Division; 
  (6) Policy and Coordination Division; and 
  (7) Central Forensic Science Laboratory 
2.2 The CBI derives the power of investigation from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 
1946, (DSPE) with jurisdiction covering all the Departments of the Government of India and Union 
Territories. As per section 2 of the Act, DPSE has jurisdiction to investigate offences in the Union 
Territories only. However, the jurisdiction can be extended by the Central Government to other areas 
including Railways and States under section 5(1) of the Act, provided the State Governments have 
accorded consent under section 6 of the Act. Duri ng the oral evidence on Demands for Grants (2005-
06), it was pointed out that this causes problem many a time and results in inordinate delays in the 
process of investigation. For example, in case of non-consent of the concerned State Government, 
there is very little that the Union Government can do. 
2.3 In the Seventh Report (1993-94) of the Committee on Home Affairs on Central Bureau of 
Investigation and Central Vigilance Commission, the following observation was made by the 
Committee: 
“The Committee notes that though most of the States give their consent for investigation by 
the CBI, sometimes with the change of Government or for some other reasons certain States 
at times withdraw their consent before or during the investigation, thus frustrating all the 
efforts of the CBI to bring the culprits to book.” (see para 31, page 6 of the 7th Report of the 
Committee on Home Affairs). 
2.4 During the meeting of the Committee, the Memb ers observed that despite these constraints, 
there was always demand for CBI enquiry from various States, organisations and other quarters. The 
following are excerpts from some of the observations made: 

“.....new cases are being passed on to the CBI at  regular intervals. Cases are coming to CBI 
because everyone says that the case should go to the CBI. Whatever is the nature of the case, 
it should be referred to the CBI”. 
Financial and administrative powers 
2.5 During discussion on Demands for Grants (2005-06), Members pointed out that lack of financial 
power acts as a major hindrance in the working of the organisation. Lack of delegation of powers is 
one of the major bottlenecks in the Department. Even for the smallest of things, CBI has to get 
clearance from the Ministry. Delegation of powers  to the subordinate and attached offices is a 
welcome step, but instead a situation has been created whereby for their trivial financial requirement, 
it has to get clearance from the Ministry. 
2.6 Discussing this aspect, the Chairman of th e Committee made certain important observations, 
whereby it was proposed that a separate Act could be brought in for the CBI. He stated as below: 
“..... Why don’t you enact a law so as to make a separate budgetary provision for them (CBI) 
and allow them to work well? The administrative control makes them kneel down before 
officials and they are not getting their work  done in time. We need an independent 
investigating authority  ........ CBI is the only agency on which we can depend ........ You have 
to come out with some proposition, so as to allow it to function as an independent agency.” 
2.7 The Committee observes that all these years, CB I has been able to maintain its reputation 
of a premier investigating agenc y of the country functioning wi th credibility and impartiality. 
However, dependence on the executive wing of th e State for both financ ial and administrative 
matters impinges upon its role  as an independent investig ating agency. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the po ssibility of promulgating a separa te Act for CBI in tune with 
the requirement of the time, rather than derivi ng its powers from the Delhi Special Police 
Establishment Act, 1946, may be examined by the Government. 
2.8 The Committee also takes note of the fact th at under the present DSPE Act, procedural 
nuances involved in obtaining th e consent of the State Governme nt for investigation of cases 
within its jurisdiction cause inordinate delays and in case of withdrawal of consent by the 
concerned State Government due to some reason or the other, investigation takes a back seat. In 
view of the aforesaid situation, the Commi ttee reiterates its earlier recommendation (para 31, 
page 6 of the 7
th Report of the Committ ee on Home Affairs) that CBI should be empowered by a 
suitable legislation to investigate all offen ces under the Central laws like Prevention of 
Corruption Act, Income Tax Act, Central Customs & Excise Act, etc. in any part of the country 
after obtaining the concurrence of the Central Government. 
2.9 Keeping in view the need for functional independence of the CBI, it is imperative that 
financial powers are commensurate with the requi rement of efficient and smooth functioning of 
the organisation. Whereas similar delegation by th e Ministry of Home Affairs to the Heads of 
Central Para Military Forces (CPMFs) has kept pace with the changing requirement, difference 
in terms of delegation of these powers to CBI ha s resulted in glaring disparities in the financial 
powers of the CBI vis-a—vis those of the Heads of CPMFs. Thus, while recommending for 
enhancement of financial powers of the CBI, th e Committee would also like to suggest that an 
Internal Financial Advisor for CBI may be appo inted to render expert advice on financial 
matters since similar system is already functioning in Central Police Organisations.

CHAPTER-III 
POLICIES, PROGRAMMES AND SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY  
I. CITIZENS’ CHARTER  
3.0 As per the Annual Report of the Ministry, for the year 2004-05 the Citizens’ Charter initiative in 
the Union Government started in 1996-97 and received endorsement of the State Governments/UT 
Administrations in the Conference of Chief Ministers held in May, 1997. As per the Action Plan for 
effective and responsive administration, it was decided that both Central and State Governments 
would formulate Citizens’ Charters starting with those Ministries/Departments/Organisations which 
have a large public interface. A Core Group under the Chairmanship of Secretary, Ministry of 
Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions is functi oning to examine the draft Citizens’ Charters of 
Central Government Ministries/Departments/Organisations. 
3.1 The Ministry has stated that as on 31st March, 2005, 29 Ministries/Departments of Government 
of India have brought out 107 Citizens’ Charter and 24 State Governments/UT Administrations have 
brought out 629 Citizens’ Charter.  
3.2 When asked as to whether any proactive steps are being taken by the Union Government to 
motivate/encourage the State Governments/UT Admi nistration to come up with their Citizens’ 
Charters, the Ministry has given the following reply: 
“(i)  The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances has been 
promoting and coordinating the formulation and implementation of the Citizens’ Charter 
among various State Governments/ UTs besides Central Government 
Ministries/Departments. The Department has been issuing guidelines along with ‘Do’s and 
Don’ts’ from time to time for formulation of Citizens’ Charters. 
(ii) Regional Seminars on Citizens’ Charters are organised by the Department with a view 
to bring National and State level organisations along with other stakeholders including 
NGOs, intelligensia, media, etc. on the same platform to share experiences in the formulation 
and implementation of Citizens’ Charters. 
(iii)  It has been further informed that with a view to address the problem relating to lack of 
awareness about Citizens’ Charter amongst employees  and citizens as well as to initiate the 
process of consultation with stakeholders, th e Department took up a project of organizing 
Department specific awareness workshop on Citizens’ Charter for State Governments. 
(iv)  The Department brings out an annual advertisement in Hindi and in English in major 
newspapers across the country containing the details of Nodal Officers of Citizens’ Charters 
of Central Government Ministries/Departments/Organisations. 
(v)  The Department has got evaluated 20 selected Citizens’ Charters of Central 
Departments/Organisations and of State Governments, through a professional agency which 
provided the feedback on the implementation of the Charter initiatives in the concerned 
Departments and the evaluation proforma devised by them has been circulated to all Central 
Government Ministries/Departments and State Governments/UT Administrations. 
(vi) A professional agency is developing an appropriate ‘Charter Mark’ Scheme to 
encourage and reward improvement in public service delivery with reference to the 
commitments and standards notified in the Charter. 
 The implementation of Citizens’ Charter is an  ongoing exercise, as it has to reflect the 
extensive and continual changes taking place in the domain of public services. Indian 
Government continuously strives to serve the ci tizens in an effective and efficient way so as 
not only to meet but to exceed their expectations.” 
3.3 The Committee notes the initiati ve taken by the Ministry in promoting Citizens’ Charters. 
The Scheme took off in 1996-97 and so far all th e State Governments have not yet come up with 
their Citizens’ Charters. Further, at the Cent ral level about 29 Ministries/Departments of 

Government of India have brough t out such Charters. The Committ ee is of the opinion that all 
Central Government Ministries/Departments/O rganisations should be impressed upon to 
come out with their Citizens’ Charters. Besides, as the nodal Ministry in the Union Government, 
proactive steps should be continued to enco urage/motivate the State Governments in this 
direction. 
3.4 The Committee feels that the programme of Ch arter Mark is indeed laudable and should 
be used extensively to encourage/motivate the various Ministries/Departments to take the 
initiative. Further, the Committee feels that apart from organising Department specific 
awareness programmes and giving advertisements in the print media for spreading awareness, 
electronic media should  also be involved in popularizing the Citizens’ Charters as these have a 
wider mass appeal. 
3.5 The Committee would like to recommend that a proper reviewing mechanism should be in 
place to evaluate the various Charters and furn ish feedback on the im plementation of these 
Charters. They should be utilised to improve the lapses in the existing system.

nomenclature ‘Plan Scheme on Administrative Reforms’ has been formulated. The World Bank 
Project on ‘Capacity

  (1) Setting up of Wide Area Network (WAN); 
  (2) Evaluation of Efficiency and Productivity in Government; 
  (3) Less Paper Office Initiativ e in the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public 
   Grievances; and 
  (4) Change Management Project.

with detailed Terms of Reference. 
3.33 The Committee is pleased to note that a number of initiatives have been taken for 
reforming the administrative system of the count ry and making it modern in tune with the 
requirement of time. However, the Committee is  constrained to observe that despite the 
initiatives in new programmes and schemes a nd claims being made by the Government 
regarding the use of information technology as a benchmark of administrative reforms, results 

in terms of concrete outcomes are yet to em erge. The Plan Scheme of Modernisation of 
Government Offices, which was earlier a distinct  scheme was launched in the year 1987-88 to 
help the Ministries/Departments modernize thei r offices. Since then an amount of Rs. 24.30 
crore has been sanctioned for implementation of only 330 proposals, which averages to about 18 
proposals in a year. The Committee feels that a fa r greater pace is needed to bring about any 
palpable change in the administrative set up of  the nation. The Committee is not satisfied with 
the reply of the Ministry that since inception of the Scheme, an amount of Rs. 27.20 crore has 
actually been disbursed by the Department fo r 341 proposals including  those of the current 
financial year. Moreover, the funding pattern of 75:25 between the Union Government and the 
concerned Ministry raises doubt that in case th e concerned Ministry fails to invest the amount 
required of it, the projects will not be forthcomin g. The Committee feels that instead of the fund 
sharing pattern, a central pool should be co nstituted with allocation to the various 
Ministries/Departments as per their needs. Further it should be made mandatory for the 
Ministries/Departments to initiate project proposals pertaining to modernization and 
administrative reforms. 
3.34 The Committee recommends that similar to the initiative of Charter Mark, whereby 
agencies that are implementing their Citizens ’ Charters exemplarily and are providing an 
excellent standard of service, receive certifica tion/recognition in the fo rm of a ‘Charter Mark’ 
after an independent assessment, rewards /incentives should be given to the 
Ministry/Department excelling in implementing the projects on administrative reforms. 
3.35 The Committee while noting the fact that init iatives in Administrative Reforms are being 
taken at the Centre/Union Government level, fe els concerned about the progress made by the 
State Governments in this direction. True  reform can only take place when the 
benefits/advantages percolate to the masses at the grassroot level. The Committee observes that 
very little has been done in this regard by th e nodal Union Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances and Pensions. The Committee recommends  that concrete steps should be taken to 
motivate/encourage the State Governments to come  up with such initiatives. If needed, Central 
allocation should be made for the State Gov ernments for taking up ad ministrative reforms 
scheme. 
3.36 The Committee notes the recent decision of the Union Cabinet, as reported in the media, to 
set up the Second Administrative  Reforms Commission to prepa re a blueprint for revamping 
the public administration system. The Commission would reportedly go into the whole gamut of 
issues relating to public administration. The Committee urges upon the Government to finalise 
the terms of reference for the Comm ission at the earliest, so th at it starts functioning and 
completes its work within the stipulated period. 
V. CIVIL SERVICES REFORMS  
3.37 The Annual Report of the Ministry states that the Government has constituted a Committee 
under the Chairmanship of Shri P.C. Hota, former  Chairman, UPSC and Secretary (P) to examine the 
whole gamut of Civil Service Reforms and make suitable recommendations to the Government. The 
Hota Committee submitted its Report to the Cabinet Secretary on 30.07.2004. 
3.38 When asked about the Government’s plan of action regarding consideration of the Report, it has 
been stated that the Hota Committee’s recommendations relating to (i) reduction of age limit for 
recruitment to civil services; (ii) weeding out of corrupt officers; (iii) changes in service rules so as not 
to use suspension as an instrument to intimidate; (iv) protection to honest civil servants; (v) code of 
ethics for civil servants; (vi) drawing up a model code of governance benchmarking the standards of 
governance; 
(vii) improving accountability in service delivery are under consideration of the Government. 

3.39 Further, it has been informed that the Government had appointed the following Committees on 
Civil Service Reforms and reforms in governan ce apart from the Hota Committee as mentioned 
before: 
  (a) Surinder Nath Committee on Performan ce Appraisal, Promotion, Empanelment and 
Placement for the All India Services and other Group A Services (2003); 
  (b) Yugandhar Committee on In-service Training (2003); and 
  (c) Alagh Committee on Recruitment and Selection Procedure (2001). 
Recommendations made by these Committees are bei ng considered and future reform measures will 
be initiated based on the decisions taken thereon. Further, it has been informed that the ARC will be

3.40 On the issue of establishing a Civil Service College, the Ministry has informed that it is at a

can be finalized. 
3.41 The Committee notes the fact that a number of  expert Committees have been constituted 
and all of them have subm itted their Reports. However, any conc rete step is yet to be taken by 
the Government in this regard. The Committee understands that in a large democracy like India 
with an intricate and complex bur eaucratic set up in place, subs tantial groundwork is needed

Demands for Grants (2005-06) of the Ministry. It was stressed that in this age of globalisation,

with the administrative requirements in a fast changing global environment.

implementation the Sub-Project on Capacity Building for Good Governance with technical assistance

Project comprises the following three major components: 
  (1) Conceptualizing and implementing a Charter Mark Scheme for recognizing excellence 
in the implementation of Citizens’ Charters; Public Grievance Redress mechanism 
rating model; 
   A scheme of Awards to institutions for excellence in Public Service Delivery; 
  (2) Training and Capacity Building of Government Employees for management of records; 
and 

  (3) Documentation and dissemination of best practices and setting up of a facility with 
digital repository of best practices and resources to support good governance initiative.

Conference of Chief Secretaries on “Good Governance, Administrative Reforms and Related Issues” 
which was held recently under the Chairmanship of  Cabinet Secretary. As the Secretary stated, the

“These regional conferences are throwing valuable information and pointing out what

district level.” 
3.47 The Committee appreciates the initiatives ta ken by the Government in the direction of 
establishing good gove rnance in the country. The Committee is  of the view that more funds 
should be allocated in the Un ion Budget for carrying out schemes under the Good Governance 
project. However, only allocation of funds will  not serve the purpose. Proper review mechanism 
should be in place to see that available f unds are optimally utilised and concrete physical 
progress takes place.  
3.47.1 Moreover the Committee feels that governance can be termed as good only when the 
benefits/advantages percolate to the bottom. Rather than keeping these initiatives restricted to 
the Central level, serious effort is needed to initiate such measures amongst the State 
Governments, which in turn can encourage/motiva te the grassroot level organisations to follow

decided based on consent.

Information Technology and Software Development. However, the Ministry has stated that no funds 
were released and no projects were entrusted to  NISG as the proposal for approval of NISG was 
pending approval of the Cabinet. 
3.49 When asked about the reasons for delay in setting up of NISG and making it functional despite 
an allocation of Rs. 9 crore under the Tenth Five Y ear Plan, the Ministry has informed that NISG has 
started its operation from its headquarters located in the campus of International Institute of 
Information Technology (IIT), Hyderabad. Chief Executive Officer of NISG has taken charge with 
effect from 6th November, 2003. Presently the ins titute is involved in projects such as UNDP 
sponsored project ICT for Development, DC A 21, e-Biz, Bangalore One, e-Governance Gateway etc. 
3.50 However, explaining the causes for delay in establishing the institute, the Ministry has informed 
that funds allocated under the 10
th plan could not be utilized as Cabinet’s approval/endorsement for 
setting up of NISG and equity contribution of the Government was awaited. For obtaining Cabinet’s 
approval, a Cabinet note seeking inter alia endorsement/approval for the setting up of NISG was 
prepared in February 2004 and was approved by the then Minister in-charge for consideration in the 
Cabinet. However, with the change of Government at the Centre, the Cabinet Note was returned and 
the Cabinet Secretariat directed the Department to obtain fresh approval of the Minister in-charge in 

the new Government after fresh inter-Ministerial consultations and to submit a revised Cabinet Note. 
Accordingly, consultations were made with the Ministry of Finance, Department of Information 
Technology and the Planning Commission and based on their comments, a fresh Cabinet Note was 
prepared and approved by the present Prime Minister, being Minister in-charge. 
3.51 The Cabinet has now approved/endorsed the proposal for setting up of NISG and the proposal 
for equity contribution to NISG on 24.03.05. Action to release the equity share is being taken. 
3.52 The Committee is unhappy to no te that even after proper allo cation of funds in the 10th 
Plan period for establishment of NISG, del ay has taken place in its establishment and 
functioning. The Committee is not convinced wi th the reason which the Ministry has adduced 
for the delay in the establishment of the institute. The Committee is  of the opinion that in order 
to fast track development initia tives, all such Governmental and bureaucratic hassles should be 
streamlined. 
3.53 The Committee is also not satisfied with the mere reply that NISG has started functioning 
from its present location in Hyderabad. Rather, it would like to know the details of activities 
taken up by the NISG so far alon g with expenditure incurred for such activities. Further, the 
Committee recommends that all formalities relating to the operationalisation of NISG should be 
completed without any further delay and all steps should be taken to ensure its smooth 
functioning. 
VIII. e-GOVERNANCE 
3.54 E-Governance has emerged as a focus area in the modern system of administration. During the 
discussion in the Committee on Demands for Grants (2005-06) of the Ministry, the Secretary(P) stated 
that capacity building exercises are being taken up in the realm of e-Governance. To quote from his 
submissions:- 
“In cooperation with other Ministries like the Ministry of Information Technology and 
Cabinet Secretariat, there is a Group of Secretaries which gives guidance in this regard. We 
have tried to work out an arrangement whereby the e-governance initiatives are all focused 
and they are all planned as per a time bound programme. These initiatives, which are 21 in 
number are currently monitored by the Cabinet Secretary and it is our hope that once they 
have run their full course, there shall be a significant impact on e-governance.” 
3.55 It has been further in formed that as a part of this initia tive, the Institute of e-Governance has 
been established which is an attempt to create an effective model for public-private partnership in 
matters of capacity building. The objective would be to make this institute to be able to leverage the 
capacities which are available all over the country, especially in the private sector for achieving fast 
results. The matter has been approved by the Cabine t. As the Secretary has stated, Rs. 1 crore has 
already been released for this institute and more money would be subsequently disbursed. The 
magnitude and scope of this institute would be quite vast. 
3.56 The Committee feels that the progress made in the direction of e- governance is laudable 
and appreciates the initiatives taken by the Un ion Government in this direction. The Committee 
is of the opinion that the benefits of informat ion technology should be utilised to the optimum 
and lack of resources should not hamper the development process on this count. 
Ministry/Department specific projects should be undertaken with an efficient monitoring system 
and regular feedback. Efforts should be ma de to involve foreign agencies and more 
bilateral/multilateral projects should be undert aken whereby foreign investments and technical 
know-how may be utilised. Best practices in this field from across the world may be thoroughly 
examined and followed suitable to the Indian context. 

3.57 The Committee feels that a holistic approach  is needed to sprea d the e-governance 
initiatives in every nook and corner of the country. State Governments should be impressed 
upon to undertake such measures  and necessary funds should be made available at their 
disposal to carry out such activities. A coordi nated approach between the Centre and the States 
will go a long way in utilizing the benefits of  information technology in the process of 
governance. 
IX. ANNUAL REPORTS  
3.58 The Committee in its earlier Report had taken up the issue of inordinate delay in submission of 
Annual Reports of various organisations and offices such as Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), 
Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), CBI etc.  It was recommended by the Committee that a 
time frame should be prescribed for each stage of processing and laying of Annual Reports, so that the 
Committee could examine the Annual Report of th e preceding year while examining Demands for 
Grants of the succeeding year. In its reply, the Mi nistry has stated that prescribing a suitable time 
frame for laying the Annual Reports of the CVC and UPSC in Parliament is under examination of the 
Government. During the discussion on Demands for Grants (2005-06), the issue regarding late 
submission of Annual Reports came to the fore. The Ministry in its reply has furnished the status of 
Annual Reports of the various bodies/offices under its purview, which is as follows:  
Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) 
The latest Annual Report of UPSC for the year 2003-2004 has since been received and will be laid 
before the Parliament as soon as the explanatory memorandum is finalized.  
Staff Selection Commission (SSC) 
So far as SSC’s Annual Report is concerned, it is prepared and published after the close of the 
financial year covering its recruitment activities from 1st April to 31st March. SSC’s Annual Report is 
for official use and is circulated in the Ministries and Departments and not tabled in the Parliament. 
Their Annual Report for the period 2004-05 is under preparation and is likely to be published and 
circulated by the end of the first quarter of the current financial year.  
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) 
As per Section 14(1) of the CVC Act, the Annual Report of the CVC should be submitted within 6 
months of the close of the year. The CVC, theref ore, has time up to June, 2005 to submit its Annual 
Report for the year 2004. The CVC has been informed of the observation of the Committee. 
Kendriya Bhandar 
Kendriya Bhandar is governed by the provision of the Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002. 
As per the provisions of this Act, the audited statements of accounts, audit report and Annual Report 
are required to be considered in the General Body meeting within 6 months after the close of the 
corresponding year. The Annual Report and the Audite d Statement of Accounts for the year 2004-05 
is, therefore, required to be considered by the General Body before 30.09.2005. Only after the General 
Body has approved the same, the Report can be sent by the Kendriya Bhandar to the Government. 
These are required to be laid in the Parliament before 31.12.2005.  
Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA)  
It has been noted by the Comm ittee that the Annual Report of  LBSNAA (2004) gives information 
about the various courses, curriculum and research units of the Institution. However, it falls short of 
giving any progress report of the Academy, data regarding various programmes taken up during the 
year, utilisation of budget allocation and an overall critical evaluation of th e activities during the past 
one year. 

3.59 The Ministry has replied that these suggestio ns of the Committee are extremely valuable and 
will be communicated to th e LBSNAA so that these are kept in  mind while preparing Annual Report 
in future. 
3.60 The Committee notes the procedure regarding s ubmission and status of Annual Reports of 
the various offices and organisations under the ad ministrative charge of the Ministry. In this 
context, the Committee would lik e to recommend that Annual Repo rts should be presented to 
Parliament on time and made available to the Committee so that it can carry out a proper study 
thereof. Further, it is felt that Annual Report s reflect the total functi oning of the concerned 
Ministry or offices under it. It will  be fruitful if instead of giving  only the positive side of the 
scheme of things, a critical evaluation and assessment of the functioning of the organisations will 
go a long way in its improvement. Rather than be ing repetitive year after year, while furnishing 
details about projects and schemes, an objectiv e approach should be adopted in framing the 
Annual Report.
