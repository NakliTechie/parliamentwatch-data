DELIMITATION COMMISSION
6.1. On a specific query raised about the progress of delimitation work, the Ministry submitted
the following written reply:—
“The Delimitation Commission was constituted in July, 2002. It became functional in
November/December 2002. The Commission - in terms of the Constitutional provision–
was to delimit the constituencies on the basis of 1991 census figures. By June 2003
the Commission had completed almost 60% of its work. The totality of the
Commission’s work (all the Parliamentary constituencies and the Assembly
constituencies in all the States in the country) would have been completed by
November/December, 2003. But it could not be done because the Parliament amended
the Constitution by the Constitution (Eighty-Seventh Amendment) Act, 2003, which
provides that the delimitation work shall be done by the Commission on the basis of
the 2001 census and not the 1991 census figures. As a consequence, the work done
by the Commission on the basis of the 1991 census became non-est by the operation
of law. The Commission, however, started preliminary work on the basis of the
provisional 2001 census figures, which were available. But the preliminary work could
not be taken to the logical extent because; even the provisional 2001 census figures
regarding the Schedule Castes and Schedule Tribes were not available.
The 2001 Census figures for most of the States were published on
31st December, 2003. Thereafter the Commission took-up the work of collecting data
and tabulating the same. While the process was going on, the Lok Sabha was dissolved
and fresh elections were declared. The Commission has five Lok Sabha Members from
each State as its associate members. Where the number of MPs in a State is less than
five, all of them become Commission’s members. With the dissolution of the Lok
Sabha, the associate members ceased to be members by operation of law. Even
otherwise during the elections, the fieldwork by the Commission was almost stopped.
After the constitution of the Fourteenth Lok Sabha, the Commission requested Hon’ble
Speaker to nominate associate members to the Delimitation Commission from each
State. After receipt of the nominations, the Commission restarted the delimitation work,
which is progressing on a tight schedule.

4
The delimitation of the Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies in all the States is a
time consuming work. Under the scheme of the Act associate members are to be
associated with the delimitation work from the very beginning. As a consequence there
have to be more than one meeting with the associate members from each State.
Thereafter, the draft delimitation proposals are published in newspapers and suggestions
and views are invited from the public. Finally, the public meetings are held by the
Commission in all the States and the draft finalized, after that.”
The Committee was informed that delimitation work in all the States in the country is at
different stages.

NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA)
7.5 Budgetary allocation (2007-08) for NALSA has been made as Rs. 17.50 crore.
7.6 National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) has been constituted under section 3 of the
Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 in the year 1995 to provide free and competent legal services
to the weaker sections of the society with the objective to ensure that opportunities for securing
justice are not denied to any citizen for the reason of economic or other disabilities. To be eligible
for seeking legal aid under the NALSA, the annual income limit fixed by the Central Government
for the cases before the Supreme Court is Rs. 50,000 at present. Out of twenty eight States Legal
Services Authorities, and 7 Union Territories, Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar,
Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh,
Mizoram, Orissa, Punjab, Sikkim, Tripura, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal, U.T. of
Chandigarh, Delhi have raised the income ceiling limit to Rs. 50,000 per annum to provide free legal
aid under Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. According to the Annual Report of the Ministry,
during the period from the 1st April, 2006 to the 30th September 2006, more than 1.90 lakh
persons have been benefited through legal aid services. Out of them, 13,191 persons belonged to
the Scheduled Castes, 8,383 to the Scheduled Tribes, 23,964 were women and 962 were children.
7.7 The Committee appreciates that its recommendation (15th Report on the Demand for
Grants (2006-07), that more and more States and UTs should raise the income-ceiling limit
for providing free legal aid under NALSA’s Scheme has re sulted in 19 States and
3 UTs raising the limit to the prescribed level of Rs. 50,000 per annum. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should speed up the entire process so that the
income-ceiling limit throughout the Country remains uniform and larger number of persons
can get benefit under the scheme.
7.8 The Committee in its 15th Report on the Demands for Grants 2006-07 had recommended
that “the counsels engaged for the poor under the legal aid programme are paid meagerly; and as
a result thereof good and reputed lawyers do not come forward to take up the cases. Even Senior
Advocates do not take up such cases. As a result the poor litigants feel that legal aid being provided
to them is mere eyewash. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the fee structure for the
legal aid counsel should be reasonably enhanced so that the experienced and competent lawyers are
attracted and motivated to take up/accept the cases of the legal aid beneficiaries with full
enthusiasm Moreover, the fee structure should be made uniform throughout the country”. The
Ministry replied as under:—
“The issue of raising of fee structure of Legal Aid Counsel and having a uniform
structure throughout the country was discussed in the All India Meet of the State Legal
Services Authorities at Kochi, Kerala. It was felt that fee structure should be such as

10
to attract the talented, competent and committed lawyers. Now, a Committee
comprising three senior Judges has been constituted to consider the matter and submit
its report within 3 months. On receipt of the Committee’s report action will follow.”
7.9 The Committee feels appreciates that its recommendation for raising of fee structure
of Legal Aid Counsel has resulted in constitution of a Government Committee to look into
the matter. The Committee desires that the Department may apprise it as and when any
decision is taken into the matter.

consolidated scheme, covering all its scheme and programme for formulating a centrally sponsored
scheme with component to provide hundred per cent funding by the Union Government for
operationalising NALSA plans. In response there to the Ministry has replied that with regard to
providing 100% funding by it for operationalising NALSA’s plans under Centrally Sponsored
Scheme, the Central Government is releasing grants to the National Legal Services Authority, i.e.,
the Central Authority (Section 14 of the Act). The Central Authority can also receive any grants,
donations made to it by any other person and also any amounts under the orders of any court
[sub-section (1) of section 15 of the Act]. the Central Authority makes grants to the State Legal
Services Authorities for meeting the costs of legal services provided under the act [sub-section (2)

11
of section 15]. Further, the State Leal Services Authorities are constituted by the concerned State
Governments [Sub-section (1) of section 6]. The administrative expenses of the State Authority
shall also be defrayed out of the Consolidated Fund of the State [sub-section (7) of section 6].
In view, of this there is no scope for evolving any Centrally Sponsored Scheme with 100% funding
to the State Legal Services Authorities for covering their infrastructural requirements.
7.13 The Committee notes that NALSA has launched a laudable mission for legal
empowerment of all the sections of the society, yet, certain grey areas need to be addressed
by the Ministry. Moreover, non-utilisation of grants by the States Legal Services Authorities
is another area of concern. The Ministry should effectively monitor not only utilisation of
the funds but also implementation of the schemes and programmes of NALSA. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the constraints of funds should not come in the
way of successful implementation of the scheme. The Ministry should, therefore, allocate
additional funds to help operationalise the various programmes to be undertaken by NALSA
at RE stage. The Committee also feels that the actual benefit of this scheme is not gaining
access to the poor litigants and the programmes are confined to high profile areas or capital
cities only and publications in English papers. The Committee therefore recommends that
the NALSA should organise legal awareness programmes all over the country at village and
slums in general and in North Eastern region in particular. The Committee has been
apprised by the Ministry that there is no scope for evolving any Centrally Sponsored
Scheme with component to provide hundred per cent funding by the Centre for
operationalising NALSA’s plans since under the provisions of the Legal Services Authorities
Act 1987 the Central Government is releasing grants to NALSA (Section 14 of the Act). The
Committee feels that the vision with which the various programmes will be launched by
NALSA could be achieved only by way of proper planning. The reason behind stressing cent
percent funding by way of Centrally Sponsored Scheme is that the programmes launched
can be easily monitored, targets set can be achieved, and the financial crunch should not
be a problem. Moreover there will be a vision to achieve set targets in a time bound
manner. The Committee, therefore, recommends that if required, necessary changes in the
Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 may be made for incorporating the idea of evolving any
Centrally sponsored Scheme with component to provide cent percent funding by the Centre
for operationalising NALSA’s plans.

EFFORTS BY THE ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA TO PREPARE CLEAN AND ERROR
FREE ELECTORAL ROLLS
7.43 To remove the names of dead voters directions have been issued to the Chief Election
Officers to co-ordinate with the Registrar of Births and Deaths and the Local Bodies to get the
list of dead persons maintained by them. Instructions have been issued to all States to appoint
Booth Level Officers from amongst the officers of the State Government/local Bodies for each part
of electoral roll who shall thoroughly study the part of the roll assigned to them, make frequent
field visits to different villages/Tolas and interact with local people particularly village elders, grass
root level elected representatives and so on to identify the names of the dead and shifted voters
for submitting their recommendation to Assistant Electoral Registration Officer (AERO)/Electoral
Registration Officer (ERO) for consideration. To make the exercise of preparation of electoral rolls
more accurate instructions have also been issued to read out the relevant parts of the roll at the
meetings of the Gram Sabhas, Ward Sabhas and Resident Welfare Associations. The services of
Post Offices are being used for display of rolls, supply and receive applications for inclusion,
deletion and corrections. In deserving cases, where situation arise, intensive revision or summary
revision with door to door verification of each existing entry in roll is ordered. The latest move

to time and on the basis of feed backs received from States; it felt the need for reducing the voter
strength per polling station in order to ensure voter convenience and an orderly poll within the
stipulated polling hours. This has resulted in the increase of polling stations across the country and
consequential requirement of about 2.5 lakh V oting Machines to cope up with the additional
requirement. The existing EVMs, which are being used presently, was introduced in the year 1990
after technical evaluation by Expert Committee headed by Professor P. V . Indiresan. On the basis
of experience in using Electronic V oting Machines and inputs gathered from the States and taking

Machines, incorporating certain features that would result in better management of elections. The
new features/improvement incorporated in the new version of the Electronic V oting Machines are-
(a) Real time Clock (b) Time Stamping of every event conduct on the machines (c) Battery Power
indication (d) Provision for power saving mode (e) Display of Hourly Total (f) Facility for totalizing
the results from various control units and (g) Switch over from numeric to alpha-numeric display.
During the current financial year, an amount of Rs. 225 crore has been provided to the Election
Commission for purchase of the upgraded version of approx. 2.5 Lakh Electronic V oting Machines.

ELECTORS PHOTO IDENTITY CARDS (EPIC)
7.49 The Committee has been informed that the use of electors’ photo identity cards by the
Election Commission is slowly and surely making the electoral process simple, smoother and
quicker. A decision was taken by the Election Commission of India in 1993 to issue photo identity
cards to electors throughout the country to check bogus voting and impersonation of electors at
elections. The electoral roll is the basis for issue of EPICs to the registered electors. The electoral
rolls are revised every year with 1st January of the year as the qualifying date. All persons who
attain 18 years or above as on that date are eligible for inclusion in the electoral roll and can apply
for the same. Once they are registered in the roll, they would be eligible for getting an EPIC. The
scheme of issuing the EPICs is, therefore, a continuous and ongoing process for the completion
of which no time limit can be fixed as the registration of electors is a continuous and ongoing
process (excepting for a brief period between the last date for filing nomination and completion
process) on account of more number of persons becoming eligible for the right of franchise on
attaining the age of 18, current programme will always constitute a residual part of the
programme. The Election Commission’s (EC) continuous effort is to provide the EPICs to the
electors who are left out in the previous campaigns as well as the new electors. The Election
Commission, which is overall in charge of implementation of the scheme of issuance of photo
identity cards to electors and has been monitoring its progress on regular basis, has informed that
more than 48.98 crore electors representing 70.39% of the eligible voters in the country have been
provided photo identity cards.
As on date, approximately 30% electors are yet to be issued voter identity card. It has been
the endeavour of the Commission to achieve 100% coverage under the EPIC scheme, as far as
practicable, in a time bound manner. Even though efforts have been constantly made to cover all
the electors under the EPIC scheme, various reasons such as natural calamities, engagement of
administrative machinery, which also attends to other works than election work, apathy of voters
themselves are some of the reasons why progress has been uneven. While in some States/UTs such
as Kerala, Punjab, West Bengal, Lakshadweep and Puducherry the EPIC card percentage is above
90%, the EPIC programme has not actually taken place due to various reasons in Assam and
Manipur.
The Commission is concerned about the fact that said 30% gap in issuance of EPIC is
continuing despite annual EPIC campaigns being undertaken. Factors that contribute to this gap are
(1) persons who attain eligibility for the first time are registered every year. This may be about

24
2%, (2) Due to death but not deleted about 1% and (3) intercity and intra city migration in major
metropolitan towns which may account to 7% to 8%. It has also come to the notice of the
Commission that migrating electors in most cases do not mention their EPIC number, if any, issued
at the place of their previous residence in the application form field for inclusion of name at their
new place of residence. Therefore, though the name is included in the part of the roll where the
elector shifts, the EPIC number remains blank and this adds numbers to the non-EPIC holder
statistics. At the place of origin, till such time, such persons’ names continue and it adds numbers
to EPIC-holders statistics creating a false picture.
Another phenomenon noticed is that from the backward and poor areas of the country
(Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, etc.) a lot of seasonal migration of the unskilled
persons takes place (mainly of men but some women too) and semi-permanent migration takes
place of skilled persons (mainly of men) but their names are not deleted from the voters list at
the place of origin as the family members who reside there, want their names to continue for
emotional and more mundane (to prevent property disputes etc.) reasons. As such persons are not
available for photography, EPICs cannot be issued and the gap remains uncovered for long as EPIC
campaign period may not coincide with the period of their visits to their homes. These are major
areas of concern. This scene gets complicated further in urban areas where other photograph-based
identity documents (like Passports, Driving Licenses, Departmental or Employer-given IDs) are
available and urbanites do not show any enthusiasm for obtaining EPICs as it involves spending
their precious time to go to a Photo location for a document which anyway has relevance for them
once in 5 years only and for which they already have alternatives.
The Commission has instructed that the EPIC number once issued will be valid throughout
the Elector’s life even if address changes. If the EPIC is declared to be an acceptable identity
Document for all Governmental purposes, the Electors will see a value to it beyond the once-in-
5-years poll day usage. With plans of Election Commission to have all States Elector database being
made available all over India, searching for an EPIC number can be made easy and if combined
with Photo of the Elector in the database, tracking would become easy. Therefore one of the ways
in which this problem is being addressed is to prepare electoral rolls with the photograph of
electors.
The Commission has decided to modify the statutory application Forms 6, 8 and 8A to
allow optional submission of photograph by the elector at the time of fresh inclusion, correction
or transposition of entry within the constituency.
Pending modification of the Forms, in West Bengal and Haryana, the facility of submitting
photograph as an option has been experimented through non-statutory forms prescribed by the
Commission. The photo is used for generating EPIC and also printing the image in the photoroll.
7.50 Issue of Photo Identity Cards to voters is one of the constituents of the budget of the

SIMPLIFICATION OF LEGISLATIVE LANGUAGE
7.56 The Committee in its 15th Report on Demand for Grants had recommended that efforts
should be made by the Department in the direction of simplifying the language used in law. In
response thereto the Department has replied that:—
“Official Languages Wing has clarified that terms and expressions used in Hindi version
of Central Laws were decided and approved by the various experts of the then official
Languages (Legislative) Commission. This Commission consisted of legal and language
experts of all India repute and finalized translation of most of the major Acts. We can
not deviate much from these terms and expressions as, it would result into affecting
the interpretation of law. However, now a days we are simplifying the language where
the strict compliance of the earlier use of the technical words is not warranted so that
Hindi text may be more comprehensible to the extent, a common man would be in a
position to understand the spirit of law. Since law-making process is a continuous one,
no drastic changes can be made. Change can only be gradual. However, Official

27
Languages wing is committed to the task of using easiest Hindi terms and expressions,
which are popular and easily understandable among the people.”
Information regarding simplification of legislative language, as far as it related to the
Regional Language Unit, is given below:
“The translation of Central Acts into eleven regional languages as enshrined in the 8th
Schedule to the Constitution of India is done by respective State Governments through
the Agency/Departments under their control. However, at the time of finalizing the
terminology used in the regional version of different Central Acts, efforts are made to
finalise the terminology, as far as practicable, which is understandable by common man
in that particular region. Officers/officials representing the respective State
Governments in the meeting of Working Group (RL) are also advised during the course
of discussion to adopt simple language so that the purpose of rendering Central Acts
into regional languages is fulfilled.”
7.57 The Committee appreciates that the Ministry has taken steps in the direction of
simplification of laws. But still the Committee feels that it is an ongoing process and efforts
should be made periodically for simplifying the legal language, which is quite often
technical, intricate and incomprehensible for the common man for whom laws are made. It
feels that, laws should be drafted in national or regional languages to convey the purpose
and intent thereof and all Central laws should be translated in order to make the provisions
known to the masses. The Committee, therefore, reiterates that the Ministry should explore
ways and means to simplify laws so as to be understood even by the people having no
specialized knowledge of legal formulations. In other words, Legislative Department should
act as a catalyst for launching a movement for plain language drafting of laws and
instruments of subordinate legislation.

DELINKING OF DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE FROM THE MINISTRY OF HOME AND CREATION
OF A SEPARATE DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE WITH A FULL TIME SECRETARY OF JUSTICE
8.5 The Committee has been recommending persistently that the Department of Justice should

29
be separated from the Home Ministry and provided with a full fledged Secretary of its own. This
long standing recommendation of the Committee can be traced in its 2nd Report on the Demand
for Grants of the Ministry of Law and Justice (2004-2005) under the heading “Delinking of Budget
of Department of Justice.” wherein the Committee had observed “the Committee on Home Affairs
has been reiterating its view since 1995, either to make the Department of Justice an independent
Department or to merge its budget with the Ministry of Home Affairs.”
8.6 The Committee was informed that the Government of India had taken a conscious decision
in 1971 to continue administrative association of the Department of Justice with the Home Ministry.
The position emerging as of now seems to be that the Department of Justice is a full-fledged
separate Department headed by a Secretary who has to be the Home Secretary. The position held
by the Ministry has been that the mere fact that the Home Secretary holds additional charge of
the Department of Justice, does not affect the status of the Department as an independent full-
fledged Department.”
8.7 The Committee on Home Affairs also in its 86th Report had made detailed observations that,
“the Committee has been persistently recommending to set right the anomaly relating to the budget
presentation and administrative control over Department of Justice since 1995 through its
17th Report (1995), 31st and 34th Reports (1996), 39th Report (1997), 45th Report (1998), 56th
Report (1999), 61st Report (2000), 69th and 72nd Report (2001) to either make it an independent
Department or merge the budget with the Ministry of Home Affairs.” While making final
recommendations the Committee on Home Affairs had observed that “it finds it difficult to
understand the advantages for the Government in keeping the anomaly intact. The need and
justification for making the Department of Justice a full-fledged Department with a Secretary of
its own was thus explained by the Committee on the 5th April, 2006 as under:—
“What is the rationale behind attaching the Justice Department to the Home Ministry?
Today, the Home Ministry is the busiest Ministry in the entire set up of the
Government. Problems have grown beyond our expectations. New issues have cropped
up. Today, the very security of the country is at stake. Naxalism has developed beyond
our imagination and scale. Today, our hands are full with problems and it is no
exaggeration if it is said that howsoever devotional we may be, we will not be able
to give full attention to the Department of Justice, which also deserves much more
greater attention today than at any time in the past. It is beyond doubt when we say
that the time has come that we should think of separation of Justice Department from
Home and create a separate full-fledged Department of Justice, with a full-fledged
Secretary devoted fully to this Department. It is unfair to the Home Secretary to
burden the Justice Department when their contribution can only be in improving
governance in various States and making sure that court dealings are done. It is
earnestly suggested that the Government should take a policy decision on this issue as
there is a valid reason for this.”
Doing that meeting the then Secretary (Justice) deposed before the Committee as
under:—
“...was regarding working of Home Secretary as Secretary (Justice). But, I just want
to bring to your notice that Home Secretary does not report to the Home Ministry for
the Department of Justice. Home Ministry is different and the Ministry of Law and
Justice is different. For the working of the Department of Justice, I report to the Law
Ministry. So, really speaking, it is a totally different Department, but I do not have
enough argument to say that the Home Secretary is not busy and hence how much
attention can be paid to Justice Department. It is a very valid point. It had useful role
when you are interacting with the Constitutional authorities on Court matters, on

30
appointments, on various other things. Perhaps, it was a good system to operate in
those days. We do give a lot of time to the Justice Department. We make sure that
nothing is kept pending. It may mean adding a couple of hours to our routine. But,
one cannot argue on your wisdom and we take back your point...”
But contrary to that, the Ministry, in their action taken reply, had submitted that the matter
was taken up earlier with the Cabinet Secretariat in 2002-03. It was clarified that in the Central
Government there are Secretaries who are heading more than one Ministry/ Department and Home
Secretary holding additional charge of the Department of justice does not affect the status or entity
of the Department as an independent full-fledged Department.
8.8 From the above observations, the Committee finds it reasonable to deduce that the
rationale behind keeping the Department of Justice under the Administrative control of the
Home Secretary might has been logical at that given point of time but the situation has
undergone a sea change since then. Keeping in view the fact that the Department of Justice
has been assigned a distinct task under the Government of India (Allocation of Business)
Rules, 1961, the Committee understands and notes that the Department of Justice has been
performing sensitive functions pertaining to judicial appointments, services’ conditions of
Judges and the administration of justice. Viewed in this context and also of the fact that
there are about 3 crore cases pending in different courts thus shaking the faith of the
people in the judicial system, it would be more appropriate if the Department is accorded
a distinct identity including strong administrative structure of its own. It is in this context
that the Committee is of the considered opinion that for the sake of administrative
efficiency and exigency, the Department is separated from the Home Ministry and provided
with a full-fledged Secretary. It is in this context that the Committee again reiterates and
recommends that it is a high time that this long-standing recommendation is implemented
in letter and spirit with immediate effect. Moreover, the Committee is inclined to believe
that its recommendation did not attract Government’s attention and as a result thereof the
Ministry, while referring to the earlier decision of the cabinet, furnished the action taken
reply.
PLAN SCHEMES
Centrally Sponsored Scheme relating to infrastructural Facilities for the judiciary
8.9 The Centrally Sponsored Scheme for the development of infrastructure facilities for the
judiciary is being implemented by this Department since 1993-94. The scheme includes
construction of court-buildings and residential accommodation for judicial officers/judges covering
High Courts and Subordinate Courts. The expenditure on the scheme is shared by the Central and

8.10 From 1993-94 upto 2003-04, an amount of Rs. 631.39 crore has been released to various
States/UTs. With the matching States’ share, (Union Territories are not required to provide matching
share) the States/UTs were required to spend an amount of Rs. 1071.12 crore on the basis of
release upto 2002-03. The State Governments/Union Territories are expected to submit the
Utilization Certificates within 18 months from the date of release of an amount. Against this, the
State Governments and Union Territories have spent an amount of Rs. 1162.34 crore upto
31.12.2004.
8.11 The Committee has been informed that so far, 91 extension buildings in various High Courts
have been completed and work on 17 extension buildings is in progress. About 125 judges’

31
residences of various High Courts have been completed and work in respect of 175 residences is

for the judiciary does not have a component of transport facilities for the judicial officers. The

RETIREMENT AGE OF JUDGES
8.44 Judicial authority in India is exercised through a system of National Courts administering
the law of the Republic and the States. All senior judges are appointees of the executive branch
of the Government, with their independence guaranteed by a variety of safeguards. Noteworthy
among these safeguards is a provision requiring a two-third vote of Parliament to remove a judge
from office. The highest court is the Supreme Court; all Supreme Court judges serve until a
retirement age of 65. The top court at the State level is the High Court; all High Court judges serve
until a retirement age of 62. Members of the Supreme Court are selected from among Judges of

38
the High Courts. Judges of the High Courts are in turn selected from Subordinate Courts operating
at the District level. Important judicial posts at the District level are filled by members of the
administrative service. Presently, a judicial debate/discussion is going on to raise the age of
retirement of Judges of High Court from 62 to 65 at par with the age of retirement of a
Supreme Court Judge. This issue was also raised in the meeting of the Committee held on
11th April, 2007 by some of the Members. Members were unanimous that the retirement
age of the Supreme Court and High Court judges should be uniform.
8.45 The Committee is also in receipt of a letter from Prof. P.J. Kurien, Member of Parliament,
Rajya Sabha on the subject ‘Raising the retirement age of judges of High Court from 62 to 65’.
In his letter the Member has justified the case on the grounds that a large number of cases are
pending in High Courts and also there are number of vacancies of Judges in the High Court.
Appointments of new judges also take a considerably long time due to procedural formalities.
Further more there is a dearth of suitable persons to be elevated to the Bench. Considering all these
aspects, it is justifiable to consider the extension of retirement age of judges from 62 to 65. He
has further stated that Supreme Court judges are retiring at the age of 65. If judges can work up
to 65 years of age in Supreme Court, what is the rationale to retire the High Court judges at the
age of 62 years? Even Chief Justices of High Courts are also to retire at the age of 62. Because
of this anomaly there could be unhealthy competition among judges to get elevated to Supreme
Court.
Conference of Chief Ministers and Chief Justices of
High Court held on March 11, 2006 at New Delhi
8.46 In the recently concluded Conference of Chief Ministers and Chief Justices of High Court
held on March 11, 2007 at New Delhi, the Prime Minister of India in his inaugural speech
announced “that the Government is presently considering a proposal to increase the age of
retirement of High Court Judges”.
Retirement age of Judges in USA and UK
8.47 In the United States, there is no age of retirement for federal judges. They are tenured
posts. If a federal judge feels that by reason of old age he cannot function, he will receive the
last drawn salary as pension for the rest of his life. In the United Kingdom, judges retire at the
age of 75. In our country, a judge of the Supreme Court retires at 65 and a High Court judge at
62. The older the judge the greater would be his contribution in the dispensation of justice.
8.48 The Committee feels that there is a valid reason behind bringing uniformity in the
retirement age of Judges of High Courts and that of Judges of Supreme Court. The
Committee also feels that there is no rationale in the argument that at 62, a High Court
Judge is too old to continue to work in the High Court, but he can be entrusted to carry
out the duties of a Supreme Court judge for three more years. In that backdrop, the
Committee recommends that Government should immediately bring forward a
Constitutional Amendment Bill to raise the retirement age of Judges of High Courts from
62 to 65 i.e., at par with the retirement age of a Judge of a Supreme Court.

India, being a pluralistic and secular society, different languages are spoken in different
parts of the country. There are 1652 different languages in our country and 22 are recognised as
major languages (Eighth Schedule). The constitution has recognised Hindi in the Devanagri Script,
as India’s official language. However, English continues to be the working language.

39
According to the Constitution, the language to be used in the Supreme Court and in the

Article 348 of the Constitution
Language to be used in the Supreme Court and in the High Courts and for Acts, Bills,
etc.—(1) Notwithstanding anything in the foregoing provisions of this Part, until Parliament by law
otherwise provides—
(a) all proceedings in the Supreme Court and in every High Court,
(b) the authoritative texts—
(i) of all Bills to be introduced or amendments thereto to be moved in either House of
Parliament or in the House or either House of the Legislature of a State,
(ii) of all Acts passed by Parliament or the Legislature of a State and of all Ordinances
promulgated by the President or the Governor of a State, and
(iii) of all orders, rules, regulations and bye-laws issued under this Constitution or under
any law made by Parliament or the Legislature of a State, shall be in the English
language.
(2) Notwithstanding anything in sub-clause (a)  of clause (1), the Governor of a State may, with
the previous consent of the President, authorise the use of the Hindi language, or any other

Provided that nothing in this clause shall apply to any judgment, decree or order passed or made

(3) Notwithstanding anything in sub-clause (b)  of clause (1), where the Legislature of a State has
prescribed any language other than the English language for use in Bills introduced in, or Acts

Article 349 of the Constitution
Special procedure for enactment of certain laws relating to language —During the period of
fifteen years from the commencement of this Constitution, no Bill or amendment making provision
for the language to be used for any of the purposes mentioned in clause (1) of Article 348 shall
be introduced or moved in either House of Parliament without the previous sanction of the
President, and the President shall not give his sanction to the introduction of any such Bill or the
moving of any such amendment except after he has taken into consideration the recommendations
of the Commission constituted under clause (1) of Article 344 and the report of the Committee
constituted under clause (4) of that Article.

Article 345. Official language or languages of a State. —Subject to the provisions of Articles

GENERAL RECOMMENDATION
Judicial Statistics Impact Statement
8.61 A Bill is a piece of legislation which after passage by both the Houses of Parliament and
given assent by the President, becomes an Act. When a Bill is prepared by the concerned Ministry

44
it tries to work on each and every clause meticulously/carefully since the bill, on introduction and
passage by the Parliament will become an Act and naturally it will have its effects positive or
negative on the society as a whole. Presently, a debate is going on that when the bill can cover
the financial memorandum, it should also incorporate the judicial impact statement with which one
can have the clear idea as to the impact on the judiciary after passage of such a legislation, how
many cases would be transferred and put pressure on our already over burdened judiciary and
under the head financial memorandum there must be provision for budgetary requirements of the
meetings, the expenses of additional cases that may arise out of the new bill along with budgetary
requirement of staff.
8.62 The Committee in its 6th Report on the Demands for Grants 2005-06 of the Ministry of
Law and Justice inter alia  had observed that:
“After introduction of economic reforms and globalisation and revolutionary
technological innovations, new challenges and complexities have cropped up before
Indian Judicial System imposing enormous burden upon the courts. As the society has
become more aware, the law, the image of the court system and administration of
justice suffer a loss of public confidence. In this context, it has become all the more
important that the grey areas confronting Indian Judiciary need prompt attention.
The Committee feels that it is time to consider the application of principles and
techniques of modern management in solving some prominent problems like law’s
inordinate delays, arrears of cases and increased workflow in the court system. This
requires scientific study of the problems. For this, the judiciary is required to develop
skills to manage its resources, setting targets and evolving concepts like case
management and time management. Simultaneously there is need to increase output.
The Committee has been informed that the Judges in the United Kingdom and the
United States of America has applied some innovative methods. These methods have
been successful in certain areas. For example, Annual Judicial Statistics Report,
published by the Chief Justices in the United Kingdom and the United States every
year, help to enable the public to understand the performance of the judiciary.
Similarly, in the United States, every Bill is accompanied by Judicial Impact Statement
describing the likely effect of the legislation upon the workload of the courts. The
Committee also notes that the Electronic Judicial Resource Management is gaining
importance and it has been found the most effective tool for judicial reforms. In Indian
context, no new legislation is required, as the Information Technology Act, 2000 has
already incorporated the requisite provisions.”
8.63 The Secretary, Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative Department) depose before the
Committee in the meeting held on 11th  April, 2007 on this aspect. His deposition before the
Committee is as under:
“...then we had Amendment to the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and
Amendment in the Representation of People Act, 1950. The most important thing is the
judicial impact. The Supreme Court of India in the Salem Advocates Bar Association
Vs. Union of India (2005) had inter alia  directed that judicial impact assessment, as
done in the United States should accompany every Bill introduced in Parliament or in
the State Legislatures and the Financial Memorandum attached to the each Bill must
estimate not only the budgetary requirement of staff but also the budgetary
requirements of the meeting the expenses of additional cases that may arise out of the
new Bill when it is passed by Legislature. I am happy to inform the Hon’ble
Committee that a high. level committee under the chairmanship of Justice Jagannadha

45

8.64 In that backdrop, the Committee feels there is valid reason behind introduction of
judicial impact statement in a Bill along with financial statement because on introduction
of such innovative methods in our Bills one can easily foresee the impact of a legislation
on the Judiciary. The Committee feels that in order to introduce new concepts, our country
require a careful study and survey of the new methods by the Government. The Committee
is also of the view that there must be a budgetary provision for meeting the expenses of
additional cases that may arise out of the new Bill along with budgetary requirement of
staff. The Committee, therefore, recommends that since the task of preparing a judicial
impact statement has been assigned to the Legislative Department, Government should as
early as possible render necessary financial assistance to the Department or bear all
expenses incurred by making special budgetary provisions for it. The Committee is of the
view that the above measures would have significant impact on reforming the Indian
Judicial System and the Government should, therefore, explore the possibility of introducing
these methods.

anguished that the building constructed from the Grants of the Government could be leased
out to other Governmental bodies. The Committee therefore, strongly recommends that
ICADR must not earn money by leasing out its building to Governmental bodies. On the
contrary, it should make all efforts in attracting foreign arbitration cases and in turn be
the hub of arbitration centres. The Committee also recommends that Ministry should look
into the issue and provide a status report in this regard while submitting the Action Taken
Reply. Further, ICADR should be in a position to give financial and strategical support to
develop new Centres by Private Participation such as Palkivala Center of Arbitration in
Chennai. Moreover, the Committee recommends that the Government Departments and
Public Sector Undertakings should include a clause in the agreements entered into with
other parties, which provides for referring matters to ICADR for arbitration in the event
of dispute, instead of going to the courts directly. This should be made mandatory by issuing
appropriate directions to all the Departments of Government of India, Public Sector
Undertakings, Public Sector Enterprises etc. The Committee has already recommended in its
9th Report on the Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Bill, 2004 that sole arbitrators
who are working as Government Officers discharging dual role should be gradually
eliminated and that the ICADR or such other Private Arbitration Centres should be allowed
to be involved in it for effective Alternative Dispute Resolution.
7.46 The Committee feels that such irregularity impedes successful implementation of the
schemes and should be avoided. The Committee is happy to note that the process of revision
of the electoral rolls in Assam, West Bengal, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, UT of Puducherry has
been completed and computerized. It is also happy to note that the electoral rolls of all the
States and UTs have been computerized to access data. It has also come to the notice of the
Committee that in the ongoing Assembly Elections in Uttar Pradesh the bonafide voters
having photo identity cards could not cast their votes due to their names missing from the
electoral rolls. The votes polled have gone nose deep to 28% totally reflects upon the
apprehension of the people in preparation of voter list. The Committee, therefore,
reiterates its earlier recommendation (para 7.32 of 15th Report) that to overcome such
shortcomings, the Election Commission should consider establishing its offices for this
purpose at the district levels and district level officer would be made accountable for
revision of the rolls. The Committee further recommends that the process of revision of the
electoral rolls must be completed within a specific time frame and it should be transparent.
7.48 The Committee feels that being one of the largest democracies of the world, the
Ministry’s constant endeavour should be directed to ensure that elections are held in free
and fair manner. Necessary infrastructure, of which, EVMs constitute a major part, should,
therefore, be in place at the time of holding elections. The Committee is satisfied that its
recommendation for up gradation of old EVMs and the process of procurement of 2.5 lakhs
new upgraded EVMs has been implemented. The Committee recommends that the
Committee constituted to decide the technical worthiness of the 1989-90 model EVMs may
be asked to submit its Report by the stipulated date of 25th May, 2007 so that the said

50
Report becomes useful in assisting the Committee likely to be constituted for disposing off
the old EVMs. The Committee hopes that the old EVMs would be discarded and new
upgraded EVMs would be used in all the coming elections and help to get the election
results early and in a speedy manner.
7.51 The Committee notes that as on the 1st January, 2006, total electors in the country
were 695,750,887 and the number of the electors, who have been issued Photo Identity
Cards, is 489,758,562 which constitute 70.39% of the total voters. Thus, there remains a
shortfall of nearly 30% of those who are yet to be covered under the EPIC scheme. The
Committee also notes that the Election Commission has started a campaign for this purpose
by identifying some States for a pilot project. The Committee feels that the Commission has
not achieved the desired goals despite the fact that considerable time has elapsed since the
inception of the EPIC scheme. It has also come to notice of the Committee that in Madhya
Pradesh, the Photo Identity Cards issued to the concerned person bears the photograph of
animals like tiger and dogs instead of having the photograph of bonafide citizens. The
Committee feels such type of action is unwarranted and tells a lot about the tall claims
made about percentage of EPIC issued. The Committee has also come to know from the
Annual Report of the Ministry (Page 33) that a total of 1033733 EPICs were issued for the
photographs done till 1997. As per the survey conducted by the Dy. Commissioner, 90% of
them are not available with the electors owing to snatching away of EPICs by the anti social
elements. The Committee feels it is a very serious issue and urges the Election Commission
to look into the matter and furnish a detailed report on the issue. The Committee
reiterates its earlier recommendation that the Commission should not only devise a
time-bound programme for achieving 100% EPIC coverage but also exercise utmost caution
and care so that such untoward incidents do not recur.
7.53 The Committee appreciates that its recommendation (15th Report Para 7.37) for
introduction of Unique Multi-Purpose Identity Card has been felt and Election Commission
of India has proposed to the Government to mandate the Electors Photo Identity Card
(EPIC) for being used as multi-purpose identity for the members of the public. The
Committee therefore, recommends that a status note in this regard may be provided along
with the Action Taken Reply.
7.55 The Committee, reiterates its earlier recommendation that the Ministry should take
up this issue on priority basis and make concerted efforts to acquire land for construction
of building for the Institute. The Ministry should also ensure that financial constraints
should not come in the way of materialization of the proposal. The Committee also feels
that the Institute should not confine itself to imparting training in the Country only, rather
it should think on expanding its area outside India. To begin with, the Institute can chalk
out a training programme in legislative drafting for the neighboring Asian Countries on the
same lines as organized by the Bureau of Parliamentary Studies (BPST) so that the
participants coming to attend such courses can be trained in legislative drafting. Similar
courses can be run for the participants coming from the different States. For that purpose,
the Institute should devise a proper fee structure for running these courses. Similarly,
Institute should try to explore means by which legislative drafting can be done in other
regional languages so that it can be of a great help to the State Governments.
7.57 The Committee appreciates that the Ministry has taken steps in the direction of
simplification of laws. But still the Committee feels that it is an ongoing process and efforts
should be made periodically for simplifying the legal language, which is quite often
technical, intricate and incomprehensible for the common man for whom laws are made. It
feels that, laws should be drafted in national or regional languages to convey the purpose
and intent there of and all central laws should be translated in order to make the provisions

51
known to the masses. The Committee, therefore, reiterates that the Ministry should explore
ways and means to simplify laws so as to be understood even by the people having no
specialized knowledge of legal formulations. In other words, Legislative Department should
act as a catalyst for launching a movement for plain language drafting of laws and
instruments of subordinate legislation.
8.8 From the above observations, the Committee finds it reasonable to deduce that the
rationale behind keeping the Department of Justice under the Administrative control of the
Home Secretary might has been logical at that given point of time but the situation has
undergone a sea change since then. Keeping in view the fact that the Department of Justice
has been assigned a distinct task under the Government of India (Allocation of Business)
Rules, 1961, the Committee understands and notes that the Department of Justice has been
performing sensitive functions pertaining to judicial appointments, services’ conditions of
Judges and the administration of justice. Viewed in this context and also of the fact that there
are about 3 crore cases pending in different courts thus shaking the faith of the people in
the judicial system, it would be more appropriate if the Department is accorded a distinct
identity including strong administrative structure of its own. It is in this context that the
Committee is of the considered opinion that for the sake of administrative efficiency and
exigency, the Department is separated from the Home Ministry and provided with a full-
fledged Secretary. It is in this context that the Committee again r eiterates and recommends
that it is a high time that this long-standing recommendation is implemented in letter and
spirit with immediate effect. Moreover, the Committee is inclined to believe that its
recommendation did not attract Government’s attention and as a result thereof the Ministry,
while referring to the earlier decision of the cabinet, furnished the action taken reply.
8.13 The Committee feels that the Centrally Sponsored Scheme relating to infrastructural
facilities, though started in 1993-94, has not been executed effectively due to lack of a proper
planning. Similarly, availability of the State Governments’ matching share for the scheme has
been a problem area. Consequently, the success of implementation of the scheme has not
been assessed properly. As informed by the Department of Justice, there was no focussed or
final target for completion of the physical infrastructure, and States have defaulted in
providing matching share in time. It has been decided on the advice of the Planning
Commission to prepare a 10 year perspective plan for the required physical infrastructure,
State-wise. The Committee welcomes the move of the Ministry but at the same time it
cautions that the perspective plan should not be a mere pretext to prolonging implementation
of the scheme. There should be proper monitoring of the scheme in terms of achievement of
the targets, allocation of funds etc. This is more so because the Committee has been given
to understand that even the basic infrastructural facilities like transport and housing are not
available to the Judicial Officers at lower judiciary level and they have to face many
difficulties. The argument of the Department that Judicial Officers are entitled to transport
facilities as part of their service benefits does not hold weight. These infrastructural lacunae
hamper to a great extent, the functioning of the Judicial Officers. The Committee, therefore,
reiterates its earlier recommendation (Para 8.12 15th Report) that the Ministry should
consider all these components seriously and include them in the perspective plan. The
Committee sees no difficulty in development of infrastructure for transport facilities and then
handing it over to the administration branch of State Government for further regulation so
that the dignity of Judicial Officers is maintained.
8.20 The Committee expresses satisfaction over the pace of computerisation and is happy
to note that the first phase of the project would be completed by 2007-08. The Committee,
however, feels that the rate of computerisation in the subordinate courts including the Fast
Track Courts need to be augmented. The Committee is of the view that computerisation of

52
judiciary would be an effective tool to bring down pendency of cases as has been experienced
in the Supreme Court. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should
ensure that necessary budgetary allocations for this purpose should be made and the targets
set to computerise the entire judicial system are met.
8.22 The Committee feels that the present position of setting up of the Family Courts is
not satisfactory. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendation (Para 8.21
15th Report) that the Ministry should fix a target for establishing at least one Family Court
in each district so as to resolve family disputes and related matters expeditiously and in
order to do so the Committee recommends to amend the relevant provision of the Family
Court Act, 1984 pertaining to eligibility criteria of population exceeding one million for
setting up of a family court, so that at least one family court is set up in each district
particularly, in the States like Goa. Efforts should also be made to impress upon the State
Governments for setting up at least one Family Court in each district.
8.24 The Committee is perturbed to know that the internal audit of the Academy from
the year 1997 onwards was not done (excluding the audit of last two years). The Committee
therefore, strongly recommends that internal audit of the Academy should be done on
regular basis and a status report on the issue may be sent at the time of Action Taken
reply. The Committee expresses its anguish that despite its recommendations as contained
in its 15th Report, the NJA has so far failed to furnish its comments in respect of the
adverse observations/queries made by the Statutory Auditors in their Audit Report. The
Committee reiterates that the NJA may furnish its comments expeditiously. The Committee
further feels that National Judicial Academy, which impar ts training to the judicial officers,
court administrators and ministerial officers of the Supreme Court, should also start
imparting training to the Government officers dealing with administration of justice like
CAT, CBI, officers of Ministry of Law, Home, Civil Servants, Police Officers and Legal
Counsel arguing in Courts on behalf of Government etc. and if any amendment, as pointed
out by the Academy, is required in the objects clause of the Memorandum of Association
of Academy must be done immediately. The Academy which is considered as the premier
Academy in the field of imparting training must not impose restrictions, but on the
contrary, it may open its gates for each and everyone so that the exposure provided by it
may help them to be good administrators and dispensers of Justice.
8.25 The Committee recommends that the Academy should immediately start recruiting
faculty from the persons having immense knowledge in the legal field. The Faculty can be
chosen from the retired judges, administrators, Jurists, Economists, lecturers, lawyers etc.
so that the officers attending the training may stand to gain immensely.
8.26 The Committee is constrained to note that the grants given to the National Judicial
Academy were not fully utilised and were kept in a bank. The Committee feels that the
grants given in the year for a particular purpose must be utilised properly and advises the
Ministry to release the grants only after receiving utilization certificate.
8.31 The Committee notes that notwithstanding its persistent recommendations to reduce
pendency of the cases before the Supreme Court, High Courts and the various subordinate
courts all over the country, there has not been any major breakthrough in the matter. Year
after year, particularly from 1999 onwards the pendency of cases has been continuously
increasing except in the Supreme Court where pendency has been arrested to some extent
due to extensive computerisation and a host of other initiatives. The above trend of
pendency is continuing instead of taking recourse to other alternative measures like setting
up of fast track courts, family courts and permanent Lok Adalats and disposal of a
considerable number of cases by these courts. The Committee has also been apprised that
the Government has been constantly reviewing accumulation of the arrears in Courts and

53
the problem is sought to be tackled by constitution of specialized benches and organizing
Lok Adalats at regular intervals; encouraging alternative modes of disposal, establishing
Fast Track Courts even at the level of Magistrates Court and the proposed Gram
Nyayalayas Bill 2006 etc. The Committee finds that specialised benches, Lok Adalats and
Fast Track Courts, though created as an alternative mode of disposal of arrears of cases,
have been suffering due to infrastructural lacunae or financial constraints and thus are not
able to meet the desired targets. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier
recommendation to call upon the Ministry to address the problem of pendency of the cases
by carefully analysing the factors responsible and searching viable solutions in addition to
the existing arrangements. The Committee further desires that since the pendency is
mounting day by day despite various measures taken in that regard, the Government,
should request the Law Commission of India to look into the whole gamut of the problem
in an holistic manner and submit its Report as expeditiously as possible. The Committee
also recommends speedy setting up of Gram Nyayalaya by the Government.
8.36 The Committee observes that there has not been any perceptible improvement in the
position of vacancies of Judges in Supreme Court and High Courts under the post 1993
arrangement. The Committee feels that time has come when the issue has not only to be
deliberated at the highest level, but also to be debated in the public domain so that there
could emerge an alternative system which could bring about an overall improvement in the
situation. The Committee, therefore, calls upon the Ministry to come forward with a
concrete alternative, addressing the issue of appointment of the Judges of the higher
judiciary by evolving a suitable mechanism involving the Executive, instead of solely
allowing the judiciary to choose the judges.
8.41 The Committee feels disgruntled with the persistent opposition for establishing
benches of the Supreme Court in other parts of the country without giving any convincing
reason or justification therefor. The Committee, therefore, endorses its earlier view that
establishment of benches of the Supreme Court atleast in Chennai on trial basis and then
in other parts of the country would be of immense help to the poor who cannot afford to
travel from their native places to Delhi. The Committee, therefore, feels that the Ministry
should come forward with a necessary Constitutional amendment to address this deadlock.
8.43 The Committee is of the view that while keeping up the efficiency and utility level
of Fast Track Courts, the Department of Justice should devise ways and means to avoid
hurried decisions by such courts so that innocent persons are not convicted for the offences
not committed by them. The Committee would like to impress upon the Ministry to
periodically monitor performance of the Fast Track Courts. The Committee also
recommends that necessary Central financial support may be accorded to the Fast Track
Courts and their numbers too may be augmented for speedy dispensation of cases. The
Committee desires that these Courts may be presided over by experienced Judges.
8.48 The Committee feels that there is a valid reason behind bringing uniformity in the
retirement age of Judges of High Courts and that of Judges of Supreme Court. The
Committee also feels that there is no rationale in the argument that at 62, a High Court
Judge is too old to continue to work in the High Court, but he can be entrusted to carry
out the duties of a Supreme Court judge for three more years. In that backdrop, the
Committee recommends that Government should immediately bring forward a
Constitutional Amendment Bill to raise the retirement age of Judges of High Courts from
62 to 65 i.e., at par with the retirement age of a Judge of a Supreme Court.
In that backdrop, the Committee feels that Tamil Language which has been declared
as the classical language by the Government of India and one of the languages mentioned

54
in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution could be considered by the Government to be the
official language of Madras High Court by providing necessary infrastructure for the same
in the first instance. Tamil language also contains legal terms/terminologies and the
Constitution of India has been translated in Tamil. The Committee therefore, feels that in
the light of the resolution passed by the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly the Government
of India by making enabling provisions in this regard should consider introduction of Tamil
as the official language of the Madras High Court. The Committee also desires that the
national languages may also be made official languages of High Courts if the concerned
State Legislatures adopt Resolution to that effect.
8.51 In that backdrop the Committee recommends that a Conference of all Registries of the
High Courts and Administrative Heads of Subordinate/Lower courts should be convened at least
once in a year under the overall supervision of the Registry of the Supreme Court. The
authorities can ventilate the problems faced by them and narrate the bottlenecks, while
participating in such fora. This would ensure that the benefits would percolate down to the lower
Courts also. In order to achieve this objective, the Committee recommends that the Supreme
Court Registry can call for the data, keeping in view the needs of the subordinate Courts also.
8.54 In view of the position explained by the Ministry, the Committee recommends that
all out efforts, including an amendment in the Constitution, if necessary, may be taken up
expeditiously to set up All India Judicial Service. The Committee presumes that the
consultations with the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of personnel, Public Grievances and
Pensions and also the UPSC would be necessary in the matter.
8.57 The Committee, therefore impresses, upon the Ministry to consider initiating
necessary action for bringing about changes in the names of the Madras, Calcutta, Bombay
and Uttaranchal High Courts in line with the official names of these metropolitan cities or
State. The Committee feels that this is an administrative matter and must be carried out
as early as possible, as recommended by the Committee in its previous Report.
8.60 The Committee endorses with the view of the Supreme Court that the collection of
data from various High Courts of the country is certainly a time consuming one, but the
Committee also feels that the requisite information must be gathered as early as possible by
making concerted efforts. The Committee also feels that the Supreme Court Journals,
containing all important judgements and cases should be publicized and commercialized to get
wider publicity and even unreported cases should also get references in the journals. In the
same way, different High Courts of the country can also publicize and commercialized their
judgments and cases through their journals and can make reference of unreported cases.
These publications may be in digital format so that any person can access it through Internet.
8.64 In that backdrop, the Committee feels there is valid reason behind introduction of
judicial impact statement in a Bill along with financial statement because on introduction of
such innovative methods in our Bills one can easily foresee the impact of a legislation on the
Judiciary. The Committee feels that in order to introduce new concepts, our country require
a careful study and survey of the new methods by the Government. The Committee is also
of the view that there must be a budgetary provision for meeting the expenses of additional
cases that may arise out of the new Bill along with budgetary requirement of staff. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that since the task of preparing a judicial impact
statement has been assigned to the Legislative Department, Government should as early as
possible render necessary financial assistance to the Department or bear all expenses incurred
by making special budgetary provisions for it. The Committee is of the view that the above
measures would have significant impact on reforming the Indian Judicial System and the
Government should, therefore, explore the possibility of introducing these methods.
