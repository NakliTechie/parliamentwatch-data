CHAPTER-I
BRIEF OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY
1.0 The Union Ministry of Law and Justice comprises of three Departments, namely, the Department
of Legal Affairs, Legislative Department and Department of Justice and through the said Departments,
the Ministry assists in the process of orderly change directed towards realization of the objectives set
out in the Constitution.
DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
1.1 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Department of Legal
Affairs renders advice to various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India on legal matters
and carry out the litigation work in various Courts, including the Supreme Court. Besides this, it is
also concerned with the appointment of Law Officers, namely the Attorney General of India, the
Solicitor General of India and the Additional Solicitor Generals of India, assists in entering into treaties
with foreign countries in matters of civil law inclusive of Hague Convention matters, executing
contracts and assurances of property on behalf of the President of India. It is also the nodal authority
in respect of Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal and National
Tax Tribunal.
1.2 The Department of Legal Affairs has a two tier set up, namely, the Main Secretariat, New
Delhi and the Branch Secretariats at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Bangalore. The nature of duties
discharged by the Main Secretariat and Branch Secretariats can be broadly classified into two areas–
Advice work and Litigation work. Advice caters to Central Government Departments/Offices at Centre
and in States/UTs and Litigation work relates to Central Administration Tribunal (CAT), High Courts
and Supreme Court.
1.3 The following are the organisations/institutions, which come under the overall supervision of
Department of Legal Affairs and get Grant-in-aid for their research works:–
(a) National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):- The National Legal Services Authority
has been constituted under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to monitor and
evaluate implementation of legal aid programmes and to lay down policies and principles
for making legal services available under the Act. The NALSA issues guidelines for the
State Legal Services Authorities to implement the legal aid programmes which are in turn
discharged through the following services:–
(i) Free Legal Services:-  It includes court fees, process fee and all charges payable
or incurred in connection with any legal proceedings, providing Advocate in legal
proceedings, etc.
(ii) Lok Adalats:- Lok Adalat is a forum where the disputes/cases pending in the
court of law or at pre-litigation stage are settled/compromised amicably. The
Lok Adalat has been given statutory status under the Legal Services Authorities
Act, 1987.
(b) Law Commission of India:-  The Law Commission of India is a non-statutory body,
constituted by the Government once in three years. The Commission was first constituted

2
in 1955. It consists of a Chairman, three full-time Members, one Member-Secretary and
three or more part-time Members, depending upon the nature of work. Law Commission
Reports are considered to be primary source of law reforms for Parliament, as well as
the judiciary.
(c) Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT):- Section 252 of the Income Tax Act, 1961
provides that the Central Government shall constitute an Appellate Tribunal consisting of
as many Judicial Members and Accountant Members as it thinks fit, to exercise the
powers and discharge the functions conferred on the Appellate Tribunal by said Act.
(d) Appellate Tribunal of Foreign Exchange (ATFE):- The Appellate Tribunal of Foreign
Exchange was established on 1
st June 2000 vide Government of India Notification No.
S.O. 531 (E), dated 1.6.2000 issued under section 18 of the Foreign Exchange Management
Act, 1999 (FEMA),
(e) Indian Law Institute (ILI):- The Indian Law Institute registered under the Societies
Registration Act, 1860 is engaged in research in the field of law.
(f) International Centre for Alternate Dispute Resolution (ICADR):- The International
Centre for Alternate Dispute Resolution was registered under the Societies Registration
Act, 1860 on 31
st May 1995. It is an autonomous organisation working under the aegis
of the Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Legal Affairs).
(g) Bar Council of India (BCI):- The Bar Council of India is a statutory body constituted
under the Advocates Act 1961, which has been empowered among other things to lay
down standards of professional conduct and etiquette for lawyers, maintain and improve
the standard of legal education in the country.
(h) Institute of Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies (ICPS):- The Institute of
Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies is a private body engaged in research in the field
of Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies and came into existence in 1965.
LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT
1.4 The Legislative Department acts mainly as a service provider in so far as the legislative
business of the Union Government is concerned. The Department is entrusted with legislative matters
and is responsible for drafting of all Government Bills and Ordinances and scrutiny and vetting of
subordinate legislations sponsored by various Union Ministries/ Departments. Rendering assistance to
Parliament and Joint/Standing Committees in respect to Bills before its enactment. It is also administratively
concerned with certain matters such as the Code of Civil Procedure, Limitation and Arbitration,
Contracts, Personal Laws, Evidence and Transfer of Property as regards legislation. There are over
hundred Central Acts falling under these subjects.
1.5 The Department has two wings under it mainly:-
(i) Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research : With a view to increase the availability
of trained legislative draftsmen in the country, the Institute of Legislative Drafting and
Research was established as a Wing of the Legislative Department, Ministry of Law and
Justice in January, 1989.
(ii) Of ficial Languages Wing and Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan : The Official Languages Wing
is a successor Organisation of the Official Languages (Legislative) Commission under the
Legislative Department. It has been entrusted with the preparation and publication of a
standard legal terminology for use in all Official Languages; authoritative texts in Hindi of
all Central Acts and Ordinances and Regulations promulgated by the President, all rules,

3
regulations and orders made by the Central Government under any Central Act and
authoritative texts of all Central Acts and Ordinances and Regulations promulgated by the
President in the respective Official Languages of the States and to arrange for the
translation of all Acts passed and Ordinances promulgated in any State into Hindi, if the
texts of such Acts or Ordinances are in a language other than Hindi.
1.6 Election Commission of India:  Legislative Department is also administratively concerned with
the Election Commission of India and Election laws connected with election to Parliament and State
Legislatures and to the offices of the President and the Vice-President. There is a separate Demand
No.63 for the Election Commission of India.
DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE
1.7 The duties of the Department of Justice, as per the Government of India (Allocation of
Business) Rules, 1961, include the administration of justice and the processing of appointment of
judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, appointment of Judicial Commissioners and Judicial
Officers in Union Territories, creation of All India Judicial Service and extension/exclusion of the
Jurisdiction of a High Court to a Union Territory.
1.8 The following institutions/autonomous bodies/policy initiatives come directly under the overall
supervision of Department of Justice:
(a) Supreme Court of India:- There is a separate demand No.65 (Appropriation) pertaining
to the Supreme Court of India. The expenditure to be incurred on the Supreme Court of
India is ‘Charged’  expenditure.
(b) Family Courts/Special Courts:-  The Family Court Act, 1984 was enacted with a view
to providing for establishment of Family Courts, to promote conciliation in and secure
speedy settlement of disputes relating to marriage, family affairs and related matters. In
accordance with the provisions of section 3 of the Act, the State/UT Governments can
establish Family Courts in the areas comprising districts, towns, city in the States having
population of ten lakhs. Even the States/UTs are competent to establish such court in
those areas with the State/UT having a population less than ten lakhs in consultation with
the concerned High Court.
(c) National Judicial Academy:-  The National Judicial Academy was registered as a Society
under the Societies Registration Act and came into existence with effect from 17
th August,
1993. The affairs of the Academy are managed by a Governing Council, which is chaired
by the Chief Justice of India. The registered office of the Academy is in Supreme Court
Building at New Delhi and another office was established in August, 2000 at Bhopal. The
main objectives of the Academy are to impart training to the Judicial Officers of the
States/Union territories and to study the court management and administration of justice
in States and Union territories.
(d) Centrally Sponsored Scheme:- The government is implementing a Centrally Sponsored
Scheme (CSS) for development of infrastructure facilities for judiciary since 1993-94.
The scheme covers construction of court buildings and residential accommodation of
Judicial officers/judges covering only Subordinate courts. Under the Scheme, funds are
provided to the States to augment their capacities for infrastructure development of the
District and Subordinate Courts. The modified CSS is to be implemented in a Mission
Mode approach over the years 2011-16 under the National Mission.
(e) National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms:  Set up for five years in
2011, and became fully functional with effect from the financial year 2012-13. The twin

4
goals of the Mission includes increasing access by reducing delays and arrears in the
system and enhancing accountability at all levels through structural changes and setting
performance standards and facilitating enhancement of capacities for achieving such
performance standards. The four schemes proposed to be pursued under the Mission are
as under;
(i) National Mission-Action Plan Implementation
(ii) Action Research and Judicial Reforms
(iii) Setting-up of Model Courts (Phase I)
(iv) Access to Justice- Government of India Project (New Scheme).
(f) Access to Justice- Externally Aided Project (UNDP assisted)
The Project started in 2009 with support under the United Nations Development Programme
(UNDP). The Project is being implemented in 7 States (Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand,
Madhya Pradesh, Orissa, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh) and aims to strengthen access to
justice for the marginalized - particularly women, scheduled castes, scheduled tribes and
minorities - by supporting strategies and initiatives that seek to address the barriers they
face.
(g)  Computerization of District and Subordinate Courts
E-courts Mission Mode Project Phase-I & II :- This is a scheme proposed in the
12
th Plan with components such as computerisation of 1000 new Courts, use of solar
energy under e-Courts Project, computerisation of the Public Prosecutors Office,
videoconferencing facility for jails, enhancement of ICT infrastructure of courts, digitization
of old case records, computerisation of Judicial libraries, up-gradation of application
software, SMS based services, Touch Screen Kiosks with a provision of R1280 crore
which would be spread over a period of five years.
(h) Establishment of Gram Nyayalayas under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008:-  The
Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 has been enacted by the Parliament to provide for the
establishment of the Gram Nyayalayas at the grass root level for the purpose of providing
speedy and inexpensive access to justice to the citizens at their door steps. The Central
Government would meet 50% of the recurring expenses to be incurred on the setting up
of these courts in the first three years subject to a ceiling of  3.20 lakhs per annum.
(i) The  Thirteenth Finance Commission:-  With the objective of improving justice delivery,
Thirteenth Finance Commission (TFC) has recommended a grant of  5000 crore over its
award period of 2010-15. This grant is aimed at providing support to improving judicial
outcomes, and is allocated for initiatives such as - operation of morning/evening courts/
shift courts, Alternate Dispute Resolution (ADR) Centres, training of mediators/conciliators
for ADR Centres, holding of Lok Adalats, provision of Legal Aid, training of judicial
officers, assistance to State Judicial Academies, training of Public Prosecutors, appointment
of Court Managers, maintenance of Heritage Court Buildings, etc.

cautions the Departments that they should take a pro active stance in the execution and
realization of the goals under the schemes and projects of the spending units under their
administrative jurisdiction and not merely play the role of a fund disbursing agency. The
Committee is hopeful that the Plan side of the demands for grants of the Ministry will go a
long way in bringing about better delivery of services.

3.7 The Commission has further informed that it has also taken up countrywide Systematic V oters
and Electoral Participation (SVEEP) programme with the twin objectives to complete enrolment and

12
maximize voter’s turnout in order to strengthen the Indian democracy. There is a scientific focus and
a range of targeted interventions to bridge the gaps in participation of youth, women, weaker sections
and others in the electoral process. This also entails the involvement of a large number of partner
agencies, media and educational institutions right up to the polling station level. Campaign for informed
and ethical voting is a major component of this programme. SVEEP entails a series of activities at the
field level and also related training and exchange of good practices within and outside the country.
Accordingly, an amount of R18.40 crores have been provided in Grants-in-Aid-General under V oters
Awareness in BE 2013-14.
3.8 The Committee appreciates the SVEEP programme under-taken by the Commission and
hopes that the programme will fill up the existing gaps in the education and empowerment of
the voters by creating awareness, especially among the rural, deprived and women in particular
so as to make our democracy more vibrant. However, the Committee feels that the actual
target of voters awareness programme should be clearly defined so that the country’s valuable
resources do not go waste. It should accordingly, implement the programme in a pre-determined
phased manner beginning with States nearer to elections.
3.9 Another important area of concern is the use of EVMs in the elections. An elector has
every right to know whether the candidate whom he has cast his vote through the electronic
voting machine has actually gone for that candidate. This is not possible in the present system.
The Election Commission has reported that they are in the process of attaching printer to the
EVM on a limited scale to begin with  for giving a printed copy of the vote cast by the elector
at the polling station. The Committee appreciates the initiative taken by the Election Commission
of India to give a printed confirmation of the vote one has cast for the candidate of his/her
choice to confirm that no manipulation in the voting machine has occurred after the vote has
been cast by the elector. The Committee hopes that this measure of the Election Commission
would go a long way in establishing the faith of the electors in the EVMs and also the
processes in the conduct of the elections.
3.10 The Committee takes note of the decrease in the budgetary proposals under heads such
as ‘Other Administrative Expenses’ and Major Works ‘Domestic Travel Expenses’ by  R25 lakh
and R4 crore, respectively, from RE 2012-13 to BE 2013-14 in compliance with the guidelines
of the Ministry of Finance regarding observance of austerity measures in expenditure. The
Committee appreciates the efficient use of Grants under these heads by the Commission and
hopes that this did not have any adverse impact on the functional efficiency of the Commission.

MODEL CODE OF CONDUCT
3.18 Model Code of Conduct is a set of guidelines laid down by the Election Commission to govern
the conduct of political parties and candidates in run-up to an election. It is intended to provide a level
playing field for all political parties, keep the campaign fair and healthy, avoid clashes and conflicts
between parties and ensure peace and order. Its main aim is to ensure that the ruling parties, either at
the Centre or in the States, do not misuse its official position to gain an unfair advantage in an election.
3.19 During the examination of the Demands for Grants (2012-13) of the Department, Members
were of the view that some paras of the Model Code of Conduct are already covered by other statutes
and the Election Commission still regards the Model Code of Conduct as voluntary in nature. The ECI,
in its Action Taken Replies on the Demands for Grants (2012-13) replied that.–
“….in the absence of any law or statute, the Election Commission uses its power conferred by
Article 324 of the Constitution and issues need based instructions/ directions regulating the
conduct of free and fair elections. This is necessitated because even after the lapse of 22 years
after the All-Party Committee on Electoral Reforms submitted its report, no statute covering
issues in respect of these reforms has been framed empowering the Commission to conduct free
and fair election. The Commission cannot avoid its responsibility by remaining a silent
spectator and merely paying lip service to the cause of electoral reforms and conduct of free
and fair elections in the country.”
3.20 The Committee had observed in its Report on the ATRs of the Government that.–
The Committee observes that Model Code of Conduct is a voluntary agreement between the
parties for regulating the conduct of political parties and its members, etc., during the process

14
of elections to Assemblies and Parliament. Its legal status is a grey area. After hearing the
views of the official of the Ministry of Law and considering the problems which have erupted
in the country, from time to time, in the last few years, the Committee feels that the Code,
which was voluntary in nature at one time has not remained so after insertion of para 16 A
in the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, which authorizes the Election
Commission, to suspend or withdraw the recognition of political parties in case of violation
of Code of Conduct. Besides, some of the paras in the code attract penal provisions in other
laws.
3.21 The Committee notes the submissions of the Election Commission. It, however, reiterate
its earlier recommendation on the issue and accordingly, urges the Government and ECI to
review the Model Code of Conduct. In this context, the Committee recommends deletion of
para 16A of the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment ) Order, 1968, and incorporate
the same in the Representation of People Act, 1951. The Committee also takes up this
opportunity to observe that the Code of Conduct be made effective from the date of Press
Note/Press Conference declaring the elections. The Committee, in this context, recommends
that the Code of Conduct should be made effective from the date of notification of elections
and not from the date of the declarations of the elections.

7.6. W ith regard to areas of cases pending in the Supreme Court, the Registrar, Supreme Court in
the meeting on the Demands for Grants (2013-14) made oral submission as under:–
“….. when we say case pendency, these are the cases filed and pending before the Court and
we will not say that there is a delayed matter. When we say that cases are delayed, we take
into consideration the total number of cases pending. Out of the statistics that I have just
given you, about 21,574 cases are only one year old. That means if we fix that cut off period,
the total number of cases where we can say that they are delayed will only be 47, 746. You
know that judge-population ratio fixed by the Ministry is the lowest in the country when
compared to any other country. So also the disposal rate of each individual judge is the highest
in the world. We can proudly say that within that limited available resources, the Supreme
Court and other courts are doing their job. We don’t have the infrastructure; we don’t have
the personnel and we are always at the receiving end where delays are attributed to the
Supreme Court or to the High Court. The amount which the Court collects as the court fees
goes to the Government, not even the half is given to the court. This is the problem.”

in enforcing discipline in the society, improvement in the compliance of the laws and consequently
curbing corruption in the society which is quite high on the Governments agenda, today.

28. The Committee takes note of the increase in the overall Budgetary allocation to the
Legislative Department. The Committee appreciates the austerity measures undertaken by the
Department following the directives of the Finance Ministry. The Committee suggests that the

58
VSP should upgrade on the quality of its publications in terms of layout, content and context.
It should also explore scope of bringing out e-books and e-publications. In Committee’s view,
active engagement of States in the promotion and publicity of its literature available in Hindi
by holding frequent competitions, seminars and conferences for school/college going students,
through the respective States, may increase the distribution of its books and journals among
more people. [Para 5.8]
29. The Committee appreciates the work undertaken by the Department during the last
financial year. The Committee takes note of the shortage of drafting personnel at various
levels. The Committee takes serious note of the shortage of drafting personnel as submitted
to it and urges the Department to coordinate with the Institute of Legislature Drafting and
Research (ILDR) in this regard for creation of a strong pool of experts in legislative drafting
from where it can draw it’s requirement. It is also of the considered view that the quality of
drafting of legislations should not be compromised at any cost and therefore, such an
important Department should be allowed to increase its strength so that the quality of
legislations drafted may not be affected. [Para 5.12]
30. A good fourteen years has gone by since the establishment of the ILDR in January,
1989. In this context, the Committee its constraint to note that a mere 24 Basic Courses of
three months duration each and 14 Appreciation Courses of fifteen days duration each has
been conducted in these fourteen years. The Committee is of the opinion that the institute has
not performed to its optimum. In this context, the Committee wishes to refer to the oral
submission of Secretary, Legislative Department stating that there is urgent need for augmenting
the Department’s personnel for legislative drafting which reflects the urgency of requirement
of skilled manpower. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the ILDR should frame
target of conducting a minimum of two Basic Courses and two Appreciation Courses each
financial year in view of the importance and necessity of having a strong pool of experts in
legislative drafting. [Para 5.13]
31. The Committee has also been continuously reminding the Department to expedite the
process of acquiring of land and construction of building for ILDR. However, the Committee
is again disappointed to note that only a token amount of R1 lakh has been allocated to ILDR
in BE 2013-14. The Committee believes that an institution of such a stature needs to have its
own building to carry out its functions. [Para 5.14]
32. The Committee appreciates the initiatives taken by the Department in facilitating the
voting right to Non-resident citizens of India and also the simplification of the process of
submission of application for registration. However, the Committee feels that these few
thousands registrations need to be increased to justify the voting rights that has been
conferred on the NRIs. The Committee recommends the Government to take more initiatives
in this regard. [Para 5.18]
33. Family Courts are important instrument to reduce the pendency of cases. Under
Section 3(1)(a) of the Family Courts Act, 1984 it is mandatory for the State Governments to
set up a Family Court for every area in the State comprising a city or a town whose population
exceeds one million. In other areas of the States, the Family Courts may be set up if the State
Governments deem it necessary. However, the Committee is not satisfied with the slow pace
of establishment of Family Courts in different parts of the country even after two decades of
the enactment of the Family Court Act, 1984. The Committee is given to understand that lack
of finances is one of the main cause for slow pace of setting up of Family Courts. There is
perhaps a persistent demand from the States for higher assistance for setting up such Courts

59
both for meeting the expenditure for establishing such Courts and also for meeting the cost
for running them. The Committee impress upon the Ministry of Law & Justice to review the
funding pattern so that more Family Courts are set up. [Para 6.11]
34. The Committee reiterates its view expressed in its earlier Reports that, the Ministry
should envisage setting up of maximum number of Family Courts across the length and
breadth of the country and continuously pursue with the State Governments to come forward
in setting of the family courts to meet the objective of Family Courts Act, 1984. The
Committee is optimistic that the Family Courts can meaningfully keep in sharing the work
pressure on the Subordinate Judiciary. [Para 6.12]
35. The Committee takes note of the information given by the Director, National Judicial
Academy. The Committee is unhappy to note that NJA could not utilize its duly allocated funds
and the purpose behind the enhanced allocation for the succeeding years was thus not fulfilled.
The Committee desires that NJA should make optimum utilization of funds allocated to it. The
Committee urges the Ministry to work in close coordination with NJA to ensure that the
allocated funds for the year 2013-14 are utilized and the procedural impediments are not
allowed to obstruct the process. [Para 6.17]
36. The Committee commends the work of the Department in reducing the number of
pending Utilisation Certificates (UCs) from State Governments, as informed by Department of
Justice to the Committee that out of R1245.35 crores released between 1993-94 to 2010-11,
UCs of only R31.84 crores are pending. The Committee desires that the pending UCs be
obtained from the concerned States/UTs so that further Grants-in-aid may be released on time.
[Para 6.24]
37. The Committee takes note of the fact that infrastructure development for judiciary
represents a major component of the plan outlay of the Department of Justice in BE 2013-14
and the programme is in operation as a Centrally sponsored Scheme since 1993-94. The
Committee is however, constrained to mind that certain States have not been able to take full
advantage of the Scheme and as a result, they have been able to draw comparatively lesser
funds. The Committee further notes that there are certain States which were not able to draw
funds under the Scheme consecutively for one or more years. The Committee is unable to
understand the reasons why certain States have not been able to take the benefit of the
Scheme, which is so much tilted in their favour. Further, the Scheme is in operation since
1993-94 and large funds of the Central Government are being spent under the Scheme in the
form of assistance to States for improvement of infrastructure for dispensation of justice by
them. Keeping these factors in view, the Committee feels that its high time for the Department
of Justice to going for a review/audit of the said Scheme so as to ascertain on both these
aspects namely (i) the uneven and irregular drawl/utilization of funds by certain States and
(ii) the actual development in infrastructure that have taken place in the States and the extent
to which this has helped in speeding up the trial of cases. The committee is, however, happy
to note that the Ministry has sought the willingness of the ILI for evaluation of this scheme.
The Committee recommends expeditious action in the matter. [Para 6.25]
38. The Committee deeply appreciates the initiative of the Department of Justice by
bringing about a National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms in June, 2011. The
Committee notes that some allocations are made for certain activities under this Mission in
the BE 2012-13 but, the work could not take off and no expenditure took place under the
Mission during the year 2012-13. The Committee finds some modest allocations for different
activities under the Scheme in BE 2013-14. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would take
action seriously under the National Mission, which visualizes a vast and laudable objective of

60
ensuring to people, including the marginalized sections of the Society prompt and quality
service. [Para 6.29]
39. The Committee is more than convinced that speedy and efficient dispensation of justice
is the need of the country today and for meeting this objective, the fast track Courts can play
a major constructive role. With the Central assistance of 75% (90% in case of North Eastern
States), the Committee does not foresee any hesitation on the part of the States to set up such
fast track Courts for speedy trial in serious crimes against society including those involving
children, the elderly or the vulnerable sections of the Society. The Committee is convinced that
in case we succeed in curtailing the trial period in the Courts, a definite message would go
in the Society creating a feeling in the mind of the common man to abide by the laws due
to the fear of being punished. Such a scenario in Committee’s view would help in bringing
down the rate of crime in the country. The Committee accordingly, recommends that the
Department of Justice should take all possible steps to ensure that the fast track Courts are
set up in appropriate situations in the States and the States do not face impediments in this
regard. [Para 6.30]
40. The Committee takes notes of the serious activities envisaged under the tentative
Action Plan of the National Mission. In Committee’s view, the various actions enumerated in
the Action Plan are complimentary and supplementary to each other and would definitely prove
to be helpful in not only in speeding up the disposal of cases but also enhancing the quality
of their disposal. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should work on the Action
Plan in all seriousness and at the right earnest so that there is considerable progress of action
in the financial year ahead. The Committee however, reiterates its recommendations on the
last year’s Demand for Grants that the Ministry should determine definite time lines and
targets for the implementation of the Mission and put in place an effective monitoring also.
[Para 6.33]
41. The Committee is happy note that an allocation of R5 crore has been made for Action
Research and Studies under Judicial Reforms. The Committee is in agreement with the view
of the Ministry that the Scheme be implemented on a continuing basis through the Twelfth
Five Year Plan as an activity under the National Mission. The Committee hopes that the
implementation of the Scheme would definitely enhance the capabilities of all those involved
in the dispensation of justice including the mediators and conciliators and speed up the disposal
of cases. [Para 6.36]
42. The Committee is highly appreciative of this ambitious initiative of the Ministry to set
up Model Subordinate Courts at selected places in the country on a pilot basis. The Committee
takes note that these Courts, set up under the pilot project would be a model in various
respects from the point of view of convenience of all concerned, efficiency and quality of
justice. The Committee is happy to take note of the allocation of R26 crore for this purpose
for the year 2013-14. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would take action in this matter
in their right earnest. [Para 6.40]
43. The Committee is optimistic that the setting up of Model Courts will result in higher
productivity in terms of reduced time lines and timely justice dispensation. However, it is
appalled to note that no fund was released in 2012-13 out of the R7.50 crore allocation for
the Scheme. It, therefore, urges the Department of Justice to beef up its strategy in
releasing its proposals by assigning a strict time frame. The Committee, therefore, hopes to
be apprised of the progress made in this respect at the time of submitting the Action Taken
Replies. [Para 6.41]

61
44. The Committee takes note of the fact that for the financial year 2012-13, an amount
of R5 crore was allocated for implementation of Scheme in the North Eastern States and J&K
also. The Committee is constrained to note that Government have not been able to fully utilize
the budgetary allocations for the Scheme during the Eleventh Plan. Now, for the year 2013-14,
the allocations have been raised to R8 crore. The Committee reiterates its earlier
recommendations that Government should sincerely speed up action under the Scheme so as
to achieve the desired results. The Committee hopes that Government would take concrete
action in the coming financial year. [Para 6.45]
45. The Committee, even on previous occasions had shown concern on the effectiveness of
implementation of the project during the Eleventh Plan period when the Ministry had not been
able to exhaust the allocated funds all through. The Committee, however, appreciates the noble
objective of the project and it hopes that the Ministry would through proper monitoring and
coordination be able to achieve the desired results. [Para 6.50]
46. A perusal of expenditure pattern reveals that except for the year 2010-11, the ratio of
funds released vis-a-vis the BE was less than 50%. The Committee wonders that since this is
a 100% Centrally Sponsored Scheme, the Department of Justice should not have faced the
problem that it faces in case of other Schemes. In the last two years the position in releasing
the funds has further deteriorated. The Committee regrets to note that despite the concerns
expressed by it in the report on Demand for Grants for the year 2012-13, the release of funds
in that year had remained very low, i.e., R90 crores out of R213.66 crores. The Committee,
therefore, directs the Governments to check this trend and remove the bottlenecks, if any, in
this regard. [Para 6.54]
47. The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry are in the process of implementing
Phase-II of the e-Courts Mission Mode Project. The Committee also takes note of the new
components added under Phase-II. The Committee hopes that the employment of IT in the
various fields associated with the functioning of the Courts, the advocates as well as the
litigants would go a long way in cutting on wasteful time and energy in the handling of
litigation work which is mostly handled manually at present.  [Para 6.58]
48. The Committee notes that no allocation has been made for the Phase-II of the Project
for the financial year 2013-14. Department has given justification that in view of the
Phase-I still in progress and slated to be completed only in March, 2014, the projection under
this component may be reflected from the financial year 2014-15. The Committee desires that
the Government may complete Phase-I as per the set Time-line so that Phase-II of the project
can be taken up without delay. [Para 6.59]
49.  ........ The Committee recommends that the Government should seriously examine the
feasibility of this proposal. [Para 6.60]
50. The Committee is happy to note that the Government has been sensitive towards the
lukewarm response from the State Governments for the setting up of Gram Nyayalayas and
a study has been carried out with reference to certain States for examining the effectiveness
of these Nyayalayas. The Committee hopes that the study undertaken by the Government
would definitely help in making necessary corrections and reinforcing the institution of Gram
Nyayalayas. In Committee’s view the initiative to have Gram Nyayalayas is a laudable one as
it is a useful link in making available justice at the door step and at a lesser cost. The
Committee hopes that the issues involved due to which there has been a reluctance on the
part of the States to have Gram Nyayalayas would be considered seriously by the Ministry and

62
all out efforts would be made to resolve them. The Committee also recommends the Ministry
to consider the feasibility of enhancing the Central assistance in order to motivate the States
to go in for the Gram Nyayalayas. [Para 6.65]
51. The Committee notes that the TFC had given a target of disposal of petty cases to the
tune of R225 lakhs per year and 75 lakhs cases by Lok Adalats in five years. By this
standards, the R20.57 lakhs cases cleared by Lok Adalats in 2012-13 is far from close to target.
The Committee also wishes to point out that the TFC had recommended a grant of  R5000
crore for the period 2010-15 out of which a mere R1425.23 crore has been released till
December 31,2012 which is not encouraging. Therefore, the Department of Justice should
review its performance in this respect and make holistic efforts to encourage States in setting
up of more shift Courts, Lok Adalats and ADR Centers to clear petty cases which are logging
the Courts. [Para 6.70]
52. The Committee is unhappy for the large number of vacancies that exist at present in the
Supreme Court and the High Courts in the country. The Committee is convinced that one of
the major factors for such large number of vacancies in the higher judiciary is the prevailing
system of selection of Judges for appointment in the Supreme Court and the High Courts. The
Committee reiterates its often stated view that the present system of appointment of Judges in
the higher judiciary calls for a review. The Committee accordingly, recommends the Government
to initiate action in the right earnest for reviewing the present system of filling up posts of
Judges in the Supreme Court and the High Courts. The Committee hopes that with Judges in
position in the Supreme Court and the various High Courts, the pendency of cases would lessen.
Incidentally, the Committee would also like to impress upon the judiciary to fill up the vacancies
of the clerical staff in the Supreme Court and the High Courts in the interest of efficiency by
reinforcing the secretarial support to the Judges. [Para 6.74]
53. The Committee is happy to note the proposal to have High Courts in the States of
Tripura, Manipur and Meghalaya. This, in Committee’s view would provide a much needed
relief to the persons belonging to these three States. This move would not only save on
litigant’s time and money but would also help in quicker disposal of cases. The Committee
would like the related modalities and procedures in the setting up of these High Courts to be
completed expeditiously. The Committee, in this context also strongly recommends that the
Circuit Bench of the Kolkata High Court at Siliguri should be made functional without any
further loss of time. [Para 6.75]
54. The Committee takes note of the justification furnished by the Supreme Court for
their projected demands. However, the details of expenses incurred under the different sub-
head of ‘Professional Services’ for the last three years may be furnished at the time of giving
Action Taken Replies. The Committee incidentally also observes in this context that the head
‘Professional Services’ be termed as ‘Security Services’ so as to reflect the correct nature of
the expenditure.  [Para 7.2]
55. The Committee feels that the unwillingness towards establishment of the other Benches
of the Supreme Court cannot be justified on the grounds that it would attract a cumbersome
process of Constitutional amendment and endanger national unity. The reply that Supreme
Court represents national symbol and a unifying force remains intact even if the benches of
the Supreme Court are set up as, several High Courts have benches and also, there are High
Courts which have jurisdiction over more than one State. The Committee strongly feels that
it does not play any divisive role in any way. The Committee, therefore reiterates its earlier
view for establishment of other Benches of Supreme Court of India in consonance with the
Article 130, Constitutional of India. [Para 7.4]

63
56. The Committee expresses its serious concern over the mounting of cases pending in
Supreme Court. In view of the submission made by the Registrar, Supreme Court to it, the
Committee urges the Government to conduct a comprehensive comparative study of the
working of the Supreme Court vis-à-vis the apex Courts of other countries in order to identify
the grey areas. Thereafter, the course of action to augment the institution may be laid down.
[Para 7.8]
57. The National Court Management System (NCMS) reports that India has one of the
largest judicial systems in the world, with over 3 crore of cases and sanctioned strength of
some 18,871 judges. The present judge to population ratio being 15.5 judges per million
population is grossly inadequate to dispense the justice in a reasonable time, which is also a
reason for the high pendency of cases. The Committee strongly recommends that the judge-
population ratio may be corrected by appointing more judges in Courts to reduce pendency and
ensure timely delivery of justice. In Committees view, timely delivery of justice would go a
long way in enforcing discipline in the society, improvement in the compliance of the laws and
consequently curbing in corruption in the society which is quite high on the Governments
agenda, today. [Para 7.9]
