CHAPTER – I 
 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
 
Ministry of Law & Justice 
1.0 The Ministry of Law & Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by the 
British Parliament. The key mandate of the Ministry includes providing policy support in 
administration of justice; furnishing legal advice to the Government of India and defending 
Government of India in various Courts and Tribunals; drafting Supreme and Subordinate 
legislations for Government of India; appointment, resignation and removal of Judges of the 
Supreme Court of India and High Courts in States; Legal aid to poor; access to Justice; 
Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms etc. 
1.1 The Ministry comprises the following three Departments: 
(i) Legislative Department; 
(ii) Department of Legal Affairs; and 
(iii) Department of Justice. 
 
1.2 The Ministry is presently under the overall charge of the Minister of Law and Justice 
assisted by a Minister of State. Each of the three departments is administratively headed by 
an independent Secretary. 
1.3 This report is on the Department of Legal Affairs.

DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS 
Functions of the Department 
1.4 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Department 
of Legal Affairs has been allocated the following matters:  
(i) Advice to Ministries on legal matters including interpretation of the Constitution 
and the Laws, conveyancing an d engagement of counsel to appear on behalf of the 
Union of India in the High Courts and subordinate Courts, where the Union of 
India is a party. 
(ii) Attorney General of India, Solicitor General of India, and other Central 
Government Law Officers of the States , whose services are shared by the 
Ministries of the Government of India. 
(iii) Conduct of cases in the Supreme Court of India and the High Courts on behalf of 
the Central Government and on behalf of the Governments of States participating 
in the Central Agency Scheme. 
(iv) Reciprocal arrangements with foreign countries for the service of summons in civil 
suits for the execution of decrees of Civil Courts, for the enforcement of 
maintenance orders, and for the administration of the estates of foreigners dying in 
India intestate. 
(v) Authorization of officers to execute contracts and assurances and of property on 
behalf of the President under Article 299 (1) of the Constitution, and authorization 
of officers to sign and verify plaints or written statements in suits by or ag ainst the 
Central Government. 
(vi) Indian Legal Service. 
(vii) Treaties and agreements with foreign countries in matters of civil law. 
(viii) Law Commission of India. 
(ix) Legal Profession including the Advocates Act, 1961 (25 of 1961) and persons 
entitled to practice before High Courts. 
(x) Enlargement of the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of India and the conferring 
thereon of further powers; persons entitled to practise before the Supreme Court, 
references to the Supreme Court under Article 143 of the Constitution of India. 
(xi) Administration of the Notaries Act, 1952 (53 of 1952). 
(xii) Income Tax Appellate Tribunal. 
1.5 Besides, the Department has also been allocated administration of the following acts: 
(a) The Advocates Act, 1961 
(b) The Notaries Act, 1952 
(c) The Advocates‟ Welfare Fund Act, 2001 
(d) The Commercial Courts Act, 2015 
(e) The India International Arbitration Centre Act, 2019 

 11 
1.6 Additionally, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) and the Law Commission of 
India fall under the administrative purview of the Department. The Department is the cadre 
controlling authority of the Indian Legal Service (ILS). The Department also handles the 
appointment of the Attorney General of India, the Solicitor General of India, and the 
Additional Solicitor General of India. The Department also approves grant-in-aid to specific 
institutions like Indian Law Institute, for encouraging legal education and research and for the 
advancement of the legal profession.  
Structure of the Department 
1.7 The Main Secretariat of the Department of Legal Affairs is located in New Delhi, and 
Branch Secretariats are located at Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata and Mumbai. The nature of 
duties discharged by the Department is broadly classified into two areas - Advice work and 
Litigation work. The Main Secretariat is headed by the Law Secretary, assisted by Additional 
Secretaries, Joint Secretary and several levels of Legal Advisers.  
1.8 There is a Central Agency Section in the Department, which is currently led by the 
Law Secretary and supported by Officers from the Government Advocates cadre of the ILS 
and other supporting staff, handles litigation at the Supreme Court on beh alf of all 
Ministries/Department of the Government of India as well as some administration of the 
Union Territories. The litigation work in High Courts and CAT (Principal Bench) is handled 
by the Litigation (High Court) Section and in Lower Courts by the L itigation (Lower Court) 
Section. 
1.9 The Department has a separate cell called the 'Implementation Cell' to handle the 
implementation of the recommendations of the Law commission of India and the 
administration of the Advocates Act, 1961 and the Advocates Welfare Fund Act, 2001. This 
Cell also administers the subject of 'Legal Education' as provided under Entry 26 of List III to 
the 7th Schedule of the Constitution of India.  
1.10 The Department tenders legal advice to all Ministries and Departments of the 
Government of India. The legal advice team of the Department is generally headed by an 
Additional Secretary or a Joint Secretary & Legal Adviser, who, in turn, is assisted by other 
officers at different levels. 
1.11 Under the Digital India initiative, the Department has created a web based application 
namely, Legal Information Management &  Briefing System (LIMBS) for monitoring all 
court cases where Union of India is a party. It is an innovative and easy to access online tool 
which is available 24x7 to all the stakeholders, viz., senior Government Officials, Nodal 
Officers and users of Mini stries/Department. LIMBS version 2.0 is an upgraded version of 
LIMBS and was launched in the year 2020 in collaboration with NIC.  
1.12 Further, the following Institutions/organizations are under the overall supervision of 
the Department of Legal Affairs: 

 12 
(i) Law Commission of India (LCI): - The Law Commission of India is a non-
statutory body, constituted by the Government once in three years. The 22nd Law 
Commission was constituted on 21.02.2020 for three years and is headed by Justice 
Ritu Raj Awasthi, Retired Chief Justice, Karnataka High Court along with three Full-
time Members and two Part-Time Members. The Commission is assisted by Law 
Officers of Indian Legal Service and a few consultants having experiences in legal 
research. A small group of secretarial staff looks after the administration. The Law 
Commission works on projects based on (a) Reference received from the Central 
Government and/or from Supreme Court of India and High Courts; and (b) Suo motu 
Cognizance - keeping in view the importance of the subject matter, the Commission 
may initiate study on specific subjects.  
(ii) Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT): - ITAT is one of the oldest Tribunals, 
set up under Section 252 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 to hear second appeals in all 
matters of direct taxes and appeals against the revision orders of Administrative 
Commissioners as well as orders denying registration under Section 12A or under 
Section 80G of the Income -tax Act 1961.  It functions as the final fact finding 
authority in the matters c oncerning Income -tax, wealth -tax, gift -tax etc. The orders 
passed by the ITAT are final; an appeal lies to the High Court only if a substantial 
question of law arises for determination. The Tribunal consists of 63 Benches, which 
is spread over 30 stations (including 02 Circuit Benches) throughout the country.  
(iii) Bar Council of India (BCI): - The Bar Council of India is a statutory body 
constituted under Section 4 of the Advocates Act, 1961, and it has been empowered, 
among other things, to lay down standards for professional conduct and etiquette for 
lawyers, and to maintain and improve standards of legal education in the country.  
(iv) Indian Law Institute (ILI):- The Indian Law Institute is a premier legal research 
institute founded on 27th December, 1956. The prime objective of the Institute is to 
promote advanced studies and research in law and to contribute substantially in 
reforming the administration of justice, so as to meet the socio-economic aspirations 
of the people through law and its instrumentalities. The Institute has been granted 
Deemed University status in the year 2004. 
(v) The India International Arbitration Centre (IIAC):- The IIAC has been 
established with a view to provide cost effective and timely services for the conduct 
of arbitration and conciliation at national and international level, facilitate conduct of 
international and domestic arbitration and conciliation and impart training in 
alternative dispute resolution and related matters in field of arbitration, conciliation 
and mediation. Its purpose is to bring reforms in the field of arbitration and 
conciliation and to develop it as a flagship institution for domestic and international 
arbitration. It will facilitate and provide administrative assistance for conduct of 
arbitration and conciliation proceedings in a professional and time-bound manner and 
in the most cost-effective way. It will also set up an arbitration chamber to empanel 
professional arbitrators at national and international level and set up an Arbitration 

 13 
Academy to train arbitrators in India, so as to empower them to compete on par with 
reputed arbitral institutions in the world. With the coming into force of the New Delhi 
International Arbitration Centre (Amendment) Act, 2022 the Centre has been renamed 
as India International Arbitration Centre (IIAC).  The Centre was notified on 26th 
July 2019. Justice Hemant Gupta, retired Judge, Supreme Court of India was 
appointed as Chairperson, of the Centre vide notification dated 23.12.2022.  Shri 
Ganesh Chandru and Shri Anant Vijay Palli have been appointed as Part-time 
Members in the Centre vide notification dated 23.12.2022. The Central Government 
has nominated Director General, Federation of Indian Export Organizations as Part-
time Member of the Centre and the Financial Adviser, Ministry of Law & Justice as 
ex-officio Member. Secretary, Department of Legal Affairs is also the ex-officio 
Member of the Centre.

2.9 From the table it can be seen that allocation made to the Department in BE 2022-23 
was significantly reduced from 397.48 crores to 292.95 crores at the RE stage. This was due 
to the reduction in the allocation to ITAT under capital head from 115 crores to 21 crores

2.10 The Department in their presentation before the Committee has informed that the 
ITAT capital outlay budget is for augmenting the infrastructure and housing needs of the 

 18 
benches of ITAT at different locations in the country. Presently there are ongoing projects of 
ITAT at four locations - Delhi, Lucknow, Ahmadabad and Kolkata. Besides fully utilizing 
the RE for 2022-23 they have a pending demand of NBCC to the tune of Rs. 29.00 crores for 
the present Financial Year. Thus they are not in a position to meet the demand of NBCC for 
the present year. Accordingly, the ITAT, in their written submission to the Committee has 
sought to enhance their BE allocation from Rs. 70 crores to Rs. 145 crores for the FY 2023-
24 to meet the demands for their ongoing capital projects.  
2.11 Out of the 30 Benches from which the ITAT is operating, only on 3 locations 
they are having their own building. On other 20 locations they are operating from the 
premises given by the Central Government and other Government bodies and on 7 
locations from buildings of private owners. Thus there is an urgent need to augment 
and enhance the infrastructural needs of the Tribunal. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends the Government to provide adequate funds to ITAT to complete their 
ongoing projects on schedule. The Department should approach the MoF to get the 
funds required for the FY 2023-24 so that the ongoing projects are not stalled due to 
scanty allocations.  
2.12 As far as Indian Law Institute (ILI) is concerned the Department of Legal Affairs is 
providing almost 40 to 45% of total salary expenditure as Grants-in-Aid every year during 
last 4 years. For the year 2022-23 Rs. 3.50 crores were provided to the ILI and for BE 2023-
24 against the projected Grant-in-aid of Rs. 6 crores 3.5 crores has been provided to the 
Institute.   
2.13 The ILI in their written submissions before the Committee has informed that the 
Institute is presently facing the following functional difficulties due to shortage of funds:  
 Since the expenses are persistently rising and funds are very limited, the Institute 
is not in a position to expand its activities due to shortage of funds. 
 The Institute is not in a position to renovate Class Rooms, Halls and other 
infrastructural developments due to paucity of funds.  
 All computers, servers and other IT related infrastructure of the Institute are 
mostly outdated and funds are needed for replacement of systems with latest 
configuration to avoid cyber security threats. 
 The Institute also needs funds to enhance research publications, books and other 
legal research activities. 
 It is also pertinent to mention that the Institute is facing huge difficulties in 
filling-up all vacant positions due to deficiency of funds. 
2.14 The ILI has further informed that research is one of the major focal point of ILI and 
its faculties are encouraged to take up research activities in interdisciplinary and 
contemporary issues. ILI provides full support in terms of administrative, academic and 
infrastructure facilities to enable faculty to take up projects. The academic findings of 
research work carried out by the Institute have been beneficial for the society at large. 
Faculty are engaged in active research by way of publication of books and articles in national 

 19 
& international journals. The proceedings of National and International Conferences/ 
Seminars are routine work of ILI, which educate the students as well as Faculty. Various 
Ministries have been allocating projects to the Institute on contemporary issues and findings 
of those studies are taken note of by the Government.  
2.15 From the submission of the Institute and analysis of the functioning of the ILI 
over the years, the Committee is constrained to observe that a premier institution 
founded for promoting and conducting legal research is becoming insignificant day-by-
day due to lack of funding from the Government. Faculty positions are remaining 
vacant, basic renovation and upkeep of the institution are held up due to lack of funds. 
Only a skeletal faculty/staff are maintained with the limited grants provided by the 
Government. As we don't have many institutions in the country for legal research and 
thus, the Government should maintain this institution and not let it die a slow death. 
The Committee wants to see the Institute to become a centre of excellence in the field of 
legal research, for which it requires the support of the Government. However, the 
Committee feels that if the Government is not in a position to maintain this institute, it 
is better to hand it over to some other institutions like Supreme Court of India or BCI

BAR COUNCIL OF INDIA (BCI) 
 
3.36 The Bar Council of India (BCI) is a statutory body constituted under the Advocates 
Act, 1961, which has been empowered among other things to lay down standards of 
professional conduct and etiquette for lawyers, maintain and improve the standard of legal 
education in the country and enforce discipline among the lawyers. The BCI also acts as an 
appellate authority in respect of disciplinary matters.  
3.37  The Bar Council of India (BCI) in its written submission before the Committee on the 
Demands for Grants (2022-23) had informed the Committee that lawyers in the country 
neither have any social security nor any protection from either State of Union Government. 
There is also lack of proper infrastructure and other facilities like sitting space, clean 
drinking water, electricity, libraries, medical aid, health insurance etc. for advocates in 
various courts.  
3.38 The BCI had further submitted that due to lack of funds, Bar Associations are not in a 
position to purchase law journals, computers, wifi facilities an d so forth, which hinders 
practice of law. The BCI has also highlighted the plight of advocates due to Covid -19 and 
has submitted that many advocates have died and dependents of advocates are facing acute 
financial problems affecting their livelihood. The BCI accordingly requested this Committee 
to consider recommending grants for the purpose through Bar Councils/Associations in order 
to carry out the welfare activities and also to meet the medical aid, health insurance facilities, 
and also library/other infrastructure facilities of Bar Associations.

Observations/Recommendations at a Glance 
 
CHAPTER – II - OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS 
ITAT 
1. Out of the 30 Benches from which the ITAT is operating, only on 3 locations 
they are having their own building. On other 20 locations they are operating 
from the premises given by the Central Government and other Government 
bodies and on 7 locations from buildings of private owners. Thus there is an 
urgent need to augment and enhance the infrastructural needs of the Tribunal. 
Accordingly, the Committee recommends the Government to provide adequate 
funds to ITAT to complete their ongoing projects on schedule. The Department 
should approach the MoF to get the funds required for the FY 2023-24 so that 
the ongoing projects are not stalled due to scanty allocations. (2.11) 
ILI 
2. From the submission of the Institute and analysis of the functioning of the ILI 
over the years, the Committee is constrained to observe that a premier 
institution founded for promoting and conducting legal research is becoming 
insignificant day-by-day due to lack of funding from the Government. Faculty 
positions are remaining vacant, basic renovation and upkeep of the institution 
are held up due to lack of funds. Only a skeletal faculty/staff are maintained 
with the limited grants provided by the Government. As we don't have many 
institutions in the country for legal research and thus, the Government should 
maintain this institution and not let it die a slow death. The Committee wants to 
see the Institute to become a centre of excellence in the field of legal research, for 
which it requires the support of the Government. However, the Committee feels 
that if the Government is not in a position to maintain this institute, it is better to 
hand it over to some other institutions like Supreme Court of India or BCI etc., 
like they did in case of Institute of Constitutional & Parliamentary Studies 
(ICPS) which was handed over to the Lok Sabha Secretariat. (2.15) 
 
IIAC 
 
3. The IIAC has been established with a view to compete with other reputed 
international arbitration centers and to strengthen an independent & 
autonomous arbitration regime in the Country. Though the Centre has now 
been formally created, but is still in its infancy, the Committee recommends the 
Government to provide it full financial support for creating the necessary 
infrastructure it needs for making it fully operational. (2.18) 
 

 36 
CHAPTER – III - ASSESSMENT OF FUNCTIONING OF THE DEPARTMENT 
STAFF STRENGTH IN THE DEPARTMENT 
4. The Department of Legal Affairs has been functioning with about 50% of the 
sanctioned strength for quite a long time, across its grades. The Department has 
admitted that the shortage of manpower for such long duration is affecting their 
smooth functioning. The Committee has been for long; in almost every DFG 
reports, is recommending the Department to take remedial actions to get rid of 
this perennial shortage of manpower in the Department. (3.10) 
5. The ILS, which is the mainstay of the Department and whose cadre control rests 
with the Department itself, has a vacancy of above 50%. The Department has 
further informed that they have initiated reforming the RRs of the cadre and 
vacancies will be filled thereafter. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Department to complete the revision of the RRs, at the earliest and start filling 
up of the vacant position in a dedicated manner. Further, the RRs should be 
revised in such a way so as to avoid possibility of future litigations, as litigations 
have been one of the reasons for vacancies remaining unfilled. The eligibility 
conditions and career prospects in the ILS should be framed in such a manner, 
that it attracts the best and the brightest candidates from the market. (3.11) 
6. For the cadres like CSS, CSSS, CSCS and CSOL, for which nominations are 
made by other cadre controlling departments, the Committee recommends the 
Department to pursue the matter vigorously on continuous basis with the DoPT 
and MHA, respectively, to get the requisite manpower for these grades.  (3.12) 
VACANCIES OF NOTARIES 
7. The Committee is concerned to note the level of vacancies in the central quota of 
public Notaries. More than 50% of posts are vacant and the pace of filling of 
vacancies is very slow. The Department has started providing facility for 
candidates to fill the application forms and to submit the fee through online 
mode. The renewal process is also being made online. These are welcome steps 
aimed to enhance the pace of filling up of vacancies. It will also bring out 
transparency in the process. The Committee expects the Department to pick the 
pace of filling of vacant posts of Notaries and to fill all the vacancies in a time-
bound manner. (3.18) 
 
8. On observing the State/UT-wise sanctioned posts of Notaries it can be observed 
that their distribution is quite uneven. For instance for a much bigger state like 
Uttar Pradesh the sanctioned posts for public notaries is 2650 only, whereas for 
Gujarat, which is a comparatively much smaller state in terms of population the 
strength of notaries is 8000. It is also not known what criteria are being used to 
sanction a particular number of notaries in a State. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends the Government to use a fair yardstick and take into account the 
factors like population, litigations etc. while sanctioning the posts of Public 

 37 
Notaries in a particular State. (3.19) 
 
9. The Committee in its previous reports had dealt with the issue of digital 
signatures and had recommended the Department to explore the feasibility of 
introducing Digital Signatures for authenticating notarized documents to reduce 
malpractices of anti-dating or/and attestation of blank document, etc. and 
improve transparency. The Committee also feels that along with digital 
signatures, the Department may also explore the feasibility of introducing e-
affidavits as practice of e-stamping is already in vogue and the Government of 
India is also promoting courts through video conferencing and virtual courts. 
Accordingly, the Committee expects the Department to give the status report on 
these recommendations and hindrances being faced in implementation of these 
recommendations. (3.20) 
ENGAGEMENT OF PANEL COUNSELS 
10. The Panel Counsels are engaged all over the Country by the Central 
Government to conduct litigations before the High Courts/Tribunals and other 
courts. They bear the major burden of litigations against the Central 
Government. From the submissions made by the Department, it is observed that 
the fees paid to these counsels are very meagre, which is much below the market 
rates. The last revision of their fees was done about 7 years back. Accordingly, 
the Committee recommends the Department to revise the fees paid to the panel 
lawyers suitably, considering the current market rates, so that they remain 
interested and committed in their jobs. It will also help in attracting competent 
lawyers for being empanelled as a panel lawyer for the Central Government. 
(3.23) 
 
11. Besides this, the Department should issue some sort of guidelines for 
empanelment of such lawyers, so that their selection criteria and process of 
selection becomes transparent and rule based. (3.24) 
INCOME TAX APPELLATE TRIBUNAL (ITAT)

are underway in accordance with the provisions of the Tribunal Reforms Act 
and all the vacancies are expected to be filled up by March, 2023. The 
Committee, therefore, is appreciative of the efforts of the Department in this 
regard and it hopes that the vacancies are filled up as per the timeline indicated 
by the Law Secretary. (3.29)
