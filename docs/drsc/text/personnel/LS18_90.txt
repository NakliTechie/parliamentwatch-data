CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY
INTRODUCTION
1.0 The Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions is the nodal Ministry responsible for
personnel matters, especially in respect of issues concerning recruitment, training, career development,
staff welfare and the post retirement dispensation. The Ministry also works towards promotion of
responsive, people-oriented and modern administration.
1.1 The Ministry comprises three Departments:
(i) Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)
(ii) Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG)
(iii) Department of Pensions and Pensioners' Welfare (DPPW)
1.2 The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister, assisted by a Minister of State.
The Department of Personnel and Training is functioning under the charge of an independent Secretary
while the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances and Department of Pensions and
Pensioners' Welfare function under the charge of a Secretary.
EVOLUTION OF THE MINISTRY
1.3 The management of personnel policies in the Government of India, to begin with, was the joint
responsibility of the Ministry of Home Affairs (Services and Establishment Wing) and Ministry of Finance
(Establishment Division), with Union Public Service Commission playing an important advisory role. The
Establishment officer to the Government of India, functioning under the Cabinet Secretary, had an
important role to play in the senior level appointments under the Government of India. All this made for
improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified central direction. To plug this organizational
loophole, the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha, in its 93rd Report (April 1966) had observed
that "the ever expanding role of the Government in a welfare state with its natural concomitant of a large
civil service calls for effective personnel control through a single agency". The Administrative Reforms
Commission (ARC) too urged upon the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel with
a full fledged secretary-in-charge, to be placed directly under the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal
agency for the personnel functions of the Government.
1.4 The Union Government accepted this recommendation, and as a consequence, led to the
reorganization of the Ministry of Home Affairs as it then stood, resulting eventually in the formation of
Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, as it stands today. This process was initiated on
June 26, 1970 when the Department of Personnel was notified as a department in the Cabinet Secretariat.
The process was taken further on August 1, 1970 with the notification of the items of work to be dealt

2
with by the Department. Eventually, on March 15, 1985, a full fledged 'Ministry of Personnel, Public
Grievances and Pensions' was notified.
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING (DoPT)
1.5 Various wings of DoP&T are as under:
(i) Establishment Officer's (EO) Wing- the Establishment Officer's Wing deals with the matter
relating to senior appointments under the Government of India requiring approval of the
Appointment Committee of Cabinet (ACC).
(ii) Services and Vigilance (S&V) Wing - the Services and Vigilance Wing handles matters
relating to cadre management of All India Services and acts as the nodal agency in the arena
of vigilance and anti-corruption. This wing in addition to governing the implementation of
RTI Act, also handles the administrative matters relating to the Administrative Tribunals and
Central Information Commission. This wing also handles the work related to cadre
restructuring in respect of the other Central Services.
(iii) Establishment (Estt.) Wing - The Establishment Wing is responsible for framing and revising
rules and regulations regarding service conditions of the employees and personnel policies
of the Central Government employees other than All India Service Officers. This wing also
handles the work related to Civil Services Officers' Institute (CSOI).
(iv) T raining (Trg.) Wing - The Training Wing acts as the nodal agency for training of Government
functionaries and is primarily responsible for formulating policies with regard to training.
(v) Administration Wing - Administration Wing deals with the matters related to cadre management
of the Central Secretariat Service, Central Secretariat Stenographers Service and the Central
Secretariat Clerical Service. This wing, also handles the administrative matters relating to
the State Re-organization, Reservation Policy of the Government and Welfare Activities for
benefit of the employees of the Government except CSOI.
1.6 The Department of Personnel and Training also handles the administrative work relating to the
following Institutions:
(i) Union Public Service Commission
(ii) Central Vigilance Commission
(iii) Lokpal

RECRUITMENT AGENCIES
1.7 The two organizations through which the Department ensures recruitment of personnel for
Government are the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and the Staff Selection Commission
(SSC). The former is constituted under a provision of the Constitution and is responsible for conducting
examinations for appointment to the higher civil services and civil posts under the Union Government,
including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a mandatory provision for consulting the
Commission on all matters relating to methods of recruitment, principles to be followed in making
promotions and transfers from one service to another and all the disciplinary matters. The SSC is
responsible for making recruitment for Group 'B' non-gazetted posts of Assistants, Stenographers, etc.
TRAINING INSTITUTIONS
1.8 Two major training institutions, directly under the administrative control of the Department, are
the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the Institute of
Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM), New Delhi. The former is mainly responsible for providing
induction training to recruits to the Indian Administrative Service and other All India Services and Central
Services. The Institute of Secretariat Training and Management provides induction as well as in-service
training to members of the Central Secretariat Service.
SENIOR APPOINTMENTS UNDER THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA
1.9 All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the approval
of Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the Government of India (Transaction of
Business) Rules, 1961 are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary to the ACC. These
include board level appointments to Central Public Sector Undertakings and appointments of Joint
Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the Ministries/Departments. In addition, all appointments
by promotion, which require approval of the ACC, are also processed through the Establishment Officer.
PUBLIC ENTERPRISES SELECTION BOARD (PESB)
1.10 The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making
recommendations for appointment to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executive and functional
Director and part-time Chairman in the Central Public Undertakings.
ADMINISTRATIVE VIGILANCE
1.11 The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for maintenance of
integrity of the public services and eradication of corruption, and coordinates the activities of various
Ministries/Departments in that area.
Central Vigilance Commission: Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Central
Vigilance Commission (CVC). It has jurisdiction and powers in respect of all matters to which the

4
executive power of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted with the
supervision of the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) so far offences investigated under the
Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 is concerned.
The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013: The Act has been brought into force with effect from
16th January, 2014. The Department is entrusted with the administration of the Act.
CENTRAL BUREAU OF INVESTIGATION
1.12 The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is the premier investigating police agency in the country,
created through a resolution of Ministry of Home Affairs dated April 1, 1963 administering Delhi Special
Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946, its jurisdiction covering all the Departments of the Government
of India, Union Territories, which could be extended to the States also with the consent of the State
Governments concerned. In due course, with the setting up of a large number of Public Sector
Undertakings, the employees of those undertakings were also brought under the purview of CBI. Similarly,
with the nationalization of the Banks in 1969, the Public Sector Banks and their employees also came
within the ambit of the CBI. By now, the CBI has become multi-disciplinary investigative agency, with
its role expanding from corruption cases to other cases viz cyber crimes, terrorist crimes, wildlife
crimes, narcotics, arms trafficking, arts and antiquities cases, counterfeiting of currencies etc.
JOINT CONSULTATIVE MACHINERY
1.13 A well structured three-tier machinery is in place for joint consultation between the Central
Government and its employees for promoting harmonious relations and securing the greatest measure of
cooperation between the Central Government and the general body of employees in matters of common
concern. The objective is to increase the efficiency of public services along with the well-being of the
employees. The three tiers of Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM) are:
(i) National Council- at the apex level, functioning under the Department of Personnel and
Training;
(ii) Department Councils- functioning at Ministries'/ Departments' level; and
(iii) Office Councils- functioning at offices/ organisations under various Ministries/ Departments.
CENTRAL ADMINISTRATIVE TRIBUNAL
1.14 In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to employees aggrieved by the Government
decisions, Government had set up the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) in 1985, which deals with
all the cases relating to service matters of Central Government employees.
RIGHT TO INFORMATION
1.15 The Department is entrusted with the administration of the Right to Information Act, 2005 and
Rules thereunder. The Department also overlooks the administration of the Central Information Commission
including selection of Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners.

STAFF WELFARE
1.16 The Department of Personnel and Training is intimately involved in various staff welfare and other
role led activities. Mention in this connection can be made of the institution of Grih Kalyan Kendra (a
society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860), the Kendriya Bhandar (a registered cooperative
society set up in 1963) and the Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board (a society registered
under the Societies Registration Act, 1860).
1.17 The Depart ment also supports various programmes, which are provided in various Ministries/
Departments, Central Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in Central Government
offices located outside Delhi, Residents' Welfare Associations/Area Welfare Officers, Benevolent Fund
and Departmental Canteens.
INDIAN INSTITUTE OF PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION
1.18 Department of Personnel and Training also provides grant-in-aid to Indian Institute of Public
Administration (IIPA), New Delhi which undertakes research studies on contemporary public issues and
provides training to officers of Government of India on public policy.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEV ANCES
1.19 The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) is the nodal agency
of the Government of India for administrative reforms as well as redressal of public grievances relating
to the States in general and those pertaining to Central Government agencies in particular. The Department
endeavors to document and disseminate successful governance practices by way of audiovisual media
and publications. The Department also undertakes activities in the field of international exchange and
cooperation to promote public service reforms. The Department is headed by the Secretary, Department
of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances and Pensions and Pensioners' Welfare. Available staff
strength is one secretary, one Additional secretary, two Joint Secretaries, 6 Directors/Deputy Secretaries
and 16 Under Secretary level officers in the Department. There are 7 Divisions in the Department namely
Administrative Reforms, Organization and Methods, e-Governance, Documentation and Dissemination,
International Exchange and Cooperation, Administration and Coordination and Public Grievances.
1.20 As per the Government of India Allocation of Business Rules, 1961 the following subjects have
been allotted to the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances:
(i) Administrative Reforms, including e-governance and dissemination of best practices.
(ii) organization and Methods.
(iii) Policy, coordination and monitoring of issues relating to —
(a) Redress of public grievances in general; and
(b) Grievances pertaining to Central Government agencies.
(iv) Research in public management and Liaison with State Governments, professional institutions
etc. in public management matters.
(v) Administration of Central secretariat Manual of office Procedure.

(iii) Capacity building, change management and Government Process Re-engineering to provide

DEPARTMENT OF PENSIONS AND PENSIONERS' WELFARE (DPPW)
1.22 The Department of Pension and Pensioners' Welfare was set up in 1985 as part of the Ministry
of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions to formulate policy and coordination of matters relating
to retirement benefits of Central Government employees (including Defence and Railway Pensioners).
1.23 The main functions of this Department are:
(i) Formulation of policies and coordination of matters relating to retirement benefits of Central
Government employees, including Defence and Railways;
(ii) Administration of:
(a) Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972;
(b) Central Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981;
(c) Central Civil Services (Extraordinary Pensions) Rules 1939;
(d) General Provident Fund (Civil Services) Rules, 1960; and
(e) Contributory Provident Fund (India) Rules, 1962;
(iii) Pension policy structure;
(iv) Examination and extending the scope of new facilities for fringe benefits to the Central
Government Pensioners;
(v) Matters relating to amendments to or relaxation of Pension Rules or any other rules concerning
retirement benefits; and
(vi) Policy and coordination relating to welfare of Central Government Pensioners/Family
Pensioners.

DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING (DoPT)
Vacancies in the Indian Administrative Service (IAS)
3.1 Indian Administrative Service (IAS) is the premier service of Government of India. The IAS has
been a hallmark of governance in India and has been manning the key and strategic positions throughout
the Union of India. Sardar Vallabhai Patel, one of the eminent leaders of the freedom struggle referred
to them as the steel frame of the country. The cadre controlling authority of the IAS is DoPT.
3.2 As per the submission made by the DoPT before the Committee, total authorized strength as per
Civil List as on 1.1.2016 for IAS and incumbency position is as under:
Table No. - XVI
Vacancies in the IAS
Authorized strength Incumbency V acant positions
       6396 4926 1470
3.3 The Committee was informed that out of 1470 vacancies, about 900 vacancies are from the Direct
Recruitment quota and rest from States promotion quotas. The Secretary, DoPT, in his presentation
before the Committee also informed that 900 direct recruitment shortfall is primarily due to two reasons.
One reason is that the cadre review has been done very recently and the number of posts has gone up.
The second one is, for ten years in the past, the recruitment was abnormally low. These two factors
resulted in shortfall in the direct recruitment. He further stated that the direct recruitment cannot be
stepped up all of a sudden as the training capacity for IAS officers is only 180 per year. Moreover, if
large number of officers are inducted in one go, there would be problem of cadre management. The
DoPT had set up B.S. Baswan Committee and that Committee recommended that it would not be
appropriate to increase the annual intake of IAS beyond 180 as it would compromise quality, exceed
LBSNAA's capacity and lead to distortion in career pyramid of IAS officers. Every year, 120 directly
recruited officers retire and as a result of this, effectively only 60 officers are added every year to the
IAS.
3.4 On a perusal of Annual Report 2016-17 of the Ministry, the Committee observes that
shortage of IAS officers in this Country has been a perennial problem since 1951. But now the
vacancy position has increased to an alarming level. With the present rate of recruitment it will
require decades to fill the existing vacancies. The Committee notes that almost all the key and
22

23
strategic positions under the Union as well as the States are being manned by the IAS and
persistent shortage in the IAS officers ultimately affects governance in the country. The Committee
expresses its serious concern over persistent shortage of IAS officers and strongly recommends
that all efforts be made to fill these vacancies. The Committee further notes that the Director,
LBSNAA has informed it that Academy has the capacity to train more than 180 officers, it,
therefore, fails to understand as to why lack of training capacity has been cited as one of the
reasons for not filling the vacancies of IAS officers. The Committee feels lack of training
capacity should not become a reason for non-filling of vacancies of IAS officers and if we are
actually short of capacity in this regard, efforts should be made to enhance our capacity to train
more IAS officers and that should not be a difficult to achieve.

at places wherever required, so that the hearing at the main Bench does not get disturbed.

of physical as well as human, should be provided to CAT, to achieve the desired level of
performance. Going by the present pace of efforts, the situation is unlikely to improve as is
evident from the fact that there are about 30% vacant positions and only about 12 crores has
been earmarked for infrastructure development. The Committee feels that more concerted
efforts are required to make CAT effective and to serve the purpose for which it was established.
3.26 The Committee notes from the submission made by CAT that it has a requirement of

settled within the system itself so that minimal number of cases go to Tribunal.

Public Grievance Redressal & Public Service Delivery
3.71 The Department is entrusted with the responsibility for policy, coordination and monitoring of
issues relating to (a) Redress of Public Grievances in general and (b) Grievances pertaining to Central
Government Agencies, in particular. The Public Grievance Division of the Department is responsible for
this activity since December 1987. From 1997, the Division has also been made responsible for several
citizen centric initiatives under the platform of 'Responsive Government'. These include Citizens' Charter,
Information Facilitation Counters (IFC), and Quality Management System (QMS) framework called
Sevottam, for bringing improvement in public service delivery on a continuous basis, which may result
in Certification under Indian Standard 15700:2005 by Bureau of Indian Standards.
3.72 The management of public grievances envisages reduction in grievances, by bringing continuous
improvement in Public Service Delivery through the extension of Quality Management System ‘Sevottam’
at the Centre as well as in the States. Statutory backing for improvements in service delivery was sought
to be given through the 'Right of Citizens For Time Bound Delivery of Goods and Services and Redressal
of Their Grievances Bill, 2011 ’ that had been introduced in the Lok Sabha on 20.12.2011. It lapsed with
the dissolution of 15th Lok Sabha. The Department informed the Committee that it is now proposed to
implement a Scheme for Delivery of Services and Redress of Public Grievances.

42

Monitoring System' (CPGRAMS) has evolved since 2007. It was submitted to the Committee that at
present its upgraded version 5.0 is accessible at http://pgportal.gov.in and also through www.darpg.gov.in.
It interlinks 139 Ministries/Departments/States/UTs and other apex organizations in CPGRAMS. There
are 26400 sub-ordinate users listed on it which includes subordinate and field offices also. The Committee
was further informed that the number of subordinate users linked to CPGRAMS has increased from
about 1500 in 2010-11 to over 26000 in 2016-17. CPGRAMS is also available in Hindi.

2017-18. The Department has also proposed to utilize the allocation for the Project Management unit
(PMU) established for monitoring systemic reforms for reducing grievances and improving public service
delivery. A Project for complete revamp of CPGRAMS with many new features like lateral transfer of
grievances, downward transfer of grievances from Ministries to State Governments provision of local
language interface, Dashboards etc. is also under consideration of the Department.
3.76 Dealing with the grievances of citizen effectively is one of the core components of Good
Governance. CPGRAMS is a good initiative of the Government for redressal of citizen grievances.
However, public at large, particularly those who are living in rural and far-flung areas needs to
be made aware of existence of such grievance redressal system. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that this system of Public Grievance Redressal (CPGRAMS) should be publicized in
mass media in vernacular languages, so that common people become aware of availability of such
avenue for redressal of their grievances against the Government organisations.
3.77 The Committee feels that the spirit of Public Grievances Redressal Mechanism is primarily
to resolve the grievances in a professional and satisfactory manner. The relief and remedial
mechanism need to fix the accountability on the decision making authorities. As in the event of
unsatisfactory redressal of their grievances, the public usually approach courts for redressal of
their grievances which increases burden of courts, every endeavour should be made that grievances
are resolved in a professional and satisfactory manner.
3.78 In order to make grievance redressal system (CPGRAMS) more effective, it is recommended
that Government may also consider making a provision for appeal in the existing system.
3.79 The Committee also takes note of the fact that over the time there has been a substantial

that all those areas where large number of grievances are received, are examined and in those
common areas of grievances, after analyzing the root cause, systemic changes should be brought
in to reduce the volume of grievances pertaining to them. Also, suo-motu information relating
to those areas should be largely publicized on the Department's website, news papers and other
such media.

additional funds in the financial year 2017-18 at RE stage, so that its work doesn't suffer due
to lack of funds. (Para 3.47)
As Examinations have gone online, the Committee recommends that the SSC must ensure
that software, hardware and other facilities at examination venues are audited and made to
conforms to global standards. Also, problems like server crashing, computer hanging, login
failure, abrupt shutting down of computers during examinations which are real time problems,
being faced in a computer based examination should be adequately taken care of so that no
candidate suffer because of this. (Para 3.48)
The CIC was established in the year 2005 and even after a decade of its establishment,
the CIC is severely understaffed. As per the information provided by the Commission, against
a sanctioned strength of 160 only 25 personnel are on permanent basis and rest are working on
ad-hoc basis. Even after Recruitment Rules are framed the appointments have not been made.
The Committee expresses its dismay over manning of the Commission's Secretariat through ad-
hoc and temporary appointments and strongly recommends that appointments of regular employees
are done at the earliest, so that Commission's working is made more responsive and accountable.
(Para 3.54)
The Committee in its previous report on Demands for Grants 2016-17 has highlighted the
problem of shortage of faculty positions in the Academy and recommended that other feasible
options of handling this problem may be explored. However, no progress is visible in this respect
and the Committee expresses its anguish over it. The Committee again recommends that the
DoPT should explore all possible options for augmenting and expanding its existing training
capacity at the LBSNAA and ISTM and ensure availability of full strength of faculty and other
supporting staff for efficient functioning of these training Institutes. (Para 3.61)
The Committee also observes that the Academy has been slow on spending on scheme/plan
side in the FY 2016-17. Out of ````` 13.70 crore allocated under Capital head, only ````` 9.21 crore have
been spent and out of 14 crore allotted under Revenue head only 8.74 crore have been spent.
In view of under-utilization of fund sought on scheme/plan side, the Committee observes that
every spending unit should make a prudent assessment of its requirement of funds under plan
schemes it proposes to undertake and seek of budgetary allocations to the extent it can utilize.
Non-utilisation of allocated funds put unnecessary financial burden on the Government and,
therefore, it should be avoided. Also, strategies should be drawn for the utilization of full funds
made available to spending units and utilization should be closely monitored to ensure efficient
utilization of funds. (Para 3.62)

51
IIPA is a premier institution on Public Administration in India under the patronship of the
Hon'ble Vice-President of India. The Committee is pained to observe that such an institution of
national importance is not able to raise adequate funds for its maintenance and performance of
its functions. Institutions like IIPA needs to be protected, nurtured and nourished, but side by
side such premier Institution should become self-sustaining and should be able to raise themselves
the resources needed by them and not depend on public funds. The Committee recommends that
Government should render all possible help to this Institute to make it a self-sustaining Institute
of global excellence and for that if public funds are to be provided for some time, it should be
done. (Para 3.68)
Dealing with the grievances of citizen effectively is one of the core components of Good
Governance. CPGRAMS is a good initiative of the Government for redressal of citizen grievances.
However, public at large, particularly those who are living in rural and far-flung areas needs to
be made aware of existence of such grievance redressal system. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that this system of Public Grievance Redressal (CPGRAMS) should be publicized in
mass media in vernacular languages, so that common people become aware of availability of such
avenue for redressal of their grievances against the Government organisations. (Para 3.76)
The Committee feels that the spirit of Public Grievances Redressal Mechanism is primarily
to resolve the grievances in a professional and satisfactory manner. The relief and remedial
mechanism need to fix the accountability on the decision making authorities. As in the event of
unsatisfactory redressal of their grievances, the public usually approach courts for redressal of
their grievances which increases burden of courts, every endeavour should be made that grievances
are resolved in a professional and satisfactory manner. (Para 3.77)
In order to make grievance redressal system (CPGRAMS) more effective, it is recommended
that Government may also consider making a provision for appeal in the existing system.
(Para 3.78)
The Committee also takes note of the fact that over the time there has been a substantial
increase in the number of public grievances received by the Department. The Committee desires
that all those areas where large number of grievances are received, are examined and in those
common areas of grievances, after analyzing the root cause, systemic changes should be brought
in to reduce the volume of grievances pertaining to them. Also, suo-motu information relating
to those areas should be largely publicized on the Department's website, news papers and other
such media. (Para 3.79)
Further, as of now, 21 States/UTs have enacted statute for time bound delivery of services.
The Right of Citizens for Time Bound Delivery of Goods and Services and Redress of their
Grievances Bill, 2011 was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 20th December, 2011. However, it could
not be passed and lapsed with the dissolution of the 15th Lok Sabha. The Committee feels that
Delivery of Government Services and Grievance Redressal should have statutory backing. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should consider re-introduction of the
said Bill in the year 2017-18. (Para 3.80)

52
The Committee appreciates the role of the Department in addressing the grievances of
pensioners. The initiatives like 'Bhavishya', 'Sankalp' and 'Anubhav' are good measures and the
Committee hopes that maximum number of pensioners will be benefited by these services. The
Committee, at the same time would like the Department to make more efforts to reach out to
its primary stakeholders i.e. pensioners and especially family pensioners about the latest
developments and changes brought about in entitlements including the additional facility of
submission of Aadhaar based Digital Life Certificate. As more funds have been allocated to the
Department for BE (2017-18) towards publicity and advertisement, the Committee hopes that
Department would ensure that its schemes reach to the target groups i.e. pensioners staying in
every nook and corner  of the country. (Para 3.90)
The Committee also notes that pensions are generally disbursed through Public Sector
Banks and many a time the banks do not disburse pension timely and correctly. Pensioners are
at times harassed by these banks and their grievances with them remain unresolved. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that Department should also take up the matter with these
banks, so that the pensioners' grievances pertaining to the disbursing banks, are also satisfactorily
resolved. (Para 3.91)
The Committee feels that filling of pension forms and pension procedures still remain
complicated, even though some welcome steps have been taken by the Department in this
regard. The Committee recommends that number of pension forms should be further rationalized/
reduced and simplified. For this, the Department may consider taking the services of National
Centre for Good Governance (NCGG). The Department may also provide option of filling online
application for pension and other pensionary benefits. (Para 3.92)
