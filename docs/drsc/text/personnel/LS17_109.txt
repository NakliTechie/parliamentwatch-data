REPORT 
 The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the 
Ministry of Law and Justice on the recommendations/observations of the Department-related 
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice 
contained in its One Hundred Seventh Report on the Demands for Grants (2021-22) of the 
Ministry. The Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on 
the 16th March, 2021.  
2. The Committee had made as many as 49 recommendations/observations in its One 
Hundred Seventh Report. Replies of the Ministry of Law and Justice thereto have been 
categorized and scrutinized under four chapters on the basis of satisfaction of the Committee. 
Final replies in respect of four recommendations/observations i.e., para nos. 2.19, 3.15, 3.20, 
and 3.22 are awaited from Departments of the Ministry. Further, three paras i.e., para nos. 
2.55, 3.4, and 3.4 of the report on the Demand for Grants (2021-22), Ministry of Law and 
Justice relates to general observations of the Committee on the functioning of 
Departments/Supreme Court of India/Election Commission of India. Out of 42 
recommendations where final replies have been received as many as 21 (50 percent 
approximately) of total action taken replies are satisfactory. 
Chapter-I: The recommendations/observations of the Committee which have been accepted 
by the Ministry contained under paras 2.14, 2.7, 2.33, 2.40, 2.47, 3.11, 4.15, 4.20, 4.21, 4.32, 
4.34, 5.40, 5.48, and 5.55. The Committee‟s further recommendations/observations are 
contained in paras 1.2, 1.10, 1.17, 1.20, 1.23, 1.27, 1.30, 1.33, 1.36, 1.40, 1.43, 1.46 and 1.49 
Chapter-II: The recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to 
pursue in view of the replies given by the Ministry relates to paras 3.31, 3.38, 3.39, 5.16, 
5.26, 5.32, and 5.41. The Committee‟s further recommendations/observations are contained 
in paras 2.5, 2.8, 2.11, 2.14 and 2.17. The Committee has accepted the explanations furnished 
by the Ministry and does not wish to pursue those further as of now. 
Chapter-III: The recommendations/observations in respect of which Committee has not 
accepted the replies of the Ministry are under paras 3.6, 3.32, 3.29, 3.9, 2.26, 4.4, 4.9, 
4.10,4.14, 4.24, 4.25, 4.26, 4.27, 4.36, 4.40, 2.9, 2.37, 2.44, 2.50, 5.15, and 5.20. The 
Committee‟s further recommendations/observations are contained in paras 3.4, 3.5, 3.8, 3.11, 
3.14, 3.17, 3.20, 3.24, 3.27, 3.30, 3.36, 3.37, 3.40, 3.43, 3.51, 3.54, 3.57, 3.58, 3.61, 3.64 and 
3.67. The Committee does not accept the reasons given for not accepting its 
recommendations and reiterates the same.  
Chapter-IV: The recommendations/observations on which final replies of the Ministry of 
Law and Justice have not been received relate to paras 2.19, 3.15, 3.20 and 3.22 of the 
Report. The Committee desires that replies to this paras may be furnished without further 
delay. 
3. The details of the ATR have been discussed in the respective Chapters in the 
succeeding pages.

OVERVIEW OF THE BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS 
LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT 
 
Recommendations/Observations of the Committee 
1.0 The Committee is of the view that allocation of Rs. 1218.40 crore in BE  (2021-22) 
against the demand of Rs. 2550.61 crore by the Legislative Department may adversely affect 
optimal execution of programmes and activities of the Department. The shortfall of Rs. 
1332.21 crore in allocation in BE (2021-22) over the projections is too large to be ignored as 
major portion of the projection was for the capital expenditure for EVMs. The Committee is, 
therefore, of the considered opinion that the Department should undertake multi -pronged 
strategies to ensure that its budgetary allocati on is appropriate, rational and adequate in 
meeting the requirements of the Department.                                                           (Para 2.14) 
 
Action taken/proposed to be taken by the Department 
1.1. Short fall of Rs. 1332.21 crore in r/o Legislative Department, the Major component of 
Rs. 428.87 crore projected for under the Major Head 2015 - Election. Against of Rs.428.87 
crore, Rs.164.30 crore has been allocated under the 2015 -Election resulting short fall of Rs. 
264.57 crore.  Rs.264.57 crore were projected in RE Stage and Election Commission 
submitted a projection for (Rs.1942.79+ 125.99=2068.78) crore for procurement of EVMS, 
VVPTs etc., the Lok Sabha Election, 2024. Against the Demand for Rs.2068.78  crore, MOF 
has allocated Rs.1005.00 crore for the current financial years resulting shortfall of Rs. 
1063.78 crore.  The same will be projected at the RE stage.  
 
Committee’s further observation 
1.2. The Committee acknowledges the reply and hopes that additional amount will be 
projected by the Department during the RE Stage.

(iii) The existing CSS for Development of Infrastructure was valid up to 31.03.2021 only. 
An EFC Note was required to be moved for continuance of the scheme. It is proposed to 
extend the scheme for a further period of 5 years i.e.up to 2025 -26.  As per the directions of 
the Ministry of Finance, the Central share for the scheme is to be restricted to 5.5 times of the 
allocation made to the scheme during FY 2019 -20 (Rs.982 crore x 5.5 = Rs.5401 crore).  
Accordingly, an EFC Memorandum has been prepared for continuation of the Scheme, 
wherein a sum of Rs.5400 crore as Central share is proposed to be allocated under the 
Scheme for five years i.e. 2021 -22 to 2025 -26.  Out of Rs.5400 crore, a sum of around 
Rs.930.00 crore has been projected for the year 2021 -22.  Originally, the scheme included 
construction of court halls and residential Units only for the Judicial Officers.  However, for 
quite some time frequent criticism was faced from civil society on account of non-availability 
of toilets in the court halls and non -availability of lawyers‟ hall etc.  Be sides, in the present 
scenario owing to COVID-19 pandemic, the need for Digital Computer Rooms was also felt.  
The Third Party evaluation of the scheme was conducted which has also recommended 
including of these facilities in the scheme.  Accordingly, prov isions of lawyers‟ hall, toilets 
and Digital Computer Rooms have been proposed to be included in the scope of the Scheme.  
Inter-Ministerial views and comments of NITI Aayog had been sought and incorporated in 
the EFC Memo.  The EFC meeting took place on 1 2.04.2021 under the Chairmanship of 
Secretary, Department of Expenditure.

FAST TRACK SPECIAL COURTS (FTSCS) 
1.7 (i) Rs.160.00 crore was projected and allocated under RE for the FTSCs Scheme 
during the FY 2020-21 and 100% has already been released by Department of Justice. It is 
pertinent to mention that the Scheme started only in October 2019 and after obtaining 
requisite consent from States/UTs, most of the funds could be released only during January to 
March 2019. Hence not much time was available with the States to physically spend this 
money during the remaining part of the FY 2019-20.  As per provisions of GFR, Utilization 
Certificates (UCs) have become due in March 2021. The Department of Justice is constantly 
following up with States/UTs for furnishing UCs in FY 2020-21, funds were released to 
concerned States/UTs only after obtaining status of utilization of funds as per norms of 
Department of Expenditure to obviate parking of funds.  In FY 2021-22, funds will be strictly 
released only after obtaining due UCs. 
 
(ii) The present Scheme was initially formulated for a period of one year spread over two 
financial years (FY 2019-20 and 2020-21). The Scheme is being processed for extension of 
another 2 years (FY 2021-22 and 2022-23) for which EFC meeting was held on 12.04.2021. 
However, Rs.200.00 crore has been allocated for the Scheme in BE 2021-22.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH THE COMMITTEE DOES NOT 
DESIRE TO PURSUE IN VIEW OF THE REPLIES GIVEN BY THE MINISTRY 
ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA 
ONE NATION, ONE ELECTION & COMMON ELECTORAL ROLL 
Recommendations/Observations of the Committee 
2.0 The Committee is of the view that idea of ‘One Nation, One Election’ or simultaneous 
election is not new to our country as the first three General Elections held 
in 1952, 1957 and 1962 were held simultaneously only and it can be brought into the 
mainstream with suitable amendments to the Constitution. In view of this, certain 
provisions of the Constitution may be amended to have fixed terms for local bodies, 
legislative assemblies and the Lok Sabha for holding simultaneous elections. The terms 
of Legislative Assemblies and the Lok Sabha are also required to be synchronized with one 
another and the mechanism in this regard requires to be evolved with political 
consensus as the term of the respective State Assemblies needs to be reduced or increased. 
                               (Para 3.31) 
 
2.1 The Committee understands that preparation of Common Electoral Rolls will 
require certain amendments in the Constitution, but it is good for the democratic health 
of our country as it will lessen the burden on exchequer. The Committee feels that wider 
consultation with all political parties should be held to reach a political consensus and 
State Governments should be persuaded to amend their respective laws for using 
voters’ list prepared by ECI for conducting election to their Local Bodies.             (Para 3.38) 
2.2 The Committee takes note of the submission of the Election C ommission that  
about 20 States are already using the Electoral Roll prepared by Election Commission  
of India for conducting their Local Bodies elections which are conducted by the State  
Election Commissions. The introduction of common electoral roll will n ot only purify  
voters’ list, check electoral malpractices and unnecessary duplication but also ensure  
that there is no discrepancy in Local Body and Assembly voters’ list. The Committee in  
its 84th, 101st Report and 105th Report had recommended for common electoral roll in  
the country for election to Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and the Local Bodies. It believes that 
common Electoral Rolls can only help realizing simultaneous elections to  
the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Local Bodies. The Committee reiter ates its 
recommendation for implementation of Common Electoral Roll in the country and  
desires that an all out efforts should be made in this regard.                                   (Para 3.39) 
 
Action taken/proposed to be taken by the Department 
2.3 As per Section 13D of the Representation of the People Act, 1950, the electoral roll 
for every parliamentary constituency shall consist of electoral rolls for all the assembly 
constituencies comprised within that parliamentary constituency, and it shall not be necessary 

20 
 
to prepare or revise separately the electoral roll for any such parliamentary constituencies. 
Therefore, there is common electoral roll for both Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assembly 
elections as of now. As informed by the Election Commission of India (ECI), twenty-five 
States and eight Union Territories are presently using the electoral roll data prepared by them 
for preparing their electoral roll for local bodies/panchayat elections. 
 
2.4 The ECI had informed that one of the reasons for the reluctance of some states to use 
the ECI Electoral roll is that there is only one qualifying date for registration/updation of the 
roll prepared by the ECI i.e. 1st day of January of every year, whereas some states have 
multiple qualifying dates. This ensures the updation of the Roll four times in a year. In order 
to persuade the remaining States to use the Electoral Roll prepared by the ECI for conduct of 
their Local Body elections, it is proposed to amend section 14(b) of the Representation of the 
People Act, 1950 to insert four qualifying dates, i.e. 1st January, 1st April, 1st July, and 1st 
October, of every year. Accordingly, a comprehensive draft Note for the Cabinet containing 
this proposal as well as a few other important electoral reform proposals taken up by the 
Election Commission of India in under preparation. 
Committee’s further observation 
2.5 The Committee appreciates the initiatives of ECI and hopes that  necessary 
amendment to Section 14(b) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 by providing 
multiple qualifying/updation dates will encourages States to use Common Electoral

3.25 The Committee further recommends that the existing process of recommendation of

the Department for selection and appointment of notaries.                                     (Para 4.10) 
 
Action taken/proposed to be taken by the Department 
 
3.26 The existing process of recommendation of notaries gives 100 percent weightage to 
Interview Board‟s decision. The candidate is assessed on the basis of his knowledge in the 
sphere of Constitutional Law, Property Law/Commercial Laws & his length of experience 
and area of expertise. A candidate is assessed on the basis of his basic knowledge about 
discharging his duties as a Notary in terms of provisions of Notaries Act, 1952 and Rules 
framed thereunder. Further, candidates are also selected on the basis of availability of exiting 
Central Public Notaries in the desired Area of Practice in a particular District/Taluka in State. 
 
Committee’s further observation 
 
3.27 The Committee Notes the reply of the Department. However, the Committee 
feels that the existing practice of awarding 100 percent weightage to the Interview 
Board’s decision gives scope for discretion and therefore, the Committee recommends 
the Department to work out a broad parameter and transparent mechanisms to 
awarding marks for selection of Notaries. The Committee also feels that weightage of 
Interview Board’s decision may be limited to a maximum of 50 percent of the total 
marks required for recommendations of notaries.

VACANCY OF JUDGES IN COURTS AND PENDENCY OF CASES 
 
Recommendations/Observations of the Committee 
3.62 The Committee notes that there are still a large number of vacancies of Judges in 
High Courts. The Committee had also taken note of the problem in its previous Reports. It is 
of the view that justice delivery system in the country is moving towards a stage, where both 
vacancy positions of judges and pendency of cases are increasing year after year and this 
state of affairs may erode that trust of public on the justice delivery system. The committee 
strongly recommends that existing vacancy positions of judges need to be filled up 
immediately and the vacancies arising in future should be filled strictly as per the extant

Action Taken/proposed to be taken by the Department 
3.63 (i) As per procedure prescribed in the Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) for 
appointment of Judges in the Higher Judiciary, prepared in 1998 pursuant to the Supreme 
Court Judgment of October 6, 1993 (Second Judges case) read with their Advisory Opinion 
of October 28, 1998 (Third Judges case), the initiation of proposal for appointment of Judges 
in the Supreme Court vests with the Chief Justice of India, while initiation of proposal for 
appointment of Judges in the High Courts vests with the Chief Justice of the High Court 
concerned. 
 
(ii) As on 23.03.2021, the sanctioned strength of Supreme Court is 34, against which 29 
Judges are in position, leaving 5 vacancies to be filled. Against the sanctioned strength of 
High Courts of 1080, 670 Judges are in position, leaving 410 vacancies to be filled. At 
present, High Court Collegiums (HCC) have made 196 recommendations against 410 
vacancies. Out of these 196 recommendations, 54 proposals are pending with SCC while 11 
proposals which have not been recommended by SCC are to be remitted by the Government 
to the High Court and the remaining 131 proposals recommended by HCC and SCC which 
have been received are under various stages of processing with the Government. There are 
214 vacancies in various High Courts for which recommendations are yet to be received from 
respective High Court Collegiums. 
 
(iii) Filling up of vacancies in the High Courts is a continuous, integrated and 
collaborative process between the Executive and the Judiciary. It requires consultation and 
approval from various Constitutional Authorities both at state and Centre level. While every 
effort is made to fill up the existing vacancies expeditiously, vacancies of Judges in High 
Courts do keep on arising on account of retirement, resignation or elevation of Judges and 
also due to increase in the strength of Judges.  
Committee’s further observation 
3.64 The Committee observes that as against the sanctioned strength of 34 judges in 
the Supreme Court; 33 judges are in position as on 08.09.2021. In the High Courts, 
against the sanctioned strength of 1098 judges, only 633 judges are in position and 465 
are vacant. Out of the 196 recommendations made by High Court Collegium, 54 (29 
percent approx.) are pending with the Supreme Court and 131 (71 percent approx.) 
with the Government. The Committee feels that all the stakeholders involved in the 
appointment of Judges should make concerted effort, especially the Department of 
Justice, as 71 percent of recommendations  for judges are pending with it, which may be 
one of the causes of vacancy of High Court judges in the country.
