CHAPTER-I
BRIEF OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY
1.0 The Ministry of Law and Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by British
Parliament. In accordance with the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the
Ministry comprises three Departments, namely, Department of Legal Affairs, Legislative Department and
Department of Justice. The key mandate of the Ministry is to provide policy support in administration
of Justice; furnishing of legal advice to Government of India and defend Government of India in various
Courts and Tribunals and draft supreme and subordinate legislations for Government of India, etc.
DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
1.1 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Department of Legal
Affairs renders advice to various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India on legal matters and
carries out the litigation work in various Courts and Tribunals. Besides this, it is also concerned with
the appointment of Law Officers, namely, the Attorney General of India, the Solicitor General of India
and the Additional Solicitor Generals of India; assists in entering into treaties with foreign countries in
matters of civil law on behalf of the President of India. It is also the nodal authority in respect of
Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange, Income Tax Appellate Tribunal and National Tax Tribunal. The
Department of Legal Affairs has a two-tier set-up, namely, the Main Secretariat at New Delhi and the
Branch Secretariats at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Banglore.
1.2 Following Institutions/organizations are under the overall supervision of Department of Legal
Affairs:-
(a) Law Commission of India:-  The Law Commission of India is a non-statutory body,
constituted by the Government once in three years. It consists of a Chairman, three full-
time Members, one Member-Secretary and three or more part-time Members, depending
upon the nature of work. The Twenty-first Law Commission headed by Dr. Justice Balbir
Singh Chauhan was constituted with effect from 1st September, 2015 for a three year term
ending on 31st August, 2018.
(b) Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT):- ITAT is one of the oldest Tribunals, setup under
Section 252 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 to hear appeals against order passed by authorities
mentioned under Section 253 of IT Act. It has 63 Benches covering all cities having seat
of High Courts.
(c) Appellate Tribunal of Foreign Exchange (ATFE): - The Appellate Tribunal of Foreign
Exchange was established on 1st June, 2000 vide Government of India Notification No.
S.O. 531 (E), dated 1.6.2000 issued under Section 18 of the Foreign Exchange Management
Act, 1999 (FEMA).

2
(d) Bar Council of India (BCI):-  The Bar Council of India is a statutory body constituted
under section 4 of the Advocates Act, 1961, which has been empowered among other
things to lay down standards of professional conduct and etiquette for lawyers, regulate the
standard of legal practice and legal education in the country.
(e) International Centre for Alternate Dispute Resolution (ICADR):-  The International
Centre for Alternate Dispute Resolution was registered under the Societies Registration Act,
1860 on 31st May 1995. It is an autonomous organisation working under the aegis of the
Department.
(f) Indian Law Institute (ILI):- The Indian Law Institute registered under the Societies
Registration Act, 1860, is engaged in research in the field of law. It was inaugurated on 12th
December, 1959 by Dr. Rajendra Prasad, the then President of India. The Institute has been
granted Deemed University status in 2004.
LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT
1.3 The Legislative Department acts mainly as a service provider in so far as the legislative business
of the Union Government is concerned. The Department is entrusted with legislative matters and is
responsible for drafting of all Government Bills and Ordinances and scrutiny and vetting of Subordinate
legislations sponsored by various Union Ministries/Departments. Rendering assistance to Parliament and
Joint/Standing/Select Committees in respect to Bills before its enactment. It is also administratively
concerned with certain matters i.e. the Code of Civil Procedure, Limitation, Arbitration, Contracts,
Personal Laws, Evidence and Transfer of Property.
1.4 The Department has following two wings under it:-
(i) Official Languages Wing and Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan: The Official Languages Wing
is a successor organisation to the Official Languages (Legislative) Commission under the
Legislative Department. It has been entrusted with the preparation and publication of a
standard legal terminology for use in all Official Languages; authoritative texts in Hindi of
all Central Acts and Ordinances and Regulations promulgated by the President, all Rules,
Regulations and Orders made by the Central Government under any Central Act and
authoritative texts of all Central Acts and Ordinances and Regulations promulgated by the
President in the respective Official Languages of the States and to arrange for the translation
of all Acts passed and Ordinances promulgated in any State into Hindi, if the texts of such
Acts or Ordinances are in a language other than Hindi.
(ii) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan: The Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan is mainly concerned with bringing
out authoritative Hindi versions of reportable judgements of the Supreme Court and the High
Courts with the objective of promoting the progressive use of Hindi in the legal field. It
brings out various publications of legal literature in Hindi and also holds exhibitions in
various States for giving wide publicity to legal literatures available in Hindi and to promote
their sales.

3
1.5 Election Commission of India: Election Commission of India is an autonomous constitutional
authority created under Article 324 of Constitution for superintendence, direction, control of elections to
Parliament, State legislature, and to offices of the President and the Vice-President of India. The Election
Commission of India is a multi-member body (since 1993) headed by the Chief Election Commissioner
who acts as Chairman of the Commission. The Commission is assisted by Deputy Election Commissioners,
Chief Electoral Officers (CEO), District Election Officers (DEO) and Electoral Registration Officers
(ERO) appointed by the Election Commission under Representation of People Act, 1951. It recognizes/
derecognizes political parties and allot symbols to them. It also implements Model Code of Conduct
(MCC) for smooth conduct of free and fair election. An exclusive demand (Demand No. 62) under which
Budgetary allocation is sought for running activities of Election Commission of India.
DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE
1.6 The duties of the Department of Justice, as per the Government of India (Allocation of Business)
Rules, 1961, include the administration of justice and the processing of appointment of judges of the
Supreme Court and High Courts, appointment of Judicial Commissioners and Judicial Officers in Union
Territories, creation of All India Judicial Service and extension/exclusion of the jurisdiction of a High
Court to a Union Territory.
1.7 The following institutions/autonomous bodies/policy initiatives come under the administrative control
of the Department:
(a) Supreme Court of India:- The Supreme Court of India is the highest constitutional court
and also the final court of appeal.
(b) National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):- The National Legal Services Authority has
been constituted under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to provide legal-aid to the
poor and marginalized sections of society. The Chief Justice of India (CJI) is the patron-
in-chief and second senior most judge of Supreme Court is the Executive Chairman of the
authority. Besides providing free legal aid, it organizes Lok Adalats, off and on, for amicable
settlement of disputes. The NALSA issues guidelines for the State Legal Services Authorities
to implement the legal-aid programmes which are in turn discharged through the following
services:-
(i) Free Legal Services : It includes court fees, process fee and all charges payable or
incurred in connection with any legal proceedings, providing Advocate in legal
proceedings, etc.
(ii) Lok Adalats : Lok Adalat is one of the Alternative Disputes Resolution Mechanisms.
It is a forum where the disputes/cases pending in the court of law or at pre-litigation
stage are settled/compromised amicably. The Lok Adalat has been given statutory
status under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. Under this Act, an award made
by a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a decree of a civil court and is final and binding
on all parties and no appeal lies against thereto before any court.

4
(c) Family Courts/Special Courts :- The Family Court Act, 1984 was enacted with a view
to providing for establishment of Family Courts, to promote conciliation and secure speedy
settlement of disputes relating to marriage, family affairs and related matters. In accordance
with the provisions of Section 3 of the Act, the State/UT Governments can establish Family
Courts in the areas comprising districts, towns, cities in the States having population of ten
lakhs. Even the States/UTs are competent to establish such court in those areas with the
State/UT having a population less than ten lakhs in consultation with the concerned High
Court.
(d) National Judicial Academy:- The National Judicial Academy was registered as a society
under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 and came into existence with effect from 17th
August, 1993. The affairs of the Academy are managed by a Governing Council, which is
chaired by the Chief Justice of India. The registered office of the Academy is in Supreme
Court Building at New Delhi and another office was established in August, 2000 at Bhopal.
The main objectives of the Academy are to impart training to the Judicial Officers of the
States/Union Territories and to study the court management and administration of justice in
States and Union territories.
(e) Centrally Sponsored Scheme: The government is implementing a Centrally Sponsored
Scheme (CSS) for development of infrastructure facilities for judiciary since 1993-94. The
scheme covers construction of court buildings and residential accommodation of judicial
officers/judges covering only Subordinate courts. Under the Scheme, funds are provided to
the States to augment their capacities for infrastructure development of the District and
Subordinate Courts.
(f) National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms: Set up for five years in 2011,
and became fully functional with effect from the financial year 2012-13. The twin goals of
the Mission include increasing access by reducing delays and arrears in the system and
enhancing accountability at all levels through structural changes and setting performance
standards and facilitating enhancement of capacities for achieving such performance standards.
The two schemes proposed to be pursued under the Mission are as under:
(i) Action Research and Judicial Reforms
(ii) Access to Justice- Government of India Project.
(g) Access to Justice- Externally Aided Project (UNDP assisted)  The Project started in 2009
with support under the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). The Project is
being implemented in seven States (Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha,
Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh) and aims to strengthen access to justice for the marginalized
- particularly women, scheduled castes, scheduled tribes and minorities - by supporting
strategies and initiatives that seek to address the barriers they face. The duration of the
project is from January, 2013- December, 2017.

5
(h) Computerization of District and Subordinate Courts
E-courts Mission Mode Project Phase-I & II:- This is a scheme proposed in the Twelfth
Plan with components such as computerisation of 1000 new Courts, use of solar energy
under e-Courts Project, computerisation of the Public Prosecutors Office, video-conferencing
facility for jails, enhancement of ICT infrastructure of courts, digitization of old case
records, computerisation of Judicial libraries, up-gradation of application software, SMS
based services, Touch Screen Kiosks with a provision of ` 1280 crore which would spread
over a period of five years.
(i) Establishment of Gram Nyayalayas under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008:- The Gram
Nyayalayas Act, 2008 has been enacted by the Parliament to provide for the establishment
of the Gram Nyayalayas at the grass root level for the purpose of providing speedy and
inexpensive access to justice to the citizens at their door steps. The Central Government
would meet 50 percent of the recurring expenses to be incurred on the setting up of these
courts in the first three years subject to a ceiling of ` 3.20 lakhs per annum.
(j) The Fourteenth Finance Commission has recommended ` 9,749 crore to the Department
of Justice for pendency reduction by establishing additional courts, Fast Track Courts,
establishment of Family Courts, redesigning existing courts to make them litigant-friendly,
augmenting technical support for e-courts, scanning and digitalization, enhancing access to
justice- Legal-aid, Lok Adalats, Alternative Dispute Resolution, Training and capacity building
of judges, public prosecutors, mediators and lawyers and establishing State Judicial Academies
for Manipur, Meghalaya and Tripura.

2017-18 are shown under the following three Demands:-

COMMON ELECTORAL ROLL
4.19 It has been observed that in many cases, a particular elector is registered as a voter at multiple
places, which is a cause of concern and worry. It gives rise to electoral mal-practices, including bogus
voting. The Commission during the meeting submitted that the Election Commission has data for the
entire country and the central server of the Commission is with C-DAC, Pune. The State Election
Commissions are independent bodies and have their own electoral rolls. Some of them prepare their own
electoral rolls and some use electoral rolls of Election Commission of India. The Commission further
submitted that with the help of software the Commission is able to detect a large number of bogus voters
in the country. In the case of recent Assembly election in the States of Uttar Pradesh (32 lakh),
Uttarakhand (42 Thousand), Goa (15 Thousand) and Punjab (2.37 lakh) bogus voters were removed
from the electoral rolls.
4.20 The Committee is happy to know that digitization of electoral rolls and use of software has
helped the Commission to detect a large number of bogus voters in the electoral rolls. This is
a welcome step towards purification of electoral rolls in the country. The Committee is of the
view that linking voter with the Aadhar Card number may further help prevent bogus voters
from enrolling in the electoral rolls. The Committee, though is aware that Election Commission
of India and State Election Commissions are independent bodies, yet an initiative should be
taken to prepare common digital electoral roll which could be used both by Election Commission

21
of India and State Election Commissions. This would not only save huge national resources but
would also be a major step forward towards purification of electoral rolls.

at General Elections. Those parties which do not fulfill the requirements of 6A and 6B of the Act in the
next General Election still continue to retain with recognition for one more General Election to the House.
If that party fails to come up with the required level of poll performance in second General Election,
the recognition of that party is withdrawn at that stage. Besides that the Election Commission is
empowered to suspend or withdraw recognition of a party or its failure to observe model code of
conduct. Recognised/Registered political parties are entitled to certain benefit which are enumerated
below:
Registered Un-recognized

Party' subject to the fulfillment of the conditions prescribed by the Commission in the
Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, as amended from time to time.
(iii) As per Section 77 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the registered political

(iv) As per para 10B of the Election Symbol (Reservation 86 Allotment) Order, 1968, as one time

(v) As per the provisions of Section 29C of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the

4.22 As to deregistration of political parties, the Committee has been apprised that the Commission has
not been vested with the powers to deregister political parties. The Hon'ble Supreme Court in its
judgement in the case of Indian National Congress (I) vs. Institute of Social Welfare and Others
(Appeal(civil) 3320-21 of 2001), [(2002) 5 SCC 685] has observed that the Election Commission can
cancel registration of political parties under the following three situations only:-
• where a political party has obtained registration by practicing fraud or forgery;
• where a registered political party amends its nomenclature of association, rules and regulations
abrogating therein conforming to the provisions of Section 29A (5) of the Act or intimating
the Election Commission that it has ceased to have faith and allegiance to the Constitution
of India or to the principles of socialism, secularism and democracy or it would not uphold
the sovereignty, unity and integrity of india so as to comply with the provisions of Section
29A(5) of the Act; and
• Any like ground where no enquiry is called for on that part of the Commission.

NOTARIES APPOINTMENT
5.21 The administration of the Notaries Act, 1952 and the Notaries Rules, 1956 are within the purview
of the Notary Cell of the Department of Legal Affairs. The Schedule under Rule 8(4A) of the Notaries
Rules, 1956, Central Government has fixed the quota of maximum number of Notaries to be appointed
by the Central Government as well as maximum number of Notaries to be appointed by State/UT
Government. Central Government enhances the quota allotted to State Governments on receipt of request
for the purpose from the State Government concerned.
5.22 In its written replies to the questionnaire on the appointment of Notaries, the Department of Legal
Affairs submitted that according to Rule 3, of the Notaries Act, 1952, any advocate who had been
practicing for ten years is eligible for appointment as Notary except for women and person belonging
to SC/ST/OBC category. Rule 4 to 8 of the Notaries Rules prescribe the procedure for appointment of
Notaries. These rules stipulate that applicant will be called for an interview and only upon recommendation
of the interview board an applicant shall be appointed as Notary. However, the Notaries Rules, 1956 do
not provide for any time-limit for these appointments. The interviews are being held on the discretion
of the appropriate Government and the decision for interview of applicants from a particular State is
taken after assessing the demand i.e. number of applications received for appointment in that particular
State. The notaries appointed by such procedure are being issued a Certificate of Practice which remains
valid for a period of five years. As regards renewal procedure of Notary, it may be mentioned that Rule
8B of the Notaries Rules provides for renewal of Certificate of practice. It stipulates that any application
for renewal of certificate of practice shall be submitted before six months of its expiry. However,
Government may after considering the reasons stated in the application, relax the condition of submission
of application for renewal of certificate of practice before the specified period.
5.23 The Committee is of the view that Notaries are important link in the judicial system and
provide cost effective services to public for various basic services viz., verification, authentication,
certification of documents etc. in the country. The Committee is of the opinion that the process
of appointment of Notaries needs to be further streamlined and made more robust so that
competent persons are appointed as Notaries.

would like the Authority to concentrate on improving the quality of legal services being provided
by it. It may also consider suitably revising the fee paid to advocates who provide legal services
under the Scheme to a reasonable level so that competent lawyers are attracted to provide
services under the Scheme. It should also make the system of empanelment of lawyers for
providing services under the Scheme more transparent, robust and accountable.
6.16 The Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, cast duty upon the court to supply certain documents
including chargesheet, First Information Report, statements of witnesses, confessions and other
documents of investigation on which the prosecution proposes to rely without delay to the
accused free of cost. In reality, these documents are supplied to the accused by the prosecution/
police who is also a party to the dispute. Referring to Sections 207 and 208 of Code of Criminal
Procedure, 1973, the Committee observes that the court instead of discharging its duty to supply
requisite documents (except voluminous one) which is essential for free and fair trial has been
largely passing the buck to Police. The Apex Court, time and again, particularly, in the case of
Shri Jahid Shaikh v. State of Gujarat (2011) 7 SCC 762 has held that when many of the accused
persons were not provided with copies of the chargesheet and the other relevant documents as
indicated in Sections 207 and 208 of CrPC, it seriously affects the right of an accused to a free
and fair trial. The Committee understands that in addition to safeguarding rights and interest
of the victim, it is also a solemn duty of the court to provide requisite documents to the accused.
The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Justice should take necessary steps
to ensure full and proper compliance of the aforesaid provisions of Code of Criminal Procedure,
1973.

will make use of experiences gained in Phase-I of the project, for robust and effective
implementation of the Phase-II. The Committee recommends that the Department should expedite
the process of digitization and hardware installations under the project and achieve targets set
in a time-bound manner. The Committee further recommends that periodic evaluations of the
project be carried out so as to make timely intervention, if any, needed for completion of the
project, as scheduled. (Para 6.25)
The Committee takes note of increased allocation by Rs. 3 crore for Gram Nyalayas in BE
(2017-18) over BE & RE (2016-17). The Committee, however, expresses its concern at the slow
pace of establishment of Gram Nyalayas in the Country as only 10 states have notified Gram
Nayalayas, and out of these not a single Gram Nyalayas is functional in the states of Karnataka,
Jharkhand and Goa. Uttar Pradesh, which has the highest population in the country, has only
2 functional Gram Nayalayas. The Committee accordingly, recommends the Department to find
reasons for slow implementation of the project as also to study if Gram Nayalayas which are
functional serving the intended purpose and inform the Committee as to how Government
proposes to proceed with the establishment of Gram Nyalayas throughout the country. (Para 6.29)
The Committee is concerned with the large no of vacancies of Judges in High Courts and
reiterates recommendations contained in 87th Report on the Committee on the Inordinate Delay
in Filling up the Vacancies in the Supreme Court and High Courts". It further recommends that
pending finalization of supplemental MoP/review of judgements/Constitutional amendments,

49
appointments of Judges should continue as per hitherto existing practice as an ad-hoc measure
so that administration of justice does not suffer. (Para 6.31)
The Committee also desires that the Bench of Higher Judiciary to be reflective of composition
of society and its diversity and recommends that suitable measures to include more and more
people from marginalized communities and women in the judiciary may be taken. (Para 6.32)
The Committee notes that the Supreme Court has been allocated 240 crores against its
demand of Rs.266 crores. The Committee also notes that the Supreme Court has not been able
to spend funds allotted to it in RE 2016-17 to the tune of Rs.29.61 crores as on 13.2.2017. The
Committee hopes that, if required, allocation made to the Supreme Court would be suitably
enhanced at RE stage. (Para 7.2)
