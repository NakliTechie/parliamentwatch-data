Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice, having been authorized by the Committee to 
submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this One Hundred Twenty Eighth Report

and Justice).

expenditure of Ministries/Departments of Government of India projected under various 
Demands for Grants for the upcoming financial year need to be voted by the Parliament.

scrutiny thereof under Rule 270 of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the 
Council of States. While making sc rutiny of Demands for Grants, the Committee has made 
an appraisal of performance, programmes, schemes and policies of the D epartment Justice

CHAPTER-I 
DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
Minstry of Law & Justice 
1.0 The Ministry of Law and Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by 
the British Parliament. The key mandate of the Ministry includes providing policy support in 
administration of justice; furnishing legal advice to the Government of India and defending 
Government of India in various Courts and Tribunals; drafting Supreme and Subordinate 
legislations for Government of India; appointment, resignation and removal of Judges of the 
Supreme Court of India and High Courts in States; Legal aid to poor; access to Justice; 
Justice Delivery & Legal Reforms; etc. 
1.1 The Ministry comprises the following three Departments: 
(i) Legislative Department; 
(ii) Department of Legal Affairs; and 
(iii) Department of Justice.

1.2 The Ministry is presently under the overall charge of the Minister of Law and Justice 
assisted by a Minister of State. Each of the three departments is administratively headed by 
an independent Secretary.

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
Functions of the Department 
 
1.4 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, functions of the 
Department are as under: 
i. Appointment, resignation and removal of the Chief Justice of India and Judges 
of the Supreme Court of India; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of 
absence (including leave allowance), pensions and travelling allowances; 
ii. Appointment, resignation and removal etc. of Chief Justice and Judges of 
High Courts in States; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of absence 
(including leave allowances), pensions and travelling allowances; 
iii. Appointment of Judicial Commissioners and Judicial Officers in Union 
Territories; 
iv. Constitution and organization (excluding jurisdiction and powers) of the 
Supreme Court (but including contempt of such Court) and the fees taken 
therein; 
v. Constitution and organization of the High Courts and the Courts of Judicial 
Commissioners except provisions as to officers and servants of these courts; 
vi. Administration of justice and constitution and organization of courts in the 
Union Territories and fees taken in such courts; 
vii. Courts fees and Stamp duties in the Union Territories; 
viii. Creation of All India Judicial Service; 
ix. Conditions of service of District Judges and other Members of Higher Judicial 
Service of Union Territories; 
x. Extension of the Jurisdiction of a High Court to a Union Territory or exclusion 
of a Union Territory from the Jurisdiction of a High Court; 
xi. Legal Aid to the poor; 
xii. Administration of Justice; and 
xiii. Access to Justice, Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms.

1.14 The primary responsibility for development of judicial infrastructure in the States 
rests with the State Governments.   The Central Government augments the resources of the 
State Governments by providing financial assistance under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme 
(CSS) for Development of I nfrastructure Facilities for Judiciary.   The Scheme is in force 
since 1993-94.  It covers the construction of court buildings and residential accommodations 
of judicial officers of District and Subordinate Judiciary.  However, the scheme does not 
cover construction of High Court buildings. 
 
1.15 Presently, the fund sharing pattern of the Scheme is 90:10 (centre: state) for the 8 
North-Eastern States and 2 Himalayan States (Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh) and 60:40 
for the rest of the States. There is 100% assistance for Union Territories. 
 
1.16 The scheme was evaluated by NITI Aayog and its continuance was recommended. 
Accordingly, the Government has approved the continuance of this CSS for a period of 5 
more years i.e. from 2021 -22 to 2025-26 with financial outlay of Rs. 9,000 crores (including 
central share of Rs. 5,307 crore). Now, in addition to the construction of Court Halls and 
Residential Units for Judges/Judicial Officer of D istrict and Subordinate Courts, the Scheme 
would now also cover construction of Lawyers‟ Hall, Toilet Complexes and Digital 
Computer Rooms.

1.18 The State is duty bound to provide easy access to Justice and timely delivery of 
Justice to all its citizen. Adequacy of judicial infrastructure plays a critical role in 
reduction of pendency and backlog of cases in the Courts. But it is an undeniable fact  
that the state of judicial infrastructure in the country is far from being satisfactory. 
Courts are functioning in rented premises with insufficient space and many in a 
dilapidated condition without basic amenities like toilets  etc. In such a scenario, the 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for Development of Infrastructure Facilities for 
Judiciary is playing a pivotal role in the development of critical infrastructure at the 
State level. 
 
1.19 From the data provided by the Department of Justice it can be seen that many of 
the States are utilizing this scheme in the best possible manner and  have created  
substantial judicial infrastructure in their States. However, in many of the States 
nothing notable has happened under this Scheme.  
 
1.20 The Committee observes that t he pace of release of funds has slowed down due 
to revised procedural guidelines for release of CSS funds under the new Public 
Financial Management System (PFMS). Due to stringent conditions attached for release 
of funds, most of the States are  finding it difficult to get their quota of funds despite

CHAPTER-II 
NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY 
 
2.0 Article 39A of the Constitution of India provides for free legal aid to the poor and 
weaker sections of the society and ensures justice for all. Also, Articles 14 and 22 (1) of the 
Constitution also make it obligatory for the State to ensure equality b efore law and a legal 
system which promotes justice on the basis of equal opportunity to all. In this background, 
the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 was enacted by the Parliament, which came into 
force on 9 th November, 1995 to establish a nationwide network for providing free and 
competent legal services to the weaker sections of the society on the basis of equal 
opportunity. 
 
2.1 The NALSA monitors and evaluates implementation of legal aid programmes and 
lays down policies and principles for making l egal services available under the Act. In every 
State, a State Legal Services Authority and in every High Court, a High Court Legal Services 
Committee has been constituted. District Legal Services Authorities, Taluk Legal Services 
Committees have been cons tituted in the Districts and most of the Taluks, respectively, to 
give effect to the policies and directions of the NALSA and to provide free legal services to 
the people and conduct Lok Adalats in the State. Supreme Court Legal Services Committee 
has been constituted to administer and implement the legal services programme insofar as it 
relates to the Supreme Court of India. 
 
FUNCTIONS OF NALSA 
 
2.2 The details of activities/programmes regarding Legal Aid and Advice undertaken by 
Legal Services Authorities  under the aegis of National Legal Services Authority are as 
follows:  
  
(i)       Legal aid and advice: Free Legal aid and assistance in the form of 
representation, drafting of notices, replies and legal advice is being provided by Legal 
Services Institutions at all levels free of cost to the eligible persons. The legal assistance 
is also provided to legal aid seekers by assigning panel lawyers to them for

CHAPTER – IV 
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 
4.0 The Supreme Court of India is at the apex of a pyramidal, unified and integrated 
judicial system. Though the political system of the country is federal in nature, the judicial 
system is unitary. The Supreme Court came into existence on 28th January, 1950 and as of 
now it comprises the Chief Justice of India and 33 Judges. The strength of the apex court was 
enhanced over the years from 8 in 1950 to 11 in 1956, 14 in 1960, 18 in 1977, 26 in 1986, 31 
in 2009 and 34 in 2019, with the increase of case load on it. The Constitution of India 
provides for the Supreme Court to be a court of record, guardian of liberties & rights and also 
as the final arbiter in most disputes. It is an appellate court, can review its own judgement and 
in its advisory capacity may answer references by the President on any question of law of 
public importance. The Supreme Court is designated as the custodian of fundamental rights 
and the 'rule of law'. It has power of judicial review and the law declared by the Supreme 
Court is binding on all courts. 
4.1 The administrative wing of the Supreme Court is called 'Registry'. The Registry is 
divided into two wings - (i) Administrative Wing; and (ii) Judicial Wing. These wings are 
further divided into various Divisions, Branches, Sections and Cells. The Chief Justice of 
India is the Administrative head of the Supreme Court. The highest Administrative Officer of 
the Supreme Court is the Secretary General, the post manned by a judicial officer of the rank 
of a District & Sessions Judge and is of the rank of Secretary to the Government of India. The 
Secretary General is assisted by Registrars, Officers on Special Duty, Additional Registrars, 
Deputy Registrars, officers of other lower ranks and subordinate staff. 
4.2 The Department of Justice is the nodal Department for the Supreme Court of India. 
There is a separate demand, Demand No. 67 pertaining to the Supreme Court of India, which 
is entirely charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be submitted to the vote 
of Parliament. However, Article 113 of the Constitution provides for those estimates to be 
discussed in either House of Parliament.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS 
 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for Development of Infrastructure Facilities for 
Judiciary 
 
1. The State is duty bound to provide easy access to Justice and timely delivery 
of Justice to all its  citizen. Adequacy of judicial infrastructure plays a critical 
role in reduction of pendency and backlog of cases in the Courts. But it is an 
undeniable fact that the state of judicial infrastructure in the country is far 
from being satisfactory. Courts ar e functioning in rented premises with 
insufficient space and many in a dilapidated condition without basic amenities 
like toilets etc. In such a scenario, the Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for 
Development of Infrastructure Facilities for Judiciary is playing a pivotal role 
in the development of critical infrastructure at the State level. (1.18) 
 
2. From the data provided by the Department of Justice it can be seen that many 
of the States are utilizing this scheme in the best possible manner and have 
created substantial judicial infrastructure in their States. However, in many 
of the States nothing notable has happened under this Scheme. (1.19) 
 
3. The Committee observes that the pace of release of funds has slowed down 
due to revised procedural guidelines for r elease of CSS funds under the new 
Public Financial Management System (PFMS). Due to stringent conditions 
attached for release of funds, most of the States are finding it difficult to get 
their quota of funds despite having huge requirements for funds for t heir 
projects both ongoing and new. From the data provided by the Department, 
11 out of 21 general category States and 5 out of 8 NE States did not get any 
funds during 2022 -23 due to such norms. Though the Committee is happy to 
note that the Department of  Justice is taking all possible measures to 
ameliorate the hardship faced by the States in this regard, and to ensure that 
all of them become PFMS compliant, the Committee, however, recommends 
the Department to approach the MoF to get the guidelines on rel ease of funds 
under the CSS relaxed wherever the States are facing genuine difficulties in 
the implementation of such guidelines. (1.20) 
 
4. The Committee in January, 2023 visited Assam, Manipur and Tripura to 
assess the judicial infrastructure and to have an  on the spot assessment of 
implementation of this Scheme in the North Eastern region. After deliberating 
with the concerned State Governments and High Court Judges on this issue 

 43 
the Committee is constrained to note that the status of judicial infrastructur e 
is in very bad shape in the region and they are not being able to make full use 
of this Scheme. (1.21) 
 
5. Apart from general PFMS related issues being faced by all the States/UTs, the 
North-Eastern States have their own issues because of their peculiar 
topography which makes availability of unencumbered land a bit difficult. 
Also the NE States and other hilly States are bestowed with short working 
season which makes movement of men and material difficult over the 
treacherous terrain and thereby making contr act implementation difficult 
with increased time and cost overruns. Because of limited railways 
connectivity in the region, the cost of transportation becomes much higher, 
leading to substantial increase in cost of material. Other reasons include late 
receipt of project proposals and non -receipt of utilization of certificates from 
the respective state governments. It has also been found that in case of North -
eastern States, their contribution to the scheme though meagre (only 10% vis -
à-vis 90% of Central sh are) gets delayed. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends the Department to explore the possibility of providing a better 
deal in terms of enhanced allocation of funds and relaxation of spending 
norms under the Scheme, considering the peculiarities of the N E States. It is 
further recommended that the 10% of the state quota may also be relaxed for 
the NE States subject to subsequent recovery from the concerned state, so that 
the ongoing projects are not stalled for want of such meagre amount of 
money. (1.22)

In smaller states like Manipur, Tripura etc. where the bench strength is small, 
high levels of vacancies leads divisional benches not being constituted for want 
of judges and if there is some retirement or elevation or transfers the working 
of such High Courts are brought to a standstill. In the High Court of Tripura, 
where the Com mittee visited in the month of January, 2023, out of the 
sanctioned strength of 5, only 2 Judges were in -position, including the acting 
Chief Justice, making it difficult for them to constitute a divisional bench. 
Thus, these peculiarities of the smaller H igh Courts should be kept in mind 
while making appointments. (1.53) 
 
18. Accordingly, the Committee recommends the Department of Justice to fix all 
the bottlenecks responsible for delays in the appointment of Judges at the 
High Court. Only recently appointment s in the Supreme Court were done on 
a fast pace and it is functioning with full strength now. The appointments to 
High Courts also deserve same treatment and the vacancies must be filled 
with same zeal. (1.54) 
 
FINALIZATION OF MEMORANDA OF PROCEDURE (MOP) FOR 
APPOINTMENT OF SC/HC JUDGES 
 
19. The Committee observes that the Supreme Court and the Government have 
failed to reach a consensus on revision of the Memorandum of Procedure 
(MOP) for appointment of Judges to the constitutional courts, though the 
same is under consideration of both for about seven years now. The 
Committee expects the Government and the Judiciary to weed out their 
differences and to finalize the revised MOP, which is more efficient and 
transparent, in terms of the Supreme Court observation cited above. (1.58) 
 
 
 

 47 
NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY 
 
20. As informed by NALSA the Legal Aid Defence Counsel Scheme (LADCS) 
engages full -time experienced lawyers to exclusively devote their effort to 
provide legal aid, assistance and representation to the beneficiaries in 
criminal matters. This is a paradigm shift in the manner in which the legal aid 
is provided in the country, whereas earlier cases were assigned to the 
empanelled lawyers who also had a private practice. The Committee 
appreciates such scheme and hope that it will improve the quality of legal aid 
to the beneficiary. It is also expected that competent and dedicated lawyers 
are empanelled through a transparent selection process for manning this 
Scheme. (2.3) 
 
ASSESSMENT OF BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
 
21. The Committee in its 116th Report while referring to the fact that per capita 
free legal aid spending in India is perhaps the lowest in the World and had 
recommended a substantial increase in the Grant-in-aid given to NALSA. It is 
a harsh reality that the prevalent social and economic disparity in the country 
is eroding the democratic objective of 'justice for all'. As majority of our 
population lack legal awareness and necessary means to approach the courts, 
they are compelled to suffer in silence. Legal aid is not a charity but an 
obligation of the State and right of the Citizen, it should be provided in the 
true spirit envisaged in the Constitution. (2.7) 
 
22. Thus there is an urgent need to give a major impetus to strengthen the legal 
aid movement in India by empowering the institutions like NALSA. 
Accordingly, the Committee strongly reiterates its recommendation that 
Grant-in-aid to NALSA should be substantial ly increased and for BE 2023 -24 
they should be provided with the amount projected by them to the MoF i.e. 
Rs. 400 crores, in order to carry out activities planned by them for the year 
2023-24. Without adequate budgetary provisions the ongoing and newly 
implemented schemes/activities of NALSA would fail to take off. The 
Department of Justice and NALSA should approach the MoF again and 
pursue the matter vigorously. (2.8) 
 
MANPOWER REQUIREMENT AT NALSA 
 
23. The Committee notes that NALSA's activities have increased manifold since 
its inception and it is actively engaged in taking the legal aid movement in 
India to every nook and corner. However, its existing sanctioned staff strength 
of 34 only is too low con sidering its mandate and reach. Accordingly, the 

 48 
Committee recommends the Government to sanction additional 40 posts as 
requested by them. (2.11) 
 
24. The Committee further recommends the NALSA to take proactive steps to fill 
up the vacant posts, in accordance  with their respective RRs, on regular basis 
and not to allow piling up of unfilled vacancies. (2.12) 
 
NATIONAL JUDICIAL ACADEMY, BHOPAL 
 
25. The mandate of the National Judicial Academy is strengthening the 
administration of Justice through Judicial Education, Research and Policy 
Development. Considering its mandate and infrastructure at its disposal, the 
Committee is of the view that the potential of the institution has remained 
underutilized over the years. There is a need to significantly enhance i ts 
performance to make it an institution of eminence. (3.6) 
 
26. The Committee notes that the Academy trains only Judges and officers of the 
Supreme Court. It is felt that the resources of the Academy may also be used 
for the betterment of other stakeholders l ike Lawyers. Accordingly, the 
Committee recommends the Academy to consider training the Lawyers also, 
for which they may charge appropriate fees. (3.7) 
 
27. The Committee also notes that the academic positions in the Academy are 
very few and as on date only 17 academic positions are filled. Accordingly, 
there is a need to fillup the vacant posts on priority and asses the need for 
enhancement of academic position. It is further suggested that the NJA should 
consider inviting noted academicians from abroad as resource persons to 
further enrich the academic content offered by them. (3.8) 
 
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 
 
28. The SCI has been allocated Rs. 437.15 crores for BE 2023-24. The Registry 
has informed that the variation in BE 2023 -24 over RE 2022 -23 (7.81%) is 
mainly due to allocation of Rs. 35 Cr in BE 2023 -24 under the head IT (now 
Digital Equipment and Information, Computer, ICT equipment) as against 
Rs. 20 Cr in RE  2022-23. From the above table it may be seen that the 
expenditure trend of the SCI has remained quite efficient as they have been 
able to timely utilize the funds allocated to them over the years. The 
Committee however expects the Government to allot the SCI the funds 
requirement projected by them, so that the activities initiated by them are 
completed on time. (4.4)
