DELIMITATION COMMISSION 
6.1. On a specific query raised about the progress of delimitation work, the Department submitted 
the following reply :- 
 The Delimitation Commission was constituted in July, 2002. It became functional in 
November/December, 2002. The Commission ---- in terms of the Constitutional provision ----- 
was to delimit the constituencies on the basis of 1991 census figures. By June, 2003 the 
Commission had completed almost 60 per cent of its work. The totality of the Commission’s 
work (all the Parliamentary constituencies and the Assembly constituencies in all the states in 
the country) would have been completed by November/December, 2003. But it could not be 
done because the Parliament amended the Constitution by the Constitution (Eighty-Seventh 
Amendment) Act, 2003, which provides that the delimitation work shall be done by the 
Commission on the basis of the 2001 census and not the 1991 census figures. As a 
consequence, the work done by the Commission on the basis of the 1991 census became non-
est by the operation of law. The Commission, however, started preliminary work on the basis of 
the provisional 2001 census figures, which were available. But the preliminary work could not 
be taken to the logical extent because, even the provisional 2001 census figures regarding the 
Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes were not available. 
 The 2001 Census figures for most of the States were published on December 31, 2003. 
Thereafter the Commission took-up the work of collecting data and tabulating the same. While 
the process was going on the Lok Sabha was dissolved and fresh elections were declared. The 
Commission has five Lok Sabha Members from each State as its associate members. Where the 
number of M.Ps in a State is less than five, all of them become Commission’s members. With 
the dissolution of the Lok Sabha, the associate members ceased to be members by operation of 
law. Even otherwise during the elections, the fieldwork by the Commission was almost 
stopped. After the constitution of the Fourteenth Lok Sabha the Commission requested Hon’ble 
Speaker to nominate associate members to the Delimitation Commission from each State. After 
receipt of the nominations the Commission restarted the delimitation work which is progressing 
on a tight schedule. 
 The delimitation of the Lok Sabha and assembly  constituencies in all the States is a time 
consuming work. Under the scheme of the Act asso ciate members are to be associated with the 
delimitation work from the very beginning. As a consequence there have to be more than one 
meeting with the associate members from each State. Thereafter, the draft delimitation 
proposals are published in newspapers and suggestions and views are invited from the public. 
Finally, the public meetings are held by the Commission in all the States and the draft finalized, 
after that. 
 Delimitation work in all the States in the country is at different stages. Four States viz; 
Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal and the Union Territory of Pondicherry are 
scheduled to go to polls in May, 2006. Out of these, Final Delimitation Order has been issued in 
respect of Pondicherry and published in the State and Central Gazette on 31.3.2005. For Kerala 
Public Hearings have been scheduled in the last week of April and Final Order is expected to be 

issued in the first week of May. Final Order in respect of the other three States will be issued 
after completion of the Delimitation exercise which is at an advanced stage. 
 It may be added that under Article 82 of the Constitution, the readjustment of 
constituencies for the House of the People “shall take effect from such date as the President 
may, by order, specify”. Similarly under Article 170 (3) of the Constitution, the readjustment of 
constituencies for the State Legislative Assemblies “shall take effect from such date as the 
President may, by order, specify”. The question of  elections to be held on the basis of the new 
delimitation exercise will be dependent on the date determined by the President of India. 
6.2 The Committee impresses upon the Delimitati on Commission to expedite the delimitation 
exercise in the Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies in al l the States, particularly the 
delimitation of assembly constituencies which should be completed we ll in advance in those 
States which are scheduled to go to polls in M ay, 2006. As regards th e readjustment of the 
constituencies of the House of People, which shall take effect from the date to be specified by the 
President, the Committee impresses upon the Legisl ative Department to ta ke timely action for 
issue of Presidential Notification.

INCOME TAX APPELLATE Tribunal (ITAT) 
7.2 Under the Major Head 2020, the ITAT has been  allocated Rs. 31.36 crore for the year 2005-06, 
as compared to Rs. 22.52 crores  in BE (2004-05). As stated by the Government, the increased 
allocation for the year 2005-06 in respect of the IT AT is primarily due to the Government approving 
the establishment of twenty additional Benches (ten temporary and ten permanent) for the Tribunal 
during 2004-05. In view of this, the salary component has been proposed to be increased and further 
increases have 
been allowed for “office expenses” and “rent rates and taxes” so as to ensure that the minimum 
requirement of funds for manpower, basic infrastructure and amenities is met. 
7.3 According to the Annual Report of the Ministry of Law and Justice for the year 2004-05 (Page 
11, Para 5) at beginning of th e year 2004-05 the penden cy of the appeals as on 1st December, 2004 
stands at 1,40,129 whereas the total number of pending appeals during 2001-02 was 2,42,153. This 
downward trend, as stated by the Annual Report, has been possible with the setting up of fifteen 
Additional Benches by the year 2001. It has also been stated that the Government has further 
sanctioned ten additional Permanent Benches w.e.f. 30.1.2004 and en Additional Temporary Benches 
w.e.f. 24.02.2004 in different parts of the country. The Government is in the process of appointing 
members for these twenty Benches. 
7.4 The Committee while scrutinising/examining th e Demands for Grants of the Ministry for 
the year 2004-05 had observed in its Second Report that despite approval for additional Benches 
at different places of the country, these could not be made functional as appointment of 
members for those Benches could not be proces sed. Since then, a considerable period has 
elapsed but the position in respect of filling up vacancies in the additional Benches has remained 
unchanged. The 
Committee has been apprised that  meetings of the Special Standing Selection Committee to 
select candidates for the post  of temporary members for newly created twenty temporary 
Benches of ITAT have been held on 26.5.20 04 and 27.10.2004. The recommendations of the 
Committee are being processed, Consequently, the additional Benches though created with zeal 
and enthusiasm are still non functional. The Commi ttee also notes that the budgetary allocation 
for ITAT for 2005-06 has been pr oposed to be increased mainly on account of creation of 
additional Benches. The Committee expresses its serious concern over the delay in filling up the 
vacancies of members. In its view , the whole objective for which additional Benches have been 
created gets defeated if they are not made functional. The Committee therefore, recommends 
that the Government should start the process of appointment of memb ers without any further 
delay and make concerted efforts to make the additional Benches functional. 
NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES Authority (NALSA) 
7.5. Budgetary alloction for NALSA has been raised from the previous year’s (2004-05) amount of 
Rs. 5.98 crore to Rs. 10.00 crore for 2005-06 for implementation of the scheme. 
7.6. To be eligible for seeking legal aid under the NALSA, the annual income limit fixed by the 
Central Government for cases before the Supreme Court is Rs. 50,000 at present. At the Fourth 
Annual Meet of the State Legal Services Authorities held under the aegis of NALSA on 29th 
February, 2004 and 1st March, 2004 at Kolkata, it was resolved to raise the income ceiling in terms of 
section 12(h) of the said Act from Rs. 25,000 to Rs. 50,000 per annum in respect of cases before High 
Courts and Courts subordinate thereto. Eight State Legal Services Authorities namely, Goa, Gujarat, 
Karnataka, Maharashtra, Orissa, West Bengal, UTs of Chandigarh and Delhi have raised the income 

ceiling limit to Rs. 50,000 per annum for providing free legal aid. This has resulted in an increase of 
number of beneficiaries. 
7.7 The Committee notes that out of twenty-eight States and seven Union Territories, only 
eight State Legal Services Authorities have raised ceiling of in come for seeking legal aid. It does 
not reflect a satisfactory response towards th e scheme. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that Government should make all out efforts to ensure that more and more States and UTs raise 
the ceiling so that larger number of persons can get benefit under the scheme. 
7.8. The Committee has been given to understand that NALSA is providing funds to the State Legal 
Services Authorities for implementation of NALSA’s scheme and legal aid programmes in the States. 
The funds, however, are too meagre and due to its paucity, the legal aid programmes are not being 
fully implemented. The Committee in this context notes that it had recommended in its Second Report 
(Para 29) on Demands for Grants of the Ministry of  Law and Justice for 2004-05 that the Government 
should examine the idea of a Centrally sponsored scheme with hundred per cent Central funding for 
implementing NALSA and also to ensure that the cent ral grants released to the State Governments are 
utilized fully. 
7.9 As per the information submitted to the Committee, NALSA has prepared a Vision Document 
with the following objects:- 
 (i) launching a door to door campaign in remote villages for awareness and education on 
legal right. 
 (ii) educating the beneficiaries of their rights and constitutional obligations of the 
following categories. 
 (a) a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; 
 (b) a victim of trafficking in human beings or beggar as referred to in article 23 
of the Constitution; 
 (c) a woman or a child; 
 (d) a person with disability as defined in clause (i) of section 2 of the Persons 
with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full 
Participation) Act, 1995; 
 (e) a person under circumstances of undeserved want such as being a victim of a 
mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste at rocity, flood, drought, earthquake or 
industrial disaster; or 
 (f) an industrial workman; or 
 (g) a person in custody, including custody in a protective home within the 
meaning of clause (g) of section 2 of the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 
1956, or in a juvenile home within the meaning of clause (j) of section 2 of 
the Juvenile Justice Act, 1986, or in a psychiatric hospital or psychiatric 
nursing home within the meaning of clause (g) of section 2 of the Mental 
Health Act, 1987; or 
 (h) in receipt of annual income less than rupees nine thousand or such other 
higher amount as may be prescribed by the State Government, if the case is 
before a court other than the Supreme Court, and less than rupees twelve 
thousand or such other higher amount as may be prescribed by the Central 
Government, if the case is before the Supreme Court. 
7.10 The Committee notes that though the NALS A has launched a laudabl e mission for legal 
empowerment of all sections of society, yet, certain grey areas need to be addressed by the 
Government. One such problem relates to paucity of funds, Moreover, non- utilization of grants 
by the States Legal Services Authorities is another area of concern. The Committee feels that the 

very objective of Legal Aids  Scheme would be defeated if these bottlenecks are not removed 
promptly. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government should  effectively monitor 
not only the utilization of funds but also impl ementation of the schemes and programmes of 
NALSA. The Committee also recomme nds that constraints of funds should not come in the way 
of successful implementation of the scheme. Gov ernment should, therefore, allocate additional 
funds to help operationalise the various pr ogrammes to be undertaken by NALSA under its 
Vision Document. 
7.11 Lok Adalat is an important constituent of NALSA. Permanent Lok Adalats under section 19 of 
the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 have been established in almost all the States However, 
Permanent Lok Adalats for Public Utility Services under section 22B of the Legal Services 
Authorities Amendment Act, 2002 have been established only in three States viz. Haryana, Jharkhand, 
Rajasthan and U.T. of Chandigarh. The Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi and 
Government of Karnataka and Punjab have also cleared the proposal of their Legal Services 
Authorities for establishment of Permanent Lok Adalats under section 22B. In the remaining States, 
proposals of such Permanent Lok Adalats are pe nding with the respective State Governments. 
NALSA has also taken up the matter with the concerned State Governments. 
 NALSA has informed that it is facing acute shortage of funds in implementation of Legal Aid 
Programmes in the country. To cope up with the financial constraints, NALSA has sent a proposal for 
Rs. 2 crore to the Government on 1st October, 2004 and 4th November, 2004. On 29th December, 
2004, the Authority requested for Rs. 50 lacs to meet essential and unavoidable administrative 
expenses. The Authority had sent a proposal for Rs . 1,67,39,400/- for rendering legal services to 
tsunami victims and the Government sanctioned only Rs. 98 lacs. On 15th February, 2005, a proposal 
of Rs. 2 crore and 55 lacs was sent to the Government. As this proposal was received towards the fag 
end of the financial year, it could not be approved due to non-availability of funds. 
7.12 The Committee notes that only three States and one UT have esta blished Permanent Lok 
Adalats for public utility services in the country a nd such proposals have been cleared so far by 
Punjab and Karnataka and National Capital Te rritory of Delhi. Remaining States/Union 
Territories are still in the process of considering the prop osals. The Committee is uphappy with 
the pace of establishment of Permanent Lok Adalats for public utility services. It also notes that 
after 
amendment of the Legal Services Authorities Act in 2002 establishing on ly few such Permanent 
Lok Adalats manifests a lackadaisical approach . The financial constraints have been met 
partially by increasing the budg etary allocation under this he ad for 2005-06. The Committee 
recommends that the Government sh ould take steps in the directi on of establishing Permanent 
Lok Adalats in the remaining States and UTs. It would be appropriate if the Government chalks 
out a time-bond programme in co nsultation with the State Le gal Services Authorities for 
constituting these Lok Adalats and monitor the pace of implementations thereof. 
Law Commission of India 
7.13. Implementation of reports of the Law Commission has been engaging the attention of the 
Government as well as the Committee. The Committee in the 2nd Report (Para 41) recommended:— 
“The Committee was informed that the Law Co mmission has so far presented 188 Reports to 
the Government, out of which 47 Reports are pending implementation at various stages/levels 
and for variety of reasons. The Committee recommends that the Department of Legal Affairs, 
being the nodal agency for monitoring the implementation of the Law Commission’s Reports, 
may ask all the administrative Ministries/Departm ents to keep record of various stages of 
implementation of recommendations contained in the Reports of Law Commission. The 
Committee has observed that there has been inordinate delay in implementing some of the 
Reports of the Law Commission. It may not be ou t of place to mention that the Sixth Report of 
the Law Commission presented to Government in 1957 is yet to be implemented. In this 

context, the Committee feels that it would be be tter to apprise Parliame nt once in a year the 
status of implementation of various Reports of Law Commission. Thus, Government may lay 
before each House of Parliament an annua l statement to that effect which could inter alia 
contain reasons for delay in implementing certain recommendations of the Law Commission. 
7.14 The Committee has now been appraised that the Law Commission has since submitted 191 
reports out of which 51 are still pending. The pres ent Law Commission seventeenth has presented six 
Reports (186th to 191st re ports) so far out of which four reports  (186th, 188th, 190th and 191st) are 
under examination. But, the Government is silent on laying before the Parliament the annual statement 
on the implementation of recommendations contained in Law Commission’s Reports. The 
Committee takes a serious view of the fact that the Government has not even thought it proper 
to respond to the recommendation of the Committee. Such type of approach is not a healthy sign 
of the Parliamentary system. The Committee reit erates its earlier recommendation that an 
annual statement pertaining to reasons for delay in implementing  certain recommendations 
contained in the Reports of Law Commission must be laid before the Parliament once in a year. 
7.15 The Committee feels that the Law Commission ’s reports form the ba sis for review of the 
judicial system and framing, amending and repealing the legisl ations as per the need of 
changing circumstances. Thus, the role of the Commission assumes special significance in the 
Indian judicial and legislativ e system. The Committee, therefore,  recommends that the reports 
of the Commission should be implemented in le tter and spirit and the Government should make 
concerted efforts to ensure timely implementati on of its recommendations. The Committee also 
recommends that the Government should consider providing st atutory backing to the Law 
Commission on the pattern of other countries like U.K. and Canada etc. so that it could function 
more effectively with the desired empowerment.

7.41 The Committee notes that the process of simplif ication of laws is the need of the day. 
Common law country like England has started simplifying its laws. The Committee observes 
that legislative language is o ften quite technical, intricate and incomprehensible for common 
man for whom laws are made. It feels that laws  should be drafted in national or regional 
languages to convey the purpose and intent of framing laws and all Central laws are translated

drafting of laws and instruments of subsidiary legislation.

Governments and Union Territories have spent an amount of Rs. 1162.34 crore upto 31.12.2004.

Computerisation of Judiciary 
8.9 The Committee has been apprised that an improved case management technique and extensive 
computerisation have reduced Supreme Court’s arr ears from 1.05 lakh cases in 1991 to 29,315 cases 
in July, 2004. As regards pace of computerisation in High Courts and subor dinate Courts it requires 
upgradation and new-generation hardware. The computerization of subordinate courts, civil and

apex to the lowest, will be computerized on one electronic grid.

Pendency of Cases 
8.21 As per the latest information available as on January, 2005, 30,151 cases were pending in the 
Supreme Court; 33,79,033 in different High Courts  and 2.35 crore cases were pending in the 
Subordinate Judiciary. 
8.22 The Government has taken the following steps to clear the pendency:— 
 (a) The Government has been constantly reviewing the accumulation of arrears in Courts. 
Besides increasing th e Judges’ strength from time to time, various steps have been 
taken by the Courts, viz; grouping of cases involving common questions of law, 
constitution of specialized benches and organizing Lok Adalats at regular intervals, 
etc. 
 (b) The Government has also encouraged alternative modes of disposal including 
mediation, negotiation and arbitration. Special tribunals like Central Administrative 
Tribunal, State Administrative Tribunals, Income Tax Appellate Tribunals, Family 
Courts, Labour Courts, Consumer Courts etc. have been set up to expedite disposal of 
cases. Information technology is being used in generation of cause lists, providing 
information to the 
litigants/Advocates etc. for speedy disposal of cases. 
 (c) Fast Track Courts (FTC) for the disposal  of long pending cases, particularly sessions 
cases and cases involving undertrials in jails are being set up. 
 (d) Central Government has sanctioned a grant of Rs. 502.90 crore, based on the 
recommendations of the Eleventh Finance Commission, for the creation of 1734 Fast 
Track Courts in the country.

 Reports, respectively, on Demands for Grants for 2003-04 and 2004-05 
of the Department of Justice had urged upon the Government to consider fresh initiatives to arrest 
pendency of cases in various courts. Government had initiated some measures to arrest and clear such 
pendency. It had also requested the State Governments/High Courts to earmark a portion of the Fast 
Track Courts to dispose of senior citizen’s cases, on priority basis. 
8.24 The Committee notes that notwithstanding its persistent reco mmendations to reduce 
pendency of cases before the Supreme Court, High  Courts and the various subordinate courts 
all over the country, there has not been any majo r breakthrough in the matter. Year after year, 
pendency of cases is increasing except the Sup reme Court where pendency has been arrested to 

some extent due to extensive co mputerisation and a host of ot her initiates. The Committee has 
been apprised that the Governme nt has been constantly reviewin g the accumulation of arrears 
in Courts and the problem is sought to be tack led by constitution of specialized benches and 
organizing Lok Adalats at regular intervals;  encouraging alternative modes of disposal, 
establishing Fast Track Courts etc. The Committee finds that specialised benches, Lok Adalats 
and Fast Track courts though created as an alternative mode of disposal of arrears of cases have 
been suffering due to infrastruc tural lacuna or financial constr aints and thus are not able to 
meet the desired targets. The Committee, therefo re, calls upon the Government to address the 
problem of pendency of cases by carefully analysing the factors responsible and searching viable 
solutions in addition to the existing arrangements. 
Vacancies of Judges 
8.25 As against the sanctioned strength of 26 Judg es, the Supreme Court of India has 24 Judges in 
position, leaving two vacancies to be filled up, as on 23.3.2005. 
8.26 In the High Courts, against the sanctioned strength of 668 Judges, 527 Judges are in position, 
leaving 141 vacancies to be filled up, as on 22.3.2005. 
8.27 Pursuant to the Supreme Court Judgement of October 6, 1993, read with their Advisory 
Opinion of October 28, 1998, the process of initiation of proposal for appointment of Judges of 
Supreme Court lies with the Chief Justice of India and for appointment of Judges in a High Court, it 
lies with the Chief Justices of respective High Courts. The Government has been requesting the Chief 
Justices of the High Courts, Chief Ministers and Governors of the States, from time to time, to initiate 
proposals for filling up of the present as well as the anticipated vacancies, during the next six months. 
8.28 The Committee held detailed deliberations on the procedure for filling up of vacant posts of 
judges in the Supreme Court and the various High Courts, as per the pre and post 1993 dispensations. 
The Committee noted that this issue has been engaging the attention of the Committee on Home 
Affairs since long, as also of the present Committee. The Committee on Home Affairs examined the 
subject in detail in its Eight-fifth Report on Laws Delay: Arrears in Courts. That Committee was, inter 
alia, of the view that the delays in  judicial appointment was a matter of serious public concern and 
pointed out that the failure to fill up judicial vacancies promptly could not be shielded or defended in 
the name of judicial independence (para 32.2 ibid) 
8.29 The Committee observes that there has not been any perceptible improvement in the 
position of vacancies of Judges in Supreme Court and High Courts under the post 1993 
arrangement. The Committee feels that time ha s come when the issue has not only to be 
deliberated at the highest level, but also to be  debated in the public domain so that there could 
emerge an alternative system wh ich could bring about an overall improvement in the situation. 
The Committee, therefore, calls upon the Gove rnment to come forward with a concrete 
alternative, 
addressing the issue of appointment of Judges of the higher judiciary, so that the vacancies 
could be filled up on the date of their occurrence. 
8.30 The Committee felt that the increased workload  of Judges in the light of mounting arrears 
of cases also needs to be addressed seriousl y and it calls upon the Government to make a 
thorough assessment of the personnel requirement for administration of Justice and come out 
with concrete proposals to improve the situation in this regard. 
Benches of Supreme Court 
8.31 The Committee has been recommending time and again for setting up of benches of the 
Supreme Court in far-flung areas, with a view to  providing justice to th e poor, for whom it is 
impossible to visit the national capital. The Committee’s recommendations for setting up of benches 

of Supreme Court in Southern, Western and North-Ea stera parts of the countr y have been considered 
by the Supreme Court and they found no justification in setting up benches of the Supreme Court. 
8.32 The Committee, in its Second Report, dealt with the issue and felt that establishment of benches 
of Supreme Court in Southern, Western and North-Ea stern parts of the country would facilitate the 
litigants to avail justice from the apex court, wi thout traveling to Delhi from far away places. The 
Committee, accordingly, recommended that Government should decide its course of action to resolve 
the matter expeditiously. 
8.33 In its Action Taken reply to the above recommendation, Department of Justice has informed the 
Committee that the subject matter of establishing benches of the Supreme Court in other parts of the 
country was discussed in a full meeting of the Hon’ble Judges and that the full court is of the 
considered opinion that the demand for establishment of the benches of the Supreme Court can not be 
accepted. 
8.34 The Committee is unhappy with the persistent opposition for establishing benches of the 
Supreme Court in other parts of the country with out giving any reasons or justification. The 
Committee endorses its earlier view that esta blishment of benches of the Supreme Court in 
other parts of the country would be of immense help to the poor who cannot afford to travel 
from their native places to Delhi. The Committe e, therefore, impresses upon the Government to 
discuss this issue once again with the Supreme Court and explore the possibility of establishing a 
bench of the Supreme Court in Chennai at least on experimental basis and thereafter, upon its 
success, in other parts of the country in future. 
Fast Track Courts 
8.35 The Eleventh Finance Commission had sanctioned setting up of 1734 Fast Track Courts, for 
expeditious trial of long pending sessions cases and also cases involving under trials, who remain in 
jails for a period of more than two years. By the clues of the financial year 2004-05, 1549 courts had 
become functional. They have disposed 6,80,230 cases, out of 13,14,477 cases, transferred to them. 
The Fast Track Courts were started on the basis of the recommendation by the Eleventh Finance 
Commission for the period 2000-05. 
8.36 Eleventh Finance Commission had approved a total grant of Rs. 502.90 crore for 
1734 Fast Track Courts in the States. Grants fo r Fast Track Courts were one of the twelve 
upgradation grants recommended by the Eleventh Finance Commission. An amount of 
Rs. 433.75 crore has been released as grants until 28th March, 2005 and Rs. 301.48 crore 
reported as utilised. 
8.37 On query about the responsibility for releasing funds and monitoring the progress of Fast Track 
Courts, the representative of the Department of Expenditure informed the Committee in its sitting held 
on 30th March, 2005 that the primary responsibility of implementing the scheme rested with the State 
Governments and for the purpose the funds as recommended by the Eleventh Finance Commission 
were released by the Ministry of Finance. Si nce the Eleventh Finance Commission was appointed 
under 
Article 280 of the Constitution for a fixed tenure of five years i.e. upto 31st March, 2005. Thereafter, 
the new regime would start under the Twelft h Finance Commission. The Eleventh Finance 
Commission had specifically stated in its main Report that any non- plan balance as on 31st March, 
2005 would lapse. In Supplementary Report, it was stated that unspent balances might be transferred 
to the incentive fund which was given to the States. The representative of the Department of 
Expenditure further stated that States were getting a much larger chunk of money from the Centre and 
therefore, they should be in a position to take care of all the requirements including for Fast Track 
Courts. In view of the situation that would arise after 31st March, 2005, some concrete decision would 
have to be taken by the Department of Justice and the Department of Expenditure to resolve this 
issues. The Ministry of Finance (Department of Expenditure) did not formulate any scheme on its 

own. The Department of Expenditure had not received any scheme or any budget proposal for the next 
year for the continuation of this scheme. 
8.38 On a specific query as to why the Department of Justice did not consider constitution of Fast 
Track Courts as plan or non-plan scheme and why suitable provisions could not be made, the Home 
Secretary, deposing on behalf of the Department of  Justice stated that cons titution of Fast Track 
Courts was not a plan scheme at all. It was outside the budget of the Department of Justice. The 
representative of the Department of Justice a ppeared before the Twelfth Finance Commission with 
certain suggestions and made a case for continued funding of already established Fast Track Courts 
and for setting up new Fast Track Courts through the Finance Commission grants. The problem, 
however, was that the Report of the Twelfth Financ e Commission came to the Department of Justice 
late after it was laid on the table of the Houses of Parliament. As there was no time to examine the 
Report the nature of the recommendation could not be known. Had the recommendation been received 
six months in advance, the Department of Justice would have formulated its views and schemes on it 
Fast Track Courts. 
8.39 The Committee was also informed that unspent  fund remaining in any of the upgradation 
grants, including grants for Fast Track Courts and local bodies grants are to be transferred to the Fiscal 
Reforms Facility and distributed amongst the States, which are able to reduce revenue deficit by 25% 
over five years. Finance Ministry has been approached by various States for extension of upgradation 
grants beyond 31st March, 2005. However, the Finance Ministry has apprised the States that these 
grants cannot be allowed to be used beyond 31st March, 2005. Commenting on the role of the States 
in this regard, the representative of the Department of Expenditure stated that the State Governments 
would be equally concerned about this issue since implementation of this scheme rested with the State 
Governments. The Central Government was assisting the States in that function. Hence, the State 
Governments could, for an interim period, the period in which a proper scheme is formulated, meet 
the expenditure. 
8.40 Having heard the views of representatives of the Department of Justice and the 
Department of Expenditure and having consider ed the relevant information presented to the 
Committee on the issue of Fast Track Courts, it observes that the Eleventh Finance Commission 
recommended for constitution of Fa st Track Courts as an interim measure for a limited period 
of five years to reduce pendency of cases and th e scheme has reportedly  been quite successful. 
Even the Supreme Court of India has also appreci ated the work of Fast Track Courts. It is, 
however, a sad state of affairs that the scheme no w seems to be in the lurch after the expiry of 
the term of the Eleventh Finance Commission as there is no mention of any recommendation for 
continuity of the scheme in the Report of the Twelfth Finance Commission beyond 31st March, 
2005. The Committee notes that while examining th e Demand for Grants of the Ministry of Law 
and Justice for 2004-05, the Co mmittee has been apprised that the Department of Justice had 
taken up with the Twelfth Finance Commission for continuation of the Fast Track Courts 
beyond 31st March, 2005  i.e., after expiry of the term of th e Eleventh Finance Commission. It 
appears that the Department of Justice acte d upon the assumption that Twelfth Finance 
Commission would adopt a positive approach in th is case. However, the report of the Twelfth 
Finance Commission could not be made available before finalisation of the budget process of the 
Department as a result of which the Department could not get time to pursue it further. The 
Committee feels that in these circumstances, th e Department of Justi ce should have either 
approached the Ministry of Finance with a no n-plan scheme or the Planning Commission for 
plan scheme for funding the Fast  Track Courts after 31st March,  2005, well in advance. On the 
contrary, the Department of Ju stice kept waiting for recommend ations of the Twelfth Finance 
Commission which ultimately re sulted in the present crisis.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Department of Justice shou ld vigorously pursue this issue with the 

Ministry of Finance or the Planning Commission  and till the scheme is finalised, take up the 
matter with the State Governments for providing funds to be reimbursed subsequently by the 
Central Government. 
Demand No. 64 : Supreme Ccourt of India

8.43 Some Members of the Committee raised the issue of changing the nomenclatures of the

8.44 The Committee therefore impresses upon th e Government to consider to initiate

Judical Reforms 
8.45 After introduction of economic reforms and globalisation and revolutionary technological 
innovations, new challenges and complexities have cropped up before Indian Judicial System 
imposing enormous burden upon the courts. As the society has become more aware, the law, the 
image of the court system and administration of justice suffer a loss of public confidence. In this 
context, it has become all the more important that the grey areas confrontin g Indian Judiciary need 
prompt attention. 
8.46 The Committee feels that it is time to consider the application of principles and techniques 
of modern management in solving some prom inent problems like law’s inordinate delays, 
arrears of cases and increased workflow in the court system. This requires scientific study of the 
problems. For this, the judiciary is required to develop skills to manage its resources, setting 
targets and evolving concepts like case management and time  management. Simultaneously 
there is need to increase output. 
8.47 The Committee has been informed that the J udges in the United Kingdom and the United 
States of America has applied so me innovative methods. These me thods have been successful in 
certain areas. For example, Annual Judicial Statistics Report, published by the Chief Justices in 
the United Kingdom and the Unit ed States every year, help to  enable the public to understand 
the performance of the judiciary. Similarly, in the United States, every Bill is accompanied by 
Judicial Impact Statement describing the likely effect of the legislation upon the workload of 
the courts. The Committee also notes that the Electronic Judicial Reso urce Management is 
gaining importance and it has been found the most effective tool for  judicial reforms. In Indian 

context, no new legislation is  required as the Information Technology Act 2000 has already 
incorporated the requisite provisions. 
8.48 The Committee is of the view th at the above measures would have significant impact on 
reforming the Indian Judicial System and th e Government should, therefore, explore the 
possibility of introducing these methods.
