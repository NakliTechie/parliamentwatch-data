Court Legal Services Committee has been constituted under the Act.

LOK ADALATS 
  
32.       Lok Adalat is one of the Alternative Dispute Resolution, mechanisms.  The institution of Lok Adalats has been 
given a statutory status by the Legal Services Authorities Act,  1987.  The statutory support to Lok Adalats has helped in 
taking justice to the door-steps of the poor and needy and made justice quicker and less expensive.  The award of a Lo k 
Adalat is deemed to be the decree of a Civil Court, is final and binding on all parties and no a ppeal lies before any cour t
against it.  
  
33.       With a view to providing compulsory pre-litigative  mechanism for conciliation and settlement of disputes relating
to public utility services, the Parliament  amended the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.  The amended Act stipulates 
establishment of Permanent Lok Adalats for exercising jurisdicti on in respect of disputes relating to public utility services,
such as, transport service, postal, communication, supply of power, service in hospital/dispensary, insurance service, etc. 
  
34.       Subsequent to the coming in to force of the said Amendment Act, the States of Haryana, Jharkhand, Rajasthan an d
U.T. of Chandigarh have established Perm anent Lok Adalats.  Steps are being take n to establish such Lok Adalats in the 
remaining States/U.Ts. Several State Lega l Services Authorities have informed that Permanent Lok Adalats could not be

LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT 
  
54.       Legislative Department is one of the oldest Departments, which was set up in the pre-independence period in 1883, 
and at one time it was part of the Department of Home. The ma in task of the Legislative De partment is to draft Bills fo r
enactment of new laws or amendment of the existing laws, and to advise and assist th e Administrative Ministries an d
Departments in legislative matters at all the stages, and also  to process the Bills at all stages, till President’s assent is 
received and the Act is published in the Official Gazette. 
  
55.       Apart from preparing the Bills in the light of th e legislative proposals, the Department finalises the Statements o f
Objects and Reasons, Financial Memoranda , the Notes on Clauses, in connection with introduction of Bills, and the
Memorandum regarding Delegated Legislation, where ever required. 
  
56.       The Department mainly performs the following functions:- 
             
(i)                  It handles the legislative business of the Government of India; 
(ii)                Administratively handles election laws; 
(iii)               Administers more than seventy Acts relating to subjects in Concurrent List such as the Registration
Act, the Contract Act, Transfer of Property Act, marriage and divor ce, civil procedure, arbitrations,
etc.; 
(iv)              It translates into Hindi all the Central Bills, Acts  and Constitution of Indi a and into other official
languages contained in the VIII Schedule of the Constitution; and 
(v)                It brings out reports in Hindi the decisions of the Supreme Court of India and various High Courts.  
It also awards prizes for various books in law brought out in Hindi.

of Rs.303 crore by a further sum of Rs. 50 crore was agreed to by the Ministry  of Finance and duly approved by the
Parliament.  The allocation for the BE 2004 -05 was meant to meet the expenditure likely to be incurred to acquire the
remaining machines required to cover the entire electorate of the country.  As of now, the entire requirement has been met, 
and the present allocation is sufficient to meet all the needs anticipated. 
  
68.       For holding simultaneous elections to Lok Sabha and All State/UT Assemblies throughout the country, the total no.
of EVMs required was 16,49,764, which mean s an additional 5,85,000 EVMs  to meet such a situation.  Further, a large

requirement of balloting units, when the number of candida tes goes beyond 16.  For the present, the Commission does not 
foresee any possibility of  holding simultaneous elections to Lok Sabha and all State/UT Assemblies, as the normal tenure
of various State/UT Assemblies are different.

ELECTORS’ PHOTO IDENTITY CARD 
  
70.       The scheme of Issuance of P hoto Identity Cards to Voters was initiate d in mid 1990s.  At the insistence of the 
Election Commission of India,  the State/Union Territory (with Legislatures ) Governments were allowed funds as
advances, to be adjusted against the Gove rnment of India’s share of the expenditure  likely to be incurred on preparation o f
such cards, in future.  The advance payments were released in 1994-95 and 1995-96.  These funds remained at the disposal 
of majority of State/UT Governments a nd on the directions of the Election Com mission of India in 2003-04, prior to the 
elections to the 14 th Lok Sabha, the States/UTs were dire cted to endeavour to cover at leas t three-fourths of the electorate 
under the Scheme.  The impetus gained pursuant to the said dire ction resulted in utilization of  funds by most of the States
and many of them exhausted the advances allowed earlier and have, in fact, incurr ed more expenditure, leading to creation
of fresh liability of the Government of  India towards the extra expenditure incu rred.  To discharge such liability the 
provision has been increased to Rs.100 crore for 2004-05. 
  
71.       EPICs have been issued in both on line and of f line modes.  Election Commission has issued guidelines that the 
electors can refuse to accept a defective card and he will get a defect free EPIC in a reasonable time-frame, after removing 
the defects.  In on-line mode, it can be done  then and there.  However, in off-line mode, the elector has to return the card, 
indicating the defects in his application (Form 8 or 8A) and a new card is issued after removing the defects.   
  
72.       The Committee on Home Affairs, in its 101 st Report had recommended that the EPIC may be made available to all
the citizens in the country, before th e forthcoming Assembly and General Elec tions.  Government informed that the 
recommendation of the Committee were communicated to all th e Chief Electoral Officers (CEOs), for their information
and necessary action.          All the CEOs had initiate d necessary action to comply w ith the recommendations of the
Committee.  However, in many States the progress has been slow, due to different reasons. 
  
73.       The work relating to EPIC in Assam had not started.   Similarly, the work of com puterization of electoral rolls had
not been completed, as the work of issue of EPIC was now an integral part of computer ization of rolls. In all othe r
States/UTs sincere efforts were made by the CEOs to ach ieve the target set by the Commission.  Some States ha d 
expressed difficulties in respect of funds, in some States in work was considerably delayed or could not start due to adverse
climatic conditions and natural calamities, like heavy rains, flood, landslide, etc. and in some States due to adverse law and
order situation.

78.       It is the Nodal Department in so far as the adminis tration of judicial services in States is concerned, although the
actual responsibility of judicial administration below the High Court level rests with the concerned State Governments and

CENTRALLY SPONSORED SCHEME (CSS) RELATING TO INFRASTRUCTURAL FACILITIES FOR THE 
JUDICIARY 
  
81.       The Centrally Sponsored Scheme for development of infrastructure facilities for the judiciary is being implemented
by this Department since 1993-94.  The scheme includes construction of court- buildings and residential accommodation 
for judicial officers/judges, covering High Courts and Subordinate Courts.  The expenditure on the scheme is shared by the 
Central and the State Governments on 50:50 ba sis.  The Central share is, however, restricted to the allocations made as pe r
criterion laid down by the Planning Commission.  However, the States are free to spend mo re amount.  Expenditure in 
respect of the Union Territories is fully met by the Centre.  In  the current year Rs.100 cror e have been earmarked for the
purpose.   
  
82.       The Centrally Sponsored Sche me relating to Infrastructural Facilities fo r the Judiciary has been under examination
of the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs since long.  That Committee, from time to
time, in its reports, has made important observations and recommendations relating to implementation of the scheme.  That 
Committee, in its 86 th report on Demands for Grants (2002-03) of the Mini stry of Law and Justic e had, made important 
recommendations which are summarised below: 
  
While Centrally Sponsored Scheme relating to Infrastructural Facilities for the Judiciary had been a good beginning
in the direction of smooth administra tion of justice, the funds provided unde r this scheme not seem to be enough,
particularly looking at the demand projected by the States vis-à-vis funds earmarked by the Planning Commission.  
There is a strong case for increasi ng the budget allocation for the Judiciar y.  The Government should conside r 
enhancing the central support to the State Governments.  The Government  should also redesign the pattern o f 
Centre-State share, so that the States would have less budgetary pressure on them.   
  
The Committee endorses the observations made by the Committee on Home Affairs in its earlier Reports on
Demands for Grants of Ministry of Law and Justice and recommends that the G overnment should be liberal in providing
grants to State Governments for development of infrastruc tural facilities for the judiciary, and persuade the State
Governments for efficient utilization of the grants for the purpose.

NATIONAL JUDICIAL ACADEMY (NJA)

FAST TRACK COURTS 
  
89.       The Eleventh Finance Commission had sanctioned setting up of 1734 fast tr ack courts, for expeditious trial of long
pending sessions cases and also cases involvi ng under trials, who remain in  jails for a period of more  than two years.  As 
on 23rd July, 2004, 1488 courts had become functional.   They have disposed of 4,83,958 ca ses, out of 10,29,792 cases 
transferred to them.  The fast track courts were star ted on the basis of the recommendation by the XIth Finance 
Commission for the period 2000-05.  The De partment of Justice has already taken up with the XIIth Finance Commission
to continue the Fast Track Courts beyond 2005 also.   The ove rall functioning of the Fast Tr ack Courts is the primary
responsibility of the respective State Governments and the conc erned High Courts.  The cases are transferred to Fast Track 
Courts under the directions of the respective High Courts.  
  
90.       An amount of Rs.413 crore ha s been released so far directly to the St ates/UTs by the Ministry of Finance.  The 
Ministry has received utilization certificates to the tune of Rs.133.90 crore from the State Governments, under the scheme. 
  
91.       Fast Track Courts are taki ng up sessions cases pending for two years or more and the cases of under-trials in jails 
on priority.  The Scheme is expected to  effect substantial saving in expenditure on jails besides addressing serious human
rights problems.  Disposal of sessions and other cases, pending for a long time, will have a salutary effect on law and order
and crime situation.  The fast track courts are doing an excellent job in quick disposal of long pending cases and their work
have been appreciated by the Supreme Court of India.  Supreme Court is monitoring the setting of Fast Track Courts by the
States.  The Central Government has also  been requesting all the State Government s to set up Fast Track Courts at the
earliest. 
  
92.       The Committee notes that out of 1734 Fast Trac k Courts sanctioned by the Eleventh Finance Commission, 246 are 
yet to be made functional.  The delay in  setting up of such Courts within th e stipulated period may cause the sanctione d
amount to lapse, thus defeating the objective of expeditious administration of justice.  Th e Committee, therefore, urges 
upon the Government to make all out effo rts to persuade the State Government s to make the remaining 246 Fast Trac k
Courts operational.  Further, while keeping up the efficiency and utility le vel of Fast Track Courts, the Department o f
Justice should devise ways and means to avoid hurried decisions by such courts so that innocent persons are not punished.  
Government should also consider to avai l the benefit of the experience of the senior retired Judges for the Fast Trac k
Courts. 
SPECIAL/FAMILY COURTS   
  
93.       The Family Court Act, 1984 was enacted with a view to providing for es tablishment of Family Courts, to promote
conciliation in and secure speedy settlement of disputes relating to marriage, family affairs and related matters. 
  
94.       These Courts are civil in nature, establishment of which has direct bearing on the reduction of pendency of civil 
suits in various High Courts.  In accordance with the provisi ons of section 3 of the Act, the State/UT Governments can
establish Family Courts in the areas comprising districts, towns, city in the States having population of ten lakhs.  Even the 
States/UTs are competent to establish such court in those areas with the State/UT having a population less than ten lakhs in
consultation with the concerned High C ourt.  The Department of  Justice is the nodal De partment, responsible fo r
administration of the said Act.

104.     The Committee was informed that suggestions are rece ived from time to time from various quarters for prescribing
a minimum tenure of the Chief Justice of  India.  However, the 9-Judges Bench of the Supreme Court, in its Judgemen t 
dated 6th October, 1993, laid down the procedure to be followed in the matter of appoi ntment of Chief Justice of India and 
the other Judges of Supreme Court.  Th e majority Judgement held the view that normally the senior-most puisne Judge o f 
Supreme Court would be appointed the Chie f Justice of India.  However, whenever  there is any doubt about the fitness o f 
the senior-most Judge to hold the Office of the Chief Justice of India, consultation will be required to be made with any 
other Judge, as provided in Article 124(2)  of the Constitution of India.  There is no proposal under c onsideration of the 
Government to make any change in the existing practice.

up the vacancies, though after the Supreme Court judgement of 6 th October, 1993, the Chief Justice of the High Court has

High Courts, from time to time, to initiate proposals for filli ng up the existing vacancies, as well as anticipated vacancies

PENDENCY OF CASES 
  
115.     As per the latest information available as on 1 st July, 2004,  29,315 cases were pe nding in the Supreme Court; 
35,27,282 in different High Courts and 2,27,57,693 cases were pending in the Subordinate Judiciary. 
  
116.     The Government has taken the following steps to clear the pendency:- 
  
•                    The Government has been constantly reviewing the ac cumulation of arrears in C ourts.  Besides increasing 
the Judge strength from time to time, various steps have been taken by the Courts, viz; grouping of cases 
involving common questions of law, constitution of specialized benches and organizing Lok Adalats a t
regular intervals, etc. 
•                    The Government has also encouraged alternative m odes of disposal including mediation, negotiation and
arbitration.  Special tribunals lik e Central Administrative Tribunal, St ate Administrative Tribunals, Income
Tax Appellate Tribunals, Family Courts, Labour Courts, Consumer Courts  etc. have been set up to expedite
disposal of cases.  Information te chnology is being used in generation of cause lists, providing information
to the litigants/Advocates etc. for speedy disposal of cases. 
•                    Fast Track Courts (FTC) fo r the disposal of long pending cases,  particularly sessions cases and cases
involving undertrials in jails are being set up. 
•                    Central Government has sa nctioned a grant of Rs.502.90 crore, based on the recommendations of the
Eleventh Finance Commission, for the creation of 1734 Fast Track Courts in the Country. 
  
117.     The Committee on Home Affairs, in its 101 st Report on Demands for Grants  (2003-04) had urged upon the 
Government to consider fresh initiatives to arrest pendency of cases in va rious courts.  Government had initiated some 
measures to arrest and clear such pendency.  It had also requested the State Governments/High Courts to earmark a portion
of the Fast Track Courts to dispose of senior citizen’s cases, on priority basis. 
  
118.     About the impact of such measures the Departme nt of Justice had informed the Committee that the process o f
judicial reforms is a continuous one, and therefore, it would not be possible to assess the impact of such measures to reduce
pendency of cases.  However, the Department has been focusing on computer ization of the entire subordinate judiciary,
which will impact on the arrears of cases.   
  
119.     The Committee hopes that the Government, with its resolve as contained in the National Common Minimum
Programme, would be able to drastically cut the delays in the High Courts and the Lower Judiciary.
