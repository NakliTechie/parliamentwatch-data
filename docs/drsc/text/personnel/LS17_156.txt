R E P O R T 
 This Action Taken Report (ATR) of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by 
the Department of Justice(Ministry of Law & Justice) on the recommendations/observations 
of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public 
Grievances, Law and Justice in its One Hundred Forty Seventh (147th) Report of the 
Committee on “Demands for Grants (2025-26)” pertaining to the Department of Justice 
(Ministry of Law & Justice), which was presented to Rajya Sabha on the 27th March, 2025 
and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 27th March, 2025. 
2. The Committee had made 24 recommendations/observations in its 147th Report. 
Replies of the Department thereto have been categorized and scrutinized under four chapters 
on the basis of satisfaction of the Committee. These replies have been categorized as follows: 
Chapter – I:  Recommendations/observations which have been accepted by the 
Department, i.e. in respect of paras:- 2.5, 2.6, 2.10, 3.24, 3.25, 3.48, 3.49,  
3.50, 3.53 and 3.60(Total -10).  
Chapter – II:  Recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to 
pursue in view of the replies given by the Department, i.e. in respect of 
paras:- 3.21, 3.22, 3.29, 3.33, 3.47, 3.61 and 3.62 (Total -07).  
Chapter – III:   Recommendations/observations in respect of which Committee has not 
accepted the replies of the Department, i.e. with regard to paras:- 2.12. 
2.15, 3.2, 3.8 3.9, 3.10, and 3.20 (Total -07). 
Chapter – IV:   Recommendations/observations (Nil) in respect of which final replies of 
the Government have not been received.  
3. The Committee desires that the Department should furnish pointed and detailed 
Action Taken Notes in respect of all the further Recommendations/Observations made by the 
Committee in Chapter – III of this report. 
4. The details of the ATR have been discussed in the respective Chapters in the 
succeeding pages. For reference purpose, para numbers of the original report (i.e. of the 
147thReport) have been mentioned at the end of each recommendation/observation.

Assessment of Budget Allocated to National Legal Servies Authority (NALSA) 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
1.6       The Department has provided Rs.400 Crore as grant-in-aid to NALSA, considering its 
activities against its projection of Rs. 730.73 Crore for F.Y. 2025-26 which may still be 
insufficient.  The Committee, therefore, reiterates its recommendations made in its 116th and 
128th Reports to increase the Grant-aid-aid for NALSA, to enable them to conduct all 
activities on time.  The committee feels that without adequate budgetary provisions, both 
ongoing and newly implemented schemes and activities of NALSA will not take off.  The 
Department of Justice and NALSA should once again reach out to the Ministry of Finance 
and press the matter with greater emphasis.                (Para 2.10) 
 
Action Taken 
1.7 As per the Budgetary Estimates 2025-26, an amount of Rs. 200 crore has been 
provisioned as Grants-in-Aid to NALSA under Head 2014.00.119.01.00.31 – Grants-in-Aid 
General, under Demand No. 65 of the Ministry of Law & Justice, against the proposed 
Budgetary Estimates of Rs. 300.00 crore for the Financial Year 2025-26. Under this head, the 
implementation of various legal aid schemes / programmes framed under the Legal Services 
Authorities Act, 1987 are undertaken, which may be adversely affected due to low budgetary 
allocation. Highlighting the implication, the matter for additional grants will be suitably taken 
up with the Ministry of Finance at the Revised Estimates (RE) stage, as per requirement. 
 
1.8 In addition, an amount of ₹200 crore has been provisioned as Grants-in-Aid for the 
Legal Aid Defense Counsel System (LADCS) Scheme in the Budgetary Estimates 2025-26 
against the proposed Budgetary Estimates of Rs. 430.73 crore for the Financial Year 2025-26. 
Presently, the LADCS Scheme has been implemented in 654 District Legal Services 
Authorities (DLSAs) and expansion to all DLSAs across the country in F.Y. 2025-26 is 
underway. Accordingly, demand for additional funds will be taken up with the Ministry of 
Finance at the RE stage, as per requirement.

SCHEMES/PROGRAMMES OF NALSA 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
1.18       The Committee recommends strengthening the E-Lok Adalat platform to improve its 
accessibility, particularly for rural population and those with limited technological access.  
The Committee opines that collaborating with local organizations will help in setting up 
digital access points in rural areas (e.g., community centers, libraries, or local panchayat 
offices).  Further, Para-Legal Volunteers (PLVs) may be trained to assist people navigating 
the digital platform.                   (Para 3.48) 
 
Action Taken 
1.19 E-Lok Adalat has already made a significant impact by improving access to justice for 
individuals who might otherwise be unable to participate in Lok Adalats. By holding Lok 
Adalats virtually, this platform has extended its reach to a broader population. The cost-
effectiveness of E-Lok Adalats is notable, as they eliminate organizational expenses and 
reduce travel costs for participants, who can now engage from their homes using internet 
technology. As of February 2025, E-Lok Adalats have been successfully organized in 28 
States by the respective State Legal Services Authorities (SLSAs). 
 
1.20 In line with this, NALSA has issued directions for the strengthening of the E-Lok 
Adalat platform to ensure better accessibility, particularly for rural areas and communities 
with limited technological access. SLSAs will also be instructed to train PLVs to raise 
awareness about E-Lok Adalats and to provide assistance in navigating the digital platform. 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
1.21       The Committee also feels that specialized legal aid units should be established 
within NALSA, focusing on women’s and children’s rights, and staffed with advocates trained 
to handle sensitive issues.  Further, the Committee recommends that more number of NGOs 
and child welfare organizations may be collaborated with, to ensure that legal assistance 
reaches every woman and child.                  (Para 3.49) 
  
 
Action Taken 
 
1.22 The Consultants - Legal / Legal Researchers of NALSA, who are Advocates, have 
been assigned with the work of suggesting ways and means to strengthen legal services 
focusing on women’s and children’s rights. The State Legal Services Authorities have also 
been directed to collaborate with the NGOs of child welfare organizations for providing legal 
assistance to Woman and child.

Gram Nyayalayas 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
2.1       The Committee feels that Gram Nyayalayas should operate every working day so that 
backlog of cases can be handled more effectively instead of just two days a week The current 
limited sitting schedule of just two days a week significantly reduces the capacity of these 
courts to deal with the volume of cases.  The Committee opines that prioritizing the 
recruitment of Nyayadhikaris (Judicial Officers) and ensuring the timely filling of vacancies 
may help in providing uninterrupted functioning of Gram Nyayalayas.           (Para 3.21) 
 
Action Taken 
2.2 The functioning and working days/hours of Gram Nyayalayas fall within the domain 
of the respective State Governments, as per the provisions of the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 
2008. Accordingly, the aspect of increasing the operational days of Gram Nyayalayas, as 
recommended by the Committee, has been duly brought to the attention of the concerned 
State Governments for necessary consideration and appropriate action (Annexure 1).  
 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
2.3       The Committee suggests that an extensive public awareness campaign may be 
launched about Gram Nyayalayas which will include information on the schedule of mobile 
courts, the process for filling cases, and the availability of legal support.  The Committee 
further recommends the jurisdiction of Gram Nyayalayas may be clearly defined to avoid 
overlap with regular courts.                  (Para 3.22) 
 
Action Taken 
2.4 A Gram Nyayalayas portal (https://dashboard.doj.gov.in/gn/) is already operational 
and available to the public, providing current details about funds allocated to States for the 
establishment of Gram Nyayalayas, the operational status of Gram Nyayalayas and the cases 
handled and pending in these courts. This portal serves as a valuable tool in raising public 
awareness about the Gram Nyayalayas.  
 
 
2.5  Regarding the suggestion for an extensive public awareness campaign, it is noted that 
the scheme was last extended for the period from 2021-22 to 2025-26 and is due for review. 
A third-party evaluation study is also being conducted by NITI Aayog in this regard. The  
  

10 
 
observations/recommendations of the Committee will be taken into account during the review 
of the scheme, including the possibility of launching a more comprehensive public awareness 
campaign. 
 
e-Court Mission Mode Project 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
2.6        The Committee is not aware of the reasons for the low fund utilization rate of 18.23% 
under the 3-Courts Project Phase -III as of December 31, 2024 which ultimately highlights 
the need for a more efficient fund disbursement mechanism.  Since funds are released based 
on requisitions from the eCommittee, Supreme Court of India, a streamlined process should 
be established to ensure timely allocation.      (Para 3.29) 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.7 The low fund utilization rate of 18.23% under the eCourts Project Phase-III as of 
December 31, 2024, primarily resulted from the delayed release of funds to the High Courts. 
A sum of Rs. 1500 crore was allocated under the eCourts Mission Mode Project Phase-III for 
BE 2024-25, which was later revised to Rs. 1200 crore in RE 2024-25. The expenditure 
during the Vote on Account Period (April-August 2024) was restricted to 5/12th of the 
estimated budgetary provision. Consequently, the Department of Justice released Rs. 464.98 
crore to the High Courts in June 2024 based on requisitions received from the e-Committee, 
Supreme Court of India. The second leg of fund release, amounting to Rs. 706.09 crore, 
occurred in November 2024. This delay in the release of funds contributed to the lower fund 
utilization rate by the end of December 2024. 
 
2.8 In response, the Department of Justice proactively conducted regular review meetings 
with the e-Committee and High Courts, closely monitoring the fund utilization pattern to 
ensure maximum expenditure. As a result, by the end of the financial year, Rs. 1029.11 crore 
(85.75% of the total revised allocation) had been utilized under the project. 
 
2.9 To ensure better allocation and utilization of funds in the current financial year (2025-
26), Rs. 752.17 crore has already been released to the High Courts in the first week of April 
2025, out of the Rs. 1500 crore allocated under BE 2025-26. These measures will help ensure 
a more efficient fund disbursement process moving forward. 
 
Fast Track Courts 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
2.10      The Committee recommends that the budget allocation of the FTSCs may be 
increased to meet their actual financial requirements, as of 31st December, 2024, 747 FTSCs 
are functional, falling short of the target of 790 for the year 2024.  The Committee urges the 
Ministry to take immediate steps to expedite the establishment of the remaining FTSCs by 
providing additional resources and infrastructural setup, specially in states with high case 
pendency.                            (Para 3.33) 

11 
 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.11 The Committee's recommendation to increase the budget allocation for Fast Track 
Special Courts (FTSCs) is noted. For the Financial Year 2025-26, additional funding for the 
FTSCs Scheme will be sought at the Revised Estimates (RE) stage, based on the actual 
expenditure incurred by the States/UTs. Since the Scheme operates on a reimbursement basis, 
the requirement of funds will depend on the pace and pattern of expenditure reported by the 
implementing States/UTs. 
 
2.12 It is important to highlight that the approved funding for the FTSCs Scheme is limited 
to covering the salaries of Judicial Officers and seven support staff per court, along with 
routine operational expenses. Infrastructure-related costs are to be met by the respective 
States/UTs from their own resources. To complement the efforts of the States, the Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for the Development of Infrastructure Facilities for the Judiciary 
supports the development of district-level judicial infrastructure. Additionally, the eCourts 
initiative is enhancing digital infrastructure in courts, further aiding State resources. 
 
2.13 Regular review meetings are being held with the concerned State Governments and 
High Courts to assess progress, address implementation challenges and expedite the 
operationalization of the remaining FTSCs. Further steps will be taken to ensure the 
establishment of the remaining FTSCs, particularly in States with high case pendency. 
  
SCHEMES/PROGRAMMES OF NALSA 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
2.14       The Committee appreciates the efforts of NALSA, specially in recent years, for its 
exceptional work in providing legal services and raising awareness about legal entitlements 
among the poor and vulnerable sections of the country.  The Committee recommends 
expanding the Legal Aid Defence Counsel System (LADCS) to more districts across the 
country.  The expansion will ensure that those from marginalized backgrounds have access to 
experienced lawyers, which will help them in getting justice quickly.           (Para 3.47)

Action Taken (Para 3.8 & Para 3.9)  
 
3.13 The Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts are appointed under Articles 124, 
217 and 224 of the Constitution of India in accordance with the procedure laid down in the 
Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) prepared in 1998 pursuant to the Supreme Court’s 
judgment of October 6, 1993 (Second Judges Case), read with its Advisory Opinion of 
October 28, 1998 (Third Judges Case). 
 
3.14 As per the MoP, the responsibility for initiating proposals for appointment of Judges 
to the Supreme Court lies with the Chief Justice of India, while for High Courts, it lies with 
the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court. The MoP stipulates that High Courts should 
send their recommendations at least six months before the anticipated vacancy; however, this 
timeline is rarely adhered to by the High Courts. For appointments to the High Courts, the 
views of the concerned State Government are obtained in accordance with the MoP. The 
recommendations are also examined in light of reports available to the Government regarding 
the proposed names. Recommendations from the High Court Collegium, the State 
Government and the Government of India are then forwarded to the Supreme Court 
Collegium (SCC) for its advice. Only those candidates whose names are recommended by the 
SCC are appointed as Judges of the High Courts. 
 
3.15 The appointment of Judges to Constitutional Courts is a continuous, integrated and 
collaborative process involving both the Executive and the Judiciary. It requires consultation 
and concurrence from various constitutional authorities at both the State and Central levels. 
While every effort is made to fill up existing vacancies expeditiously, vacancies in High 
Courts continue to arise due to retirement, resignation, elevation of Judges to the Supreme 
Court and also due to periodic increases in the sanctioned strength of Judges. 
 

18 
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
3.16 The Committee takes note of the Ministry’s response detailing the constitutional 
and procedural framework governing appointments to the Supreme Court and High 
Courts as prescribed under the Memorandum of Procedure (MoP). However, it is 
constrained to observe that, despite clearly defined timelines, several High Courts 
continue to delay the submission of recommendations so at least six months prior to 
anticipated vacancies.  The Committee finds this situation deeply unsatisfactory, as 
these delays adversely affect judicial capacity and impede the timely dispensation of 
justice.   The Committee reiterates that effective coordination between the Judiciary 
and the Executive is crucial to ensure timely appointments. 
3.17  The Committee, therefore, recommends that a structured coordination 
mechanism be instituted between the Ministry of Law and Justice and the Judiciary to 
regularly monitor the status of pending recommendations, compliance with the 
timelines under the Memorandum of Procedure (MoP), and projected vacancies. The 
Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation that High Courts with the heaviest 
pendency particularly those of Allahabad, Bombay, and Rajasthan be accorded priority 
in the appointment process.

Gram Nyayalayas 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
 
3.21       The Committee notes that out of 488 entities notified across various states, only 319 
are functional.  It is observed that states like Andhar Pradesh, Telangana and UTs like 
Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh have notified entities but none are functional.  The 
Committee feels that these states/UTs should prioritize operationalization of the notified 
entities through development of their infrastructural facilities and deployment of required 
workforce.  The Committee also opines that states with partially functionals entities (e.g. 
Odisha, Maharashtra, Jharakhand, Punjab, Haryana) should address their challenges such 
as funding allocation, administrative bottlenecks, and workforce shortages.        (Para 3.20) 
 
 
Action Taken  
 
3.22 Section 3(1) of the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 provides that the State Governments, 
after consultation with their respective High Courts, may, by notification, establish one or 
more Gram Nyayalayas for every Panchayat at the intermediate level or for a group of 
contiguous Panchayats at the intermediate level in a district. In States where there is no 
Panchayat at the intermediate level, Gram Nyayalayas may be established for a group of 
contiguous Gram Panchayats. However, the Act does not make the establishment of Gram 
Nyayalayas mandatory for State Governments. 
 
3.23 As regards the specific States mentioned in the Committee’s observations, the matter 
of operationalization of Gram Nyayalayas in Andhra Pradesh is currently sub judice before 
the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Writ Petition (Civil) No. 1067 of 2019. The State of Telangana 
has informed that it is not in a position to operationalize the Gram Nyayalayas at present and, 
owing to audit objections, has refunded the amount of Rs. 6.93 crore earlier released to them 
for the purpose, along with interest. No funds have been released to the Union Territories of 
Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh for this purpose. 
 

20 
 
3.24 With regard to the Committee’s recommendation that States with partially functional 
Gram Nyayalayas (Odisha, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Punjab and Haryana) should address 
challenges such as funding allocation, administrative bottlenecks and workforce shortages, it 
is submitted that these matters fall within the domain of the respective State Governments 
under Chapter II of the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008. Accordingly, all State Governments 
where Gram Nyayalayas have been notified or are operational have been requested to take 
appropriate action on the Committee’s recommendations. 
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
3.25 The Committee notes the Ministry’s submission on the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 
2008, and acknowledges that establishing such courts is not mandatory for States. 
However, it underscores that the objective of the Act is to provide accessible, affordable, 
and speedy justice in rural areas. The Committee expresses concern that several States 
and Union Territories, including Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Jammu & Kashmir, and 
Ladakh, have not operationalized the notified Gram Nyayalayas, while it is partially  
functional in States like Odisha, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Punjab, and Haryana with 
infrastructural and manpower constraints. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
formulation of concrete action plans and adoption of performance-based incentives for 
effective implementation. The Committee also desires to be apprised of the steps 
undertaken in this regard.
