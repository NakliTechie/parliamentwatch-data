CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY
1.0 The Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions is the nodal Ministry responsible
for personnel matters, especially in respect of issues concerning recruitment, training, career development,
staff welfare, administrative reforms and post retirement dispensation. This Ministry is under the direct
charge of the Prime Minister of India, assisted by a Minister of State.
1.1. The Ministry comprises of the following three Departments:
(i) Department of Personnel and Training;
(ii) Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances; and
(iii) Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare.
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING
1.2 The vision of the Department is to develop an enabling environment for the development and
management of human resources of the Government for efficient, effective, accountable, responsive
and transparent governance.
1.3 The Department’s mission is:
(i) Providing a dynamic framework of personnel policies and procedures for the effective
functioning of the Government.
(ii) Developing competence and innovation in Government.
(iii) Building capacity of human resources at all levels of Government for efficient delivery
of public services.
(iv) Inculcating and supporting a culture of transparency, accountability and zero tolerance of
corruption in public affairs.
(v) Institutionalizing a system for a constructive ongoing engagement with stakeholders.
1.4 The Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) acts as the formulator of policies pertaining
to recruitment, regulation of service conditions, and deputation of personnel, besides advising all
organisations of the Central Government on issues pertaining to personnel management.
1.5 The Department is entrusted with the work relating to framing/interpretation of rules and
regulations relating to service matters; recruitment, promotion and reservation policy; induction,
training and refresher courses for all levels including senior and middle management level; service
conditions, vigilance, discipline, career and manpower planning, implementation of Right to Information
Act, 2005 etc.
1.6 The Department of Personnel and Training under the charge of Secretary (Personnel) comprises
of six wings, namely: Establishment Officer, Services and Vigilance, Establishment, Administrative
Tribunal and Administration and Training and Central Services. Each of these wings is headed by an
officer of the rank of Joint Secretary or an Additional Secretary.

2
1.7 The Department also has under its administrative control four attached offices, namely:
(i) Staff Selection Commission (SSC) : Set up by a Resolution of the Government and is
entrusted with the conduct of examinations for recruitment of lower grade staff in
Central Ministries/Departments, etc.;
(ii) Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM) : A multi-disciplinary
organization specializing in capacity building, consultancy and research support, particularly
for the Central Secretariat;
(iii) Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie :
India’s premier institution dedicated to training Officers of the country’s higher civil
services; and
(iv) Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) : Entrusted with the investigation and prosecution
in corruption cases against public servants, private persons, firms and other cases of
serious crimes.
1.8 The other offices under the administrative control of the DoPT are:
(i) Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) – a Constitutional body responsible for
conducting examination for appointment to the higher civil services and civil posts under
the Union Government including recruitment to the All India Services. UPSC is created
under Article 315 of the Constitution of India. The Commission’s charter of duties is
given in Articles 320 and 321 of the Constitution. In brief, it is the duty of the
Commission to conduct examinations for appointment to services of the Union and to
advise Central Government on all matters relating to framing of recruitment rules, method
of recruitment and the principles to be followed in making promotions and transfers from
one service to another. There is a provision for consulting the Commission on matters
relating to methods of recruitment, principles to be followed in making promotions and
transfers from one service to another and on disciplinary matters. Article 322 of the
Constitution envisages, inter-alia, that the expenses of the UPSC including salaries,
allowances and pensions payable to members and staff of the Commission shall be
‘charged’ on the Consolidated Fund of India;
(ii) Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) – a statutory body empowered to inquire or
cause an inquiry or investigation on a reference made by the Central Government. It is
also empowered to exercise superintendence over the vigilance administration of the
various Central Government Ministries, Departments and organizations;
(iii) Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) –  a statutory quasi-judicial body set up to look
into the grievances exclusively of public servants so as to avoid delay in redressal of their
grievances;
(iv) Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) –  a body set up by a resolution of the
Central Government with the objective of evolving a sound managerial policy for the
Central Public Sector Enterprises. It is entrusted with the responsibility of making
recommendations for appointments to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executives
and functional Directors and part-time Chairman in the Central Public Undertakings; and
(v) Central Information Commission (CIC) –  a statutory body set up as per provisions
in the Right to Information Act, 2005 responsible for the implementation of the Act by
exercising powers conferred to it under Section 18 of the Act.
1.9. The Department of Personnel and Training is the nodal agency for four registered societies set
up for the welfare of Government employees and their families. These societies are:–

3
(i) The Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board –  an autonomous body set up
with the purpose of promoting cultural and sports activities among Government servants;
(ii) Grih Kalyan Kendra – an autonomous body set up with the objective of taking up
various activities for welfare of Central Government employees and their dependants;
(iii) Kendriya Bhandar – a welfare project of the Government of India set up in 1963
for the benefit of Central Government employees, their families and other customers;
and
(iv) Civil Services Officers Institute – set up with the objective to provide prompt welfare
of the officers of the Civil Services and to bring them together in order to secure
complete integration of the services and to build a corps of officers imbibed with the
spirit of co-operation in all aspects of Civil Services.
1.10 Some other activities of the Department of Personnel and Training are enumerated hereunder:–
(i) Senior Appointments under the Government of India
1.11 All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the
approval of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the Government of India
(Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961, are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary
of the ACC. These include board level appointments to Central Public Sector Undertakings and
appointments of Joint Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the ministries/departments. In
addition, all appointments by promotion, which require the approval of the ACC are also processed
through the Establishment Officer.
(ii) Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB)
1.12 The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making
recommendations for appointments to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executives and functional
Directors and part-time Chairman in Central Public Undertakings.
(iii) Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM)
1.13 A well structured three-tier machinery is in place for joint consultation between the Central
Government and its employees for promoting harmonious relations and securing the greatest measure
of cooperation between the Central Government and the general body of employees in matters of
common concern.The objective is to increase the efficiency of public services along with the well-
being of the employees. The three tiers of JCM are:
(i) National Council- at the apex level, functioning under the Department of Personnel and
Training;
(ii) Department Councils- functioning at Ministries’/Departments’ level; and
(iii) Office Councils- functioning at offices/ organisations under various Ministries/ Departments.
(iv) Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
1.14 In spite of the elaborate system of rules and regulations which govern personnel management,
there are employees who feel aggrieved by the Government decisions. In order to provide speedy and
inexpensive justice to them, the Government set up the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) in 1985,
which now deals with all cases relating to service matters, which were previously dealt with by Courts
up to and including the High Courts.

4
(v) Staff Welfare
1.15 Realizing that improvement in the working and living conditions of the employees and their
families leads to efficiency and high morale, the Department supports various employee welfare
measures in Delhi and other cities. The Department of Personnel and Training is the nodal agency for
four registered societies set up for the welfare of the Government employees and their families. These
societies are the Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board, Grih Kalyan Kendra, Kendriya
Bhandar and Civil Services Officers Institute (CSOI). These are located in Delhi. In addition, the
Department lays down policies for departmental canteen and supports Resident Welfare Associations.
(vi) Administrative Vigilance
1.16 The Administrative Vigilance Division of the Department of Personnel and Training is the nodal
agency responsible for formulation and implementation of policies in the field of vigilance and anti-
corruption. Its main task is to oversee the Government’s programme of maintenance of discipline and
eradication of corruption from the public service. The Division lays down policies in the field of anti-
corruption and co-ordinates the efforts of various Ministries/Departments through the Anti-corruption
Action Plan.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEV ANCES
1.17 The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of the
Government of India for administrative reforms as well as redressal of public grievances relating to
the States in general and those pertaining to Central Government agencies, in particular. The Department
endeavors to document and disseminate successful good governance practices by way of audio-visual
media and publications. The Department also undertakes activities in the field of international exchange
and cooperation to promote public service reforms.
1.18 The vision of the Department is to facilitate pursuit of excellence in governance for the benefit
of all citizens.
1.19 The Department’s mission is to foster excellence in governance and pursuit of administrative
reforms through:–
· Improvements in government structures and processes.
· Promoting citizen-centric governance with emphasis on grievance redressal.
· Innovations in e-Governance.
· Documentation and dissemination of best practices.
1.20 The Department is headed by the Secretary, Department of Administrative Reforms and Public
Grievances and Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare, assisted by an Additional Secretary, two Joint
Secretaries, five Director/Deputy Secretary and seventeen Under Secretary level officers. There are
seven Divisions in the Department namely, Administrative Reforms, Organization and Methods,
E-Governance, Documentation and Dissemination, International Cooperation, Administration and
Coordination and Public Grievances.
DEPARTMENT OF PENSION AND PENSIONERS’  WELFARE
1.21 The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare was set up in 1985 as part of the Ministry
of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions to cater to the central civil pensioners across the
country. This Department is the nodal Department of the Government of India for formulation of
general policy on pension and other retirement related benefits, besides facilitating redressal of

5
grievances relating to pension and retirement benefits. The Department has taken a number of steps
over the years for streamlining the pension administration system for greater convenience and welfare
of the pensioners.
1.22 The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare administers the following Rules:
(i) CCS (Pension) Rules, 1972.
(ii) CCS (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981.
(iii) CCS (Extra-ordinary Pension) Rules, 1939.
(iv) GPF (CS) Rules, 1960.
(v) CPF (India) Rules, 1962.
1.23 In addition, formulation as well as interpretation of Rules of New Pension System in respect
of Central government employees who entered into Central Government Services on or after 01.01.2004
has also been entrusted to Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare in terms of Government
of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961.

National Training Policy, 2012
3.1 The Committee noted from the Annual Report (2012-13) of the Ministry that the National
Training Policy, 2012 has been circulated to all Ministries/Departments, Government of India for
adoption. The policy has been formulated to strengthen the institutional mechanism for capacity
building measures for civil servants to face the emerging challenges in the changed environment in all
spheres of governance. The policy has been drafted with a view to matching individual‘s competencies
with the jobs they have to perform and bridge the competency gaps for current and future roles
through training and capacity building. Guidelines for implementation of the Policy especially on
preparing Cadre Training Plan (CTP), Annual Training Plan (ATP) etc. have been issued on 04.07.2012.
3.2 The Secretary, Department of Personnel and Training, while placing the Demands for Grants
(2013-14) of his Department before the Committee, stressed upon the relevance of the policy as
follows:
....We have not been training our lower level officials, especially, Group C and Group D at
all in most Departments. Group B officers get a little bit training at the beginning of their
career, and, after that, there is nothing. For Group A, there is a little more training. Now we
want to spread this whole thing down the line right to the lowest level, from the top, to see
that everybody should be trained three or four times a year. Our estimation is that it will cost
2.5 per cent of the salary budget.....

recognizes the importance of training in the scheme of personnel administration and hopes
that the implementation of this policy would effectively bridge, through training, the likely
gaps, between the present roles performed by the Government employees and future roles to
be executed by them. The Committee also expects an improved efficiency in Governments
functioning as a result of the implementation of the new training policy.

Post-retirement jobs
3.46 In the Annual Report of the Ministry, it has been stated that as per Rule 26 of the All India
Services (Death-Cum-Retirement Benefits) Rules, 1958 a pensioner shall not accept any commercial
employment before the expiry of one year from the date of his retirement, except with the previous
sanction of the Government and that during the year 2012, permission of the Central Government has
been granted to 4 retired IAS officers for accepting commercial employment (post retirement) under
this rule.
3.47 In response to a query in this regard, the Ministry has enumerated the circumstances under
which a pensioner retired from All India Services is granted permission for accepting commercial
employment within one year, from the date of retirement as follows:–
(i) Whether the organization the pensioner proposes to join has any conflict of interest or
activities prejudicial to India’ s foreign relations, national security and domestic harmony;
and whether the organization is undertaking any form of intelligence gathering;
(ii) Whether the pensioner has been privy to sensitive or strategic information in the last
three years of his service which is directly related to the areas of interest or work of the
organization which he proposes to join or the areas in which he proposes to practice/
result;
(iii) Whether there is conflict of interest between policies of the office(s) held by the pensioner
during the last three years and the interest represented or work undertaken by the

19
organizations he proposes to join. Such conflict of interest, however, should not be
interpreted narrowly to mean normal economic competition with Government or its
Undertakings;
(iv) Whether the service record of the pensioner is clear, particularly with respect to integrity
and dealings with Non-Government Organizations;
(v) The emoluments offered by the proposed employer to the pensioner and whether the
proposed emoluments and pecuniary benefits are far in excess of those currently prevalent
in the Industry. (The work “far in excess” should not be narrowly interpreted to cover
increases in such benefits that may be result of buoyancy in the industry or in the
economy as a whole); and
(vi) Any other relevant factors.
3.48 One of the Members of the Committee, while raising some concerns regarding Civil Servants
and other Senior Government officials taking up post-retirement jobs, said as follows:
...So, there could be a reform in the system where at the age of 55 one can be given an option
whether he would like to continue in the Government or go to the private sector. If he wants
to go to the private sector, he need not stay in the Government till 60 years and take a
decision in favour of his future employers and then cause embarrassment to so many people
at a later stage. Or, he can continue in the Government after 55 years, and get lot of posts
in the Government till the age of 65. So, that option should be there. You can’t have the cake
and the carrot. Thirdly, there should be a two-year cooling period, after retirement, for any
post-retirement job. You don’t retire; on the day of retirement, you are given a five-year
extension...
3.49 The Committee takes note of such concerns and feels that the possibility of Senior
Officials who hold vital and sensitive posts while in Government, being influenced by extraneous
factors related to their intention to take up post-retirement jobs, cannot be ignored. In order
to avoid the incidence of such considerations, the Committee desires that the Ministry may
consider this aspect in depth and come out with fresh guidelines on the subject  in consultation
with  concerned stake holders. The Committee recommends that this exercise be done within
a definite time-frame of six months.

CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION
4.1 The Committee, while examining the Demands for Grants, had an opportunity to peruse the
Annual Report of CVC (2011). In the process, the Committee considered the following issues:
Chief Vigilance Officers
4.2 The Committee took note that as on date, six Departments of the Government of India,
(i.e. CBDT, CBEC, Department of Telecom, Department of Posts, Ministry of Railways and CPWD)
and a majority of the Public Sector Enterprises, Public Sector Banks and Insurance Companies have
full time CVOs while others have only part-time CVOs. The functions of CVOs in other organisations
are performed by part time CVOs who are officers of appropriate level from within the organizations
concerned.
4.3 The Ministry, in its reply furnished to the Committee, has stated that there were 201 posts of
full time CVOs. Out of these 201, three organizations namely (1) National Small Industries Corporation
(NSIC), (2) Indian Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Limited (IDPL) and (3) State Bank of Indore have been
removed from the list. At present there are 198 full time CVOs.
4.4. Also, the Annual Report of the CVC states that at the close of the year 2011, 16987 complaints
were pending with the CVOs concerned for investigation, out of which 6910 complaints were pending
beyond a period of six (6) months.
4.5 The Committee is not satisfied with the current situation that only six Departments of
the Government of India have full time CVOs. The Committee is of the considered view that
CVOs play a vital role in the vigilance mechanism of an Organisation. In order to ensure
financial transparency and accountability, appointment of full time CVOs is an absolute
necessity, so that they can devote their entire time to strengthen the vigilance mechanism and
also ensure  timely disposal of complaints received.
4.6 The Committee strongly recommends that CVC should pursue the matter vigorously
with all the remaining Departments/Ministries and that DoPT should impress upon the Ministries
to take urgent steps to appoint full time CVOs.
4.7 The Committee desires that the CVC should closely monitor the disposal of cases by
CVOs and that necessary assistance/guidance may be given to CVOs to ensure zero pendency,
keeping in view the need for quick and effective action. Most importantly, the Committee
would like the network of the CVOs  in the Government Departments/Organizations to be
truly effective, meeting the purpose for which the institution has been set up. For this
purpose, there should be the requisite and desired support and protection from the CVC to
enable the CVOs to discharge their functions objectively and fearlessly.
4.8 In the Annual Report of the Ministry for 2012-13, it has been stated that the cases of
disagreement between the CVC/CBI and Sanctioning/Disciplinary Authority in the matter of Prosecution

4.12 While going through the Annual Report of CVC, the Committee noted the cases of non-
compliance and delay in the implementation of the Commission’s advice by the organisations falling
under its jurisdiction and also the cases of “selective approach”. The Report states that Cases of
“selective approach” are those cases where the organizations falling under the jurisdiction of the
Commission failed to seek the Commission’s advice in vigilance related matters with respect to officers
under the Commission’s normal or mandatory jurisdiction. Secondly, unwillingness on the part of
organization to accept the Commission’s advice against some officers also constitutes cases of
“selective approach”.  According to the Report, such failure or unwillingness, as the case may be,
would prima-facie seem to be an attempt by the concerned disciplinary authorities to favour/ dis-
favour certain officers and reflect adversely on the creditability, objectivity and impartiality of vigilance
administrations. Whenever, such cases come to the notice of the Commission, its concerns are
conveyed to the organizations concerned.
4.13 The Committee is disappointed to note that, as reflected in the Annual Report of CVC,
in some of the cases, either the due consultation process with the Commission have not been
followed despite the fact that the suspected public servants were falling within the categories
where consultation with Commission was mandated or the advice had only partly been acted
upon or were badly delayed. The Committee also notes that there have been instances where
the advice of the Commission has been diluted considerably without approaching the Commission
for reconsideration of its advice. The Committee feels that such instances undermine the
mandate of the CVC and accordingly desires that suitable steps may be taken to prevent such

Application of RTI Act to entities under PPP arrangement
4.64 Regarding the application of provisions of the RTI Act to various entities under the Public
Private Partnership (PPP) arrangement, the CIC has observed, in its Annual Report that due to lack
of clarity about this at various levels and especially since the RTI Act does not expressly refer to such
bodies while defining the Public Authorities; a lot of confusion persists and such entities have, by and
large, remained outside the purview of the RTI Act. Even the Ministries and Departments concerned
in the Government do not volunteer to provide information regarding these entities by claiming that
these are private bodies outside the Government control.
4.65 In this regard, the Ministry replied that Section 2(f) of the RTI Act, 2005 enables an
information seeker to access information relating to a private body from a public authority with whom
that information is available. The information that is to be provided to the public authority by private
party in PPP could be provided under RTI. In addition, if Public services are proposed to be provided
through a Public Private Partnership (PPP), all information relating to the PPPs should be disclosed
in the public domain by the Public Authority entering into the PPP contract/concession agreement.
This should include details of the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), if any set up, detailed project reports,
concession agreements, operation and maintenance manuals and other documents generated as part of
the implementation of the PPP project. Further, information about fees, tolls, or other kinds of revenue
that may be collected under authorization from the Government, information in respect of outputs and
outcomes, process of selection of the private sector party should also be disclosed. All payments made
under the PPP project should also be disclosed, along with the purpose of making such payment.
However, the documents under the ambit of the exemption from disclosure of information under
section 8(1)(d) and 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act should not be disclosed.
4.66 The Committee is of the view that the role and responsibility of the PPP entity for
implementing the RTI Act should be clarified, in order to bring in greater transparency in
implementation of such projects. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may take
appropriate action in this regard.

The Committee impress upon the Ministry as well as the CVC to  take stern action against

of the Commission has been diluted considerably without approaching the Commission for
reconsideration of its advice. The Committee feels that such instances undermine the mandate
of the CVC and accordingly desires that suitable steps may be taken to prevent such
incidences. The Committee  accordingly,  directs the Ministry to take corrective measures in
consultation with the CVC. (Para 4.13)

Vacancies in CBI
61. The Committee recognizes the difficulties faced by CBI in getting Officers/personnel on
deputation. However, the Committee had, on earlier occasions, in its 24th, 38th and 51st Reports
recommended that if vacancies prevail due to non-availability of officers on deputation, these
may be filled up by taking recourse to direct recruitment or promotion. However, the Committee
is unhappy to note that not much has been done in this direction. The Committee wishes to drive
home the point that a premier investigating agency such as CBI should have an independent and
proficient departmental Cadre of its own, rather than being dependent on the States and other
Organisations to spare their officers/personnel for deputation in CBI. (Para 4.36)

45
62. The Committee fails to understand as to how the vacancy position can be expected to
improve by not resorting to direct recruitment at such levels. (Para 4.38)
63. The Committee is unable to comprehend the reason for fixing such a high percentage
as the quota for deputation in the ranks from Constable to Joint Director. The Committee is
apprehensive that the policy of not opting for direct recruitment in the ranks of Constable and
Dy. SP would, in the long run, lead to a situation wherein CBI would be totally dependent on
other Departments/States for their personnel. Therefore, the Committee recommends that this
policy should be re-looked and the Committee may be apprised of the action taken in this
regard in the Ministry’s Action Taken Reply. (Para 4.41)
64. The Committee does recognize the utility and the desirability  to have deputation as
one of the channel to have manpower in the CBI. Deputationists  bring in a different exposure
into the organization. But, the Committee is more than convinced that  the organization
should never be dominant by the deputationists as such a state would never be congenial for
the functioning of the CBI in the desired spirit. The CBI should have  strong cadres of their
own at all levels so that it can implement its procedures and practices discretely, uniformly,
confidently and with a continuity. (Para 4.42)
65. The Committee takes cognizance of the above deposition of the Director, CBI. In this
regard, the Committee strongly feels that a delinquent official/personnel should be meted out
disciplinary action, irrespective of whether he is a deputationist or cadre personnel. The
argument, that punitive action is taken against cadre personnel, while a deputationist is
merely repatriated to his parent Department, in itself, is indicative that unequal working
conditions prevail in the organization for the departmental personnel vis-a-vis the deputationists.
The Committee does not see this as a healthy trend. In Committee’s view, the above
statement of Director, CBI reflects only the state of helplessness in relation to the situation
of manpower in the CBI  which is not a desirable situation.  The Committee also desires that
Cadre personnel/officers should be given a major role in policy making. (Para 4.44)
66. Further, the Committee is disappointed to note that as reflected in the Annual Report
of CBI, deputationist officers in the rank of SP were directly promoted as DIG, whereas cadre
officers in the rank of SP were promoted as SSP only which is a lower rank. Should  the
Committee derive that the departmental officer is placed at a disadvantageous position,
although he is having an obvious edge over the deputationist, in terms of skill, experience and
expertise, owing to his continuous exposure to specialized investigation which is regularly being
done in CBI? (Para 4.45)
67. Taking into account the above mentioned facts, the Committee strongly recommends
that sincere efforts should be made to ensure that the departmental personnel get adequate
promotional avenues and that their expertise and knowledge should be optimally utilized.
(Para 4.46)
