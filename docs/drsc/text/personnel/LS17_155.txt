R E P O R T 
  
 This Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the Legislative 
Department (Ministry of Law and Justice) on the Recommendations/ Observations of the 
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and 
Justice in its One Hundred and Forty Nine (149th) Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants 
(2025-26) of the Legislative Department (Ministry of Law and Justice), which was presented to the 
Rajya Sabha on 27th March, 2025 and laid on the Table of the Lok Sabha on the 27th March, 2025. 
2. The Committee had made 11 recommendations/observations in its 149th    Report. Replies of 
the Department thereto have been categorized and scrutinized under four chapters on the basis of 
satisfaction of the Committee. These replies have been categorized as follows: 
Chapter – I: Recommendations/observations which have been accepted by the Ministry: 
Para:- 2.20 (Total- 01). 
Chapter – II:   Recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue 
in view of the replies given by the Ministry: Paras:- 2.35 and 2.38 (Total-02).  
Chapter – III:   Recommendations/observations in respect of which Committee has not accepted 
the replies of the Ministry: Paras:- 2.21, 2.22, 2.27 and 2.36 (Total-04). 
Chapter – IV:   Recommendations/observations in respect of which final replies of the Ministry 
have not been received by the Committee: Paras:- 3.5, 3.6, 3.10 and 3.11  
(Total-04).  
3. The Committee desires that the Department should furnish pointed and detailed Action 
Taken Notes in respect of all the further Recommendations/Observations made by the Committee in 
Chapter – III of this report. 
4. The details of the ATR have been discussed in the respective Chapters in the succeeding 
pages. For reference purpose, para numbers of the original report (i.e. of 149th Report) have been 
mentioned at the end of each Recommendation/Observation.

CHAPTER I 
 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE 
MINISTRY 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
1.1 The Committee observes that the Department has been able to spend about 80% of the RE 
(2024-25) upto 31st January, 2025. The Committee appreciates the Department for incurring the 
expenditure uniformly as a whole till 31st January, 2025 keeping in view the instructions issued by 
the Ministry of Finance (MoF), wherein the expenditure in the last quarter has to be restricted to 
33% ceiling and in last month (March) expenditure has to be restricted to 15% of ceiling. The 
Committee hopes that the Department shall strive to utilize the budget uniformly in the remaining 
part of FY 2024-25 and in forthcoming financial year 2025-26.             (Para 2.20)  
 
Action Taken 
 
1.2 Recommendations/ observations made by the Committee have been noted for future 
compliance. The Legislative Department will put in its best efforts to comply with the prescribed 
ceilings of expenditure per quarter/month of the financial year, as provided by the Department of 
Expenditure, Ministry of Finance.

CHAPTER II 
 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH THE COMMITTEE DOES NOT 
DESIRE TO PURSUE IN VIEW OF THE REPLIES GIVEN BY THE MINISTRY 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
2.1  The Committee in its previous reports, 101st, 107th, 114th and 130th reports on Demands for 
Grants suggested the Department to take the work related to translation of the Constitution in 
scheduled languages on priority. The Committee also observes that translation work have been 
done upto 106th Amendment in Hindi, Maithili, Malayalam, Marathi, Punjabi, Sanskrit and Urdu 
this year, as the updation work upto 105th amendment was done till the last Demands for Grants. 
However, so far 106 Amendments have taken place.               (Para 2.35) 
Action Taken 
 
2.2 As regards the Committee’s observation, it is submitted that besides Hindi, Maithili, 
Malayalam, Marathi, Punjabi, Sanskrit and Urdu, the Constitution of India is updated up to 106th 
Amendment in Kashmiri language also. The publication of updated version of the Constitution of 
India in Telugu is ready to be published soon. The Central Institute of Indian Languages has been 
assigned the work of translation and updation of the Constitution of India in Assamese, Bodo, 
Gujarati, Konkani, Nepali, Odia, Santhali and Sindhi, and the work is under process. This 
Department is making all efforts to speed up the updation and translation of the Constitution of 
India in Bengali, Dogri, Kannada, Manipuri and Tamil languages, with the cooperation of 
respective State Governments. 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
 
2.3 As far as repealing of Obsolete laws is concerned, the Committee appreciates the work done 
in this regard and that 1824 redundant and obsolete laws have been identified to be repealed. The 
Committee also feels that the laws prevalent in a country should be representative of the political 
and social structure existing at that time. The Committee hopes that the department will undertake 
such exercise of identifying and repealing the obsolete laws on regular and continuous basis in 
future also.                    (Para 2.38) 
 

4 
 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.4 As regards the Committee’s observation, it is stated that the present Government, as part of 
its resolve to bring reforms in the legal system had constituted a two Member Committee in 2014 to 
undertake comprehensive review of laws with a view to remove obsolete and redundant laws. On 
the basis of the recommendations of the said Two Member Committee and the Law Commission, 
the Legislative Department had identified 1824 redundant and obsolete Acts. After due examination 
and consultations with concerned Ministries and Department, a total of 1562 Central Acts have 
since been repealed through various Repealing and Amending Acts, Finance and Income Tax Acts. 
Apart from this 14 more pre-constitution Acts have been repealed through various Acts. Further, as 
different Ministries and Department are allocated different subject Matters, this Department has, 
vide its various D.O. letters, time and again requested all Ministries/Departments to take immediate 
necessary action to ensure the obsolete laws administered by them are repealed or re-enacted if so 
required as expeditiously as possible. Further, the Twenty-third Law Commission of India has been 
constituted with a mandate inter alia to examine review/repeal of obsolete laws.

Recommendation/Observation 
3.1  The Committee observes that huge reductions in allocations in BE (2025-26) over the 
projections made to the Ministry of Finance for the year 2025-26 under various heads have been 
done, namely Legislative Department (about 36%), Official Language Wing(OLW) (about 61%) 
and Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan (about 27%) on the revenue Side and EVMs for Election Commission

Department is failing on its part to seek realistic financial projection targets from the Ministry of

therefore, of the view the department should improve its financial forecasting methods using past 
expenditure trends to prevent such large discrepancies between its projections and actual 
allocations. Further, the department should engage with the Ministry of Finance (MoF) regularly to 
avoid budget cuts.                     (Para 2.21)

Recommendation/Observation 
 
3.15  The Committee, accordingly, recommends that the work related to translation and updation 
of the Constitution of India in all the Scheduled languages of the Constitution be expedited. The 
Committee, further, recommends that a policy framework or a legal mandate may be introduced by 
the Department to ensure that every constitutional amendment is translated within a fixed 
timeframe in all scheduled languages. Further, the feasibility of outsourcing a part of the

may be explored.                   (Para 2.36)  
 
Action Taken 
 
3.16 As regards the Committee’s observation, it is submitted that there is already a constitutional 
mandate on the part of the Department to make available the Constitution of India in Regional 
Languages and the Regional Languages Unit. Official Language Wing is making all efforts to 
expedite the translation and updation work of the Constitution of India in all Languages enshrined 
in the 8th Schedule to the Constitution of India, i.e., Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, 
Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, Sanskrit, Sindhi, Kashmiri, Nepali, 
Manipuri, Dogri, Bodo, Santhali, Maithili and Konkani. With the cooperation of the concerned 
State Governments, efforts are being made to update the Indian Constitution into the respective 
language within the time frame.  
  
3.17 It is also mentioned that the translation of Constitution of India in the respective language is 
provided by the concerned State Governments. For the languages for which no officer is available 
in Regional Languages Unit, the Department has entrusted the work of updation and translation to 
the Central Institute of Indian Languages (CIIL), a government organisation under the aegis of 
Ministry of Education. The translation of any statute or law should be done by the person who are 
expert in legal language. Every State Government has a Directorate of Language and Commission, 

11 
 
who have their own infrastructure with reference to printing, publication and experts of legal 
language for translation. Hence, it is not feasible to outsource translation work to language experts 
or Universities.   
 
Further Recommendation/Observation: 
3.18 The Committee notes the submission of the Ministry that outsourcing the translation 
work to language experts or universities may not be feasible due to administrative and 
procedural constraints. However, the Committee emphasizes that the timely translation of 
every Constitutional Amendment is of utmost importance to ensure consistency and 
accessibility of legal texts in all official languages. Therefore, it recommends that the 
Department should explore and adopt an effective alternative mechanism, to ensure that all 
translation work is completed within the fixed timeframe.

CHAPTER - IV 
 
THE RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS IN RESPECT OF WHICH FINAL 
REPLIES OF THE MINISTRY HAVE NOT BEEN RECEIVED 
 
Recommendation/Observation 
4.1 It is also observed that out of the total allocation of Rs 361.76 crore in RE(2024-25), Rs 
268.34 crore has been spent upto 31st January, 2025, which is approximately 74 percent of RE 
(2024-25). The Committee appreciates the Commission for incurring the expenditure uniformly as a 
whole till 31st January, 2025 keeping in view the instructions issued by the Ministry of Finance 
(MoF), wherein the expenditure in the last quarter has to be restricted to 33% ceiling and in last 
month (March) expenditure has to be restricted to 15% of ceiling. The Committee hopes that the 
Commission will be able to utilize the remaining budget for the remaining part of Financial year 
2024-25 uniformily.                            (Para 3.5)  
4.2 However, the Committee, also observes that out of the total allocation of Rs. 27.91 crore in 
RE (2024-25) under the Capital head, the commission has been able to spent only Rs. 3.93 crore 
upto 31st January, 2025 which is about 14 percent of the RE (2024-25). The Committee fails to 
understand the reason behind such under-utilization of funds. Low capital expenditure utilization 
(14% of RE 2024-25 spent as of 31st January, 2025) indicates either inefficiencies in fund 
deployment or delays in procurement. Accordingly, the Committee recommends the ECI to improve 
its spending capacity on its capital side and ensure timely utilization of allocated capital funds, as 
non- expenditure tantamount to under-utilisation of resources.                   (Para 3.6) 
4.3 The Committee observes that out of the total 850 post, 275 posts are vacant which is about 
32% of the total sanctioned strength. The Committee shows its concern as a large number of 
vacancies still exist, especially at the level of Assistant Section Officer, Senior Secretariat Assistant, 
Steno Grade 'D', Junior Secretariat Assistant, MTS which is hampering its working. The vacancies 
in the Commission have accumulated over a period of time and accordingly, recommends the 
Commission to expedite the process of recruitment.                    (Para 3.10) 
4.4 The Committee is given to understand that whenever the elections are announced in states, 
the Model Code of Conduct comes into place immediately on announcement and trade gets hit. 
Though, Digital transactions have made a huge impact, still a large portion of the country’s 
economy is cash economy. In the pretext of implementation of the Model Code of Conduct, even the 

13 
 
genuine transactions of the traders are seized which impacts the trader and the economy. The 
Committee desires that ECI should have a mechanism to verify the purpose for which the money is 
being transacted. On verification, if it is found to be for legal purpose, then the entire money should 
be released immediately.                       (Para 3.11)  
Further Recommendation/Observation:  
4.5  The Committee has not received replies to recommendations of the Committee of the 
above paras i.e., 3.5, 3.6, 3.10 and 3.11 from the Legislative Department/Election Commission.  
The Committee would like to remind the Department that Action taken Replies should be 
furnished before the Committee within three months from the day when Report on Demands 
for Grants is presented in Parliament.
